# Response to follow-up criticisms of the Project A referee report

**Date:** 24 October 2026. Conventions as in the packet and the report. Throughout, "\(a\) is hyperconnected" means \(\downarrow a\) is a hyperconnected frame (any two nonzero elements below \(a\) meet); for \(a\in L\) and \(e\in L\) I write \(a\boxtimes e\) for the rectangle in \(Q=L\oplus L\). Two standard coproduct facts are used: (i) \(a\boxtimes(\bigvee_i q_i)=\bigvee_i a\boxtimes q_i\); (ii) if \(u\ne0\ne v\) and \(u\boxtimes v\le a\boxtimes e\), then \(u\le a\) and \(v\le e\) (the C-ideal generated by \((a,e)\) is \(\downarrow(a,e)\cup(L\times0)\cup(0\times L)\)).

## 1. M2 — finite Alexandrov closed sets

You are right: the sentence "every closed subset of a finite Alexandrov space is regular closed" is false (two-element chain, \(\{0\}\)). The correct general statement is: in a finite Alexandrov space (opens = up-sets), a closed set \(F\) satisfies \(\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}F=\{x:\ x\le m\text{ for some ambient-maximal }m\in F\}\), so \(F\) is regular closed iff every element of \(F\) lies below an ambient-maximal element of \(F\). Your repair is exactly the verification that \(\mathcal U(P)\) has this property: \((x,y)\in\mathcal U(P)\iff\downarrow\!\uparrow\! x\cap\downarrow\!\uparrow\! y\ne\varnothing\), and the witnesses \(u\ge x\), \(v\ge y\) with a common lower bound can be pushed up to ambient-maximal \(m,n\), which still have that common lower bound; \((m,n)\) is then an isolated point of \(P\times P\) lying in \(\mathcal U(P)\) above \((x,y)\). This is the argument I intended and I adopt it. Nothing else in M2 changes: \(G(S\times P)=0\) and F3 follows from E2.

## 2. Section 5 — the hyperconnected obstruction

### 2.1 The error

Your counterexample is correct. The lemma \(u^*=a^*\) for \(0\ne u\le a\) is fine, but I then inferred "\(u\boxtimes v\le d_U\Rightarrow a^*\vee v^*=1\)" as if a rectangle below \(d_U\) had to be far. That is false precisely because the set \(\{q:\ a^*\vee q^*=1\}\) is not closed under joins (\(q\mapsto q^*\) is not), so \(d_U\), a join of far rectangles, contains non-far rectangles. In your space, \(e:=\bigvee\{q:\ a^*\vee q^*=1\}=\mathbb N\) is not itself far from \(a=\{t\}\).

### 2.2 What the lemma actually gives

**Lemma 1 (pure-rectangle form).** Let \(a\in L\) be hyperconnected and put
\[
e_a:=\bigvee\{q\in L:\ a^*\vee q^*=1\}.
\]
Then \(d_U\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=a\boxtimes e_a\). Consequently, for \(0\ne u\le a\) and any \(v\): \(u\boxtimes v\le d_U\iff v\le e_a\). Moreover \(e_a\le a^*\) (for \(a\neq 0\)).

*Proof.* \(a\boxtimes e_a=\bigvee_{a^*\vee q^*=1}a\boxtimes q\le d_U\) by (i). Conversely \(d_U\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=\bigvee\{(p\wedge a)\boxtimes q:\ p^*\vee q^*=1\}\). If \(p\wedge a\ne0\) then \(p^*\le(p\wedge a)^*=a^*\), so \(a^*\vee q^*=1\) and \(q\le e_a\). The equivalence follows from (ii). For \(e_a\le a^*\): if \(a^*\vee q^*=1\) and \(q\wedge a\ne0\), then \(q^*\le(q\wedge a)^*=a^*\), forcing \(a^*=1\). \(\square\)

So the correct replacement for the erroneous equivalence is "\(v\le e_a\)", and the erroneous one holds exactly when \(e_a\) is itself far from \(a\) (e.g. when \(a^*\) is complemented, in which case \(e_a=a^*\)). In spatial terms, for a hyperconnected open \(a\) every nonempty open \(u\subseteq a\) has \(\overline u=\overline a\), and \(\mathcal U\cap(a\times X)=a\times F_a\) with \(F_a=\{y:\ \overline v\cap\overline a\ne\varnothing\text{ for every open }v\ni y\}\) — in your example \(F_a=\{z,t\}\).

### 2.3 The obstruction, recovered and strengthened

The key is that the "constancy along \(a\)" of Lemma 1 is preserved by the binary operator and lets an arbitrary cover be replaced by a binary one. Say \(d\in Q\) has property **(H)** if \(d\wedge(a\boxtimes1)\) is a pure rectangle \(a\boxtimes e\) for every hyperconnected \(a\). (By (ii), \(e\) then depends only on \(d\), and \(d\wedge(a'\boxtimes1)=a'\boxtimes e\) for every \(0\ne a'\le a\).)

**Lemma 2 (binary reduction).** Let \(d_0\) have (H) with \(\Phi_2(d_0)=d_0\), and let \(d\ge d_0\) satisfy \(d\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=d_0\wedge(a\boxtimes1)\) for every hyperconnected \(a\). Then \(\Phi(d)\wedge(a\boxtimes1)\le d_0\) for every hyperconnected \(a\).

*Proof.* \(\Phi(d)=\bigvee_{\mathscr C}(w_{\mathscr C}\to d)\). Let \(x\le w_{\mathscr C}\to d\), \(x=\bigvee_jp_j\boxtimes q_j\); it suffices to show \((p_j\wedge a)\boxtimes q_j\le d_0\). Put \(a'=p_j\wedge a\), \(q=q_j\); if \(a'=0\) there is nothing to prove, so let \(a'\neq0\) (hyperconnected, \(\le a\)) and let \(a'\boxtimes e=d_0\wedge(a'\boxtimes1)\). For each \(c\in\mathscr C\) with \(c\wedge a'\ne0\): \((a'\wedge c)\boxtimes(q\wedge c)\le d\wedge(a'\boxtimes1)=a'\boxtimes e\), so \(q\wedge c\le e\) by (ii), hence \(a'\boxtimes(q\wedge c)\le a'\boxtimes e\le d_0\). Put
\[
c_1=\bigvee\{c\in\mathscr C:\ c\wedge a'\ne0\},\qquad c_2=\bigvee\{c\in\mathscr C:\ c\wedge a'=0\}.
\]
Then \(c_1\vee c_2=1\) and \(a'\wedge c_2=0\), so
\[
(a'\boxtimes q)\wedge v(c_1,c_2)=a'\boxtimes(q\wedge c_1)=\bigvee_{c\wedge a'\neq0}a'\boxtimes(q\wedge c)\le d_0 .
\]
Hence \(a'\boxtimes q\le v(c_1,c_2)\to d_0\le\Phi_2(d_0)=d_0\). \(\square\)

**Lemma 3.** \(d_U\) has (H) (Lemma 1), and (H) is preserved by \(\Phi_2\) and by arbitrary joins. Hence \(d_{W_2}=\Phi_2^{\infty}(d_U)\) has (H).

*Proof.* Joins: \((\bigvee d_\alpha)\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=\bigvee a\boxtimes e_\alpha=a\boxtimes\bigvee e_\alpha\) by (i). For \(\Phi_2\): let \(d\) have (H), \(a\) hyperconnected, \(x\le w_F\to d\) for a finite cover \(F\), \(x=\bigvee p_j\boxtimes q_j\). With \(a'=p_j\wedge a\ne0\) and \(c_1,c_2\) built from \(F\) as in Lemma 2 (\(c_1,c_2\) are now finite joins), hyperconnectedness of \(a\) gives \(a\wedge c_2=0\) (otherwise \(a\wedge c_2\) and \(a'\) would meet inside \(\downarrow a\)), and as before \(a\boxtimes(q_j\wedge c_1)\le a\boxtimes e\le d\). So \((a\boxtimes q_j)\wedge v(c_1,c_2)\le d\), i.e. \(a\boxtimes q_j\le\Phi_2(d)\). Thus \(\Phi_2(d)\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=\bigvee\{a\boxtimes q:\ a\boxtimes q\le\Phi_2(d)\}\) is a pure rectangle. \(\square\)

**Theorem (unconditional localization of \(\mathcal W\neq\mathcal W_2\)).** Let \(h_L=\bigvee\{a\in L:\ a\text{ hyperconnected}\}\) and \(o=h_L\boxtimes1\vee1\boxtimes h_L\). Then
\[
d_W\wedge o=d_{W_2}\wedge o,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \mathcal W\cap O(o)=\mathcal W_2\cap O(o).
\]
Equivalently, \(\mathcal W\) and \(\mathcal W_2\) can differ only inside the closed sublocale \(C(o)=C(h_L)\times C(h_L)\), the square of the "nowhere-hyperconnected" closed part of \(X\).

*Proof.* By transfinite induction, \(\Phi^\alpha(d_{W_2})\wedge(a\boxtimes1)\le d_{W_2}\) for every hyperconnected \(a\) and every \(\alpha\): the successor step is Lemma 2 with \(d_0=d_{W_2}\) (which is \(\Phi_2\)-fixed and has (H) by Lemma 3) and \(d=\Phi^\alpha(d_{W_2})\), whose restriction to \(a\boxtimes1\) agrees with that of \(d_{W_2}\) by the induction hypothesis; limits are joins. Hence \(d_W\wedge(a\boxtimes1)=d_{W_2}\wedge(a\boxtimes1)\); join over hyperconnected \(a\) (using \(d\wedge(\bigvee a_i\boxtimes1)=\bigvee d\wedge(a_i\boxtimes1)\)) and use the coordinate symmetry of \(d_U,\Phi,\Phi_2\). \(\square\)

**Corollaries.**

1. **(Q′), hence (Q), holds for every frame in which hyperconnected elements cover** (\(h_L=1\)): \(\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D_2=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D\). For sober spaces this is the class with a basis of irreducible (equivalently, in a sober space, opens containing a point dense in them) open sets: Scott topologies of dcpos with local tops, Alexandrov spaces of posets in which every \(\uparrow x\) is directed, products of such, and in particular the Scott space \(S\) of §8 — giving a second proof of F2 that does not use \(d_U=0\).
2. **A G4 witness \(r=a\boxtimes b\) must satisfy \(r\wedge o\le d_U\):** neither coordinate can meet the hyperconnected part in a way that matters. This is the obstruction I was after, now proved: in any gluing of Scott-type pieces the pieces themselves are inert, and a counterexample to (Q) or (Q′) has to be manufactured entirely inside \(C(h_L)\times C(h_L)\) (and, by E3, inside the nonregular sector \(G\)).
3. For the packet's examples: \(C(h)=0\) for \(S\); \(C(h)=S\times\{1\}\) for \(S\times P\); \(C(h)=\{z\}\) for your space; \(C(h)=\{1\}\) for the zigzag \(P\); and \(C(h)=X\) for the \(\mathbb R\sqcup\{p,q\}\) example of §6 of my report, which is why (Q)-style failures (\(\mathcal W_2\ne\mathcal U\)) are possible there. The theorem thus explains uniformly why none of the Scott-based constructions could produce anything.

### 2.4 Answers to the two questions

*Additional hypothesis for the rectangle equivalence.* None is needed once it is stated correctly: for hyperconnected \(a\) and \(0\ne u\le a\), \(u\boxtimes v\le d_U\iff v\le e_a\) (Lemma 1). The form I wrote, \(a^*\vee v^*=1\), is equivalent only when \(e_a\) is far from \(a\), e.g. when \(a^*\) is complemented.

*Recovery of the restriction on gluing constructions.* Yes, and in a stronger form: the correct consequence of hyperconnectedness is not a constraint on pseudocomplements of cover members but the fact that on \(a\boxtimes 1\) every arbitrary-cover descent problem reduces to a binary one (Lemma 2). This gives the unconditional theorem above. The specific sentence in my report — that a counterexample needs failing-cover generators with distinct pseudocomplements — should be deleted and replaced by Corollary 2.
