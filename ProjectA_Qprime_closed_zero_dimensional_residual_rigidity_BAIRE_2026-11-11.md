# Project A — Q′ residual rigidity: closed zero-dimensional cores and a perfect Baire boundary

**11 November 2026. New INTERNAL working theorem + connected counter-control; not independently audited.** Direct continuation of [mixed coverwise compact remnants](ProjectA_Qprime_mixed_coverwise_compact_remnants_CONNECTED_2026-11-11.md). It does **not** solve the universal questions \(Q'\) or \(Q\). The chief new theorem is that **finite-cover fitting restricts to the diagonal on the square of ANY closed zero-dimensional sublocale**, even when it is noncompact, pointless, or nonspatial. Together with the established mixed strip localization it yields a noncompact, non-scattered residual criterion for \(Q'\). A connected second-countable sober control has a full closed Baire-space residual and \(\mathcal D<\mathcal D_2\) but nevertheless \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2\).

## 0. Framework and established dependencies

For \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), let \(Q=L\oplus L\), \(w_\mathcal C=\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}c\boxtimes c\),
\[
\mathcal D=\bigwedge_{\mathcal C\text{ cover}}\mathfrak o(w_\mathcal C),\
\mathcal D_2=\bigwedge_{F\text{ finite cover}}\mathfrak o(w_F),\
\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D,\quad
\mathcal W_2=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D_2=C(d_0).
\]
Q′ asks whether \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal W\) for every locale. Q asks whether \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\) forces \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\). **Both open.**

For any cover \(\mathcal C\), let
\[
t_{\mathcal C}=
\bigvee_{\substack{F\subseteq\mathcal C\\ F\text{ finite}}}
\bigvee\{v:v\prec\bigvee F\},\qquad
v\prec u\iff v^*\vee u=1.
\tag{0.1}
\]
An open \(e\) is **hull-sound on strips** if
\[
\mathcal W_2\wedge\mathfrak o(e\boxtimes1\vee1\boxtimes e)
=\mathcal W\wedge\mathfrak o(e\boxtimes1\vee1\boxtimes e).
\tag{0.2}
\]
The previous hyperconnected-strip result gives \(e=h_{\rm hc}\), the join of all hyperconnected opens (an inherited *working* result, not to be silently upgraded to independent external certification). The stronger compact-closed-neighborhood strips can also be added.

**Imported exact witness/localization:** If \(\mathcal W<\mathcal W_2\), there is a cover \(\mathcal C\) (no finite subcover) and a disjoint rectangle \(r=a\boxtimes b\not\le d_0\) with
\[
r\wedge w_\mathcal C\le d_0,\qquad r\le d_\mathcal W.
\tag{0.3}
\]
Let \(\ell_\mathcal C=t_\mathcal C\vee e\). The reviewed rather-below localization and (0.2) imply
\[
r\wedge(\ell_\mathcal C\boxtimes1\vee1\boxtimes\ell_\mathcal C)\le d_0.
\tag{0.4}
\]
Hence the **nonzero relatively open**
\[
E=\mathcal W_2\wedge\mathfrak o(r)
\tag{0.5}
\]
is supported in
\[
K_\mathcal C\times K_\mathcal C,\qquad
K_\mathcal C=\mathfrak c(\ell_\mathcal C),
\tag{0.6}
\]
and satisfies
\[
\mathcal D\wedge E=0,\qquad
\mathcal D_2\wedge E\ \text{dense in }E.
\tag{0.7}
\]
The support statement uses that the meet of the two pullbacks of the closed sublocale \(K_\mathcal C\) is its localic square \(K_\mathcal C\times K_\mathcal C\). This is an equality of sublocales, not just a point-set inclusion.

## 1. Main new lemma: finite covers see all clopens in a CLOSED sublocale

**Lemma 1.1 (closed clopen detection).** Let \(i:K\hookrightarrow X\) be **any closed sublocale**, and let \(f,g:T\to K\) be arbitrary locale maps, where T is any locale. Suppose their pair into \(X^2\) factors through \(\mathcal D_2(X)\). Then
\[
\boxed{f^*(e)=g^*(e)\quad
\text{for every complemented }e\in\mathcal O(K).}
\tag{1.1}
\]
No assumption on compactness, regularity, spatiality, sobriety, or choice of points is used.

*Proof.* Let \(a\in\mathcal O(X)\) define the closed sublocale \(K=\mathfrak c(a)\). The restriction \(i^*:\mathcal O(X)\to\mathcal O(K)\) is surjective. For a complemented e in \(\mathcal O(K)\), choose opens u,v∈L with \(i^*(u)=e\), \(i^*(v)=e^c\). Since \(i^*(u\vee v)=1_K\), we have \(a\vee u\vee v=1_X\). Thus \(\{a,u,v\}\) is a finite cover of X. Both endpoint maps factor K, so \(f^*i^*(a)=g^*i^*(a)=0\). Finite compatibility gives
\[
1_T=f^*(e)\wedge g^*(e)\ \vee\
f^*(e^c)\wedge g^*(e^c).
\tag{1.2}
\]
Put \(p=f^*(e),\,q=g^*(e)\). These are complemented elements of \(\mathcal O(T)\), with complements \(f^*(e^c),g^*(e^c)\). Meeting (1.2) with p and then with q gives p≤q and q≤p, respectively. Hence p=q. □

Let \(\mathcal O(K)_0\) denote the subframe of \(\mathcal O(K)\) **generated by its complemented opens**, and \(q_0:K\to K_0\) the locale quotient determined by inclusion \(\mathcal O(K)_0\hookrightarrow\mathcal O(K)\). This is the universal *zero-dimensional quotient* (zero-dimensional reflection) of K: a map from K to a zero-dimensional locale factors uniquely through q₀ because inverse images preserve complemented elements and their joins. Lemma 1.1 yields the intrinsically stated containment
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_2(X)\wedge(K\times K)
\ \le\ K\times_{K_0}K.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

**Corollary 1.2 (exact closed zero-dimensional slice).** If K is **zero-dimensional**, then \(\mathcal O(K)_0=\mathcal O(K)\), so (1.1) forces \(f=g\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_2(X)\wedge(K\times K)=\Delta_K.}
\tag{1.4}
\]
The reverse inclusion follows from \(\Delta_X\le\mathcal D(X)\le\mathcal D_2(X)\). In particular \(\mathcal D(X)\) has the same restriction there.

**Caution:** Closedness of K is essential to the simple three-element cover \(\{a,u,v\}\). The result is not asserted for an arbitrary nonclosed zero-dimensional sublocale K.

## 2. New Q′ criterion with NO compactness or scatteredness assumption

**Theorem 2.1 (coverwise zero-dimensional residual).** Let e be any hull-sound open on coordinate strips. If
\[
\boxed{K_\mathcal C=\mathfrak c(t_\mathcal C\vee e)
\text{ is zero-dimensional for EVERY cover }\mathcal C,}
\tag{2.1}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2.}
\tag{2.2}
\]
In particular \(e=h_{\rm hc}\) is an available working instance.

*Proof.* Suppose W<W₂ and choose \(\mathcal C,r,E\) from §0. The nonzero \(\mathcal D_2\wedge E\) (dense in nonzero open E) is supported in K_C². By Corollary 1.2,
\[
0\ne \mathcal D_2\wedge E
\le\mathcal D_2\wedge(K_C^2)
=\Delta_{K_C}\le\mathcal D,
\]
contradicting \(\mathcal D\wedge E=0\). Thus W=W₂. □

**Fixed-boundary corollary 2.2.** Suppose \(K\hookrightarrow X\) is one fixed **closed zero-dimensional sublocale** and, for every cover C,
\[
\mathfrak c(t_C\vee e)\le K.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Then Q′ holds: each coverwise remainder is a **closed sublocale of K**, hence zero-dimensional (images of complemented opens of K generate its quotient frame). No compactness, scatteredness, or spatiality of K is needed.

Thus a hypothetical Q′ counterexample must have SOME cover whose closed remainder is not only noncompact (older theorem) but also **not zero-dimensional** (new theorem), even after removing all available hull-sound strips. More sharply, its nonzero finite-cover witness can live only within a nontrivial fibre of the zero-dimensional quotient \(q_0:K_C\to(K_C)_0\), by (1.3).

## 3. Complementary positive class: totally spatial remainder squares

**Theorem 3.1 (totally spatial square criterion).** Under the same hull-sound-strip framework, Q′ holds whenever
\[
\boxed{K_\mathcal C\times K_\mathcal C
\text{ has NO nonzero pointless sublocale, for every cover }\mathcal C.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Indeed a counterexample yields the nonzero sublocale \(\mathcal D_2\wedge E\le K_C^2\), but it has **no points**, since it is disjoint from D and pt(D₂)=pt(D) for every locale. Contradiction.

One sufficient instance: every K_C is the locale of a **locally compact Hausdorff scattered space** (possibly noncompact and non-zero-dimensional). The ordinary product represents the localic product in this setting, and the finite product of scattered spaces is scattered: given a nonempty subset of the product, choose an isolated point of its projection onto the first factor, then an isolated point in the corresponding second-coordinate fibre. Every nonzero sublocale of a scattered space has a point: iterate the Cantor–Bendixson derivative of the space; at each stage the isolated-point open stratum is discrete, so any nonzero sublocale intersecting it has a point; a pointless sublocale would lie in each succeeding closed derivative, impossible at the terminal empty derivative. This is consistent with the classical Simmons/Niefield–Rosenthal total-spatiality theory, and independently reproves the property needed here.

This criterion is complementary to the zero-dimensional criterion: a nonregular finite Alexandrov space is scattered but need not be zero-dimensional; conversely noncompact Baire space is zero-dimensional but is not scattered.

**Scope:** A locally compact scattered space with nonspatial *localic product* would require separate analysis. The statement above assumes locally compact **Hausdorff** K_C so that K_C² is represented by the ordinary spatial product, avoiding an automatic pointwise-product assumption for arbitrary locales.

## 4. Hard control: a noncompact PERFECT zero-dimensional residual

Let \(Y=\ell^2\) (separable infinite-dimensional real Hilbert space), \(S=\mathcal P(\mathbb N)\setminus\{\varnothing\}\) with the Scott topology (hyperconnected, noncompact, sober and second-countable), and \(T=\omega^\omega\) the noncompact, **perfect, nowhere locally compact, zero-dimensional Polish Baire space**.

As a set let
\[
X=Y\sqcup S\sqcup T.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Declare Y and S to be disjoint open subspaces carrying their original topologies, and for every t∈T give a basis of neighborhoods
\[
N(U,n,F)= U\ \cup\ \{y\in Y:\|y\|>n\}\ \cup\ \uparrow F,
\tag{4.2}
\]
where U is an ordinary open T-neighborhood of t, n∈N, and F⊆N finite nonempty. (These sets are the basic open neighborhoods for ALL t∈U; intersections give N(U∩V,max(n,m),F∪G).)

**Topology and geometry.**
- Y and S are open; T is closed with its original Baire topology.
- X is second countable: combine bases of Y,S,T and countably many n,F. It is T₀.
- \(t\in\overline Y\cap\overline S\) for every t∈T, while \(\overline Y=Y\cup T\), \(\overline S=S\cup T\). Both Y and S are connected, and their connected closures meet along the nonempty T; hence X is **connected**.
- X is sober: if nonempty irreducible closed A meets either open sober piece Y or S, then A∩Y or A∩S is nonempty irreducible closed in that piece, with unique generic y or s. As a nonempty open of the irreducible A it is dense in A; thus A=cl_X{y} or cl_X{s}. If A meets neither open piece, A⊆T and is irreducible closed in Hausdorff T, hence singleton. Uniqueness follows from T₀.
- X is noncompact because the closed Baire subspace T is noncompact. It is nonregular since the open Scott sublocale S is nonregular; hence noncoherent.
- \(h_{\rm hc}=S\). Every nonempty open subset of S is hyperconnected. Any open meeting Y contains disjoint nonempty Y-opens; any T-neighborhood meets Y, so no hyperconnected open meets Y or T.

**Rather-below patches cover the Hilbert piece.**
For any cover C of X and any y∈Y, choose c∈C with y∈c and a small bounded open metric ball b around y whose closed ball lies in c∩Y. The closed ball is closed in X (all T-neighborhoods have sufficiently distant Hilbert tails), so \(b\prec c\). Thus
\[
\boxed{Y\subseteq t_C\quad\text{for EVERY cover C}.}\tag{4.3}
\]
With e=h_hc=S, the closed remainder \(K_C=C(t_C\vee S)\) is a closed sublocale of T, hence **zero-dimensional** for all C. Theorem 2.1 proves
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X).}\tag{4.4}
\]

**Yet \(\mathcal D(X)<\mathcal D_2(X)\).**
Let \(B=\operatorname{RO}(2^\omega)\), and define the standard Scott/Cantor frame maps \(h_+,h_-:\mathcal O(S)\to B\) by
\[
h_+(U)=j\left(
\bigcup_{\substack{F\text{ finite nonempty}\\\uparrow F\subseteq U}}
\{z:z(n)=1\text{ for every }n\in F\}\right),\quad
h_-(U)=j\left(
\bigcup_{\substack{F\text{ finite nonempty}\\\uparrow F\subseteq U}}
\{z:z(n)=0\text{ for every }n\in F\}\right),
\tag{4.5}
\]
where j is regular-open/double-negation quotient. These are frame maps: finite-meet preservation follows from union of finite conditions; for arbitrary joins, if a finite F belongs to a union of Scott opens, it belongs to some member. Their top images are dense (all Cantor sequences except one constant endpoint), hence top in B.

Every finite cover of S has some member containing infinitely many singleton subsets \(\{n\}\); its images under h_+ and h_- both equal 1_B, since unions of the corresponding infinitely many positive and negative coordinate cylinders are dense in Cantor space. Thus (h_+,h_-) is finite-cover compatible. But for each n,
\[
h_+(\uparrow\{n\})=[n=1],\
h_-(\uparrow\{n\})=[n=0],\
h_+(\uparrow\{n\})\wedge h_-(\uparrow\{n\})=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Compose with restriction \(\mathcal O(X)\to\mathcal O(S)\) to obtain frame maps H_±. Let \(\{U_m:m\in\omega\}\) be a countable ordinary open cover of T (e.g. its basic cylinders). Set
\[
\mathcal C^\star=
\{Y\}\ \cup\
\{N(U_m,1,\{0\}):m\in\omega\}
\ \cup\
\{\uparrow\{n\}\subseteq S:n\in\omega\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
This covers X. Every member restricts to 0 or to \(\uparrow\{n\}\) on S, so \(\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C^\star}H_+(c)\wedge H_-(c)=0\). Thus
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D(X)<\mathcal D_2(X)\quad\text{but}\quad
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X).}\tag{4.8}
\]

**Both old compact-remnant tests genuinely fail.**
For the exhibited cover C*, every finite subfamily has restriction to S equal to a finite union of singleton principal upsets, which is proper in S. If e≺ a finite union of C* members and e meets S, then e* restricts to 0 on S (S hyperconnected), so \(e^*\vee c_F=1\) would force the impossible c_F|_S=1. Any open meeting T meets S, so e cannot meet T either. Combining with (4.3),
\[
\boxed{t_{\mathcal C^\star}=Y.}\tag{4.9}
\]
Hence the original remainder C(t_C*)=S∪T is noncompact, while the mixed **new remainder** C(t_C*∨S)=T is also **noncompact** and **non-scattered** (perfect Baire). Neither the earlier compact-remnant theorem nor the scattered-square theorem applies.

Furthermore the compact-closed-neighborhood strip h_cc is zero: a compact closed sublocale containing a nonempty Y-open would contain a closed noncompact Hilbert ball; containing a nonempty S-open or T-neighborhood would contain its closure, including the noncompact closed T. Thus \(h_{\rm cc}=0\). The closed complement of h_hc=S contains noncompact T. So the old fixed hyperconnected+compact-closed remainder theorem does not apply either.

The zero-dimensional closed-core lemma nevertheless excludes any W/W2 discrepancy on the entire T×T, which is where the previous mixed-strip localization would force one.

### 4.1 The Urysohn relation is genuinely nonregular-closed

This X also avoids the trivial explanation \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)\). Indeed the open sublocale Y is a non-discrete regular Hausdorff space. Within its **open square** \(Y^2\subseteq X^2\), the Urysohn relation restricts to the diagonal \(\Delta_Y\): distinct Y-points admit relatively bounded neighborhoods whose closures in X are disjoint (a bounded closed Hilbert ball is closed in X). Thus
\[
\mathcal U(X)\wedge\mathfrak o(Y\times Y)=\Delta_Y.
\]
Since Y has no isolated points, \(\Delta_Y\) has empty interior in \(Y^2\). Therefore \(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U(X)\) is disjoint from this open square, and so is its **closure** when restricted to the open square, whereas \(\mathcal U(X)\) still contains \(\Delta_Y\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U(X))<\mathcal U(X).}
\]
This ensures that the positive Q′ result for X is not merely an application of the established universal regular-core inclusion.

**Qualification:** This argument establishes nonregular-closedness of U, but does not claim to compute the entire boundary \(H_2=\mathcal W_2\wedge G\), or that the Baire residual \(T^2\) itself meets \(H_2\) nontrivially.


## 5. Conceptual residue and an exact Boolean-reflection diagnosis

We now have THREE independently meaningful residual-positive mechanisms:
1. **compactness** of the cover-dependent closed remainder K_C (previous theorem);
2. **zero-dimensionality** of K_C (new clopen-detection lemma), with no spatiality or compactness assumptions;
3. **total spatiality of the square** K_C×K_C, e.g. K_C locally compact Hausdorff scattered (new corollary).

These cannot be collapsed into one purely pointwise classification: compact nonregular K need not be zero-dimensional; a perfect noncompact zero-dimensional K is not scattered; and a nonzero-dimensional scattered K may have nontrivial specialization.

A counterexample to Q′ must produce, for ONE cover C, a **noncompact, non-zero-dimensional closed K_C** whose square carries a nonzero **pointless sublocale** D₂∧E, and its pair of coordinate maps must agree on all clopens of K_C (by Lemma 1.1). Equivalently the discrepancy is restricted to the **kernel pair of the zero-dimensional quotient** \(q_0:K_C\to(K_C)_0\), which records the portion of K_C not detected by complemented opens.

This is a structural no-go criterion, not a proof that the obstruction cannot exist. Noncompact, non-zero-dimensional and non-scattered locales with such pointless sublocales certainly exist; the outstanding work is whether one can couple the specific finite-cover/fully-cover defect to the nonregular Urysohn boundary in their squares.
