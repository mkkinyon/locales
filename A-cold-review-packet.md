## Mathematical setup

For any locale X and its frame of opens L=O(X), let Q=L⊗L=O(X×X). Write a⊠b for the basic rectangle; a*=a→0. Define three opens of Q:

    d_I = ⋁_{a∧b=0} a⊠b;
    d_U = ⋁_{a*∨b*=1} a⊠b;
    d_F = ⋁_{f:X→R}(f×f)^*((R×R)\Δ_R).

Let I=C(d_I), U=C(d_U), F=C(d_F), the complementary **closed sublocales**. Prove or disprove Δ⊆I⊆U⊆F, and that I is closure of Δ. For a,b∈L define a≺b iff a*∨b=1 and a≺≺b by the standard rational scale of Picado–Pultr.

## Assertions requiring independent scrutiny

**A. Entirely localic formulas.**
- d_F = ⋁_{a≺≺ b*}a⊠b.
- F is the kernel pair of r_X:X→Loc(C(L)), where C(L) is the largest completely regular subframe generated by real-function inverse images.
- I=U and U=F are characterized by appropriate conditions on parallel frame maps L⇒M for **every** frame M.
- Extremally disconnected L implies I=U=F, although the common sublocale may be larger than Δ.

**B. Pointless strictness.**
For P the seven-vertex poset zig-zag a1<b1>a2<b2>a3<b3>a4, I(P)⊊U(P)⊊F(P). For any nontrivial pointless locale Y, the product P×Y is pointless and retains **both strict inclusions** of closed localic relations. Check the generalized-point proof and the formula for O(P×Y).

**C. Compact/coherent criteria.**
- On *every algebraic frame*, I=U iff disjoint compact opens k,l have k*∨l*=1. This uses the finite-rectangle claim: if a⊠b is covered by finitely many u_i⊠v_i with u_i*∨v_i*=1, then a*∨b*=1, **without spatiality assumptions**.
- On coherent frames, I=U iff compact-open lattice K(L) is conormal; for Zariski spectra this is claimed equivalent to the ring being an mp-ring.
- On coherent frames, U=F iff far compact k,l satisfy k≺≺l*.
- On compact spaces, if I=U as ordinary point relations, then I=U=F because E=clΔ is a closed equivalence relation with compact Hausdorff quotient. Check this even in non-Hausdorff spaces.

**D. Continuous and covering criteria.**
- Classically, every continuous frame has a locally compact sober spatial representation; for u≪a there is compact saturated K with u⊆K⊆a.
- On continuous frames: I=U iff a∧b=0,u≪a,v≪b implies u*∨v*=1; and U=F iff a*∨b*=1,u≪a,v≪b implies u≺≺v*.
- On kω spaces, closed and transitive E=clΔ implies quotient X/E **normal Hausdorff** via countable compact stages and Tietze extension. This lifts to locally compact σ-compact sober locales.
- On locally compact paracompact spaces (**paracompact = locally finite open refinements, NO Hausdorff axiom**), transitivity of E forces E=F. Check the claimed clopen σ-compact partition and whether any reasoning incorrectly assumes compact subsets closed. Similar statement for a locally countable open cover by σ-compact opens.

**E. Central counterexample: TWO TAGS AND A NONNORMAL SPACE.**
Let T be any locally compact Hausdorff space with disjoint closed subsets A,B that cannot be separated by disjoint open neighborhoods. Let

    X=T ⊔ {p_A,p_B}

with O⊆X open exactly when V=O∩T is open in T and

    V∩A ≠∅ ⇒ p_A∈O,
    V∩B ≠∅ ⇒ p_B∈O.

Claim: X is locally compact sober (thus O(X) continuous); its ordinary I and U coincide and have exactly the nontrivial equivalence classes A∪{p_A} and B∪{p_B}; yet every continuous f:X→R satisfies f(p_A)=f(p_B), while the singleton tag opens have disjoint closures. Therefore

    I(X)=U(X) ⊊ F(X)

as **localic** closed sublocales. The compact opens u={p_A}, v={p_B} satisfy u≪u,v≪v,u*∨v*=1, but u not≺≺v*.

Explicit T is the **deleted Tychonoff plank**

    T=([0,ω1]×[0,ω])\{(ω1,ω)},
    A={ω1}×[0,ω), B=[0,ω1)×{ω}.

Be especially skeptical about:
(i) whether the assigned opens form a topology;
(ii) whether an ordinary point of A/B has an appropriate compact neighborhood in X for *every* open neighborhood;
(iii) whether the two-tag X is sober;
(iv) computing closures of arbitrary opens and verifying **U=I for all types of pairs**, not only the two tags;
(v) whether an ordinary X×X point witness is enough to establish strictness of **closed sublocales** of the localic product;
(vi) whether the plank edges are genuinely closed and nonnormally separable.
