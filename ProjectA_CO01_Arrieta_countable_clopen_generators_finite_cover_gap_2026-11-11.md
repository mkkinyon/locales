# CO-01 new theorem — countable Cantor-clopen normal form and strict finite-cover truncation gap

**11 November 2026. Internally checked original segment; CO-01 cube associativity remains UNRESOLVED.** This is the first concrete use of the Θ-admissible-cover presentation in the bounded continuation of [CO-01](ProjectA_CO01_Arrieta_cube_symmetry_spatial_invisibility_2026-11-11.md). It gives a controlled **countable clopen generator class** for the endpoint frame congruences and proves a sharp **infinitary obstruction:** finite admissible-cover equations do not suffice even to define the starting fitted relation A. Classical background: Arrieta 2024 Construction 4.2/Example 4.5; explicit Boolean nontransitivity test in [audit VIII](A-relational-composition-associativity-audit-VIII-nontransitive-Arrieta-2026-11-07.md). ## 0. Setup

Let \(C=2^\mathbb N\). For n≥1, let \(K_n\subseteq C\) be all nonzero finitely supported 0/1 sequences with final 1 at coordinate n. Thus |K_n|=2^{n-1}, and for any m<n, every assignment of the first m coordinates is realized by some \(x\in K_n\). Let \(D=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K_n\) and \(V=C\setminus D\); both D and V are dense in C. Set \(F=\mathcal O(V)\) and \(Q_2=F\oplus F\).

For \(a\in F\), define \(\Theta(a)=\{n:\exists\text{ Cantor-open }W\supseteq K_n\text{ with }W\cap V\subseteq a\}\). An open cover \(\mathcal C\) of V is **Θ-admissible** if \(\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal C}\Theta(a)=\mathbb N\). Let
\[
\gamma_{\mathcal C}=\bigvee_{a\in\mathcal C}a_1\wedge a_2\in Q_2,\qquad
A=\bigcap_{\mathcal C\,\Theta\text{-admissible}}\mathfrak o(\gamma_{\mathcal C}).
\tag{0.1}
\]
The meet is in the localic sublocale lattice. Every cover, finite or infinite, here may contain infinitely many elements.

## 1. Countable Cantor-clopen normal form

**Theorem 1.** Every Θ-admissible open cover \(\mathcal C\) of V has a countable Θ-admissible refinement \(\mathcal R\) whose members are **traces of clopen subsets of the Cantor space C**, not merely relatively clopen in V. It may be chosen in the special shape
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal R=\{W_n\cap V:n\ge1\}\ \cup\ \{P_i\cap V:i\ge1\},
\quad K_n\subseteq W_n,
\quad \{P_i\}\text{ pairwise disjoint Cantor-clopens covering V}.
\quad}\tag{1.1}
\]
Each W_n and P_i can be chosen subordinate to an original member of \(\mathcal C\).

**Proof.** By Θ-admissibility for each n choose a_n∈C and Cantor-open O_n with K_n⊆O_n and O_n∩V⊆a_n. K_n is finite compact and C zero-dimensional; choose Cantor-clopen W_n with K_n⊆W_n⊆O_n. Separately, since \(\mathcal C\) covers V and Cantor-clopen traces form a countable base of V, choose countably many Cantor-clopens U_i with V⊆∪_i U_i and U_i∩V⊆c_i for some c_i∈C. Disjointify:
\[
P_i=U_i\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}U_j.
\]
Each P_i is Cantor-clopen, pairwise disjoint, subordinate to c_i, and their union contains V. Discard empty traces. The W_n enforce n∈Θ(W_n∩V) for each n; the P_i ensure covering V. Thus R is a countable Θ-admissible refinement. QED.

Since R refines C, \(\gamma_{\mathcal R}\le\gamma_{\mathcal C}\) in Q₂. Hence \(\mathfrak o(\gamma_{\mathcal R})\subseteq\mathfrak o(\gamma_{\mathcal C})\). Every original cover equation \(\gamma_{\mathcal C}=1\) follows from one of these countable Cantor-clopen normal-form equations. Conversely every R is itself admissible. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
A=\bigcap_{\mathcal R\text{ of shape (1.1)}}\mathfrak o(\gamma_{\mathcal R}),
}
\tag{1.2}
\]
and the defining frame congruence G_{ij} in any tensor Q_n is generated by **countable joins of Cantor-clopen coordinate rectangles** of this shape. This is an *exact* reduction, not a finite approximation.

## 2. Unbounded clopen complexity is compulsory

**Lemma 2.** Let W⊊C be a proper Cantor-clopen set depending only on the first m coordinates. Then \(K_n\nsubseteq W\) for every n>m. Consequently
\[
\Theta(W\cap V)=\{n:K_n\subseteq W\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Proof. For n>m, the prefixes of points of K_n exhaust all binary m-tuples. Since W is proper, some m-prefix cylinder is disjoint from W; K_n meets it, proving K_n not⊆W. If any Cantor-open O⊇K_n satisfies O∩V⊆W∩V, density of V and closedness of W imply O⊆W: otherwise the open O\W would meet V. Thus \(\Theta(W\cap V)\) is exactly the set of n with K_n⊆W. QED.

**Corollary.** Every Θ-admissible cover by Cantor-clopen traces either contains **V itself** (making γ_C=1 trivially) or is infinite with clopen defining depths unbounded. In particular:
- every **finite** Θ-admissible Cantor-clopen cover produces only the trivial equation γ_C=1;
- **no bounded-cylinder-depth theory** can define A through Θ-admissible cover equations;
- any genuine normalized generator of G_{ij} is intrinsically infinitary.

No claim that *finite arbitrary open covers* are trivial: V\{p} is dense proper open and Θ-admissible as a singleton member; it is not generally the trace of a Cantor-clopen set.

## 3. Every finite admissible open cover has a dense member

**Lemma 3.** If \(\mathcal C=\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}\) is finite and Θ-admissible, at least one a_i is a **dense open subset of V**.

**Proof.** Assign each n a member a_i with n∈Θ(a_i). By infinite pigeonhole, some fixed a_i contains K_n in Cantor-open neighborhoods O_n⊆C with O_n∩V⊆a_i for infinitely many unbounded n. For any nonempty basic Cantor cylinder B of length m, choose such n>m. Then K_n∩B≠∅, so O_n∩B is nonempty Cantor-open; its intersection with dense V is nonempty and lies inside a_i. Thus a_i meets every basic Cantor cylinder's V-trace and is dense in V. QED.

Let \(B=\mathrm{RO}(V)\), \(q:F\to B\) be double-negation reflection, and for a Cantor homeomorphism ρ preserving D,V set \(h_\rho=q\circ\rho_V^*:F\to B\). Dense open a_i pulls back to a dense open under homeomorphism, so \(h_\rho(a_i)=1\). Therefore, for **every** pair ρ,λ in the indicated homeomorphism group,
\[
\bigvee_{a\in\mathcal C}h_\rho(a)\wedge h_\lambda(a)=1_B
\qquad\text{for EVERY finite Θ-admissible cover }\mathcal C.
\tag{3.1}
\]

## 4. Strictness of the finite-cover truncation

Define the *finite-cover approximation*
\[
A_{\rm fin}=\bigcap_{\substack{\mathcal C\,\Theta\text{-admissible}\\|\mathcal C|<\infty}}
 \mathfrak o(\gamma_{\mathcal C}).
\tag{4.1}
\]
It is a symmetric reflexive fitted relation containing A. From (3.1), the pair \((h_\rho,h_\lambda):\operatorname{Loc}(B)\to V^2\) factors through \(A_{\rm fin}\) for **every** two Cantor homeomorphisms preserving D.

In particular, let \(\rho=\mathrm{id}\), \(\lambda=\widehat\tau\), where τ is the explicit bijection of N with one bi-infinite orbit in [audit VIII §2](A-relational-composition-associativity-audit-VIII-nontransitive-Arrieta-2026-11-07.md). The earlier all-cover argument produced a countable Θ-admissible cover C* for which
\[
\bigvee_{a\in\mathcal C^*}h_{\rm id}(a)\wedge h_\tau(a)\ne1_B.
\]
Hence this Boolean-valued generalized pair **does not factor through A**, despite factoring through \(A_{\rm fin}\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\qquad A\ \subsetneq\ A_{\rm fin}.\qquad}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Equivalently, the frame congruence generated by finite Θ-admissible equations is strictly smaller than the full frame congruence G_A. Thus finite-cover equations fail already at the *one-edge* level. Any procedure for the CO-01 endpoint kernels which replaces the full A by A_fin without proof is invalid; the omitted infinite conditions are not cosmetic.

## 5. Consequence for the endpoint-congruence problem

The corrected exact tests remain (see [CO-01 symmetry and kernel note](ProjectA_CO01_Arrieta_cube_symmetry_spatial_invisibility_2026-11-11.md)):
\[
H_L\subseteq J_L,\qquad
H_L=J_L\ \Rightarrow\ (A^2)A=A(A^2),
\]
and even the weaker endpoint equation \(\alpha_{13}^{-1}(H_L)=\alpha_{13}^{-1}(J_L)\) suffices. The present results **do not establish either equality**. They do provide a strictly smaller valid generator class: every G_ij may be defined using covers in the countable Cantor-clopen normal form (1.1). Every genuinely nontrivial generator then involves **unbounded cylinder depth**, and all finite admissible-cover tests miss some generalized pairs that the full relation excludes.

This is concrete progress on the nature of the congruences, not just a change of notation. It also explains why simulations with finite clopen partitions or finite covers cannot be trusted as proof of Arrieta's cube associativity. The next discriminating attempt must preserve these countable, unbounded-depth equations.
