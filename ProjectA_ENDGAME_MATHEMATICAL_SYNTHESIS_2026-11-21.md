# From graphs to localic relations: a mathematical synthesis
## Unified Point-Free Topology project · editorial synthesis following independent structural review · 21 November 2026

**Purpose.** This is the shortest *self-contained mathematical account of the program as a program*, rather than another theorem ledger, independent referee verdict, or publication claim. It is intended for an algebraist or topologist encountering the project for the first time. Proofs and precise verification statuses reside in the [47-group theorem ledger v3.2](ProjectA_ENDGAME_THEOREM_LEDGER_CURRENT_2026-11-21.md), [notation/assumption reference](ProjectA_ENDGAME_NOTATION_AND_ASSUMPTIONS_2026-11-21.md), [proof dependency atlas](ProjectA_ENDGAME_PROOF_DEPENDENCY_ATLAS_2026-11-21.md), and [independent seven-interface structural assessment](ProjectA_ENDGAME_STRUCTURAL_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-21.md). The conclusions marked *project-specific* are not thereby proved novel relative to all earlier literature. Earlier work of Isbell, Johnstone–Sun, Vermeulen, Picado–Pultr, Arrieta, Dowker, Brun–Salbu, Haine, etc. is explicitly acknowledged in [the literature crosswalk](ProjectA_ENDGAME_LITERATURE_CROSSWALK_2026-11-21.md).

> **Central question.** When graph relations built from topological separation are made into genuine **sublocales**, what can be recovered from the underlying point graph, what genuinely requires point-free information, and which operations allow that information to be composed or compared?

The answer is not simply “nothing is determined by points.” Rather, we have identified several **precise recovery regimes** and several **sharp obstructions**. Those two directions, taken together, organize the project.

## 1. Why Cameron–Kumar's graph question becomes a locale question

Peter Cameron and Vinod Kumar P. B.'s *Graphs on topological spaces* (arXiv:2610.14783) motivated the project through ordinary graphs of specialization, inseparability, closure-meeting, Urysohn nonseparation, and functional nonseparation. Ordinary graphs are informative, but their vertices are **points**. For a locale X, a more intrinsic object is a **symmetric, reflexive sublocale relation** \(R\hookrightarrow X\times_{\rm Loc}X\) (with an oriented specialization relation when appropriate).

There is a critical difference among three operations often conflated in point-set treatments:
1. An ordinary subset of the pairs of points of \(X\).
2. The **sublocale** generated by such points, which may have nonzero pointless parts.
3. The **genuine localic relation composite** \(R\circ S\): the image of the actual pullback \(R\times_XS\).

The last operation need not agree with the composite of point graphs and **need not associate in all of Loc**. This general nonassociativity is known in the category-theoretic literature; the project's contribution is to exhibit and analyze useful **restricted calculi** rather than pretending it disappears.

A recurring motif is therefore: **prove** when a relation is reconstructed from its patch-closed point set, and otherwise **do not** infer equality of sublocales from equality of ordinary pairs. Conversely, point-free structures can expose exact algebraic paths hidden from ordinary graph geometry.

Our specialization order convention for coherent spectral points is \(x\le y\) when every open containing x contains y (so opens are upsets). This orientation should appear once at the start of any exposition.

## 2. Branch A — separation as a hierarchy generated by the diagonal

Write \(Y=X\times_{\rm Loc}X\), with localic diagonal Δ. Define
\[
\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}\Delta,\quad
\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta,\quad
\mathcal V=\operatorname{fit}(\mathcal I),\quad
\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D),
\]
and let \(\mathcal H\) be the least simultaneously **closed and fitted** super-sublocale of Δ. The other central relations are the Urysohn relation \(\mathcal U\) and the functional relation \(\mathcal F\) (kernel pair of completely regular reflection). Let
\[
\mathcal M=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D,\quad
\mathcal J=\mathcal I\vee\mathcal D,\quad
\mathcal T=\mathcal V\wedge\mathcal W.
\]
Let \(\mathcal A\) be oriented specialization, \(\mathcal B=\mathcal A^\smile\), and \(\mathcal S=\mathcal A\vee\mathcal B\).

The main conceptual point is that **closure** and **fitting** are two different ways to enlarge the diagonal. They interact with graph relations but are not themselves a disguised graph-theoretic transitive closure. The universal inclusions and more complete Hasse diagram are in the [research status/diagram references](ProjectA_Cameron_Kumar_revisit_graph_to_locale_hierarchy_2026-11-10.md); no total order among the named relations is claimed.

### 2.1. A positive theorem: coherence makes graph words reliable

For **coherent locales** and **proconstructible spectral sublocale relations**, the actual localic pullback and image coincide with the **patch-closed ordinary relation composite**. This gives an associative **finite-word** calculus with diagonal as identity, converse, and finite joins. The central dictionary is classical Priestley duality, but its use for genuine localic relation composition requires verifying **once** that the restricted proconstructible pullback and image calculations agree with patch-closed point composition. After that verification, the coherent formulas are naturally short order-theoretic consequences.

The coherent calculus yields, as genuine sublocale equalities,
\[
\boxed{\mathcal I=\mathcal A\mathcal B,\qquad
\mathcal D=\mathcal B\mathcal A,\qquad
\mathcal V=\mathcal D^2,\qquad
\mathcal W=\mathcal I^2,\qquad
\mathcal S^2=\mathcal J.}
\]
**Separately** importing the established coherent theorem \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) then yields
\[
\boxed{\mathcal U=\mathcal I^2,\qquad
\mathcal S^4=\mathcal U\vee\mathcal V.}
\]
These are **not** asserted for arbitrary localic relations or noncoherent locales, nor for unrestricted infinitary joins. The two different proof steps \(\mathcal W=\mathcal I^2\) and \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) must remain visible. [Coherent proof](ProjectA_coherent_composition_canonical_proof_2026-11-08.md); [structural referee reconciliation](ProjectA_ENDGAME_STRUCTURAL_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-21.md).

The algebraic equality classes also have intrinsic frame descriptions: for coherent X, \(\mathcal I=\mathcal U\) corresponds to **conormality** of \(K(\mathcal O(X))\) (Cornish's minimal-prime condition), whereas \(\mathcal D=\mathcal U\) corresponds to **normality** of that compact-open lattice. The different normal/conormal directions should be respected rather than compressed into “nice separation.” [Dual characterization](A-coherent-dual-characterizations-2026-10-21.md).

On **stably compact** locales, the named relations \(\mathcal I,\mathcal D,\mathcal V,\mathcal W,\mathcal J,\mathcal T\) have spatial/perfect representations and pointwise order-saturation formulas. There are corresponding precise interval-chain and oriented-specialization criteria in stably **locally** compact classes. Crucially, **locally compact and sober without stability** is not sufficient: a compact sober T₁ example has \(\mathcal A>\Delta\) even though the point specialization graph is diagonal. [Stably compact graph theorem](ProjectA_J_T_COMPARABILITY_GRAPH_STABLY_COMPACT_CANONICAL_2026-11-17.md); [negative A example](ProjectA_LCA_COUNTEREXAMPLE_CANONICAL_2026-11-15.md).

### 2.2. Three obstructions that say *what coherence does not settle*

**First: closed and fitted need not imply transitive.** A coherent spectral locale from two convergent Priestley zigzag rays has
\[
\mathcal U\vee\mathcal V<\mathcal H<\mathcal F,\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal H\circ\mathcal H\nleq\mathcal H.}
\]
The example explicitly exhibits a point triple \((a_0,t,b_0)\) composable through the common patch limit t while \((a_0,b_0)\) lies outside the least closed/fitted hull. The key theorem is a **sublocale** statement, secured by coherent closed/fitted reconstruction. In the coherent class, \(\mathcal H\) is transitive **iff** \(\mathcal H=\mathcal F\). Related closed-stage hull constructions first stabilize at every ordinal \(\omega+m\), \(m\ge1\); their limit stages involve **closure of the preceding join**. These are separate, earlier-reviewed theorems, not results rechecked by the 21 November final structural packet. [Two-ray example](A-H-two-convergent-zigzag-rays-nontransitive-coherent-counterexample-2026-10-24.md); [ordinal stages](A-H-formal-hull-odd-powers-omega-plus-m-2026-10-24.md).

**Second: bars certified by finite-rank separation trees need not cut out the full fitted relation.** A *bar* is a frame-coproduct open w satisfying \(w\vee d_{\mathcal I}=1\). Well-founded rectangular separation trees measure the depth of such a proof. For a countable first-countable T₁ construction (original blind reviewed) and a simpler sober T₀ refinement (post-verdict referee suggestion checked internally),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal V\subsetneq \mathcal V_{\mathrm{fin}},}
\]
where \(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{fin}}\) uses **all bars of finite separation-tree rank**. Exact rank-\(\omega\) bars supply the obstruction. This is stronger than exhibiting bars of arbitrarily high finite rank: saturation of their fitted nuclei must also be ruled out. The positive low-depth bounds in compact/regular/continuous settings are useful complementary internal results, but are not to be confused with a general theorem covering all locales. [Vfin consolidated proof](A-Vfin-negative-consolidated-proof-2026-10-23.md).

**Third: finite versus all covers can agree on point pairs but diverge after taking localic hulls.** The audited dense-rational bridge constructs a **second-countable, locally compact, sober, T₁ but non-Hausdorff étale space** \(p:X\to\mathbb R\) with
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D=\Delta,\qquad
\mathcal W=\mathcal I<\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U=X\times_{\mathbb R}X.}
\]
Here \(\mathcal D_2\) uses **finite** open covers, while \(\mathcal D\) uses **all** covers; they have the same ordinary pairs. Yet on an open cross-sheet sector \(J\), every finite cover leaves a dense matching locus, and the **nonzero pointless Booleanization** \(B(J)\) survives their sublocale intersection. The full (infinite) étale section atlas kills that sector. Closing \(\mathcal D_2\) fills it. By the published theorem of de Brecht, this particular countably based locally compact sober X is **quasi-Polish** (a post-audit literature corollary, not a separate audited construction). These results settled historical project questions Q and Q′ **negatively**. [Canonical bridge](ProjectA_Q_DENSE_RATIONAL_BRIDGE_CANONICAL_2026-11-14.md).

Together the positive coherent formulas and these examples show that the hierarchy is neither an arbitrary collection of names nor a simple universal Hasse chain. It is controlled by **where the proof witnesses live** and **which closure/fit operations preserve them**.

## 3. Branch B — when localic relations really form a calculus

This branch begins with a failure: ordinary set-relational composition is always associative, but **genuine localic image-defined composition can fail associativity**, because the requisite image/epi stability is unavailable in general. That difficulty is established in prior category-theoretic literature; the project identifies **positive fragments** where the actual localic operations can be trusted.

The first positive fragment is already the coherent proconstructible calculus used in Branch A. It is **one shared theorem**, not two independent theorems in separate papers.

### 3.1. The compact-Hausdorff rectangular rule

For a compact Hausdorff space G and ordinary subsets A,B define
\[
p(A,B)=[A\times B]_{G^2},\qquad
q(A,B)=[A]_G\times_{\rm Loc}[B]_G.
\]
The weak/full distinction between p and q is classical (Johnstone–Sun, 1988): generally \(p\le q\), with p dense in q, and **not** necessarily equal. The project's separately checked **mixed composition rule** is
\[
\boxed{
r(A,B)\circ s(C,D)=
\begin{cases}
q(A,D),&[B]_G\wedge[C]_G\ne0,\\
0,&[B]_G\wedge[C]_G=0,
\end{cases}
\quad r,s\in\{p,q\}.}
\]
The outer q may also be zero if its own supports are empty. The proof uses least compact **closed support**, Isbell's Booleanization and dense-middle saturation. It never assumes a relation equals the join of its ordinary points.

For example, with \(B=\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\) and \(C=[0,1]\setminus\mathbb Q\), the ordinary middle sets are disjoint, but the two dense **induced sublocales** have nonzero overlap. Thus
\[
p(G,B)\circ p(C,G)=q(G,G)=G^2.
\]
In particular, localic composition can be **maximal despite there being no ordinary middle witness**. [Rectangular canonical](ProjectA_CLIQUE_RECTANGULAR_CALCULUS_POSTREFEREE_CANONICAL_2026-11-20.md).

Finite joins of these rectangles form an associative, converse-closed, finite-join-distributive algebra with zero, **but generally no unit**. The independent final referee supplied a new, internally checked post-report theorem sharpening this point:
\[
\boxed{\mathscr R_\vee(G)\text{ has a two-sided multiplicative unit }
\iff G\text{ is finite}.}
\]
(For empty G, use the one-element zero-algebra convention.) The necessity follows by testing a hypothetical finite-rectangle identity against every singleton diagonal \(e_x\): all its nonzero summands must be diagonal singletons, forcing G finite. Thus the diagonal is absent from the finite-join rectangle algebra on infinite G. **Adjoining the diagonal alone** would trivially adjoin a unit (since Δ is the unit for every genuine localic relation); the nontrivial content of the **separately proved graph-extension theorem** is the safe associative interaction with additional locally closed partial-homeomorphism graph generators. Arbitrary graph adjunctions, infinite charts and general partial-graph “TWIST” remain outside that proof. [Addendum reconciliation](ProjectA_ENDGAME_STRUCTURAL_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-21.md); [graph-extension canonical](ProjectA_GRAPH_EXTENSIONS_POSTREFEREE_CANONICAL_2026-11-21.md).

### 3.2. Scattered indices allow exact matrices — but local finiteness buys association

Take \(G=P\times K\), with **arbitrary locale P** and **locally compact scattered Hausdorff K** (the older compact-Hausdorff hypothesis was superseded by the independently accepted MAT* extension). The parameterized scattered-sector theorem gives, for the finite products used in composition,
\[
\mathsf{Sub}(P^n\times K^n)
\cong\prod_{\mathbf k\in K^n}\mathsf{Sub}(P^n).
\]
These are **closed singleton index sectors**, possibly pointless in the P-coordinate. The decomposition is an isomorphism of **sublocale lattices**, **not** an isomorphism of \(P\times K\) with a topological/discrete coproduct.

For arbitrary binary localic relations R,S, the **actual** composition obeys
\[
\boxed{
(R\circ S)_{ik}=\bigvee_{j\in K}(R_{ij}\circ_P S_{jk}).}
\]
The proof is delicate: form the **already-defined composability meet** \(R_{12}\wedge S_{23}\) first, decompose *that sublocale* into closed triple-index sectors, and **only then** use arbitrary-join preservation of localic **image**. One must **not** distribute an arbitrary sublocale meet over two infinite joins. Nor does the binary formula imply unrestricted associativity.

Instead, when the P-fibre entries lie in a genuinely associative **unital**, finite-join algebra and matrices are finite in **both** rows and columns, the matrix multiplication of each entry is a **finite** join. Now both bracketings expand into the same finite double sum; one obtains an **associative unital converse-closed** matrix algebra and a localic version of finite path expansion. The independent structural referee verified both this distinction and the actual matrix mechanism. [Scattered matrix canonical](ProjectA_SCATTERED_INDEX_LOCALLY_FINITE_LOCALIC_RELATION_MATRICES_2026-11-21.md); [external reconciliation](ProjectA_ENDGAME_STRUCTURAL_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-21.md).

The resulting compositions can behave unlike ordinary graph powers. Audited Stone examples have ordinary connected components that are **cliques** (diameter ≤1), while the **localic relation-composition rank** grows with a path of nonzero **pointless overlaps of induced component sublocales**. A sharper exact diameter-one formula \(n+2\) was referee-origin and later internally checked; the earlier larger-diameter exact formulas were originally blind reviewed. This composition rank is **not** the separation-tree rank of \(\mathcal V\). [Stone rank canonical](ProjectA_STONE_CLIQUE_OVERLAP_RANK_CANONICAL_2026-11-20.md).

### 3.3. The more technical quotient branch has a specific place

The properness/Scott-continuity converse \(L^*\) and the finite-fence **fixed-bracketing** four-step effectivity model are valuable technical extensions of this branch, but they should not dominate the introduction. In particular, \(L^*\) is a precise theorem for **closed localic relations** over arbitrary locales: directed-join preservation by the saturation operator is equivalent to properness of the relevant projection in Vermeulen's sense. The four-step model requires specific generic-fibre/cutoff hypotheses and does not imply arbitrary associativity. Published Vermeulen closed-proper equivalence effectiveness must be attributed. These are well placed in a technical chapter or separate research note. [Corrected L*](ProjectA_Lstar_canonical_corrected_proof_2026-11-08.md); [four-step canonical](ProjectA_FourStep_Short_Proof_Consolidated_2026-11-19.md).

## 4. Branch C — from binary graphs to higher simultaneous witnesses

A graph records pairs that have **some** witness; a higher complex asks whether an entire tuple has **one simultaneous witness**. This is the essential distinction in the Dowker/homotopy program.

For a **coherent** locale X with ordered Priestley patch space V, set
\[
\mathrm{Ch}_n=\{x_0\le\cdots\le x_n\},\quad
I_n=\{(x_i):\exists u,\ x_i\le u\ \forall i\},\quad
D_n=\{(x_i):\exists a,\ a\le x_i\ \forall i\},
\]
and \(M_n=I_n\cap D_n\). These are patched Stone simplicial spaces arising from the appropriate coherent/proconstructible setup. Their realizations \(\mathbf C,\mathbf I,\mathbf D,\mathbf M\) are taken in a **specified sufficiently large Stone-sheaf \(\infty\)-topos** \(\operatorname{Shv}_\infty(\operatorname{Stone}_\kappa,\text{finite jointly-surjective covers})\), a **κ-condensed-anima setting** (without an asserted hypercompletion or site-size-independence theorem), not automatically in the intrinsic shape of X. Important parts of ordinary Dowker, nerve, and classifying-anima theory are already in the literature; our setting adds ordered patch/sheaf and comparison-map questions.

**Positive reconstruction.** The binary common-upper graph **plus patch topology** determines **all** higher patched \(I_n\): maximal-element clique neighborhoods reconstruct principal down-cones; dually the common-lower graph reconstructs all \(D_n\). This is a theorem about binary graph **isomorphisms** with patch structure, not about arbitrary binary-graph-preserving maps. The latter need not preserve \(M_2\).

A further (post-fourth-verdict but independently crosschecked) theorem identifies the **chain fundamental groupoid** with the meet-witness fundamental groupoid. Its key arrow on a meet edge \((x,y)\) is
\[
\gamma(x,y)=[x,u][y,u]^{-1}=[a,x]^{-1}[a,y],
\qquad a\le x,y\le u.
\]
A **local Stone-surjective witness cover**, not a global choice, makes this independent of witnesses and allows sheafified descent. Accordingly, the **chain 1-type comparison data** can be reconstructed from patched binary I,D and suitable comparison maps. The under-\(\mathbf C\) arrow equivalence \(\mathbf C\to\mathbf I\) versus \(\mathbf C\to\mathbf D\) is a **separately established imported theorem**, not a consequence of binary graphs alone.

**Sharp bound, not full homotopy.** A second independently crosschecked post-verdict result gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbf C(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf M(X)\text{ is 2-connective,}}
\]
meaning π₀,π₁ are isomorphisms and π₂ is an epimorphism, **not** that π₂ is injective. The proof uses degreewise **effective Stone-cover Čech descent** and the **standard realization-connectivity lemma** in the specified sheaf \(\infty\)-topos. These are named imports, not hidden under “obvious Dowker duality.” [Higher meet canonical](PointFree_binary_meet_hull_CANONICAL_2026-11-11.md); [premise crosswalk](PointFree_ENDGAME_FOURTH_AUDIT_PREMISE_TRACE_2026-11-21.md).

The sharp six-point example is especially economical. Let
\[
P=A_2\oplus B_{\rm anti}\oplus C_2,\qquad
Q=A_2\oplus B_{\rm chain}\oplus C_2,
\]
where the two ends are two-element antichains. For both, a tuple has a common upper bound iff it does not contain both top elements, and a common lower bound iff it does not contain both bottom elements. Thus **all patched higher \(I_n,D_n\) agree** on the same **discrete patch labels**, including simplicial maps; the spectral orders differ. Nevertheless,
\[
\boxed{\mathbf C(P)\simeq S^2,\qquad
\mathbf C(Q)\simeq1,\qquad
\mathbf M(P)\simeq\mathbf M(Q)\simeq1.}
\]
So even knowledge of **all** higher I/D witness spaces does not recover chain 2-type. A binary graph description can recover **more than one expects** (the 1-type) and simultaneously **less than one might hope** (the full higher homotopy). No equality of localic subobjects across differently ordered spectral ambients is being asserted.

Other fourth-audit developments—Stone-indexed nonconstant Čech constructions, relative gluing, twisted cohomology, nonsuspension examples, classifying-anima identification, and crosscut connectivity ladders—extend this third branch. They have varied E/X/I evidence statuses and specific site restrictions, so they belong in the technical ledger rather than in every introductory presentation. [Chain and Čech canonical](PointFree_coherent_chain_hulls_CANONICAL_2026-11-10.md); [fourth audit reconciliation](PointFree_audit4_FINAL_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-11.md).

## 6. The synthesis is neither one giant theorem nor three disconnected research projects

Three links are mathematically genuine:

1. **A ↔ B, exact:** the coherent localic relation-composition theorem **computes** diagonal hulls and graph powers. It is a **shared proof interface**, counted once.
2. **A ↔ C, geometric:** higher upper/lower witnesses are higher-arity analogues of common-upper/common-lower relations. Their binary graphs and **patch topology** reconstruct the higher I/D complexes, but their simplicial realizations carry additional information; the passage is not merely ordinary transitive closure.
3. **B ↔ C, methodological:** both insist on **actual localic/Stone-sheaf constructions and witnesses** instead of silently replacing them by pointwise formulas. They share coherent/patch infrastructure, but the higher-sheaf comparison theorem is **not a consequence of finite relation-word associativity**.

And four seductive implications are **false or unsupported**:

- **All coherent-localic subrelations associate**: false as a conclusion from the project; only specified proconstructible, rectangular and locally finite algebra classes have proofs.
- **Same ordinary point relation ⇒ same sublocale**: false; the finite-cover bridge and weak/full rectangular distinction show why.
- **Binary graphs determine chain higher homotopy**: false already for six points, although the patched I,D graphs recover **specific** higher witness complexes and 1-type.
- **Finite proof-tree ranks, graph diameter and localic composition rank are equivalent measures**: unsupported; they measure different constructions, with different independent counterexamples.

This is the most defensible unifying statement: **the project studies the boundary between pointwise shadows and effective localic information, and identifies exact situations in which that information can be recovered, composed, or compared.** It does not seek a universal replacement for graphs by a single associative relation algebra.

## 7. What we can confidently say, and what is still provisional

The **independent 21 November structural referee** examined seven interfaces (coherent composition, rectangular RC, MAT/MUL/FINMAT, patched binary reconstruction/groupoids, sharp 2-connectivity, coherent nontransitive H, rational bridge). It found **four valid** and **three valid after explicitly stating imported premises and ambient restrictions**, and no counterexample in the expanded sources. **This does not certify all 47 ledger groups**, even though many have their own earlier blind reviews. Referee-origin addenda (e.g. rectangle algebra unital iff G finite; 2-connectivity) have different provenance from original blind submissions.

Several pieces of infrastructure were in the literature: the basic closed/fitted diagonal, Cornish normal/conormal lattice theory, Johnstone–Sun weak/full products, Booleanization, categorical nonassociativity, properness/effectivity, Dowker/crosscut and realization methods. **Potentially distinctive** exact formulas and constructions merit individual priority checks before publication; a favorable structural referee is not a novelty referee.

The most important **settled** questions include: coherent proconstructible finite composition; \(V<V_{\rm fin}\) examples; nontransitive coherent H; Q/Q′ negative finite-cover bridge; mixed p/q RC; finite-support scattered matrix algebra; all higher patched I/D reconstructed from binary graph + patch and sharp chain→M connectivity in the chosen site; rectangular unitality iff finite.

The important **unsettled or deferred** issues are not these completed theorems but better general necessary/sufficient **safe graph-adjunction** conditions, possible further **MAT/MUL** index-class extensions beyond the already blind-accepted arbitrary-parameter locally compact scattered theorem, broad **TWIST** common-ambient support, general classification of arbitrary-locale hull collapses, more systematic rank realization/bounds, and extension from patched chosen-site comparisons to genuinely intrinsic **localic shape**. Earlier research notes and the [current frontier register](ProjectA_ENDGAME_OPEN_FRONTIERS_2026-11-21.md) give narrower versions; they need not be solved before an archive wrap-up.

## 8. How to resume or develop this work later

For **learning**, the appropriate archived outcome is a self-contained mathematical map: **this synthesis → five worked examples → exact assumption table → theorem ledger → selected canonical proofs and referee reconciliations**. The numerous exploratory notes and negative experiments are not failures; they show which tempting substitutions and generalizations were tested and rejected.

For potential **writing**, there are natural *topics*, but not a claim that three papers are already written:
- **Diagonal-hull hierarchy:** coherent formulas, strict noncoherent examples, V rank, H nontransitivity, finite-cover bridge.
- **Actual localic graph/relation algebra:** weak/full rectangles, exact RC, chart/graph extensions, scattered matrices, and ordinary-diameter versus localic rank.
- **Patched simultaneous-witness homotopy:** higher I/D/M, chain comparison, 1-type and sharp 2-connectivity, with Čech/gluing technical work.

Choosing an actual article should begin with a focused **priority and hypothesis check** for its selected theorems, not an omnibus novelty claim. The project was undertaken chiefly to understand point-free topology, and it need not yield an article to have achieved that purpose.

**Endgame thesis.** The graph intuition that prompted the investigation survives, but in a more exact form: **ordinary graphs are useful shadows of localic relations; coherent patch geometry and selected associative fragments explain when the shadows compute correctly; Booleanization, nonspatial supports and higher simultaneous witnesses explain when they do not.** This accounts for both the positive reconstruction results and the sharp counterexamples without hiding the classical foundations, the proof dependencies, or the remaining boundaries.
