# Project A — Structural study of I=U in arbitrary frames

**20 October 2026. New working observations, with point-free proofs; no claim of novelty or exhaustive literature review.** This note addresses the second stage of the research agenda after the adversarial audit of the subfit arithmetic example. It extracts genuinely frame/locale-theoretic consequences without imposing coherence, algebraicity, continuity, spatiality, or choice. The interpretation via a complete binary orthogonality uses the standard cp-ideal presentation of frame coproducts (Picado–Pultr, *Frames and Locales*, Appendix; also *Separation in Point-Free Topology*, Appendix A.8).

Let L be any frame, X=Loc(L), Q=L⊗L, and set

\[
d_I=\bigvee_{a\wedge b=0}a\boxtimes b,\qquad
d_U=\bigvee_{a^*\vee b^*=1}a\boxtimes b,
\quad I=C(d_I),\quad U=C(d_U).
\]

Always \(d_U\le d_I\).

## 1. The diagonal complement needs only one parameter

**Proposition 1 (all frames).**

\[
\boxed{d_I=\bigvee_{a\in L} a\boxtimes a^*.}
\]

**Proof.** Each \(a\wedge a^*=0\), giving one inclusion. If \(a\wedge b=0\), then \(b\le a^*\), hence \(a\boxtimes b\le a\boxtimes a^*\). Join all pairs. □

**Corollary.**

\[
\boxed{I=U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\forall a\in L)\ a\boxtimes a^*\le d_U.}
\]

This avoids testing arbitrary disjoint pairs separately, although it still mentions the coproduct.

## 2. A universal relational property: closure does not grow

For an element \(a\), let \(O(a)\) be its open sublocale and \(\operatorname{cl}O(a)=C(a^*)\) its closure. For a **closed** relation \(R\hookrightarrow X\times X\), define *closure-nonexpansiveness on opens* by

\[
R\cap (O(a)\times X)
   \subseteq p_2^{-1}(C(a^*))
\qquad\text{for every }a\in L,
\]

where \(p_2\) is the second projection. Equivalently,

\[
R\cap(O(a)\times O(a^*))=\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }a.
\]

**Proposition 2 (all locales).** \(I\) is the **largest closed relation** that is closure-nonexpansive on opens. Thus

\[
\boxed{
I=U\ \Longleftrightarrow\
U\text{ is closure-nonexpansive on all open sublocales}.}
\]

**Proof.** If \(R=C(d_R)\), then \(R\cap O(w)=\varnothing\) iff \(w\le d_R\), because the pullback of \(w\) to the closed locale \(C(d_R)\) is \(w\vee d_R\) and is bottom exactly when \(w\le d_R\). Consequently the displayed property is equivalent to \(a\boxtimes a^*\le d_R\) for every \(a\), hence to \(d_I\le d_R\) by Proposition 1, equivalently \(R\subseteq I\). Since \(I\subseteq U\) always, taking \(R=U\) gives the corollary. □

**Spatial interpretation (ordinary original points, no sobriety needed for this equivalence of point relations).** For a topological space and the ordinary Urysohn-inseparability relation U on its points,

\[
\boxed{
I=U\text{ pointwise}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U[A]\subseteq\overline A\text{ for every open }A.
}
\]

As U is reflexive, this is also \(\overline{U[A]}=\overline A\). If the inclusion fails, \(y\in U[A]\setminus\overline A\), choosing \(x\in A\) with \(xUy\) and a neighborhood of \(y\) disjoint from A shows \(xIy\) fails. Conversely if \(xUy\) but \(x\not Iy\), choose disjoint neighborhoods A of x and B of y: \(y\notin\overline A\) but \(y\in U[A]\), a contradiction.

This separates the precise property from the stronger extremal-disconnectedness identity \(a\wedge b=0\Rightarrow a^*\vee b^*=1\), which would demand that *each entire disjoint rectangle be far without covering/refinement*.

## 3. A tensor-free complete binary orthogonality construction

A **complete orthogonality relation** on \(L\) means a subset \(S\subseteq L\times L\) satisfying:

- downward heredity in both variables;
- closure under **arbitrary** joins in either variable separately: if \((a_i,b)\in S\) for all i then \((\bigvee_i a_i,b)\in S\), and symmetrically;
- symmetry under swapping coordinates.

Because empty joins are allowed, the zero-coordinate axes lie in every such S.

Let \(\mathsf{Far}=\{(a,b):a^*\vee b^*=1\}\), which is downward closed and symmetric but need not be complete under joins. Let \(\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\) be the **least complete orthogonality relation** containing it (intersection of all such relations). Let

\[
\mathsf{Disj}=\{(a,b):a\wedge b=0\}.
\]

The latter is already a complete orthogonality relation by frame distributivity, and \(\mathsf{Far}\subseteq\mathsf{Disj}\), so \(\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\subseteq\mathsf{Disj}\).

**Proposition 3 (all frames, by cp-ideals).**

\[
\boxed{ I=U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}=\mathsf{Disj}. }
\]

**Proof.** In the standard cp-ideal representation of \(L\otimes L\), elements correspond to downward-closed subsets of \(L\times L\), containing zero axes and closed under arbitrary joins in each coordinate. A pure rectangle \(a\boxtimes b\) corresponds to the principal cp-ideal generated by \((a,b)\). The element \(d_U\) is therefore exactly the cp-ideal generated by \(\mathsf{Far}\), namely \(\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\); similarly, because \(\mathsf{Disj}\) already has the requisite closure properties, \(d_I\) corresponds to \(\mathsf{Disj}\). Equality in the frame coproduct is equality of these cp-ideals. □

**Operational test:** Start from pairs satisfying \(a^*\vee b^*=1\) and close under (i) shrinking either coordinate and (ii) arbitrary joins in a fixed coordinate. Then I=U **if and only if this process eventually derives every pair with zero meet**. Transfinite iteration is allowed (the closure operator exists on the powerset of \(L\times L\)); this is an intrinsic proof calculus even for frames with no compact elements. It is not asserted that finitely many rounds always suffice.

## 4. A unary “separation polar”

Define

\[
a^\diamond
=\bigvee\{b\in L:(a,b)\in\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\}.
\]

By closure under joins in the second coordinate, \((a,b)\in\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\) exactly when \(b\le a^\diamond\).

**Proposition 4 (all frames).**

1. \(a^\diamond\le a^*\);
2. \(a\le b^\diamond\iff b\le a^\diamond\) (self-adjoint antitone polarity);
3. \((\bigvee_i a_i)^\diamond=\bigwedge_i a_i^\diamond\);
4. \(a\le a^{\diamond\diamond}\);
5. \(\boxed{I=U\iff a^\diamond=a^*\text{ for every }a\in L.}\)

**Proof.** (1) follows from \(\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}\subseteq\mathsf{Disj}\). Symmetry of the complete orthogonality proves (2), and the separate-coordinate arbitrary join rule proves (3); (4) is the adjunction consequence of (2). Finally, complete disjointness has unary polar exactly \(a^*=\bigvee\{b:a\wedge b=0\}\), and both complete relations are determined by their polars, proving (5). □

This unary operator is mathematically intrinsic but is constructed by a *completion process*, not by a simple closed formula in \(\wedge,\vee,{}^*\). Whether it is an established construction in frame proximity/nearness theory is worth checking.

## 5. Open heredity, completely point-free

**Theorem 5.** If \(I(X)=U(X)\), then \(I(V)=U(V)\) for **every open sublocale** \(V=O(c)\hookrightarrow X\), without spatiality or continuity assumptions.

**Proof.** Put \(L_c=\downarrow c\), the open-sublocale frame with top \(c\). Its product identifies with the open part \(\downarrow(c\boxtimes c)\subseteq Q\). Under this identification,

\[
d_I(L_c)=d_I(L)\wedge(c\boxtimes c),
\]

because disjointness is inherited by restriction and every ambient disjoint rectangle restricts to a disjoint rectangle below \(c\).

For \(u,v\in L\) with \(u^*\vee v^*=1\), the restricted pair \(u\wedge c,v\wedge c\) is far in \(L_c\), since the relative pseudocomplements satisfy
\((u\wedge c)^{*_{c}}\ge c\wedge u^*\) and likewise for v. Therefore

\[
d_U(L_c)\ \ge\ d_U(L)\wedge(c\boxtimes c).
\]

If \(d_I(L)=d_U(L)\), these inequalities, together with the universal \(d_U(L_c)\le d_I(L_c)\), imply equality. □

Thus I=U is **open-hereditary in Loc**, not only for the original points of spatial spaces. This is a nontrivial stability property for arbitrary noncoherent/noncontinuous frames.

## 5½. Closed heredity fails, even among coherent locales

Open heredity is genuinely asymmetric: **I=U need not pass to closed sublocales**.

Take a field k and let \(X=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y]\). This space is irreducible because k[x,y] is an integral domain. Consequently **every two nonempty opens intersect**, and the only rectangles in \(d_I\) and \(d_U\) with disjoint/far coordinates are zero. Hence \(I(X)=U(X)=X\times X\).

Now take the **closed** subspace

\[
Z=V(xy)\ \cong\ \operatorname{Spec}(k[x,y]/(xy)).
\]

In \(Z\) the compact Zariski opens \(D(x)\) and \(D(y)\) are disjoint because \(xy=0\) in the quotient ring. However, their closures are the two irreducible components \(V(y)\) and \(V(x)\), intersecting at the maximal ideal \((x,y)\). Thus they are **not far**. Since \(D(x),D(y)\) are **compact elements**, the algebraic-frame criterion (or the finite-rectangle farness lemma) implies \(I(Z)\ne U(Z)\). The compactness is essential to this inference: merely finding disjoint opens with intersecting closures would *not* contradict I=U in a general frame.

This is a simple coherent/spectral example showing the equality is open-hereditary but **not closed-hereditary**. It also rules out attempts to characterize I=U by a property inherited under arbitrary sublocale inclusions.

## 6. Recovering previously proved subclasses

- **Regular frames:** if \(a\wedge b=0\), decompose \(a,b\) into rather-below approximants \(u\prec a,v\prec b\). Each pair is far since \(u^*\vee v^*\ge u^*\vee a=1\). Thus disjointness is generated from far pairs by arbitrary joins in the two coordinates; I=U.
- **Algebraic frames:** equality holds iff every disjoint pair of **compact elements** is far. Necessity uses compactness of their tensor and finite farness; sufficiency uses compact generation. This includes noncoherent arithmetic examples.
- **Coherent frames:** the compact criterion becomes conormality of the bounded distributive lattice K(L), using compactness of 1.
- **Continuous frames (classical):** equality holds iff \(u^*\vee v^*=1\) for \(u\ll a,v\ll b\) whenever \(a\wedge b=0\), via the finite-rectangle lemma and relative compactness of \(u\boxtimes v\). The proof uses Hofmann–Lawson spatial representation. This is not exported to arbitrary noncontinuous frames.

These agree with the earlier proofs in [A-coherent-conormality.md](A-coherent-conormality.md), [A-continuous-frames.md](A-continuous-frames.md), and [A-equality-characterizations.md](A-equality-characterizations.md).

## 6½. Frame–coframe theorem: I=U collapses to extremal disconnectedness

The initiating far relation \(\mathsf{Far}\) has **more finite structure** than might appear: it is downward closed and closed under **finite joins in either coordinate**, even without assuming I=U. Indeed,

\[
\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^n a_i\right)^*\vee b^*
=\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^n a_i^*\right)\vee b^*
=\bigwedge_{i=1}^n(a_i^*\vee b^*)=1
\]

whenever each \(a_i^*\vee b^*=1\), by finite distributivity. Thus only **infinite coordinate joins** can enlarge the initial far relation in the complete orthogonality generated in §3.

**Theorem 6 (arbitrary frame–coframes, point-free).** Suppose \(L\) is both a **frame and a coframe**: finite joins distribute over arbitrary meets as well as finite meets distributing over arbitrary joins. Then

\[
\boxed{I=U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L\text{ is extremally disconnected},\ \ a^*\vee a^{**}=1
\text{ for every }a.}
\]

**Proof.** Coframe distributivity makes \(\mathsf{Far}\) closed under **arbitrary** joins in either coordinate. For a fixed b and a family (a_i) of far partners,

\[
\left(\bigvee_i a_i\right)^*\vee b^*
=\left(\bigwedge_i a_i^*\right)\vee b^*
=\bigwedge_i(a_i^*\vee b^*)=1.
\]

The other coordinate is symmetric. Hence \(\widehat{\mathsf{Far}}=\mathsf{Far}\) in §3, so I=U iff **every disjoint pair is far**.

That elementwise implication is equivalent to extremal disconnectedness: choose b=a* to deduce \(a^*\vee a^{**}=1\); conversely if \(a\wedge b=0\), then \(b\le a^*\) gives \(b^*\ge a^{**}\), and thus \(a^*\vee b^*\ge a^*\vee a^{**}=1\). □

**Corollary (arbitrary Alexandrov spaces).** If \(P\) is any poset with its **upper-set Alexandrov topology**, its open frame is a sublattice of the powerset lattice closed under arbitrary unions and intersections. Hence it is a frame and coframe. Consequently

\[
\boxed{I(P)=U(P)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal O(P)\text{ is extremally disconnected}.}
\]

There is **no finiteness assumption** on P. The finite-frame criterion in [A-coherent-conormality.md](A-coherent-conormality.md), §5, therefore extends to all upper-set Alexandrov spaces. The result also applies to other abstract frames which are simultaneously coframes, regardless of compact generation or spatiality.

**Boundary:** The additional coframe distributivity is sufficient for this collapse, not known to be necessary. For example \(L=\mathcal O(\mathbb R)\) has I=U by regularity, but is not extremally disconnected; its far relation is strictly smaller than disjointness and only their **complete closure** agrees.

## 7. Genuine remaining questions

1. Can the separation polar \(a^\diamond\) be computed directly from \(a^*\) and standard frame operations, **without** free binary completion or the coproduct? Is it already studied under another name in proximity/nearness theory?
2. Does equality I=U imply the **far-grid property** (join decompositions \(a=\bigvee a_i,b=\bigvee b_j\), with every cross pair \(a_i,b_j\) far) for arbitrary frames? This is known in our *continuous* and *algebraic* examples but was proved only **sufficient** in [A-equality-characterizations.md](A-equality-characterizations.md) for arbitrary L.
3. Can the binary inference closure from §3 require genuinely transfinite stages, and what is its closure ordinal? This question is meaningful precisely when coherent or continuous approximants do not control the frame.
4. Compare the closure-nonexpansiveness principle (§2) with existing localic proximity, nearness, diagonal, and strong-inclusion axioms before claiming novelty.
5. Study the behavior under other sublocales and quotient maps. The open-sublocale hereditary result above is a first strictly point-free stability theorem; no general hereditary claim for arbitrary sublocales is made.

**Priority:** Picado–Pultr's tensor/cp-ideal and rather-below apparatus is established. The current synthesis and particular formulations have not received a systematic novelty audit. Relevant nearby literature includes J. Picado and A. Pultr, *On Strong Inclusions and Asymmetric Proximities in Frames* (2010), and Bezhanishvili–Harding, *Proximity Frames and Regularization* (2013); neither has been checked for the exact binary-completion characterization here.
