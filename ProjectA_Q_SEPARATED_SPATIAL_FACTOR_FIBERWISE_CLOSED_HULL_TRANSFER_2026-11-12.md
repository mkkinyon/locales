# Project A — Fiberwise closed-hull transfer over spatial separated factors; exact descent of rank-one holes

**12 November 2026. NEW INTERNAL theorem, adversarially checked in this segment, NOT externally refereed or literature-priority checked. Universal Q and Q′ remain OPEN.** This is the next bounded test after the [symmetric-hole/Boolean doubling and Cantor-fence control](ProjectA_Q_SYMMETRIC_HOLE_SEPARATE_DENSITY_AND_BOOLEAN_DOUBLE_NO_GO_2026-11-12.md). Instead of demanding ordinary finite product-box decompositions of a fibered diagonal, we use **generalized-pair slices along the points of a spatial separated factor** to compute the ENTIRE **CLOSED** diagonal hulls. The result works with an arbitrary potentially POINTLESS second locale, and with no local-compactness/compactness assumption on either factor. It provides an exact preservation/REFLECTION theorem for Q/Q′ and a constructive **fiberwise descent of an arbitrary bad cover's nonzero open hole**.

**No assertion about the NONCLOSED fitted diagonals \(\mathcal D,\mathcal D_2\) themselves multiplying** is made. The key is their DENSITY in the closed hull.

## 0. Framework

Let Z be a NONEMPTY spatial locale (i.e. its ordinary points detect nonzero opens), let Y be ANY locale (possibly pointless), and put \(X=Z\times Y\). Reorder
\[
X^2=(Z\times Y)^2\cong Z^2\times Y^2.
\]
For a locale T let \(\mathcal D(T)\) and \(\mathcal D_2(T)\) be the full and finite fitted diagonals generated by the open diagonal cover squares \(w_C=\bigvee_{c\in C}c\boxtimes c\). Let
\[
\mathcal W(T)=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D(T),\quad
\mathcal W_2(T)=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D_2(T),\quad
\mathcal U(T)=\mathfrak c(d_{\mathcal U(T)})
\]
be the CLOSED diagonal relations, where \(d_{\mathcal U}=\bigvee_{a^*\vee b^*=1}a\boxtimes b=\Phi(0)=\Phi_2(0)\).

For \(\epsilon\in\{\mathrm{full},2\}\), abbreviate \(\mathcal D_\epsilon,\mathcal W_\epsilon\) accordingly. We import the already established **lax naturality** of each closed hull under locale maps:
\[
\mathcal W_\epsilon(X)\le(f\times f)^{-1}\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y),
\qquad \mathcal U(X)\le(f\times f)^{-1}\mathcal U(Y).
\tag{0.1}
\]
For U, the latter is the elementary fact that **far rectangles pull back to far rectangles**, using \(f^*(a^*)\le(f^*a)^*\).

## 1. A point-slice lemma requiring NO spatiality of Y

For any ordinary locale point \(z:1\to Z\), let \(s_z:Y\to Z\times Y\) be \((z,\operatorname{id}_Y)\); let \(j_z=s_z\times s_z:Y^2\to X^2\).

**Lemma 1.1 (full/finite diagonal-cover lifting along a fixed fiber).** For either ε and any generalized pair T→Y² factoring through \(\mathcal D_\epsilon(Y)\), the composite T→Y²→X² along j_z factors through \(\mathcal D_\epsilon(X)\). More precisely
\[
\boxed{j_z(\mathcal D_\epsilon(Y))\le\mathcal D_\epsilon(X).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

*Proof.* Let C be ANY X-open cover, finite in the ε=2 case. Its restrictions \(C_z=\{s_z^*c:c\in C\}\) cover Y (and remain finite if C is). The pair-square open pulls back as
\[
j_z^*w_C^X=\bigvee_{c\in C}(s_z^*c)\boxtimes(s_z^*c)=w_{C_z}^Y.
\]
Every Dε(Y)-generalized pair satisfies the latter cover. Therefore its composite satisfies every appropriate X-cover, i.e. factors through Dε(X). □

**Lemma 1.2 (far relations lift along each fixed fiber).**
\[
\boxed{j_z(\mathcal U(Y))\le\mathcal U(X).}
\tag{1.2}
\]
*Proof.* Every far pair of X-opens restricts along s_z to a far pair of Y-opens, because frame inverse image satisfies \(f^*(a^*)\le(f^*a)^*\). Thus \(j_z^*(d_{\mathcal U(X)})\le d_{\mathcal U(Y)}\), proving the displayed sublocale containment. □

**Elementary product-density lemma (point-free in the second factor).** Let R≤Y² be ANY nonzero sublocale, S≤R dense, and suppose \(Z\) is spatial and nonempty. Then the sublocale **join**
\[
\bigvee_{z\in\operatorname{pt}Z}(\{z\}\times S)
\]
is DENSE in \(Z\times R\). To check, any nonzero relative open of the product contains a nonzero elementary open \(a\boxtimes b\) with \(a\ne0_Z\), \(b\ne0_R\). Choose a point z∈a (spatiality). Since S is dense in R, S∧O_R(b)≠0, so this relative rectangle meets the z-slice S. Nothing about ordinary points of R or S is used.

## 2. THE EXACT closed-hull product theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (full and finite closed diagonal hulls over a spatial W₂-separated base).** Suppose
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W_2(Z)=\Delta_Z.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Because \(\Delta_Z\le\mathcal W(Z)\le\mathcal W_2(Z)\), the two closed hulls on Z both equal Δ_Z, which is therefore a CLOSED sublocale of Z². For EVERY locale Y:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W(Z\times Y)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W(Y),\qquad
\mathcal W_2(Z\times Y)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W_2(Y).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

*Proof.* Naturality (0.1) for projections onto Z and Y gives the upper inclusions
\[
\mathcal W_\epsilon(X)\le
(\mathcal W_\epsilon(Z)\times Y^2)\wedge(Z^2\times\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y))
=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y).
\tag{2.3}
\]
For the reverse, choose z∈pt Z. Lemma 1.1 says the slice sublocale \(\{z\}\times\mathcal D_\epsilon(Y)\) lies in \(\mathcal D_\epsilon(X)\), hence in the CLOSED sublocale \(\mathcal W_\epsilon(X)\). The joins of these slices are dense in the CLOSED product \(\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y)\cong Z\times\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y)\), since Dε(Y) is dense in its closure Wε(Y). As Wε(X) is closed and contains all the slices, it contains the entire closed product. This proves the reverse containment and equality. □

**THE IMPORTANT LIMITATION:** The proof only concludes equality for the **CLOSED HULLS**. It does not infer
\(\mathcal D_\epsilon(X)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal D_\epsilon(Y)\), because a sublocale can contain all point slices without containing their join's **closure**. Exactly this nonclosed-versus-closed distinction is why the proof is valid even for POINTLESS Y.

**Corollary 2.2 (Q′ transfer and reflection).** If Z satisfies Theorem 2.1, then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X)
\iff
\mathcal W(Y)=\mathcal W_2(Y).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
*Proof.* The forward implication restricts along one ordinary z∈Z, and the reverse follows from (2.2). Thus a separated spatial factor cannot create, hide or repair a Q′ gap. □

## 3. Exact Urysohn transfer and Q when the base is U-separated

**Theorem 3.1 (Urysohn-separation factor).** Assume the stronger condition
\[
\boxed{\mathcal U(Z)=\Delta_Z.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This implies the hypothesis (2.1). For EVERY locale Y,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W(Z\times Y)&=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W(Y),\\
\mathcal W_2(Z\times Y)&=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W_2(Y),\\
\mathcal U(Z\times Y)&=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.2}
\]

*Proof.* The first two formulas are Theorem 2.1. For U, naturality for the two projections gives
\(\mathcal U(X)\le \Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y)\). Conversely, every z-slice \(\{z\}\times\mathcal U(Y)\) lies in the CLOSED U(X) by Lemma 1.2. Their join is dense in the product \(\Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y)\) since Z is spatial. Hence the reverse inclusion. □

Examples of the hypothesis: a spatial zero-dimensional Hausdorff locale, including **Cantor space or Baire space**, and a spatial regular Hausdorff locale such as \(\mathbb R\). For the last two standard locally compact examples the localic square is spatial and far-separation of distinct points is ordinary regularity; for arbitrary non-locally-compact zero-dimensional spaces, the pointfree clopen-generation calculation also gives \(\mathcal U(Z)=\Delta_Z\). The theorem itself assumes equality rather than relying on terminology conventions.

**Corollary 3.2 (Q and Q′ factor invariance).** Under (3.1), with nonzero Z, for EVERY Y:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W_2(X)=\mathcal U(X)&\iff \mathcal W_2(Y)=\mathcal U(Y),\\
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal U(X)&\iff \mathcal W(Y)=\mathcal U(Y),\\
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X)&\iff \mathcal W(Y)=\mathcal W_2(Y).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Thus **Question Q holds on X iff it holds on Y**, and likewise Q′; a genuine counterexample to either question transfers in BOTH directions.

This is stronger than the October product-with-a-common-predecessor-dense-factor transfer (which handled a different class of factors), and independent of the November finite-Alexandrov W product theorem. It makes NO claim of a product formula for Dε itself.

## 4. Constructive descent and ascent of a SPECIFIC bad-cover hole

The relation formulas alone already transfer strictness. In fact an arbitrary specific **bad cover** on X descends to a bad cover on some Y-slice, and a Y bad cover ascends by projection.

Fix ε=2, and put \(d_X=d_{\mathcal W_2(X)}\), \(d_Y=d_{\mathcal W_2(Y)}\). Let \(C\) be ANY cover of X such that its canonical FULL-first-step hole
\[
E_C^X=\mathcal W_2(X)\wedge O_{X^2}(\rho_C^X)>0,\qquad
\rho_C^X=w_C^X\to d_X
\tag{4.1}
\]
is nonzero. By Theorem 2.1 identify \(\mathcal W_2(X)\cong Z\times\mathcal W_2(Y)\).

**Theorem 4.1 (uniform open-base descent).** There exist NONZERO opens \(a\in O(Z)\) and \(b\in O(\mathcal W_2(Y))\) such that
\[
\boxed{a\boxtimes b\le E_C^X,\qquad b\wedge\mathcal W(Y)=0,}
\tag{4.2}
\]
and **for EVERY ordinary point** \(z\in a\),
\[
\boxed{
0<b\le
E^Y_{C_z}
=\mathcal W_2(Y)\wedge O_{Y^2}\bigl(w_{C_z}^Y\to d_Y\bigr).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Here \(C_z=\{s_z^*c:c\in C\}\) is a cover of Y. In particular EACH C_z with z∈a witnesses a Q′ gap on Y and the SAME nonzero b works on the entire open base patch a.

*Proof.* E_C^X is a NONZERO relatively OPEN sublocale of the product \(Z\times\mathcal W_2(Y)\). The product rectangle basis gives nonzero a,b with a×b≤E_C^X. Since
\(\mathcal W(X)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W(Y)\) and the original full-hole E_C^X misses W(X), the nonzero product a×(b∧W(Y)) is impossible; thus b∧W(Y)=0.

For each z∈a, the slice of a×b is b, so \(b\le\mathcal W_2(Y)\wedge O(j_z^*\rho_C^X)\). By the closed product formula \(j_z^*(d_X)=d_Y\), and \(j_z^*w_C^X=w_{C_z}^Y\). Because Heyting implication is preserved only LAXLY,
\[
j_z^*\rho_C^X
\le (j_z^*w_C^X\to j_z^*d_X)
= w_{C_z}^Y\to d_Y.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus b is included in the canonical Y-hole for every z∈a. □

**Theorem 4.2 (ascent by pulled-back cover).** Conversely, if \(C_Y\) is a Y-cover with \(E_{C_Y}^Y>0\), let \(C_X=\{\pi_Y^*c:c\in C_Y\}\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_Z\times E_{C_Y}^Y\le E_{C_X}^X.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
*Proof.* Put \(\rho_Y=w_{C_Y}\to d_Y\). Naturality of W₂ gives \((\pi_Y^2)^*d_Y\le d_X\), and
\[
(\pi_Y^2)^*\rho_Y\wedge w_{C_X}
=(\pi_Y^2)^*(\rho_Y\wedge w_{C_Y})
\le(\pi_Y^2)^*d_Y\le d_X.
\]
Therefore \((\pi_Y^2)^*\rho_Y\le w_{C_X}\to d_X\). Intersect with
\(\mathcal W_2(X)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal W_2(Y)\) to obtain (4.5). The right-side inclusion is nonzero since Z is nonempty. □

**Rank-one Q specialization.** Under Theorem 3.1, if \(\mathcal W_2(X)=\mathcal U(X)\) then \(\mathcal W_2(Y)=\mathcal U(Y)\). Replacing \(d_X,d_Y\) throughout by \(z_X=d_{\mathcal U(X)},z_Y=d_{\mathcal U(Y)}\) makes (4.1)–(4.5) exact statements about **rank-one canonical holes**. Hence no bad cover on a separated spatial product can represent an irreducibly fiber-entangled counterexample: one open patch of base fibers already witnesses a bad Y-cover.

**Very important scope:** A fixed bad X-cover C may restrict to different bad covers C_z as z varies; the theorem supplies a COMMON nonzero Y-hole b but does NOT claim the covers C_z are constant or isomorphic. Nor does it assert the complete canonical X-hole is the product of base Z and one Y-hole.

## 5. An additional formula for inseparability and the perfect-base complexity control

For this section assume Z has CLOSED diagonal (true if \(\mathcal U(Z)=\Delta_Z\)), and is spatial and nonempty.

**Lemma 5.1 (inseparability product).**
\[
\boxed{\mathcal I(Z\times Y)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal I(Y).}
\tag{5.1}
\]
*Proof.* \(\Delta_X=\Delta_Z\times\Delta_Y\) lies in the closed sublocale \(\Delta_Z\times\operatorname{cl}\Delta_Y\), giving the upper bound. For each z, the point z is CLOSED in Z; the closed slice \(j_z:Y^2\hookrightarrow X^2\) carries the closure of ΔY to the closure of its image, namely \(\{z\}\times\mathcal I(Y)\), hence \(\{z\}\times\mathcal I(Y)\le\mathcal I(X)\). These slices are dense in \(\Delta_Z\times\mathcal I(Y)\) by spatiality, yielding equality. □

If Z has **no isolated points**, then \(\Delta_Z\) has empty ambient interior in \(Z^2\); hence \(\mathcal U(X)=\Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y)\) has empty interior in X² (for nonempty Y), and its regular-closed core is ZERO. Thus the entire nonregular off-inseparability sector is
\[
\boxed{
G_\perp(X)=\Delta_Z\times\bigl(\mathcal U(Y)\wedge O_{Y^2}(d_{\mathcal I(Y)})\bigr).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
For e.g. Z=Cantor and a finite Alexandrov Y with U(Y)>I(Y), this is NONZERO despite \(\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X)=\mathcal U(X)\) whenever W(Y)=U(Y).

**Lemma 5.2 (no finite ordinary boxes over a perfect separated base).** If Z is spatial, Hausdorff, and has no isolated points, then EVERY nonzero relatively OPEN sublocale
\[
V\le \Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y)
\]
has infinite product-box rank in X² whenever \(\mathcal U(Y)\ne0\). No finite union of *genuine product sublocales* \(S_i\times T_i\subseteq X\times X\) can equal V.

*Proof.* A nonzero product sublocale \(S\times T\le\Delta_Z\times\mathcal U(Y)\) must have its two Z-coordinate closed image supports contained in the SAME singleton {z}. Indeed, if the supports contained distinct z≠z′, choose disjoint opens a∋z,b∋z′; the restrictions of S,T to their coordinate a,b are nonzero by density in their closed images, so their product is nonzero, contradicting the inclusion inside the closed diagonal over Z. Thus every nonzero product box is supported over a SINGLE base z. Any nonzero open V in \(Z\times\mathcal U(Y)\) contains a nonzero open rectangle a×v. Since Z has no isolated points, a contains infinitely many z; each z-slice of a×v is nonzero. Finitely many single-fiber product boxes cannot cover V. □

**Interpretation:** Rank-one geometry of spatial separated products is often **INFINITELY** box-complex at every open scale even when Q holds; the exact transfer theorems (3.2) and (4.2) explain why this complexity is irrelevant. The central invariant should be the **fiberwise CLOSED relation** or the actual nonzero OPEN hole, not the number of ordinary product boxes needed.

## 6. CLOPEN-locally-trivial bundles: the product formulas glue without a global product

The previous transfer argument also applies to a bundle which is only locally a product, provided the trivializing charts are **CLOPEN** in the base. The clopen condition is not cosmetic: it supplies both exact closed heredity of fitted diagonals and openness needed for closure localization. No arbitrary open-trivialization theorem is claimed.

Let \(p:X\to Z\) be a locale map, where Z is nonempty, spatial and \(\mathcal U(Z)=\Delta_Z\). Suppose \(\{e_i:i\in I\}\subseteq O(Z)\) is a cover by **complemented opens**, and for each i there is a fixed-fiber **over-base** isomorphism
\[
\boxed{p^{-1}O_Z(e_i)\ \cong\ O_Z(e_i)\times Y.}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Here \(O_Z(e_i)\) denotes the open-and-closed sublocale of Z, and Y is the SAME arbitrary locale for all i. The charts may be GLUED NONTRIVIALLY: X need not be globally isomorphic to Z×Y.

**Lemma 6.1 (clopen localization of closed relations).** If J↪X is clopen, then for \(R\in\{\mathcal W,\mathcal W_2,\mathcal U,\mathcal I\}\):
\[
\boxed{R(J)=R(X)\wedge J^2.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

*Proof.* For W and W₂, J is CLOSED, so the independently audited fitted-diagonal heredity says
\(\mathcal D_\epsilon(J)=\mathcal D_\epsilon(X)\wedge J^2\).
Because J² is ALSO OPEN in X², relative closure commutes with its restriction:
\[
\operatorname{cl}_{J^2}\bigl(S\wedge J^2\bigr)
=\operatorname{cl}_{X^2}(S)\wedge J^2.
\]
For U, far rectangles restrict to far rectangles. Conversely, a pair of J-far opens a,b≤J extends to a pair of X-far opens: write J=O(e), with complement f=e^*, and note \(a^*\vee b^*\) contains both e (by J-farness) and f (since a,b≤e), hence is 1. Thus the complement-defining far open restricts exactly and U(J)=U(X)∧J². For I, ordinary closure of the diagonal commutes with open restriction, proving the same. □

**Lemma 6.2 (closed-relation gluing on the fiber square).** Put \(F=X\times_Z X=(p\times p)^{-1}\Delta_Z\le X^2\). This is CLOSED, and
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W(X),\mathcal W_2(X),\mathcal U(X)\le F}
\tag{6.3}
\]
by naturality. Write \(J_i=p^{-1}O_Z(e_i)\). The open sublocales \(F\wedge J_i^2\) COVER F: on F the two base coordinates agree, so the restriction of the ith square is simply the pullback of e_i along the common base map, and \(\bigvee_ie_i=1\). If two CLOSED sublocales R,S of F have equal restrictions to all these open patches, then R=S. Indeed the complements of R,S within F are OPENS of F, and their meets with the covering opens agree, hence the complements themselves agree. No unsupported distributivity of arbitrary sublocale meets over infinite joins is used.

**Theorem 6.3 (clopen-trivial bundle invariance; NEW INTERNAL).** Under (6.1) for a fixed Y:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W_2(X)=\mathcal U(X)&\iff \mathcal W_2(Y)=\mathcal U(Y),\\
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal U(X)&\iff \mathcal W(Y)=\mathcal U(Y),\\
\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X)&\iff\mathcal W(Y)=\mathcal W_2(Y).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus Q(X) iff Q(Y), and Q′(X) iff Q′(Y). A nontrivial locally constant bundle over a spatial zero-dimensional separated base does not create or destroy either counterexample.

*Proof.* For each i, Lemma 6.1 and the local product formula Theorem 3.1 compute the three restrictions on \(J_i^2\) as
\[
\mathcal W_\epsilon(X)\wedge J_i^2
\cong\Delta_{O_Z(e_i)}\times\mathcal W_\epsilon(Y),\qquad
\mathcal U(X)\wedge J_i^2
\cong\Delta_{O_Z(e_i)}\times\mathcal U(Y).
\tag{6.5}
\]
If the desired Y equality holds, all local X relations agree, hence the global X relations agree by Lemma 6.2. Conversely, if a global X equality holds, restrict to ANY nonzero e_i, use (6.5), and then take one ordinary point of that spatial nonempty e_i to reflect equality in Y. At least one e_i is nonzero because Z is nonempty and the family covers it. The three equivalences follow separately. □

**Generalization with varying fibers:** If the same clopen charts have fibers Y_i which may vary, each of the THREE global equality statements holds precisely when it holds for EVERY Y_i. But do NOT infer that the logical implication "Q holds" on X is equivalent to "Q holds" on each Y_i without a shared antecedent: one fiber could fail finite stabilization, making the global implication true vacuously despite a different Q-bad fiber.

**Cover-level descent and ascent.** A nonzero relative OPEN W₂ or rank-one U hole E⊆F must meet \(J_i^2\) nontrivially for SOME i, because these clopen squares cover F. By Lemma 6.1 this is a genuine hole on \(J_i\cong O_Z(e_i)\times Y\); the uniform fiberwise descent theorem §4 then yields a bad Y-cover on a nonempty base-open patch. Conversely, any bad Y-cover lifts to a hole on a nonzero trivializing chart, and by adjoining the clopen exterior \(X\setminus J_i\) to that chart's cover obtains a global X-cover with a hole. Thus even an **arbitrary genuinely twisted clopen-locally-trivial bundle has no irreducibly cross-chart Q or Q′ obstruction**.

**Corollary 6.4 (ALL locally trivial fixed-fiber bundles over spatial zero-dimensional bases).** If Z is nonempty spatial ZERO-DIMENSIONAL and \(p:X\to Z\) is ANY locally trivial locale bundle with the SAME fiber Y, then (6.4) holds even if only an arbitrary OPEN cover of local trivialisations was initially specified. **Reason:** zero-dimensionality means every base open is a JOIN of CLOPEN opens. Refine each trivializing open by such complemented subopens; the restricted bundle charts remain trivial and yield the CLOPEN cover required in Theorem 6.3. This step needs no paracompactness or global finite partition. In particular the result applies to ALL fixed-fiber locally trivial bundles over Cantor space or Baire space, even without a globally chosen product identification.

**Scope / practical geometry.** The CLOPEN-triviality condition is a real hypothesis on a GENERAL non-zero-dimensional separated base. For a ZERO-DIMENSIONAL base it follows automatically from ordinary open local triviality, as just proved. The theorem makes no claim about NONCLOSED Dε, POINTLESS bases without enough points, or arbitrary locally trivial maps over non-zero-dimensional bases.

## 7. Adversarial limits and next test

- This does NOT solve Q or Q′ universally. It proves a strong preservation-and-reflection class and explicitly descends actual bad covers.
- **Spatiality of Z is essential to this proof**: the reverse containment uses dense ordinary point slices. A POINTLESS zero-dimensional locale is not covered, even if \(\mathcal U(Z)=\Delta_Z\). Removing spatiality would require a genuine pointfree local patching/base-change argument, not an unsupported “same fiber” analogy.
- **Nonempty Z is essential for reflecting strictness**. The empty factor kills all relations.
- No equality for NONCLOSED D or D₂ is asserted; the slices only prove Dε(X) is DENSE in the stated closed product.
- The assertion U(Z)=Δ_Z is an explicit LOCALE equality, not a statement merely that ordinary U-related points of Z coincide with ordinary diagonal points.
- The I formula uses closedness of the z slices. It is not being incorrectly inferred from the far-rectangle formula for U.
- The uniform descent theorem uses the slice-specific lax Heyting inequality, not the false assertion \(j_z^*(w_C\to d_X)=w_{C_z}\to d_Y\).
- The infinite-box control is a strategy falsification check, not a Q obstruction: fiberwise diagonal complexity need NOT be finite even in Q-positive locales.

**NEXT BOUNDED TEST:** Can the theorem extend to locally trivial bundles over separated SPATIAL bases that are NOT zero-dimensional (where arbitrary open trivialisations need not refine to clopen ones), or to bases without enough points? Those steps would require new locality or pointfree base-change results. The theorems here establish exact closed-hull transfer and local hole descent for products and clopen-trivial bundles; they do not infer a general product law for arbitrary locale factors.
