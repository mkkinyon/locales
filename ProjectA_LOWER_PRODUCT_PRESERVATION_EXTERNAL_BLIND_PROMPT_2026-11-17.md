# Independent blind referee request — Project A: products of the lower hierarchy

**17 November 2026**

Thank you for examining this packet. The subject is product behavior of five relations attached to a locale: inseparability \(\mathcal I\), the fitted diagonal \(\mathcal D\), the fitted inseparability relation \(\mathcal V\), the closure of the fitted diagonal \(\mathcal W\), and their meet \(\mathcal M\).

## Blind-review protocol

Please first review **only the accompanying mathematical packet** (and external published references as needed). Do not consult Project A's internal audit, consolidation, repository README, prior referee reports, or any other account of the authors' conclusions until you have completed your own independent report. The internal audit is *not* included here. We would especially appreciate your testing the proofs in ways not suggested by their presentation.

The investigation is partly motivated by the difference between **locale products** (dual to frame coproducts) and **frame direct products** (dual to locale coproducts), and by the possibility that relations which have tractable point descriptions nonetheless fail to preserve products *as sublocales*. The packet includes both positive results and a proposed counterexample that exploits the nonspatial localic product of two rational locales.

This is a mathematical research audit, not a request to endorse a manuscript. No novelty or priority assertion is made. Please consider **all** assertions in scope, including side remarks, hypotheses, notation, and bibliographical attributions.

## Questions for the referee

1. **Global correctness.** Which assertions are correct as *equalities/inclusions of sublocales*? Are any arguments proving only an equality of point sets? Are hypotheses inadvertently missing?
2. **Previously established inputs.** Check that the stably locally compact (SLC) point-induced formulas and fitted-saturation formula stated in §2 are used correctly. You may take the cited SC+ theorem as a previously proved input, but please flag any use stronger than its stated content. In particular, \([P]\) need not have exactly \(P\) as its points.
3. **The Cantor witness example.** Is the compactified partial order closed, and is the resulting space genuinely SLC with an algebraic frame? Is the rational cross-sheet slice computed correctly? Is the open-sublocale identification rigorous? Does the nonspatiality argument really give a *strict* \(\mathcal V\)-product inequality?
4. **Positive \(\mathcal W\) theorem.** Does \(\mathcal W\) commute with binary locale products in the SLC class as an equality of actual closed sublocales, even though \(\mathcal D\) may not?
5. **Compact-factor theorem.** Does the proof that \(\mathcal V\) preserves locale products when one factor is stably compact correctly invoke patch-closedness, inherited stable compactness, the localic-versus-topological product comparison, and naturality of the induced embeddings? Is the statement true as written?
6. **The \(\mathcal D\)- and \(\mathcal M\)-counterexamples.** Check the proposed extension by a greatest specialization point, particularly restriction to the old closed subspace. Can the proofs be simplified or repaired if needed?
7. **Frame direct products.** Check the claim for *arbitrary index sets and arbitrary locales*, not merely finite coproducts or spatial examples. In particular, are the purported clopen square-block decomposition and componentwise fitting/closure sufficient?
8. **Presentation and further ideas.** Report anything that is confusing upon first reading, potential simplifications, alternative arguments, overlooked special cases, counterexamples, useful related results, or worthwhile next questions. Explicitly distinguish established results from suggestions.

Please give an independent written verdict, preferably organized as (i) major errors or unresolved gaps, (ii) verified assertions with justification, (iii) presentation and sourcing, and (iv) optional further mathematical thoughts. It is perfectly acceptable to find no further results.
