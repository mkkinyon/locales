# A — Closed saturation: what it does and does not capture

**18 October 2026. Mathematical research note.** Main conclusions are proved below, subject to subsequent critical checking. This note follows [the primitive-ideal-space investigation](A-primitive-ideal-spaces.md) and [the continuous compactness question](A-continuous-compactness-question.md).

## Executive conclusions

1. For the inseparability equivalence relation E=cl(Δ), **closed saturation is a sufficient but not necessary condition** for E=F in the locally compact σ-compact case.
2. We construct an elementary **locally compact, second-countable, T1, sober** space with I=U=F and a **nonclosed saturation of a closed discrete set**, even though its quotient by E is the ordinary real line and its quotient map is open.
3. The obstruction has a precise form: pairs of inseparable points can **escape every compact subset in one coordinate while their partners converge in the other**.
4. A closed relation always sends **compact subsets to closed saturations**; on a σ-compact space it sends **closed subsets to F_sigma saturations**. These are useful automatic weaker conditions.
5. Preservation of compactness by saturation is a sufficient (not necessary) local compactness/properness criterion ensuring closed saturation.

## 1. Definitions and exact quotient interpretation

Let X be a topological space with E = closure of the diagonal in the ordinary topological product X×X. Thus

    x E y   iff  x,y have no disjoint open neighborhoods.

Suppose E is an equivalence relation (for example, if the inseparability and Urysohn-inseparability relations agree). Write q:X→Q=X/E for the quotient with its **quotient topology**. For any C⊆X define E[C]=q^{-1}(q(C)).

**Proposition 1.1.** The following are equivalent:

    (CS): every closed C⊆X has closed saturation E[C];
    q:X→Q is a closed map.

This is immediate from the definition of quotient topology and saturation.

**Proposition 1.2 (exact functional criterion).** If E is an equivalence relation then

    E = F  iff  Q=X/E is functionally Hausdorff,

where "functionally Hausdorff" means that for any distinct q(x),q(y) there exists a continuous real-valued function on Q taking different values.

**Proof.** Each real-valued continuous f:X→R is constant on E-related pairs, because different real values could be separated by disjoint inverse-image neighborhoods. Thus f factors uniquely as g∘q with g:Q→R continuous (by the universal property of the quotient topology). Every g pulls back to a continuous f. Hence F is the pullback of the equality-of-all-real-functions relation on Q. □

This isolates the distinction: **(CS) controls the closed-map behavior of q, whereas E=F only demands pairwise separation by real functions on Q.** Even a Hausdorff and completely regular quotient can have a nonclosed quotient map.

For locally compact sober X, O(X×X) agrees with O(X)⊗O(X), and all three relations are closed sublocales. Hence pointwise equalities among I,U,F correspond to equality as closed localic relations (under classical spatiality assumptions).

## 2. A concrete counterexample: the real line with countably many duplicated points

Let r_n=1/n (n≥1). Define a set

    X = R ⊔ {e_n : n≥1}

and a projection p:X→R by p(t)=t for t∈R and p(e_n)=r_n.

Define a topology generated by:

- every ordinary open interval I⊆R, treated as a subset of X **without** any e_n;
- for each n and open interval I containing r_n, the set

      V(n,I) = {e_n} ∪ (I\{r_n}).

These are a basis (check finite intersections: at ordinary real points they reduce to ordinary open sets; at e_n intersecting its own basic neighborhoods reduces to the same form).

**Proposition 2.1.** X is second-countable, first-countable, T1, locally Hausdorff, locally compact, and sober.

**Proof.**
- Rational endpoints give a countable basis. The usual R is an open Hausdorff subspace.
- Each V(n,I) is open and p restricts to a homeomorphism V(n,I)→I. It gives a Hausdorff, locally compact chart at e_n (choose I small enough and then a compact interval in I).
- X is T1: ordinary real points have ordinary open neighborhoods excluding every e_n; to avoid any given real t at e_n choose I omitting t unless t=r_n, which V(n,I) omits automatically; distinct e_n are separated by basic sets.
- Every T1 locally Hausdorff space is sober: if C is a nonempty irreducible closed set, intersect it with an open Hausdorff chart containing one of its points; the intersection is an irreducible closed set in that chart, hence a singleton. That singleton is a nonempty relatively open subset of C, which is dense because C is irreducible; T1 forces C itself to be that singleton. □

**Proposition 2.2.** The map p is a continuous **open** surjection. Its fibers are exactly the inseparability equivalence classes, and

    I(X)=U(X)=F(X)

as ordinary point relations and hence as **closed localic relations**.

**Proof.** A basic open interval has open image under p; V(n,I) maps to I. This proves p is open; continuity follows by inspecting inverse images of intervals. If p(x)≠p(y), separate their values in R by open intervals whose closures are disjoint; the inverse images are open neighborhoods of x,y with disjoint closures. Thus such pairs are neither U-related nor functionally equivalent.

The two points r_n and e_n are inseparable: every neighborhood of r_n contains an ordinary interval around r_n, and every neighborhood of e_n contains all the ordinary points of an interval around r_n **except r_n**, so the neighborhoods meet. Therefore r_n E e_n. All three relations lie between E and the kernel relation of the real-valued map p, and these two relations coincide. □

**Proposition 2.3.** Closed saturation fails.

**Proof.** Put C={e_n:n≥1}. The complement X\C=R is open, so C is closed. Moreover C is discrete in its relative topology. Since E-classes are the fibers of p,

    E[C]={e_n:n≥1} ∪ {1/n:n≥1}.

The real point 0 belongs to the closure of E[C], since every ordinary neighborhood of 0 contains 1/n for sufficiently large n, but 0 does not belong to E[C]. Hence E[C] is not closed and p=q is not a closed map. □

**Key conclusion:** This example satisfies all three separation-relation equalities, and its quotient is R, which is locally compact Hausdorff and completely regular. Nevertheless saturation of closed subsets can fail. Thus (CS) is not necessary even in an unusually well-behaved continuous-frame example.

## 3. Exact 'escape to infinity' diagnosis

Let E⊆X×X be **any closed binary relation**, not necessarily an equivalence relation. For a subset C define E[C]={y:∃x∈C, xEy}.

**Lemma 3.1 (compact saturation is closed).** If K⊆X is compact, then E[K] is closed in X.

**Proof.** The set E∩(K×X) is closed in K×X. The projection K×X→X is a closed map because K is compact (tube lemma), even when K is not Hausdorff. Its image is E[K]. □

**Corollary 3.2.** If X is σ-compact and C⊆X is closed, then E[C] is F_sigma in X.

**Proof.** Write X=⋃_n K_n with K_n compact. The sets C∩K_n are compact because C is closed, so

    E[C]=⋃_n E[C∩K_n],

a countable union of closed sets by Lemma 3.1. □

For equivalence E with quotient q, this says **q maps compact subsets to closed subsets**, and, when X is σ-compact, **q maps closed sets to F_sigma subsets** in the quotient topology: q(K) is closed because q^{-1}(q(K))=E[K] is closed.

**Proposition 3.3 (escape-net obstruction).** Let E be closed, C closed, and suppose

    y∈cl(E[C]) \ E[C].

Then there exist nets (c_i) in C and (z_i) in X such that:

    c_i E z_i,        z_i→y,
    and c_i eventually lies outside each compact K⊆X.

**Proof.** For every compact K, the set E[C∩K] is closed by Lemma 3.1 and does not contain y. Let U_K be an open neighborhood of y disjoint from it. Index a net by pairs (V,K), with V a neighborhood of y and K a compact subset, ordered by shrinking V and growing K. Since y∈cl(E[C]), choose z_(V,K)∈V∩U_K∩E[C], and c_(V,K)∈C with c_(V,K) E z_(V,K). Since z_(V,K)∉E[C∩K], c_(V,K)∉K. The z-net converges to y, and the c-net escapes every compact subset. □

The converse with y∉E[C] is tautological: any such net witnesses the failure of closed saturation if z_i→y. The significance is that failure necessarily requires escaping companions; **it cannot be witnessed inside one fixed compact subset**.

For §2, take c_n=e_n and z_n=1/n→0. The c_n escape every compact subset of X, while their E-partners converge to 0. This is precisely the phenomenon in Proposition 3.3.

## 4. A properness-style sufficient condition

**Proposition 4.1.** Let X be locally compact in the sense that every point has a compact neighborhood, and let E⊆X×X be a closed symmetric relation. Suppose

    K compact ⇒ E[K] compact.

Then for every closed C⊆X, E[C] is closed.

**Proof.** Let y∈cl(E[C]) and choose a compact neighborhood K of y with y∈int K. If z∈K∩E[C], choose c∈C with cEz. By symmetry c∈E[K]. Set H=C∩E[K]. This is a closed subset of compact E[K], hence compact. The sets E[C] and E[H] agree on K: any z∈K∩E[C] lies in E[H], and the reverse inclusion E[H]⊆E[C] is immediate. Since E[H] is closed by Lemma 3.1 and y∈cl(E[C]∩int K), we have y∈E[H]⊆E[C]. □

This is a sufficient *compact-control* condition; the example of §2 fails it. Take K=[0,1]⊆R⊂X. Then E[K] contains the infinite closed discrete family {e_n} and cannot be compact.

In locale language the underlying principle is related to **proper/closed relation maps**, but translating it correctly for arbitrary nonspatial locales requires attention to images of closed sublocales and proper locale morphisms; this note makes no unsupported equivalence claim.

## 5. Why closed saturation was useful in operator algebra

Archbold–Kaniuth–Somerset, *Norms of inner derivations for multiplier algebras of C*-algebras and group C*-algebras, II* (2015), **Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3**, [arXiv:1504.07030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.07030), establish a strong result for σ-unital C*-algebras. They show (in their terminology) that closure of every ∼-component-saturation of a closed set is equivalent to closedness of the Glimm map plus connectedness of Glimm classes. In the special case Orc=1, this yields E=F.

Their proof does **not** say that closed saturation follows from transitivity or Urysohn separation, and it does **not** assert closed saturation is necessary for E=F. Our example in §2 shows the latter would be false in general topological spaces.

The operator-algebra sources highlight a useful fact: closed saturation provides a **uniform separation of entire saturated closed sets**, whereas E=F only asks that **individual inequivalent pairs** be distinguished by continuous real functions. The split-point example separates these two demands vividly.

## 6. An example where neither openness nor closedness of the quotient is necessary

Take the disjoint topological union of the split-real-line space of §2 and the earlier **compact sober non-open-quotient** example in [A-continuous-compactness-question.md](A-continuous-compactness-question.md), §3. Since the summands are clopen, they each have I=U=F and so does the union. The union remains locally compact, sober, second-countable and σ-compact.

Its inseparability quotient is neither open (the compact summand witnesses this) nor closed (the split real line witnesses this), yet

    I=U=F.

This is not a deeper connected counterexample, merely an explicit demonstration that **neither** quotient openness **nor** closedness is a necessary ingredient of functional equality.

## 7. Outcome and next direction

**Settled:**
- I=U does **not** imply closed saturation, even for locally compact sober T1 spaces.
- Closed saturation is **not necessary** for I=F, even when I=U.
- Compact subsets always have closed E-saturation, and closed subsets of σ-compact spaces have F_sigma saturation.
- The possible failure is necessarily an *escape-to-infinity* phenomenon in the relation.
- Compact-preserving saturation suffices for the closed-saturation property under local compactness.

**Still unresolved:** whether I=U implies I=F for every continuous frame. This note shows that any successful general proof must **not rely on forcing closed saturation**. Alternative approaches should focus instead on pairwise real-valued separation, the quotient's functional Hausdorff property (Proposition 1.2), and the way-below characterization in [A-continuous-frames.md](A-continuous-frames.md).
