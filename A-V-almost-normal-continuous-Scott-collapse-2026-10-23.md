# Project A: almost-normal Scott domains have V = V2 = F
23 October 2026. **Working theorem; independent mathematical audit still required.**

## Statement

Let P be a **continuous dcpo**, equipped with its Scott topology, and let L=O_σ(P). Suppose the frame-theoretic rather-below relation a≺b iff a*∨b=1 interpolates (almost-normality). Put Q=L⊕L, d_I=⋁_{a∧b=0}(a⊠b), d_F=⋁_{a≺≺b*}(a⊠b), I=C(d_I), F=C(d_F), V=fit(I), and V_2 the fitted relation generated by rank≤2 rectangular bars.

**Theorem:**

    V = V_2 = U = F.

This theorem is NOT the claim I=F. It is a positive answer to the almost-normal V-question for Scott locales of continuous dcpos, including nonalgebraic rootless domains. The stronger cover statement holds: every w∈Q with w∨d_I=1 satisfies w∨d_F=1.

## Key domain-theoretic lemma (the new ingredient)

Let x,y∈P satisfy

    (*) for every p≤x and q≤y, p and q have disjoint Scott-open neighborhoods.

Then there exist Scott-open neighborhoods A of x and B of y with **disjoint closures**, provided O_σ(P) has interpolating rather-below.

**Proof.** Choose p≪_P x, and let U_p={z∈P:p≪_P z}. This is Scott open in a continuous dcpo and contains x. Fix q≤y and choose r≪_P q. Then U_r is Scott open containing q. By (*), p≤x and r≤q≤y possess disjoint Scott-open neighborhoods. Since all Scott opens are upper sets, p and r can have no common upper bound. Thus U_p∩U_r=∅: a point in their intersection would be a common upper bound of p and r. Hence q∉cl(U_p). Because q≤y was arbitrary,

    cl(U_p) ∩ ↓y = ∅.

The principal lower set ↓y is Scott closed. Therefore U_p≺ P\↓y. Almost-normality gives an open C with

    cl(U_p)⊆C,    cl(C)⊆P\↓y.

Let B=P\cl(C). Then y∈B, B is Scott open and cl(B)⊆P\C. Since cl(U_p)⊆C, the closures of U_p and B are disjoint. QED.

Notice that (*) is stronger than ordinary disjoint-neighborhood separation of x,y. It is automatically forced by a BAR that excludes (x,y), because bar open sets are specialization-upper in both coordinates.

## Application to every bar

Scott spaces of continuous dcpos are locally compact sober. Their localic binary product is represented by the ordinary topological product, so its opens can be tested on ordinary pairs (x,y).

Let w∈Q be a bar (w∨d_I=1). At any (x,y) outside w, for every p≤x,q≤y, the point (p,q) lies outside w, because w is open and hence upward closed in specialization order. Thus (p,q)∈d_I by the bar equation. Such a pair has disjoint Scott-open neighborhoods, giving hypothesis (*).

The lemma supplies A∋x,B∋y with cl(A)∩cl(B)=∅. Therefore A*∨B*=1, i.e. A≺B*. By interpolation, A≺≺B*, so A⊠B≤d_F. Hence (x,y) lies in d_F.

Thus every pair in P×P lies in w∨d_F; spatiality of the locally compact sober product yields w∨d_F=1 in Q. Consequently F is contained in every bar open neighborhood, giving F⊆V. The previously established arbitrary-frame theorem for almost normality gives V_2=F and universally V⊆V_2, so V=V_2=F=U.

## Related general no-go theorem: closed predecessors

Let X be any spatial almost-normal T0 space for which the localic square is spatial. Suppose every point x has at least one **closed specialization predecessor** p≤x (i.e. p is a closed point and all open neighborhoods of p contain x). Then V=V_2=F.

Proof: if (x,y) outside a bar w, all predecessors (p,y) are outside w and hence disjoint-neighborhood-separated. If p is closed, p and y are completely separated by the elementary almost-normal interpolation argument: choose opens A∋p and B∋y with A∩B=∅; then cl(B)⊆X\{p}, interpolate B≺X\{p}, and construct neighborhoods with disjoint closures. An open neighborhood of p also contains x, so x,y are completely separated. Finish by spatiality, as above. (The same argument works if y has a closed predecessor.)

In Scott spaces, closed points are precisely minimal elements. Thus a hypothetical *point-pair* counterexample in a general Scott space must involve coordinates with **no minimal elements below them**. The continuous-dcpo theorem above rules it out even in that rootless case, using way-below approximants.

4. Verify locally compact sober Scott-product spatiality (or instead directly the Scott product basis).
5. Verify that (p,q)∉w for p≤x,q≤y and therefore belongs to d_I.
6. Verify the earlier separate theorem V_2=F on almost-normal frames; this result relies on it for V_2 equality but proves the stronger w∨d_F=1 directly.

**Important scope:** The argument establishes the result for Scott spaces of continuous dcpos, not automatically for every continuous frame. A general continuous-frame locale is locally compact sober but need not be the Scott space of a continuous dcpo. General almost-normal frames remain unresolved.
