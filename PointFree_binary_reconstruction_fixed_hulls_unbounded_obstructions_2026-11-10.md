## 1. Six-point counterexample with identical higher hull simplicial objects

Take disjoint two-element sets A,B,C. Let P=A⊕B_anti⊕C and Q=A⊕B_chain⊕C, with A,C antichains and B respectively an antichain or two-element chain. Identity identifies their finite patched sets. A tuple has a common upper witness exactly when it does not contain both distinct elements of C; a common lower witness exactly when it does not contain both distinct elements of A. Thus I_n(P)=I_n(Q) and D_n(P)=D_n(Q) as patched subsets, with identical simplicial maps, for every n. But C(P)≃S² and C(Q)≃1 by the ordinal-sum join theorem; I(P)=D(P)≃1=I(Q)=D(Q) by the ordinal-sum hull contraction. The Q comparisons are equivalences; the P comparisons are not.

This fixes **all patched higher hulls**, not merely the binary pair. It does not identify the spectral topologies or the actual localic relation embeddings across different locales. The specialization order and the chain nerve differ.

## 2. Binary reconstruction of the patched higher hulls for coherent locales

**Theorem A.** A homeomorphism of patched coherent point spaces preserving the common-upper-bound relation I induces a strict isomorphism of all patched I_n simplicial Stone objects. The dual statement holds for D. Indeed I_n is determined by the I graph alone.

Proof: Every point extends to a maximal point: the upper cones are patch closed and compact, so any increasing chain has an upper bound by compactness, and Zorn applies. For x, let N_I(x)={y:x I y}, including x. Call x graph-simplicial when N_I(x) is a clique. If x has two distinct maximal extensions m,m', both are in N_I(x), and they are not I-related. Conversely, if x has unique maximal extension m, then any y I x has a common upper witness extending to m, so N_I(x)=↓m. This is a clique. Every maximal m is itself graph-simplicial. Consequently the distinct neighborhoods of graph-simplicial points are exactly the maximal-witness downsets. Thus
\[
(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\in I_n \iff
\exists z\ [N_I(z)\text{ a clique and }x_i\in N_I(z)\ \forall i].
\]
Dualize for D. The known coherent proconstructible witness descriptions identify the actual patched hulls with the corresponding closed subsets of patch powers; a patch homeomorphism carrying these subsets gives their topological identification, compatible with deletions/repetitions. QED.

**Scope:** The formula is classical, including Zorn. It is not asserted to be an internal first-order formula in arbitrary frames, nor to recover the spectral sublocale topologies without the specialization order. A clique of I itself need not have a common upper witness (triangle boundary vertices are an example); the formula uses the **entire graph**. This corrects the initial intuition in Binary feedback III that the binary data might fail to determine higher hulls.

## 3. Finite chain fundamental groupoids are determined by I,D together

**Lemma B (flattening).** For finite P remove all strict comparisons between nonextremal points, keeping every comparison with a minimal or maximal endpoint. The resulting P^flat is a poset: if two retained strict comparisons compose, their first endpoint is minimal or last endpoint maximal (in fact both if the middle point is nonextremal). The inclusion of order nerves induces an isomorphism of edge-path fundamental groupoids with object set P.

Proof: For any original edge x≤y assign the path [x,b][y,b]^{-1} through a maximal b≥y, in the flattened groupoid. Choose minimal a≤x. Both triangles a≤x≤b and a≤y≤b are retained, allowing repeats. Thus that path equals [a,x]^{-1}[a,y], independent of b. For retained edge x≤y, the triangle x≤y≤b is retained, so the assignment is the original edge. For original chain triangle x≤y≤z use the same maximal b≥z for all three, giving p(x,y)p(y,z)=p(x,z). The assignment induces a functor inverse to inclusion on edge generators (in the original groupoid its value equals the original edge by the original triangle). QED.

**Theorem C (finite binary determination of chain 1-type).** If finite posets have the same I,D on the same underlying set, their chain fundamental groupoids are equivalent, canonically via an auxiliary poset built from the two graphs. In fact the canonical maps of 1-types to the fixed hull 1-types are equivalent.

Proof: Recover from Theorem A the families L of distinct principal minimal upsets and U of distinct principal maximal downsets. Construct R on the disjoint union of L, the original points, and U. Comparisons are L<x iff x∈L, x<U iff x∈U, L<U iff L∩U≠∅, and reflexivity. These define a poset, determined by the two graphs. For any original realization P, form P_hat by retaining all original comparisons and adding one new minimal copy for each original minimal point and one new maximal copy for each original maximal point, with the incidences specified by R. New minimal copies are below their old counterparts, new maximal copies above theirs, including both copies for an isolated original point. This defines a poset; R is its flattening, since every original point now has both a strict lower and a strict upper extension. The projection P_hat→P taking the new copies to the actual extrema is order preserving. The inclusion P→P_hat splits it. Collapse new minimal copies first (a monotone endomorphism pointwise ≥ id), then new maximal copies (pointwise ≤ id on the remaining poset); these give homotopies relative to P by the standard order-homotopy argument. Hence C(P_hat)≃C(P). By Lemma B, C(R)→C(P_hat) induces a fundamental groupoid isomorphism. The composite gives the desired equivalence with C(P) in degree one.

Compatibility with the upper/lower comparison: For two original realizations P,Q of the same graph pair, compose R→P_hat→P→I(P) and R→Q_hat→Q→I(Q), identifying the last simplicial hulls by Theorem A. For every chain tuple in R choose a maximal new upper node U above the entire tuple. Both maps send all coordinates into the same recovered block U. Any interleaving of their image tuples is therefore in I_n for the appropriate arity. The usual simplicial prism (concatenate the first map through coordinate i with the second map from coordinate i) supplies a homotopy of the two maps to the common upper hull. Dual argument uses a new lower node L below the tuple for the lower hull. These maps agree on original vertices. After 1-truncation the maps from C(R) to C(P), C(Q) are equivalences, so the canonical comparison diagrams at the 1-type level are equivalent. This is stronger than only equality of π_1 groups. No assertion of equivalence of the full chain homotopy types. QED.

**Boundary:** This finite construction does not yet give a coherent-locale sheaf analogue. Extremal point loci need not be patch compact, and the auxiliary incidence construction would require additional topology/descent. Theorem A does apply to all coherent locales.

## 4. One fixed infinite patched relation structure, unbounded obstruction degrees

Fix nonempty infinite Stone S and the original triangle wedge X_0=W_S(P_triangle,v). It has the audited common object E_S=h(S)_+∧underline(S¹), and the working full displayed triangle hierarchy and formal-hull rank 2 from Binary feedback II. Fix also a Cantor Stone space B disjoint from X_0. Adjoin a second bottom point a and two distinct top points p,q (new labels distinct from triangle labels). For each order on B described below form the coherent ordinal sum
\[
Q=(\{v,a\}_{anti})\oplus B\oplus(\{p,q\}_{anti}),
\]
with v shared with X_0, and let X=X_0∨_v Q, with **no** other comparisons between nonshared pieces. All these X have exactly the same underlying Stone space
\[
X_{\rm patch}=X_{0,\rm patch}\sqcup\{a,p,q\}\sqcup B.
\]
The new orders on B will be Priestley; the ordinal sum and gluing at a patch-isolated common minimal point are Priestley. For the latter, closedness follows from the finite union of closed order graphs of the two closed pieces, and Priestley separation by extending clopen upsets from one piece: if they contain v extend by the other piece's ↑v, otherwise extend by empty. Since ↑v in both pieces is patch clopen here, these extensions are clopen. To separate distinct nonshared points of different pieces use an upset avoiding v in the first piece (possible by Priestley separation against v); this excludes the second. Thus the actual coherent locales exist. For S Cantor their patch spaces are compact metrizable zero-dimensional with countably many clopens, so K(X) is countable.

**Fixed binary and higher data:** On Q every tuple has a common upper witness iff it does not contain both top points, and a common lower witness iff it does not contain both bottom points. This is independent of B's order. Also ↑_Q v={v}∪B∪{p,q}, independent of the order. In the glued X, a tuple with nonshared coordinates in both pieces has no common upper witness. It has a common lower witness iff all coordinates are above v, because v is the only possible common lower witness. For tuples within one piece, witness membership is intrinsic to that piece (folding retractions, or inspect possible shared witness). Hence the entire point subsets I_n and D_n of the fixed patched powers, with their induced patch topology and simplicial maps, are identical in all X. In particular I,D literally fixed as patched relations. By the coherent finite composition calculus the relations U=W=I², V=D², M,J,T(J), U∨V, cl V=I³ and fit U=D³ are also fixed as patched subsets. The triangle strictness witnesses survive by spectral retraction onto X_0 (the new Q collapses to v). Each Q point is I-related to v; each minimal point of X_0 is also I-related to v. Thus fit U=full, H=F=full, and the displayed named Hasse pattern is exactly that of Binary feedback II. I-distance ≤2 from v everywhere, while r_s,r_t in distinct triangle fibres still have distance four by the retraction (or paths through v). Hence I³ proper, I⁴=full, first fixed closed stage exactly 2.

**Canonical comparisons:** The chain and I_n patched objects are wedges along the shared constant-v point in every degree (the shared point is clopen in each patched tuple space). Applying h preserves these coproduct-summand pushouts; realization gives C(X)≃E_S∨C(Q), I(X)≃E_S because I(Q)≃1 by the ordinal-sum theorem. Under these identifications the canonical arrow is projection E_S∨C(Q)→E_S, and the canonical edgewise theorem gives the same for D. No false degreewise D gluing is asserted. The targets are not merely equivalent but the entire I,D simplicial objects are fixed.

**Case 0: equivalence.** Give B the star order with a fixed point b0 below every other point, with no further strict comparisons. The graph is Δ_B∪({b0}×B), closed; Priestley separation holds because every noncomparison x≰y has x≠b0, so choose a clopen neighborhood of x excluding y and b0 (if y=b0 simply exclude b0); it is an upset. Its chain nerve contracts by inserting the global least b0. Hence C(Q)≃1 by the ordinal-sum join formula, and both comparisons are equivalences.

**Case n≥1: arbitrarily high first obstruction.** Partition the *same* Cantor B into n nonempty clopen Cantor subsets B_1,...,B_n, order them as an ordinal sum of equality-ordered Stone spaces. This is Priestley. Then
\[
Z_n=\mathbf C(Q_n)\simeq
\underline{S^0}*h(B_1)*\cdots*h(B_n)*\underline{S^0}.
\]
There are n+2 nonempty 0-truncated factors. Before sheafification the join can be modeled objectwise as the ordinary join of n+2 nonempty discrete sets (use constant presheaf S^0 for the two outer factors, represented presheaves for B_i). It is n-connected and its only possibly nonzero reduced integral homology is in degree n+1, where it is the tensor of augmentation kernels. This follows from the ordinary augmented simplicial join chain formula (or iterate the suspension-smash equivalence after choosing a point in each factor). Sheafification preserves colimits, finite products, truncations and the associated homology sheaves. Equivalently apply the standard free abelian chain linearization and the join chain formula before sheafification. Thus Z_n is n-connected and
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal H}_{n+1}(Z_n;\mathbb Z)
\cong K_{B_1}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb Z}K_{B_n},
\qquad
K_{B_i}=\ker(\mathbb Z[h(B_i)]\to\underline{\mathbb Z}),
\]
all other reduced homology zero. This is ordinary tensor, not an unproved flatness assertion: the presheaf augmented free groups and their kernels are objectwise free, and sheafification of abelian groups is exact and monoidal. At the terminal point the tensor is a nonzero free abelian group, since every B_i has ≥2 points. It is a **nonconstant sheaf**: choose b_i∈B_i and c_i≠b_i for i≥2; over B_1 use [id]-[const_b1] in K_B1 tensored with the fixed classes [c_i]-[b_i]. Its point values are distinct in the tensor of free ordinary augmentation kernels (the fixed tensor of primitive basis differences is nonzero and extends to a basis). For n=1 take the empty tensor of other factors as 1. Infinite point-image proves nonconstancy.

The common target E_S is fixed and genuinely nonconstant, while C(X_n)≃E_S∨Z_n and the arrow is projection. The reduced homology kernel is precisely the above nonconstant sheaf in degree n+1, and all lower-degree homology maps are isomorphisms. Its pointed homotopy cofiber is Σ_red Z_n, hence has first nonzero reduced homology in degree n+2 and none elsewhere. The comparison induces a π_1 isomorphism (Z_n simply connected; model wedge objectwise, then sheafify). Thus **even one fixed underlying patched Stone space with identical I_n,D_n in every arity, identical displayed binary hierarchy and rank 2, and identical nonconstant hull targets admits equivalence comparisons and non-equivalence comparisons with homological obstruction in unbounded degrees**. This is not a statement about identical localic relation structures including their spectral topology.

## 5. Scope and next questions

Theorems A–C and the fixed infinite family are new internal work, not independently audited. No external literature-priority search was attempted. The finite degree-one reconstruction suggests the next useful boundary: whether the binary pair determines the internal chain fundamental groupoid for all coherent locales, or whether noncompact extremal loci can defeat the finite incidence proof. The stronger original question of varying even π_1 at fixed patched binary data is now ruled out in the finite case, but remains open in general. Also open: whether fixed binary data constrain comparison maps after 2-truncation under additional order restrictions (the six-point example rules out unrestricted 2-type reconstruction).
