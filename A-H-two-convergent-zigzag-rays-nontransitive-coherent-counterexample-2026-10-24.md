# Project A — Two convergent zigzag rays: coherent H not transitive and H strictly between U∨V and F

24 October 2026. **NEW WORKING THEOREM, internallly checked but NOT externally audited.** This is the opening research segment in the user-approved upper-diamond/alternating-hull program. It supplies a particularly direct graph-connectivity phenomenon that CANNOT appear in a finite Alexandrov locale.

## 1. Statement

There is a **compact coherent, sober, spectral locale X** with the following three strict properties, as actual relations/sublocales of X×loc X:

  **U(X)∨V(X) ⊊ H(X) ⊊ F(X),**
  **H(X)∘H(X) ⊈ H(X).**

Here H is the LEAST sublocale of the localic square containing Δ that is both closed and fitted. In particular, H need NOT be an internal equivalence relation, **even when X is coherent**. This disproves the unrestricted H-transitivity question, and the earlier intuition that the finite-Alexandrov equality H=F might persist in the full coherent class.

The witnesses are ordinary points of X×loc X, but every sublocale inclusion is supported by actual coherent/spatial sublocale arguments, not inferred from point sets in an arbitrary locale.

## 2. A countable Priestley space with two zigzag rays

As an underlying set put
  P={t}∪A∪B,
  A={a_n:n∈N_0}, B={b_n:n∈N_0}.
Give P the topology of the one-point compactification of the countable discrete set A∪B: all a_n,b_n are isolated, and a neighborhood base at t consists of cofinite tails. This is a compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional metrizable space; call it the PATCH topology.

Define a partial order with t only comparable to itself, no comparabilities between A and B, and on each ray exactly the alternating adjacent relations
  a_(2n) < a_(2n+1), a_(2n+2) < a_(2n+1),
  b_(2n) < b_(2n+1), b_(2n+2) < b_(2n+1)
for n≥0, along with reflexivity. There are no other strict comparisons. All even-index points are minimal, all odd-index points maximal; thus the declared relation is transitive.

The order relation is closed in P_patch×P_patch: every strict comparable pair is an adjacent-index pair of isolated points. Any sequence of such pairs with unbounded indices converges only to (t,t), already in the order. Since the patch topology is metrizable, sequential closedness suffices.

This is a PRIESTLEY space. For x∈A∪B, its principal upset ↑x is finite, patch-clopen and upward closed, and it excludes any prescribed y with x≰y. For x=t and y≠t, the set P\↓y is patch-clopen and upward closed, contains t and excludes y; ↓y is finite. Therefore every x≰y is separated by a patch-clopen upset.

Equip P with its SPECTRAL topology: the open sets are patch-open UPSETS. By Priestley/Hochster duality, this defines a coherent spectral sober locale X, whose patch topology and specialization order are exactly the ones above. Binary products of spectral locales are coherent/spectral with ordinary topological product underlying the localic product because spectral spaces are locally compact sober.

Call A, B the two connected components of the comparability graph and {t} the third. In the patch topology both A and B converge to t, but t is not comparable to any a_n or b_n.

## 3. A standard coherent-fittedness lemma, proved explicitly

**LEMMA (Priestley compact-upper-set lemma).** Let Z be a spectral space with Priestley patch space Z_p. If S⊆Z is patch-CLOSED and upward closed for specialization, then there exists a fitted sublocale of Z whose ordinary points are exactly S and that sublocale is spatial/coherent. If S is also spectral-CLOSED, this fitted sublocale is precisely the closed sublocale C(Z\S).

Proof. For each p∉S and each s∈S, s≰p. By Priestley separation choose a patch-clopen upset U_s with s∈U_s, p∉U_s. Compactness of S in Z_p gives finitely many U_s covering S; their union U_p is patch-clopen upset, hence a COMPACT OPEN of Z, with S⊆U_p, p∉U_p. Let F be the filter of compact opens containing S. Then the intersection of its open sublocales
  K=⋂_{u∈F} O(u)
is fitted and its ordinary point set is precisely S. By the coherent compact-open filter presentation, K is a coherent hence spatial sublocale: its frame is Idl(K(O(Z))/θ_F), θ_F generated by imposing the compact opens F as top (more explicitly, a θ_F b iff a∧f=b∧f for some f∈F). Prime filters containing F are exactly the ordinary points of K, which by the separation above are exactly S.

If S is spectral-closed, its closed sublocale C(Z\S) is spectral/spatial with the same embedded point set S. Two spatial sublocales with the same embedded point set of a spatial ambient locale coincide (their induced subspace topologies agree). Hence C(Z\S)=K is fitted. QED.

Application to the square: use Z=X×loc X, a spectral locale with underlying patch space P_patch² and product specialization order.

## 4. An explicit closed-and-fitted relation R

Put
  S_A=A∪{t}=P\B,   S_B=B∪{t}=P\A.
Both are CLOSED in the spectral topology because A and B are spectral-open upsets (unions of finite principal upsets). Their squares are closed. Define the actual CLOSED sublocale
  R = (S_A×loc S_A) ∨ (S_B×loc S_B).
Its point set is
  R_pt=S_A²∪S_B²
       = (A²∪(A×{t})∪({t}×A))
         ∪(B²∪(B×{t})∪({t}×B))
         ∪{(t,t)}.
Finite joins of closed sublocales agree with their closed union (the point set is the indicated ordinary union here); equivalently R=C((A×B)∪(B×A)), where the complement is an ordinary OPEN subset of the spectral square.

The set R_pt is patch-closed (finite union of products of patch-closed sets), and is BOTH upward and downward saturated under the product specialization order: no comparability crosses between the rays or t. By §3 its closed sublocale R is also FITTED. Clearly Δ⊆R.

By minimality of the closed-and-fitted diagonal hull,
  **H⊆R.**

## 5. The points forced into H

Since H is closed, pt(H) is downward closed under the product specialization order. Since H is fitted, pt(H) is upward closed (intersection of opens, each an upset).

Starting from the diagonal and moving coordinatewise along the alternating comparability edges, we obtain
  A×A ⊆ pt(H),    B×B ⊆ pt(H),    (t,t)∈pt(H).

This is completely elementary: comparability edges connect all points on each ray, and a relation containing Δ that is both upward and downward saturated must contain every pair of points in the same connected component of the undirected comparability graph.

Now H is CLOSED in the spectral product, hence its ordinary point set is spectral-closed and therefore also patch-closed. For every fixed a_i, the patch sequence (a_i,a_n)→(a_i,t). Similarly (b_n,b_j)→(t,b_j). Hence
  **(a_i,t),(t,a_i),(b_j,t),(t,b_j)∈pt(H)**
for all i,j.

Therefore R_pt⊆pt(H). Both R and H are closed sublocales of the spatial spectral product, and are determined by their ordinary point sets. Combined with H⊆R, this proves the exact identity
  **H=R.**                              (5.1)

## 6. H is NOT transitive

Fix a_0∈A and b_0∈B. Equation (5.1) yields
   (a_0,t)∈H,    (t,b_0)∈H,
but (a_0,b_0)∉H (it lies in the open complement A×B).

The ordinary point (a_0,t,b_0) factors through the composable-pair sublocale H_12∩H_23 of X³. Its image under the projection to coordinates 1 and 3 is (a_0,b_0), outside H. Thus the image relation H∘H is NOT contained in H:
  **H∘H⊈H.**

This is a genuine internal localic failure detected by an ordinary point; no product-spatiality sleight of hand.

## 7. Functional relation F is FULL

Every locale map f:X→R (ordinary real locale) corresponds to a continuous spectral-topology function on the sober spectral space P.

Since the real line is T1, f is constant along every specialization comparison, hence constant on the entirety of each ray A and B. The spectral topology is COARSER than the patch topology, so f is patch-continuous. Both sequences a_n→t and b_n→t in the patch topology. Therefore the constants along A and B both equal f(t). Thus every continuous real-valued f is constant.

Its kernel pair is the entire localic square, and so
  **F=X×locX**.
Since H=R omits (a_0,b_0),
  **H⊊F.**

This is a coherent/spectral example, unlike the older noncoherent deleted-plank example H<F.

## 8. U∨V is strictly smaller than H — fitted/open separation proof

In coherent locales our independently checked identities are
  U=I∘I,     V=D∘D.
Ordinary point relations are common-upper/common-lower paths, respectively. Since no point of A is order-comparable with any point of B or with t, both pt(U) and pt(V) are subsets of
  E=A²∪B²∪{(t,t)}.
This can also be seen from the fact that all I/D paths remain inside a single comparability component.

Both pt(U) and pt(V) are PATCH-CLOSED. For U this follows because U is spectral-closed; for V use V=D², where D's point relation is the projection of a closed subset of the compact patch P³, and composition of patch-closed relations is patch-closed by compact projection. Alternatively V=up(I) and closedness of the order in Priestley patch implies compactness of up(I), hence patch-closed.

Put K=pt(U)∪pt(V). It is patch-compact and lies within E. For p=(a_0,t), there is NO k∈K with k≤p, since the second coordinate t is incomparable with every point of the rays and the pair (t,t) is NOT ≤(a_0,t). By Priestley separation on the PRODUCT patch space, and compactness of K, there exists a patch-clopen product-UPSET W containing K but excluding p. This W is a compact open of the spectral square.

Because U is a CLOSED spatial sublocale, and V is a COHERENT spatial sublocale in this setting, the point containments pt(U),pt(V)⊆W imply genuine sublocale inclusions
  U⊆O(W),    V⊆O(W).
Therefore U∨V⊆O(W), whereas p∉O(W) and p∈H. Thus
  **U∨V⊊H.**

The compact patch separation, rather than the mere set-theoretic union of point relations, is what makes this a valid localic strict inclusion.

Combining §7 and §8,
  **U∨V ⊊ H ⊊ F.**

## 9. Why this example is not seen by finite posets

For a finite Alexandrov locale, there is no extra patch limit t in the closure of two disjoint comparability components. Closed-and-fitted relations simply take unions of comparability components of the product, and H is the corresponding component equivalence F.

Here the two distinct comparability rays each PATCH-accumulate at t, so the requirement that H be closed forces relation edges from BOTH rays to t. However CLOSEDNESS and FITTEDNESS do NOT force the composite a_0~t~b_0, because the relation R excluding A×B is itself closed and fitted. This is a precise topological explanation of why H is not transitive.

It strongly supports treating H as a pointfree analogue of graph-connectivity CLOSURE rather than graph connectivity/equivalence itself: compact limiting phenomena can break transitivity of the closure.
