# Project A: Hochster duality for I, D, U, V, F on spectral locales
23 October 2026. **Working synthesis of previously audited coherent results with an independent Priestley/Hofmann–Mislove argument; audit the spectral-sublocale lifting before publication.**

## Setup and scope

Let X be a spectral space, L=O(X) coherent, Q=O(X×_loc X) coherent; finite localic products of spectral spaces are spatial and agree with topological products. The patch space X_p is compact Hausdorff and the specialization relation (our convention x≤y iff x∈cl{y}) is a closed partial order. Compact opens of X are patch-clopen UPSETS. The Hochster dual X^vee keeps X_p and reverses ≤; its compact opens are complements of the original compact opens. From the Priestley representation, (X×X)^vee canonically identifies with X^vee×X^vee.

Let Δ be the localic diagonal; I=cl Δ; D=fit Δ; U=C(d_U); V=fit I; F the real-functional equivalence sublocale.

## 1. Point relations for arbitrary spectral X

Let R={(x,y):x≤y}, write R^{-1} its converse, and C for comparability-connected equivalence (finite zigzag generated by R∪R^{-1}). Relation composition uses ordinary existential quantification on points.

  pt(I) = R∘R^{-1} = {(x,y): some t≥x,y};
  pt(D) = R^{-1}∘R = {(x,y): some t≤x,y};
  pt(U) = (R∘R^{-1})² = ↓pt(D);
  pt(V) = (R^{-1}∘R)² = ↑pt(I).

Proof sketches:

- I: if x,y have inseparable open neighborhoods, compact-open neighborhoods of x and y yield a family of patch-clopen upsets with the finite intersection property. Patch compactness gives t in all such neighborhoods, which is equivalent to x≤t,y≤t. Conversely t forces inseparability.
- D: fit Δ; a pair is in every open diagonal neighborhood iff cl{x} meets cl{y} (proved independently in A-fitted-diagonal.md), equivalently common specialization predecessor.
- U: for compact-open neighborhoods K of x, H of y, cl_X(K)=↓K and cl_X(H)=↓H. Absence of disjoint-closure neighborhoods is equivalent to every such ↓K,↓H meeting. Compactness of the patch space and closedness of the specialization order give u≥x,v≥y,z≤u,v, namely a length-four zigzag. This is ↓pt(D).
- V: pt(I) is patch compact/closed as the projection of a closed relation on X_p³. A point outside ↑pt(I) can be excluded from a patch-clopen UPSET containing pt(I), by Priestley separation and compactness of pt(I). Such compact open is a bar. Hence the point set of fit(I) is exactly ↑pt(I).

All four point relations are PATCH CLOSED: the order relation is patch closed and each formula is the projection of a finite conjunction of closed order conditions from a finite patch power, hence compact and closed.

## 2. Why these are actual spectral sublocales, not merely point relations

I and U are closed sublocales of Q; they are spatial spectral subspaces with patch-closed sets of points.

Δ is a compact sublocale (X is compact), and I is compact (closed in compact Q). Their fittings D=fit(Δ) and V=fit(I) are COMPACT FITTED sublocales. By the Hofmann–Mislove theorem (pointfree form; e.g. Jakl–Suarez, *Canonical extensions via fitted sublocales*, Prop. 6.9), a compact fitted sublocale in sober spatial Q corresponds to its compact saturated subset of ordinary points, as an actual spatial sublocale. In a spectral space every compact saturated subset is patch closed. Equivalently, because Δ and I are compact, every neighborhood is refinable to a compact-open neighborhood, and the intersection of the latter open sublocales is the spectral subspace on their patch-closed intersection (coherent compact-open filter congruence).

Thus I,D,U,V are spatial spectral sublocales completely determined by the above patch-closed point relations. Same for F, which is closed.

The point-relation equations therefore transfer under Hochster order reversal to canonical *Hochster-dual spectral-sublocale identifications* inside (X^vee)^2:
  (I_X)^vee ≅ D_(X^vee),
  (D_X)^vee ≅ I_(X^vee),
  (U_X)^vee ≅ V_(X^vee),
  (V_X)^vee ≅ U_(X^vee).

These do NOT identify arbitrary sublocale lattices or preserve the same topology on the common point set. They apply to the named spectral sublocales, with induced spectral topology dualized.

## 3. Property/equality correspondence

Hochster duality swaps spectral CLOSED subsets (patch-closed specialization DOWNSETS) and compact FITTED subsets (patch-closed specialization UPSETS). Accordingly
  V_X closed ⇔ U_(X^vee) fitted;
  U_X fitted ⇔ V_(X^vee) closed;
  V_X=I_X ⇔ U_(X^vee)=D_(X^vee);
  U_X=I_X ⇔ V_(X^vee)=D_(X^vee).

Previously independently audited coherent-frame equivalences:
  I=V ⇔ I=U ⇔ compact-open lattice K is conormal;
  D=V ⇔ D=U ⇔ K is normal.
Order-dual bounded lattices K↔K^op exchange these equivalence classes. Therefore the two same-space equalities U=I and V=I coincide on every coherent X, although they are NOT the literal Hochster duals of one another.

## 4. A useful stronger coherent characterization of closedness/fittedness

Let E⊆X×X be the equivalence relation of lying in the same finite comparability-connected component. Since both U and V have a length-four order-zigzag formula, their point relations lie in E. Both contain the diagonal.

If V is closed, its point relation is both specialization UPward closed (V fitted, compact/saturated) and DOWNward closed (closed). Any coordinate comparability path propagates a diagonal pair through this relation; hence pt(V)=E. In particular E is patch closed.

Likewise, if U is fitted, its point relation is both up- and down-closed; hence pt(U)=E, so E patch closed.

Conversely, if E is patch closed, then it is simultaneously spectral closed (downset) and compact fitted (upset). Since the given U and V are each subsets of E and contain diagonal, E itself need not be equal to U or V merely from E patch closed; use E's order equivalence and the zigzag formulas: if E is a closed equivalence relation identifying each comparable pair, the compact Hausdorff quotient X_p/E exists, and U/V may STILL be proper subsets of E (finite connected five-point zigzag illustrates it). Thus no converse 'E patch-closed ⇒ U/V equality' is claimed.

However, when E is patch closed, E is exactly pt(F): any map f:X→R is patch-continuous and order-constant, so E⊆pt(F). Conversely quotient X_p/E is compact Hausdorff and continuous real-valued functions separate its classes; composing such functions with the quotient is patch-continuous and order-constant, hence continuous in the spectral topology. Thus pt(F)=E.

Therefore
  **V closed ⇔ V=F**,
  **U fitted ⇔ U=F**
for every coherent/spectral locale X. (Forward directions via the diagonal-component propagation and E patch-closed, reverse because F is closed and fitted universally.)

As a separate consequence, the continuous real-valued maps X→R and X^vee→R are EXACTLY the same functions: f is continuous iff it is patch-continuous and constant on comparable points (preimages of open intervals are patch open and order invariant, hence open in both up- and downset spectral topologies). Thus F has a Hochster-self-dual spectral sublocale, (F_X)^vee≅F_(X^vee).

There is an additional equivalent *transitivity* condition for each relation. Each of U and V is reflexive, symmetric, contains all comparable pairs, lies within E, and is a spectral (patch-closed) sublocale. If U or V is pointwise transitive, it contains every finite comparability path, hence equals E; patch closedness makes E a closed equivalence relation on the patch space and implies F=E as above. Internal transitivity implies pointwise transitivity. Conversely F is the kernel pair of the real-function evaluation map and internally transitive. Thus

  V closed ⇔ V=F ⇔ V internally transitive,
  U fitted ⇔ U=F ⇔ U internally transitive

for spectral/coherent X. These two triple equivalences are Hochster dual. A point-set-only transitivity criterion suffices in this spectral setting because U,V,F are already spectral sublocales, so equality of point sets detects equality of these particular sublocales. This argument is special to coherent locales.

The equivalence V closed ⇔ V=F is specific to coherent/spectral locales; e.g. Claude Fable's NONCOMPACT almost-normal two-chain space has V=I properly below F while V remains closed.

## 5. Connection to almost-normality

Coherent locales are compact. By the established compact-coordinate tube lemma every canonical bar has depth≤2; hence for a COMPACT almost-normal locale the audited theorem V2=U=F gives V=U=F. Consequently (7) 'almost-normal ⇒ V closed' holds automatically for coherent frames. A negative example must be noncompact.

Hochster duality need not preserve almost-normality; it is the structural equalities/properties above that are exchanged, NOT necessarily the hypothesis almost normal.

## 6. Limitations and next checks

- The localic Hochster identifications rely on compactness and the coherence/spectral representation, especially the compact-fitted-sublocale ↔ compact saturated spatial-subspace theorem. The purely pointwise identities do not by themselves prove localic equivalences.
- For arbitrary noncoherent/noncompact locales Hochster duality need not be defined, and `V=I` may decouple from `U=I`; Claude Fable's example has V=I<U.
- The identity U=cl(D) as actual sublocales was previously externally corroborated for coherent L. The identity V=D∘D was additionally supported by an external coherent audit using compact-open filter congruences. The order-duality theorem here does NOT require either localic composition identity, only its spectral point relations plus spatiality of the compact fitted hulls.
- Priority/novelty is not assessed: these descriptions overlap classical Priestley and Hochster duality, Hofmann–Mislove, normal/conormal lattice theory.
