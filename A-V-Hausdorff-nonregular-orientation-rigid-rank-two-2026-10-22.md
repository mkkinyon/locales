# Hausdorff regularity characterized by orientation balance of rank-two bars
Date: 22 October 2026. Status: proved point-free tree construction plus elementary Hausdorff spatial analysis.

Let X be any Hausdorff topological space and L=O(X). Let p∈X and a∈L be an open neighborhood of p. Set e=X\{p} (open since T1). Define two bar-neighborhood candidates in Q=L⊕L:
  w=(a⊠1)∨(e⊠e),
  s=w∧τw=(a⊠a)∨(e⊠e).
Define B_w={ (u,v):u⊠v≤w } and B_s similarly, with disjointness leaves D={(u,v):u∧v=0}.

THEOREM A (asymmetric one-sided rank two).
(1) For ANY such p,a in Hausdorff X, w is a rectangular bar and has an H→V separation tree of depth at most two.
(2) w has a V→H separation tree of depth at most two IF AND ONLY IF there exists an open neighborhood b of p with cl_X(b)⊆a.
(3) If no such b exists, w has EXACT rank two and is orientation rigid H-first. In that case s is a symmetric bar of EXACT rank THREE, and consists of just TWO tensor rectangles a⊠a and e⊠e.

Proof (1). For every x≠p, Hausdorffness yields opens u_x∋x, v_p∋p with u_x∩v_p=∅. Thus cl(u_x)⊆e, equivalently u_x*∨e=1. The initial leaves include (u_x,e)∈B_w (as u_x≤e) and (u_x,u_x*)∈D; vertical join gives (u_x,1)∈V(S_w). Also (a,1)∈B_w⊆V(S_w). Since X=a∪⋃_{x≠p}u_x, a horizontal root join proves (1,1)∈H(V(S_w)). Since any such proof implies d_I∨w=1 (by tensor rectangle universal property), barhood follows.

Proof (2), sufficiency: suppose b∋p with cl b⊆a; then b*∨a=1, b≤a. Thus (a,b)∈B_w, (b*,b)∈D, and (1,b)∈H(S_w). For any y≠p, take c_y∋y with cl c_y⊆e, and c_y≤e. Then (e,c_y)∈B_w (because e⊠e≤w), and (c_y*,c_y)∈D, so (1,c_y)∈H(S_w). The second opens b and c_y cover X, giving (1,1)∈V(H(S_w)).
Necessity: suppose V(H(S_w)) contains (1,1). Then second-coordinate opens t with (1,t)∈H(S_w) cover X. Choose one t containing p. For any first-coordinate B_w leaf (u,t) with p∈t, evaluate ordinary points (x,p): w contains (x,p) precisely when x∈a (since e×e excludes second p). Thus u⊆a, hence the B_w first-coordinate saturation r_w(t)≤a. All D first-coordinate leaves satisfy u≤t*=X\cl(t). Therefore (1,t)∈H(S_w) forces a∨t*=1, equivalently cl(t)⊆a. Since p∈t, this is the requested closure-shrink neighborhood.

Proof (3). If no such b exists, (2) rules out V→H depth2; (1) gives H→V depth2. Rank 0 fails since w≠1: a is proper (otherwise a closure-shrink exists), take x∈X\a, and (x,p) is ordinary point outside w. Rank1 for any nontrivial canonical bar is impossible: an H-only root would require (u,1)∈D∪B_w covering first coordinate; D forces u=0 and the remaining B_w rectangles imply w=1. V similarly. Thus ρ(w)=2 rigid H. Its transpose τw is rank2 rigid V. By parallel-tree/symmetrization theorem ρ(s)=3. Direct expansion of w∧τw:
   [(a⊠1)∨(e⊠e)]∧[(1⊠a)∨(e⊠e)] = (a⊠a)∨(e⊠e),
since cross terms are ≤e⊠e. Barhood of s follows d∨s=(d∨w)∧(d∨τw)=1.

THEOREM B (rank-detecting two-rectangle symmetric bar). For any Hausdorff X and p∈a:
   ρ(B_s)=2 iff p possesses b∋p with cl b⊆a;
   ρ(B_s)=3 iff p possesses no such b.
Proof second case above. First case: w has both depth2 orientations by A(1),(2); apply parallel-tree lemma to w and τw (each supports H-first or V-first depth2), giving ρ(s)≤2. Since s≠1 (if a is proper), ρ(s)=2. If a=X, then s=(1⊠1)∨e⊠e=1 and ρ(s)=0; so the exact rank-2 vs rank-3 dichotomy is for proper a. In a Hausdorff space with at least two points, the nonregular witness a necessarily proper.

COROLLARY (topological characterization). A Hausdorff space X is REGULAR iff EVERY canonical rectangular bar on O(X) of minimum rank 2 admits BOTH H→V and V→H trees of depth 2. (Regular ⇒ balanced via previously proved point-free regular-frame theorem in A-V-regular-frames-universal-depth-two-2026-10-22.md; conversely, if X is nonregular, there exist p∈a with no open b∋p satisfying cl b⊆a, so w above is a rigid rank2 bar.) Equivalently, every two-rectangle symmetric candidate s_(p,a)=(a⊠a)∨((X\{p})⊠(X\{p})) has depth≤2 iff X is regular; all these s_(p,a) are bars with depth≤3 regardless.

CONCRETE INSTANCE: K-topology on R, generated by ordinary intervals and intervals minus K={1/n:n≥1}. It is Hausdorff nonregular at p=0. Choose a=(-1,1)\K, open containing0. Every neighborhood b of0 contains some (-ε,ε)\K, whose closure contains some 1/n∈K∩(-ε,ε), so cl b⊈a. Let e=R\{0}. Then w=(a⊠1)∨(e⊠e) is exact-rank-2 H-rigid, s=(a⊠a)∨(e⊠e) is symmetric exact rank3. No graph-null construction, dense bridge indexing, or complicated localic boundary lemma needed.

EXTREMELY IMPORTANT: Frame regularity is the standard localic definition a=∨_{c≺a}c and for Hausdorff spatial X is equivalent to topological regularity. Converse is stated for HAUSDORFF SPATIAL locales, not arbitrary frames without enough points. No rank4 is exhibited. The theorem demonstrates rank-two orientation rigidity DOES EXIST and cannot be ruled out as a general principle; the outstanding challenge is rank-three orientation rigidity.
