## B. Ranked substantive tasks

### Rank 1 — Identify our C with the condensed classifying anima of coherent points of Sh(X). (AB 1; foundational, bounded.)

**Why first:** Direct reading reveals more than superficial resemblance. For spectral X, the literature states that the coherent point category \(\mathrm{Gal}(X_{zar})(S)\) on a profinite test S is the poset of *quasicompact* maps \(S\to X\) ordered by pointwise specialization (Haine et al., §3.30; Haine, *Classifying anima of condensed infinity-categories of points*, Example 0.1, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21330). Such a map is exactly a continuous map \(S\to X_{\rm patch}\): preimages of compact opens are clopen. Our patched \(\operatorname{Ch}_n(X)\) on a test S is exactly the continuous n-tuple of such maps satisfying pointwise specialization. Therefore the **nerve diagrams agree degreewise**, suggesting the canonical equivalence
\[
\mathbf C(X)\stackrel{?}{\simeq}B\,\mathbf{Pt}^{coh}(\operatorname{Sh}(X)).
\]
The open work is to check site/universe conventions, the precise target (condensed vs hypersheaf), restriction to extremally disconnected tests, and naturality in coherent locale maps. This is an identification with the condensed classifying anima of the **Zariski/spectral sheaf topos of X**, *NOT* in general with the condensed étale homotopy type of a scheme and *NOT* automatically with the intrinsic shape of the locale. Site-size independence might benefit but is not an automatic corollary. **Exit test:** a self-contained comparison of the two degreewise definitions or an exact obstruction; do not branch into the full etale theory.

### Rank 2 — Upper-witness crosscut connectivity ladder; then two-sided meet. (C 4, AB 3; main new mathematics.)

For tuples \(u_0,\ldots,u_j\) with a common lower witness, form the family \(F_j(u_\bullet)=\downarrow u_0\cap\cdots\cap\downarrow u_j\). A double Čech rectangle resolution should express C as realization over \(D_j\) of the **chain objects of these intersections**, and I as realization of \(D_j\). Apply the fourth-audit \(\infty\)-topos realization-connectivity lemma: suitable \((n-j)\)-connectivity of the chain-intersection fibres implies n-connectivity of the canonical C→I arrow. j=0 is a cone. Potentially: nonempty intersections give 1-connectivity; connected pairwise intersections yield \(\pi_1\)-isomorphism when higher j intersections remain nonempty; all intersections contractible recovers crosscut P3. Verify *exactly* what each connectivity convention and fibre hypothesis means, including compact Stone witness covers and pointwise-vs-internal contractibility. Then seek the two-sided version for C→M using \(↑\{a_i\}\cap↓\{u_j\}\). This could unify P3, rectangle relations, interpolant tests and the new meet-hull 2-connectivity theorem. **Exit test:** one precise theorem with two finite tests (successful face-poset case, failed noncontractible intersection), or a counterexample.

### Rank 3 — Return deliberately to the original binary hierarchy, including noncoherent locales. (Original program A, PF-Q12, W-phase and Q/Q′.)

Our coherent homotopy results now make clear what I,D can and cannot encode, but the original classification problems \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\), \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\Rightarrow\mathcal W=\mathcal U\), noncoherent residual/image behavior, and Q/Q′ remain meaningful and separate. The first two ranks should **inform** rather than displace these. Choose one sharply formulated question whose proofs genuinely require frame/locale methods rather than just finite posets, and retain adversarial checks against the known counterexamples. A useful conceptual boundary: can a connectivity/crosscut certificate imply any *binary* separation equality under explicit hypotheses, or are the independence counterexamples definitive? Avoid treating pointwise patched homotopy as an automatic test for noncoherent localic subobjects. A limited reset segment may be more pedagogically valuable than indefinite homotopy work.

### Rank 4 — Recover the Stone parameter from free-product sheaves with non-free factors. (AB 5; high upside, substantial difficulty.)

AB proposes one-ended freely indecomposable Higman H and Kurosh to recover factors of \(∗_{h(S)}H\), potentially contrasting with the **non-reconstruction** for the free group \(\mathrm{Fr}(h(S))\) in R2. First test a *simpler group*: \(G=C_2\). In the abstract free product \(∗_{s\in S_{\rm set}}C_2\), the conjugacy classes of nonidentity involutions correspond precisely to the factor labels; this is a classical normal-form/torsion-in-free-products consequence. Ask whether the **sheafified** quotient of the involution subsheaf by conjugation recovers h(S). Important: pointwise set recovery is not enough; work over Stone tests and prove descent and continuity of the recovered label. This clean initial test may reveal the correct notion of subgroup/conjugacy-class quotient before attempting Higman. A failure would also be informative. **Do not claim the sheaf reconstruction is already proved**.

### Rank 5 — Internal cohomology of folded vs intrinsic coefficients beyond degree one. (AB 4, AB 7; worthwhile but more technical.)

First test the proposed exponential constancy \([h(S),\underline Z]\simeq\underline{\text{appropriate locally constant maps }S\to Z}\) for truncated spaces Z, with an exact definition on both sides and all test objects (not merely evaluation at the point). Determine whether this controls *all* folded internal cohomology sheaves with constant local coefficients. Separately compute full \(\mathcal H^1(∗_{h(S)}G;\mathbb Z[\Pi])\) and augmentation-ideal coefficients, e.g. by an internal Bass–Serre resolution. Do not infer the all-degree assertion from the degree-one derivation formula without addressing sheafified totalizations and coefficients. Benefit: a refined characterization of the continuity threshold discovered in B3/B4, now strengthened for all nontrivial G.

### Rank 6 — Comparability information ladder and filtered/pro-homotopy. (C 5, earlier referee 1 PF-Q03/04.)

For a finite poset the undirected comparability graph determines the order complex as a clique complex. In coherent Stone families the ordered clique construction need not have an obviously continuous sorting operation, so the passage from the symmetric comparability nerve to \(\mathbf C\) is **not** automatic. Investigate the simplicial Stone topology, nerve equivalence and naturality. This would complete the three-rung picture: comparability (maybe full chain homotopy), binary I∧D with M₂ (chain 1-type plus sharp 2-connective approximation), and I or D alone (hull type). Coordinate with the previous first referee's Čech–Vietoris pro-homotopy and PF-Q03 finite-stage map; the fence W already refutes naive limits. This can be a strong later paper organization but is less immediate than Rank 2.

### Rank 7 — Intrinsic localic shape / site-size invariance / unpatched descent. (AB 1 caveat; older PF-Q05/08.)

Do NOT infer these from Rank 1 without a separate comparison. Site-size independence for **all** coherent locales requires accessibility and changing universes. Intrinsic locale shape is a different construction from the condensed classifying anima of coherent points. Esakia/co-Esakia classifications of unpatched lower-witness descent remain deferred in light of known counterexamples. These matter technically, but have weaker near-term theorem/learning payoff than ranks 1–3.

## C. Immediate proposal and pause conditions

**Suggested next sequence:**
1. A *short foundational/literature identification* session (Rank 1), incorporating A.1 and the Dowker/Markov references editorially.
2. The *crosscut connectivity ladder* (Rank 2), with exact hypotheses, finite controls and two-sided extension only if justified.

**Avoid:** treating every referee proposal as correct; claiming scheme étale shape; treating point-evaluation as a test for arbitrary sheaf colimits; presenting 2-connectivity as 2-type equivalence; assuming arbitrary binary-relation-preserving maps preserve M₂; or restarting already settled homotopy examples merely because they appear in historical open-problem snapshots.

**Current recommendation:** begin with Rank 1. It is a small verification with unusually high explanatory payoff. Then Rank 2 is the strongest new theorem program.
