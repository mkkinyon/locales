# Project A — Question (A-SC): specialization-order sublocale on stably locally compact locales

**Independent blind mathematical audit packet · 15 November 2026**

**Status.** Proposed theorem and proof for independent checking. This packet is self-contained at the level of basic frame/locale theory. The argument is classical when it invokes spatiality of coherent locales (prime ideal theorem). No claim of constructive spatiality is made.

## 1. Background and statement

Let (X) be a locale with frame (L=\mathcal O(X)). We write (u_i=\pi_i^*(u)\in\mathcal O(X\times X)), (i=1,2). Define its **canonical specialization-order sublocale**

\[
\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X\times X
\tag{1.1}
\]

to be the sublocale universally presented by the inequalities

\[
u_1\le u_2\qquad (u\in\mathcal O(X)).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus a locale map (g:Z\to X^2) factors through \(\mathcal A(X)\) if and only if (g^*(u_1)\le g^*(u_2)) for all (u). Equivalently, \(\mathcal A(X)\) is the meet of the sublocales enforcing each inequality. This specifies a genuine sublocale by a frame quotient, not merely a subset of points.

For ordinary points (x,y:1\to X), their pair factors through \(\mathcal A(X)\) exactly when

\[
x\le_X y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\forall u\in L)\,[x\in u\implies y\in u].
\tag{1.3}
\]

Write (G_{\le_X}\subseteq\operatorname{pt}(X^2)) for this graph. Let \([G_{\le_X}]\) denote the **point-generated sublocale** of (X^2): the join in the sublocale lattice of the image point sublocales indexed by (G_{\le_X}). In the classical setting, its frame is the lattice of traces of ambient opens on (G_{\le_X}); it is spatial.

A frame (L) is **continuous** if each (u\in L) is the join of the elements (v\ll u), where \(\ll\) denotes the usual way-below relation (tested against directed joins). It is **stably continuous** if it is continuous and \(v\ll u\), \(v\ll w\) imply \(v\ll u\wedge w\). A locale is **stably locally compact** if its frame is stably continuous, and **stably compact** if it is also compact, meaning (1\ll1). A **coherent** locale has a compact algebraic frame whose compact elements are closed under finite meets. We use the classical spatiality of coherent locales.

**Proposed theorem (A-SC).** For every stably locally compact locale (X),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(X)=[G_{\le_X}].}
\tag{1.4}
\]

In particular, the localic order sublocale \(\mathcal A(X)\) is spatial, even though its definition requires inequalities on generalized points rather than only the ordinary specialization pairs.

The proof proceeds through the following lemmas.

## 2. Functoriality and retractions

**Lemma 2.1.** The assignment \(X\mapsto\mathcal A(X)\) is the object part of a covariant functor \(\mathcal A:\mathbf{Loc}\to\mathbf{Loc}\). The map \(\mathcal A(f):\mathcal A(X)\to\mathcal A(Y)\) lies over \(f\times f:X^2\to Y^2\).

*Proof.* Let \(f:X\to Y\) be any locale map. For every \(v\in\mathcal O(Y)\), the pullback along \(f^2\) of the inequality \(v_1\le v_2\) is \((f^*v)_1\le(f^*v)_2\). This latter inequality holds on \(\mathcal A(X)\) by definition. Therefore \(f^2|_{\mathcal A(X)}\) factors uniquely through the sublocale \(\mathcal A(Y)\). Uniqueness gives preservation of identities and composition. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2.2.** If (X) is a locale retract of (Y), then \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a locale retract of \(\mathcal A(Y)\).

*Proof.* If \(s:X\to Y\) and \(r:Y\to X\) satisfy \(rs=1_X\), functoriality gives \(\mathcal A(r)\mathcal A(s)=1_{\mathcal A(X)}\). \(\square\)

**Lemma 2.3.** A locale retract of a spatial locale is spatial.

*Proof.* Suppose \(R\xrightarrow{p}S\xrightarrow{i}R\) has \(pi=1_S\), with \(R\) spatial. The frame map \(p^*:\mathcal O(S)\to\mathcal O(R)\) is injective because \(i^*p^*=1\). Distinct opens of (S) have distinct inverse images in (R); a point of (R) separates those inverse images, and its composite with (p) separates the original opens. \(\square\)

## 3. Coherent base case

**Lemma 3.1.** If \(Y\) is coherent, then \(\mathcal A(Y)\) is coherent and therefore spatial (classically).

*Proof.* Put \(K=K(Y)\), the bounded distributive lattice of compact opens. We have \(\mathcal O(Y)\cong\operatorname{Idl}(K)\), and finite locale products of coherent locales are coherent, with compact-open lattice (D=K\amalg K), the coproduct in bounded distributive lattices. Thus

\[
\mathcal O(Y^2)\cong\operatorname{Idl}(D).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because every open is a join of compact opens, it is enough to impose (k_1\le k_2) for every (k\in K); this implies (1.2) for all opens. Let (D'\) be the bounded distributive lattice quotient of (D) by the congruence generated by

\[
k_1=k_1\wedge k_2\qquad(k\in K).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The adjunction between ideal completion and the forgetful functor from frames to bounded distributive lattices shows that the **frame** universally presented by (3.1) and (3.2) is \(\operatorname{Idl}(D')\). (Explicitly, a bounded lattice homomorphism \(h:D\to M\), with (M) a frame, extends uniquely to \(I\mapsto\bigvee_{d\in I}h(d)\).) Thus \(\mathcal A(Y)\) is coherent. By classical spectral duality it is spatial. \(\square\)

No inference from mere coincidence of ordinary point sets is made here: the presentation produces the *actual frame quotient* defining the sublocale.

## 4. The stable-compact spectral retraction

**Lemma 4.1.** Every stably compact locale (X) is a retract, in the full category of locales and arbitrary locale maps, of a coherent locale.

*Proof.* Let \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), a compact stably continuous frame. Regard (L) as a bounded distributive lattice and put \(K=\operatorname{Idl}(L)\). This is a coherent frame: its compact elements are the principal ideals \(\downarrow u\), and these form a bounded distributive lattice.

Define

\[
\sigma:K\longrightarrow L,\quad I\longmapsto\bigvee I,
\qquad
\iota:L\longrightarrow K,\quad u\longmapsto\{v\in L:v\ll u\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Each \(\iota(u)\) is an ideal. The map \(\sigma\) preserves arbitrary joins and finite meets. For meets, because ideals are lower subsets closed under finite joins,

\[
\sigma(I)\wedge\sigma(J)
=\bigvee_{a\in I,\,b\in J}(a\wedge b)
=\bigvee(I\cap J).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The map \(\iota\) preserves binary meets, by stable continuity:

\[
v\ll(u\wedge w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(v\ll u\text{ and }v\ll w).
\tag{4.3}
\]

It preserves top because \(1\ll1\) implies every (v\in L) satisfies \(v\ll1\).

It also preserves arbitrary joins. Given \(u=\bigvee_{i\in I}u_i\), let (E\) be the set of all finite joins of elements (v\ll u_i\) for varying indices (i). Continuity gives \(\bigvee E=u\), and (E\) is directed. If (w\ll u\), then (w\le e\) for some (e\in E\); hence (w\) belongs to the ideal generated by the union of the ideals \(\iota(u_i)\). The reverse inclusion follows from monotonicity of \(\iota\). Therefore

\[
\iota\!\left(\bigvee_i u_i\right)=\bigvee_i\iota(u_i).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus \(\sigma\) and \(\iota\) are **frame homomorphisms**. Continuity gives

\[
\sigma\iota(u)=\bigvee_{v\ll u}v=u.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let (Y) be the coherent locale with frame \(K\). Under locale–frame duality, \(\sigma\) is the inverse-image homomorphism of a map \(s:X\to Y\), while \(\iota\) is that of \(r:Y\to X\). Equation (4.5) becomes \(r\circ s=1_X\). \(\square\)

The existence of this retraction is standard; see Vickers and Kawai in §7. The proof is included to make the exact morphism category and the role of compactness explicit.

**Corollary 4.2.** If (X) is stably compact, then \(\mathcal A(X)\) is spatial.

*Proof.* By Lemmas 4.1 and 2.2, \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a retract of \(\mathcal A(Y)\) for a coherent (Y). Lemmas 3.1 and 2.3 give spatiality. \(\square\)

## 5. Removing compactness

**Lemma 5.1 (bottom compactification).** For every stably locally compact locale (X), there is a stably compact locale (X_\bot\) containing (X) as an **open** sublocale.

*Proof.* Let \(L=\mathcal O(X)\). Form (L^\top=L\cup\{1'\}\) by adjoining a fresh greatest element (1'\) strictly above the old top (1_L). The old part is a principal downset in (L^\top), and its frame is (L), so it corresponds to the open sublocale (X\hookrightarrow X_\bot\) determined by the old top (1_L).

The new top is compact: if a directed family has join (1'\), it must contain (1'\), since the join of elements of the old (L) is at most (1_L). For (a,b\in L), one checks

\[
a\ll_{L^\top}b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\ll_Lb.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every (a\in L^\top) satisfies \(a\ll_{L^\top}1'\). Hence continuity for old elements is inherited from (L), continuity for (1'\) is automatic, and binary stability reduces to stability in (L) whenever both target elements are old. The remaining cases are immediate. Thus (L^\top) is compact and stably continuous. \(\square\)

**Lemma 5.2 (open restriction).** If (j:U\hookrightarrow Y) is an open sublocale inclusion, then

\[
\mathcal A(U)\cong U^2\times_{Y^2}\mathcal A(Y).
\tag{5.2}
\]

*Proof.* The inverse-image frame map \(j^*:\mathcal O(Y)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(U)\) is surjective. For any locale map \(g:Z\to U^2\), factoring (g) through \(\mathcal A(U)\) means that both coordinate pullbacks satisfy (g_1^*(u)\le g_2^*(u)\) for every open (u\in\mathcal O(U)\). By surjectivity of \(j^*\), this is equivalent to requiring (g_1^*(j^*v)\le g_2^*(j^*v)\) for every (v\in\mathcal O(Y)\), which is exactly the condition that the composite (Z\to U^2\to Y^2\) factor through \(\mathcal A(Y)\). The claimed isomorphism follows from these identical universal properties. \(\square\)

**Corollary 5.3.** If \(\mathcal A(Y)\) is spatial and (U\hookrightarrow Y) is open, then \(\mathcal A(U)\) is spatial.

*Proof.* The inclusion (U^2\hookrightarrow Y^2) is open, so (5.2) is an open sublocale of \(\mathcal A(Y)\). Open sublocales of spatial locales are spatial. \(\square\)

Apply Lemma 5.1 and Corollaries 4.2 and 5.3 to conclude that \(\mathcal A(X)\) is spatial for every stably locally compact (X).

## 6. Identification with the point-generated sublocale

It remains to check that spatiality is enough to conclude (1.4) *as a localic equality*.

Let (i:S\hookrightarrow Z) be any spatial sublocale of a locale (Z), with quotient frame homomorphism (q:\mathcal O(Z)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(S)\). Let (P\subseteq\operatorname{pt}(Z)) be the image of \(\operatorname{pt}(S)\) under (i). The point map of a monomorphism is injective. Since (S) is spatial, for (u,v\in\mathcal O(Z)),

\[
q(u)=q(v)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u\cap P=v\cap P.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed, the forward implication is automatic, and the reverse follows because points distinguish opens of (S). Thus the quotient frame of (S) is exactly the frame of traces of opens of (Z) on (P); equivalently, (S) is the sublocale generated by its point images.

For (S=\mathcal A(X)\subseteq X^2), the set (P) is exactly (G_{\le_X}\), by (1.3). Hence

\[
\mathcal A(X)=[G_{\le_X}],
\]

which proves the proposed theorem (A-SC). \(\square\)

## 7. Context and references

The original investigation sought to determine whether imposing specialization inequalities on **generalized points** introduces strictly more localic information than the ordinary specialization-order graph. The result was already known in the coherent case. Direct tests on noncoherent examples included total orders, finite products of total orders, and a compact continuous two-arm diamond; all were positive. The theorem above claims that *no stably locally compact counterexample exists*, irrespective of branching or of the existence of continuous binary joins.

The present reduction avoids an earlier, potentially dangerous strategy: pulling a specialization relation back from the patch locale and then assuming a perfect surjection stays surjective under an arbitrary pullback. **No such assertion occurs in this proof.** Only actual split retractions and open sublocale pullbacks are used.
