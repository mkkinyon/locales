# Project A — finite-fence restricted equalizers on the Cantor blocks

**18 November 2026.** Research theorem note, following the internal audit of
the cross-gap Cantor-graph construction. This note concerns the **nine closed
Cantor blocks** of the finite-fence pospace. It does not establish global
effectivity of the component quotient on the open geometric gaps.

## 1. Conventions

Use the finite-fence construction and notation of
`ProjectA_FiniteFence_EquivalenceHull_Theorem_2026-11-18.md`, subject to
its post-referee correction. In particular, \(K,M,L\subset P\) are disjoint
compact Cantor antichains, \(A,B\subset M\) are disjoint countable dense sets,
and the selected endpoint traces are

\[
 D_K=f(A),\qquad D_M=A\cup B,\qquad D_L=g(B).
\]

Let \(q:X\to Q\) be the locale map defined by component-saturated upper
opens, \(\mathcal K_q=X\times_QX\), \(H_G=[E_G]\), and \(T=H_G\circ H_G\).
We retain the fixed bracketing \(T\circ T\). Let

\[
 \Lambda_J:=\Lambda_E\wedge J
  =\bigwedge_{\substack{D\subseteq J\ \mathrm{closed}\\
                    D\cap D_J\ \mathrm{finite}}}
             \mathfrak o_J(J\setminus D),\qquad J\in\{K,M,L\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

The second equality follows by restricting the defining patch cofinite-tail
meet of \(\Lambda_E\) to \(J\): closed subsets of \(J\) are patch closed,
and traces of globally closed finite-selected sets are of the displayed
type. Each \(\Lambda_J\) has **no ordinary points**, since the singleton
of any \(x\in J\) is among the allowable closed sets in (1).

Write

\[
 R_{IJ}=E_G\cap(I\times J),\qquad
 \mathsf R_{IJ}=[R_{IJ}]_{I\times J}
 \quad (I,J\in\{K,M,L\}).
 \tag{2}
\]

For distinct blocks, the only nonempty \(R_{IJ}\) are the selected
endpoint graphs between \(K,M\) and \(M,L\) (and their transposes).
\(R_{KL}=R_{LK}=\varnothing\). On a single block,
\(R_{JJ}=\Delta_J\), since each fence component meets each block in at
most one point. Square brackets always mean the *sublocale generated by
the indicated ordinary pairs*, not the closure of that point set.

## 2. Direct calculation on the forbidden cross-gap block

**Proposition 1 (exact restricted equalizer).** Without assuming the global
patch kernel formula (65) or any patch-descent result,

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_q\wedge(K\times L)=\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L.}
 \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** For closed \(D\subseteq K\) meeting \(f(A)\) only finitely,
adjoin to \(D\) the finitely many complete \(P_n\)-components whose
\(K\)-endpoints belong to \(D\). This gives a Euclidean-closed,
component-saturated lower subset \(S_D\subseteq P\). Its complement
\(U_D=P\setminus S_D\) is a saturated upper open, hence \(U_D=q^{-1}(V_D)\)
for a quotient open \(V_D\subseteq Q\). The \(K\)-trace of \(U_D\) is
\(K\setminus D\), whereas its \(L\)-trace is all of \(L\).
On the equalizer of \(q|_K,q|_L\), the pullbacks of \(V_D\) agree;
therefore

\[
 \mathcal K_q\wedge(K\times L)\le (K\setminus D)\times L.
\]

Meeting over \(D\), and repeating symmetrically for closed
\(D'\subseteq L\) with \(D'\cap g(B)\) finite, gives the upper bound
\(\mathcal K_q\wedge(K\times L)\le\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L\).

Conversely, let \(V\in\mathcal O(Q)\), and put
\(S=P\setminus q^{-1}(V)\). The finite-fence forcing lemma implies
that either \(S\) meets the selected vertex set \(E\) in finitely many
points, or \(S\) contains *all* fence vertices and hence the three
Cantor sets. In the former case both traces \(q^{-1}(V)\cap K\) and
\(q^{-1}(V)\cap L\) contain the respective \(\Lambda\)-tails; in the
latter case both traces are empty. Thus the two maps
\(\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L\rightrightarrows Q\) agree on every frame
element \(V\), yielding a map through their equalizer. This proves
the reverse inequality. \(\square\)

## 3. The graph step for (5)

We state explicitly the **restricted Cantor-fusion principle (CG)** used
in the preceding internal audit. Let \(I,J\) be two of the Cantor blocks,
and let \(S\ne0\) be a sublocale of \(I\times J\) with
\(S\le[D_I]_I\times[D_J]_J\), where every one-point vertical and
horizontal fibre of \(S\) is zero. If \(C\) is the least closed support
of \(S\), then a ternary clopen-rectangle fusion in the zero-dimensional
compactum \(C\) produces a Cantor graph \(\Gamma\subseteq C\) whose
coordinate projections \(C_I,C_J\) are Cantor sets and whose selected
traces \(C_I\cap D_I,C_J\cap D_J\) are dense. A version with **specified
colours** follows by a finite Baire localization, as detailed in §4.

For clarity, the fusion uses (i) the spatiality of
\([D_I]_I\times J\) and \(I\times[D_J]_J\), since the other factor is
compact Hausdorff, to obtain separately dense selected-coordinate
anchors in the support; (ii) fibre vanishing to make each coordinate
section nowhere dense; and (iii) three disjoint coordinate rectangles
at each node, with two designated persistent selected-coordinate
anchors. The descendant diameters tend to zero; persistence ensures
the selected traces are dense, and disjoint coordinate projections
make the limit set a Cantor graph. This is the restricted, zero-dimensional
form of the fusion mechanism, not a claim about the full patch square.

For a graph whose selected projections are dense,

\[
 \mathcal B(\Gamma)\le\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J. \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, the preimage in \(\Gamma\) of each closed set meeting the
selected trace finitely is nowhere dense: a nonempty relatively open
subset of its perfect projection contains infinitely many selected
points. Its open complement therefore contains the least dense
sublocale \(\mathcal B(\Gamma)\). Apply this in each coordinate.

The *repaired perfect-Cantor dense-strip lemma* (DS') in the post-referee
note, coupled with the graph-restricted \(\Gamma\times M\) fibre-product
argument, gives

\[
 \mathcal B(\Gamma)\le T\circ T. \tag{CG-to-T}
\]

For \(K\times L\), the colours are necessarily \(P,Q\). Since
\(\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L\) is pointless on both coordinate fibres,
we can apply (CG) to each nonzero closed restriction
\(F\wedge(\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L)\). If a closed \(F\) annihilated
\(T\circ T\) but met \(\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L\), the resulting
\(\Gamma\subseteq F\) would contradict (CG-to-T). On the regular
locale \(K\times L\), the closed-annihilator test applies to sublocale
inclusions: regular locales are fit, so sublocales are fitted; an open
containing the right-hand sublocale but not the left has a complementary
closed witness. Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L\le T\circ T.} \tag{KL}
\]

Finally \(H_G\le\mathcal K_q\), and kernel pairs are equivalence
relations, so \(T\circ T\le\mathcal K_q\). Restricting to \(K\times L\),
(3) and (KL) yield the promised internally checked equality

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_q\wedge(K\times L)
       =(T\circ T)\wedge(K\times L)
       =\Lambda_K\times\Lambda_L.} \tag{5}
\]

This does **not** prove \(\mathcal K_q=T\circ T\) in \(X\times X\).

## 4. Extension to the other eight blocks: argument to audit

The same reasoning suggests the stronger **nine-block formula**

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_q\wedge(I\times J)
       =\mathsf R_{IJ}\vee(\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J)
       =(T\circ T)\wedge(I\times J)}
 \quad(I,J\in\{K,M,L\}). \tag{6?}
\]

Here joins are sublocale joins **within** \(I\times J\). The
unquestioned inclusions from right to left are
\(\mathsf R_{IJ}\le H_G\le T\circ T\le\mathcal K_q\) and
\(\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J\le\mathcal K_q\), the latter by the
same two-case saturated-open argument as in Proposition 1.

There are two further proof obligations:

1. **Ordinary-support split.** For closed \(D\subseteq I\setminus D_I\),
   the set \(D\) is component-saturated. Its complement descends to \(Q\).
   On \(D\times J\), the quotient kernel pair is empty if \(I\ne J\),
   and is the diagonal of \(D\) if \(I=J\): in the latter case the
   saturated-open complement first forces the second coordinate into
   \(D\), and the quotient restriction \(q|_D\) is a locale embedding,
   because all opens of the compact Hausdorff \(D\) are traces of
   saturated opens of \(X\). Taking the meet over all such \(D\),
   using that \(I\times J\) is regular/fit, gives
   \[
    \mathcal K_q\wedge(I\times J)
       \le \mathsf R_{IJ}\vee([D_I]_I\times[D_J]_J).
       \tag{7?}
   \]
   This step must preserve the join with the ordinary component graph:
   it is **not** valid to delete the diagonal when \(I=J\).

2. **Coloured Cantor fusion and strip inclusion.** If a closed \(F\)
   annihilates \(\mathsf R_{IJ}\vee(\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J)\)
   but meets the kernel pair, (7?) makes the remaining sublocale
   supported on selected coordinates. The ordinary fibre over any
   \(x\in I\) consists of at most the one ordinary pair in \(R_{IJ}\):
   \(q^{-1}([q(x)])\) is its finite fence component, because the
   complement of that component is a saturated upper open. Hence the
   remaining sublocale has zero point fibres. Apply clopen-rectangle
   fusion to its closed support. For the middle block, split
   \(D_M=A\cup B\) into its two colours; the four closed subsets of
   the support obtained by taking the coordinate-wise closures of
   colour-selected traces give a finite closed cover by pairwise
   intersections. Baire yields a nonempty relatively open set inside
   one colour pair; a relatively clopen refinement allows the fusion
   to use that fixed pair. The Cantor graph then has dense selected
   traces and (4), contradicting the choice of \(F\).

Independently, applying this coloured fusion to
\(\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J\) and using the corrected (DS') strip
lemma with the chosen colours proves
\(\Lambda_I\times\Lambda_J\le T\circ T\). This would complete (6?).

**Status at creation:** (5) has been rechecked and recorded. The
all-nine-block formula (6?) has a coherent internal proof route,
but the support split and coloured localization should receive a
separate adversarial check before (6?) is promoted to a theorem.

## 5. Remaining global issue

Even (6?) would cover only the nine closed Cantor rectangles.
It does not control any pair with a coordinate in the open geometric
gaps or in other fence vertices. The prohibited shortcut remains a
global proper-descent assertion for the patch counit, which the
external referee correctly rejected.
