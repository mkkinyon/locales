# Independent blind referee request — Project A, SC+ theorem

Please read the accompanying file `ProjectA_SCplus_EXTERNAL_BLIND_PACKET_2026-11-15.md` **independently and adversarially**. You have **not** been given the authors' internal audit, and we would like your own assessment before showing you any internal report.

## Context and purpose

The work concerns sublocales of the localic square \(X\times X\) extending the specialization, inseparability, and closure-meeting graphs studied by Cameron and Kumar. The particular question is whether the known finite/coherent interval-chain criterion for the equality \(\mathcal S=\mathcal M\), with \(\mathcal M=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D\), extends from compact to **stably locally compact locales without global compactness**.

The packet proposes an affirmative theorem and, more generally, a spatiality result for the fitted diagonal and the mixed meet. The proposed proof turns on a lemma about fitted spatial sublocales of saturated subsets, proved using patch topology and compact saturated neighborhoods.

## Referee instructions

1. **Check the main theorem from first principles**, not merely its plausibility. In particular, seek a counterexample to the saturation lemma or to the exact equality of sublocales claimed for \(\mathcal D\) and \(\mathcal M\).
2. Examine **every displayed equation and every assertion**, including the background reductions, the pointwise common-upper-bound lemma, the use of sobriety, and any remarks about perfectness. Do not assume that point-set equality implies localic equality.
3. Check that the proof is valid for **noncompact** stably locally compact locales, rather than silently importing a theorem that requires stably compactness.
4. Scrutinize the step from a **pointwise equality of opens** to a **join of nuclei**. Nucleus joins are not generally computed pointwise.
5. Verify the claim that \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}(\Delta)\) can itself be treated as a stably locally compact spatial locale, and that fitting inside \(\mathcal I\) gives \(\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D\).
6. Check which arguments use classical choice, local compactness, sobriety, stability, and spatiality of the product; recommend the sharpest hypotheses you can justify.
7. Distinguish **correct**, **correct after repair**, **unproven**, and **false** conclusions. If a repair is needed, propose the smallest usable correction and its revised scope.
8. If possible, give finite, spectral, Scott/domain-theoretic, or noncompact controls. If you discover a counterexample, specify the topology, locale, sublocale, and exact failure.
9. Note anything confusing on a first reading, even if mathematically harmless. Mention possible simplifications and relevant prior literature when known. A correctness review is the priority; no novelty claim is made.

Please return a standalone referee report with a clear verdict, a ranked list of issues, explicit reasoning for each, and suggestions for the next research step. Do not seek the authors' internal audit before finishing your independent assessment.

**Research-project convention:** Order is \(x\le y\iff x\in\overline{\{y\}}\); open sets are upper sets. The reverse order convention appears in portions of the original graph literature.
