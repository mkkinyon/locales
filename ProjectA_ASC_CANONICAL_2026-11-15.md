# Project A — Oriented specialization is spatial on stably locally compact locales (A-SC)

**Canonical post-referee theorem note · 15 November 2026.** The theorem and the original ideal-completion/bottom-lift proof received an independent blind referee verdict of **correct**, with no repairs required. The shorter open-retraction proof below was suggested by that referee and checked internally. Classical spatiality of coherent locales uses the prime ideal theorem. No literature-priority claim is made.

**Related notes:** [Sierpiński exponential and diagram locales](ProjectA_SIERPINSKI_EXPONENTIAL_2026-11-15.md); [external referee reconciliation](ProjectA_ASC_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-15.md); [audit archive](project-a/audits/2026-11-15/ASC/README.md); [earlier coherent proof](ProjectA_coherent_composition_canonical_proof_2026-11-08.md), §4.

## 1. Statement and conventions

For a locale \(X\), put \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), \(X^2=X\times X\), and \(u_i=\pi_i^*u\). The **canonical oriented specialization relation** is the sublocale

\[
\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X^2
\]

presented universally by \(u_1\le u_2\) for every \(u\in L\). Thus a map \(g:T\to X^2\) factors through \(\mathcal A(X)\) exactly when \(g^*u_1\le g^*u_2\) for all \(u\). On points, this is the specialization graph

\[
G_{\le_X}=\{(x,y):x\le_X y\},\qquad
x\le_X y\iff (\forall u)(x\in u\Rightarrow y\in u).
\]

Write \([P]\hookrightarrow X^2\) for the *sublocale generated by* the point images \(P\subseteq \operatorname{pt}(X^2)\). One must not infer equality of sublocales from equality of their point sets alone.

A frame is **stably continuous** if it is continuous and its way-below relation \(\ll\) is stable under nonempty finite meets: \(v\ll a\) and \(v\ll b\) imply \(v\ll a\wedge b\). Here *stably locally compact* need **not** mean compact; *stably compact* adds \(1\ll1\). Some sources, notably Vickers, call the latter “stably locally compact.”

**Theorem (A-SC).** For every stably locally compact locale \(X\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(X)=[G_{\le_X}].}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, the actual presented sublocale \(\mathcal A(X)\) is spatial. The proof is classical at the final spatiality step, not asserted constructively.

## 2. Formal properties of \(\mathcal A\)

**Functoriality.** For any locale map \(f:X\to Y\), the restriction of \(f\times f\) to \(\mathcal A(X)\) satisfies \((f^*v)_1\le(f^*v)_2\) for every \(v\in\mathcal O(Y)\), so it factors uniquely through \(\mathcal A(Y)\). Uniqueness shows that \(X\mapsto\mathcal A(X)\) is a covariant endofunctor of \(\mathbf{Loc}\). Consequently it preserves split retractions.

**Sublocale restriction.** For every sublocale \(j:U\hookrightarrow Y\), not necessarily open, the frame quotient \(j^*:\mathcal O(Y)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(U)\) is surjective. By the same universal property,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(U)=U^2\times_{Y^2}\mathcal A(Y)}
\tag{2.1}
\]

as sublocales of \(U^2\). If \(U\) is **open** in \(Y\), then \(\mathcal A(U)\) is an open sublocale of \(\mathcal A(Y)\).

**Spatial retracts.** If \(S\) is a locale retract of a spatial locale \(R\), then \(S\) is spatial. Indeed, for a split surjection \(p:R\to S\), \(p^*:\mathcal O(S)\to\mathcal O(R)\) is injective. A point of \(R\) distinguishing the pullbacks of two opens of \(S\) composes with \(p\) to distinguish those opens.

## 3. Coherent base case

**Lemma.** If \(Y\) is coherent, then \(\mathcal A(Y)\) is coherent, hence spatial classically.

*Proof.* Let \(K\) be the bounded distributive lattice of compact opens of \(Y\), so \(\mathcal O(Y)=\operatorname{Idl}(K)\). Ideal completion is left adjoint to the forgetful functor from frames to bounded distributive lattices. Thus

\[
\mathcal O(Y^2)=\operatorname{Idl}(D),\qquad D=K\amalg K
\]

for the bounded distributive lattice coproduct \(D\). Every open is a join of compact opens, so it suffices to impose the relations \(k_1\le k_2\) for \(k\in K\). Let \(D'\) be the bounded distributive quotient of \(D\) generated by \(k_1=k_1\wedge k_2\) for all \(k\). The ideal-completion adjunction shows that the **actual frame quotient** imposing the inequalities is \(\operatorname{Idl}(D')\). It is coherent and, using the prime ideal theorem, spatial. \(\square\)

## 4. Direct open-retraction lemma

**Lemma.** If \(L\) is any stably continuous frame, its locale \(X\) is a retract of an **open sublocale of a coherent locale**. No compactness assumption on \(L\) is needed.

*Proof.* Regard \(L\) as a bounded distributive lattice and write \(K=\operatorname{Idl}(L)\). The frame \(K\) is coherent: its compact elements are the principal ideals \(\downarrow a\). Put

\[
\sigma:K\to L,\quad \sigma(I)=\bigvee I,\qquad
\iota:L\to K,\quad \iota(a)=\{v:v\ll a\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

These satisfy the order adjunction chain

\[
\boxed{\iota\dashv\sigma\dashv(a\mapsto\downarrow a).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, \(\sigma(I)\le a\iff I\subseteq\downarrow a\). For the other adjunction, \(\iota(a)\subseteq I\) implies \(a=\bigvee\iota(a)\le\bigvee I\); conversely, if \(a\le\bigvee I\) and \(v\ll a\), directedness of the ideal \(I\) provides \(w\in I\) with \(v\le w\), so \(v\in I\). Thus \(\iota(a)\subseteq I\iff a\le\sigma(I)\).

In particular \(\iota\) and \(\sigma\) preserve arbitrary joins. The latter preserves binary meets by frame distributivity:

\[
\sigma(I)\wedge\sigma(J)
=\bigvee_{a\in I,b\in J}(a\wedge b)
=\bigvee(I\cap J).
\]

The former preserves binary meets by stable continuity:

\[
v\ll(a\wedge b)\iff(v\ll a\ \text{and}\ v\ll b).
\]

Continuity supplies \(\sigma\iota=1_L\).

The only possible failure of \(\iota:L\to K\) to be a frame homomorphism is preservation of the top. Remedy this by setting

\[
W=\iota(1_L)=\{v:v\ll 1_L\}\in K.
\]

Since \(\iota(a)\subseteq W\) for all \(a\), it corestricts to \(\iota:L\to\downarrow W\), sending \(1_L\) to the top \(W\). The restriction \(\sigma:\downarrow W\to L\) also preserves top, since \(\sigma(W)=1_L\). Hence both are frame homomorphisms, and their composite is \(1_L\).

If \(Y\) is the coherent locale with frame \(K\), the element \(W\) determines an **open sublocale** \(V\hookrightarrow Y\) with frame \(\downarrow W\). Under frame–locale duality the two maps above exhibit \(X\) as a locale retract of \(V\). \(\square\)

In the compact case \(1\ll1\), so \(W=K\) and \(V=Y\); this is the standard spectral-retraction theorem. The direct open argument avoids the older bottom-compactification proof.

## 5. Proof of the theorem

Take stably locally compact \(X\). By §4, choose a coherent locale \(Y\) and an open sublocale \(V\hookrightarrow Y\) such that \(X\) retracts from \(V\).

By §3, \(\mathcal A(Y)\) is coherent and spatial. By (2.1), \(\mathcal A(V)\) is open in \(\mathcal A(Y)\), hence spatial. By functoriality, \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a retract of \(\mathcal A(V)\), hence spatial.

For completeness, if \(S\hookrightarrow Z\) is a **spatial** sublocale with quotient \(q:\mathcal O(Z)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(S)\) and point-image set \(P\subseteq\operatorname{pt}(Z)\), then

\[
q(u)=q(v)\iff
\{p\in P:p^*u=1\}=\{p\in P:p^*v=1\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus its quotient congruence is exactly that of the join of the point sublocales \([P]\); hence \(S=[P]\). Applying this to \(S=\mathcal A(X)\), whose point images are exactly \(G_{\le_X}\), proves (1.1). \(\square\)

## 6. Further consequences and scope

**Corollary (referee-origin; internally rechecked after the blind review).** The functor \(\mathcal A\) preserves stable local compactness. Thus \(\mathcal A(X)\) is not merely spatial, but itself stably locally compact whenever \(X\) is.

*Proof.* An open sublocale of a coherent locale is stably locally compact. This class is closed under locale retracts. Here is the frame argument for the latter, to avoid an unsupported closure assertion. Let \(M\) be stably continuous, and let \(i:L\to M\), \(p:M\to L\) be frame homomorphisms with \(pi=1_L\). If \(m\ll_M i(a)\) then \(p(m)\ll_L a\), by testing against directed sets in \(L\) and using \(i\) to transport their joins. As \(i(a)=\bigvee_{m\ll i(a)}m\), applying \(p\) shows \(L\) is continuous. If \(b\ll_L a,c\), select \(m\ll_M i(a)\), \(n\ll_M i(c)\) such that \(b\le p(m)\) and \(b\le p(n)\). Then
\[
b\le p(m\wedge n)\ll_L p(i(a)\wedge i(c))=a\wedge c,
\]
using stability in \(M\) and preservation of finite meets by \(p\). Thus \(L\) is stably continuous. Now \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a retract of the open sublocale \(\mathcal A(V)\) of coherent \(\mathcal A(Y)\); apply this closure result. \(\square\)

**Categorical identification.** There is a natural isomorphism \(\mathcal A(X)\cong X^{\mathbb S}\), where \(\mathbb S\) is the Sierpiński locale. See the [separate canonical note](ProjectA_SIERPINSKI_EXPONENTIAL_2026-11-15.md). It implies \(\mathcal A\) preserves all limits. The statement that arbitrary sublocales of spatial locales are spatial is **false** and is not used.

**Boundary.** For a locally compact, sober, non-stably-locally-compact locale \(X\), the above retraction proof need not apply: the map \(\iota\) generally fails to preserve binary meets. Spatiality of \(\mathcal A(X)\) in that broader class is a separate question. Regular locales provide a positive class, since \(\mathcal A(X)=\Delta_X\) for regular \(X\); non-spatial regular locales show there can be no unrestricted all-locale spatiality theorem.
