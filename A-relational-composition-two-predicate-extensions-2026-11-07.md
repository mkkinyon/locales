## 1. Fiberwise semilattice criterion (any arity)

**Proposition.** Let X↪Y×S^n (n finite or any indexing set), with Δ_Y both closed and fitted. Suppose either:

(MIN) for every locale T and all maps f,g:T→X with rf=rg, the coordinatewise meet of their S-predicate opens defines a map h:T→X over the same base;
or
(MAX) the corresponding coordinatewise join defines such a map.

Then

    **I_X∘D_X = V_X = K.**

Proof: (MIN) applied to the universal K-pair shows K≤B_X A_X; the universal inequality B A≤D≤K makes D=K, hence I D=K by reflexivity of I and transitivity of K, and V=K since D≤V≤K. Dually (MAX) gives I=K via A B≤I≤K; hence V=K and I D=K.

It is enough that a common lower / upper specialization exists for every universal K-pair, not necessarily the actual pointwise meet / join. The coordinatewise operations are explicit because maps into S correspond to opens.

## 2. Ambient closed and fitted sublocales give semilattice closure

**Corollary (any arity).** If X is a CLOSED sublocale of Y×S^n then (MIN) holds; if X is a FITTED sublocale then (MAX) holds. Consequently either hypothesis implies I D=V=K.

Proof: for fixed base map t:T→Y, the parameterwise meet h of two maps (t,(p_i)) and (t,(q_i)) lies below both in the ambient specialization order. A closed sublocale is downward closed under specialization on generalized points, so h factors through X. Similarly the parameterwise join lies above both; a fitted sublocale is upward closed and hence contains it. These are genuine localic arguments on T=K, not only ordinary points.

An OPEN X is in particular fitted, so a counterexample inside Y×S² cannot be a simple open or closed piece of the ambient product. Nontrivial mixed open/closed/infinitary frame equations are necessary.

## 3. Finite diamond truth-pattern control

For n=2, four classical values are 00,10,01,11 (Boolean lattice 2²). Direct enumeration: every NONEMPTY subset of these four elements is closed under binary meet OR under binary join, except exactly the antichain {10,01}. To fail meet closure one needs both incomparable values without 00; to fail join closure one needs them without 11. To fail both requires precisely {10,01}.

This elementary fact cannot be substituted for the generalized-point hypothesis of §1: sublocale presentations encode nondecidable open values, and the operation that works may vary with the base point. The failure of naive Booleanization/descent is a main obstacle.

## 4. Boolean-base finite-poset theorem (new strong no-go)

**Theorem (working).** Let Y be a BOOLEAN locale, i.e. B=O(Y) a complete Boolean algebra (possibly atomless/pointless), and P ANY FINITE Alexandrov poset. For EVERY sublocale X↪Y×P,

    **I_X=A_X B_X and D_X=B_X A_X.**

Hence I_X D_X≤V_X and the full coherent two-idempotent mixed-composition inclusion hold, without compactness, coherence, or atomicity assumptions on B.

**Step 1 (finite slices jointly generate the ambient).** The frame O(Y×P) identifies with Mon(P,B), the monotone B-valued functions on finite P, with pointwise meets and joins. For each p∈P, restriction to the slice Y×{p} is evaluation at p. These maps are jointly injective on Mon(P,B), so

    Y×P = join_{p∈P} (Y×{p})

in the sublocale lattice. Its finite distributivity gives for any X sublocale

    X = join_{p∈P} (X∧(Y×{p})).

**Step 2 (each Y-slice is clopen).** Any nucleus j on a complete Boolean algebra B has j(a)=a∨j(0): indeed j(a)∧a^c≤j(a)∧j(a^c)=j(0), and a∨j(0)≤j(a). Thus every sublocale of Y is clopen. Each slice X∧(Y×{p}) is b_p×{p} for a unique complemented b_p∈B.

**Step 3 (finite clopen partition).** There are finitely many b_p. Decompose 1 into the finitely many pairwise disjoint elements

    b_J = (meet_{p∈J} b_p) ∧ (meet_{p∉J} b_p^c),    J⊆P.

On b_J all slice memberships are fixed. Therefore

    X ≅ coproduct_{J⊆P, b_J≠0} (b_J×J),

with J a finite Alexandrov subspace of P. This decomposition takes place along the ambient clopen base partition and is a localic isomorphism.

**Step 4 (relations on a Boolean×finite component).** For a Boolean locale b, every specialization pair of maps to b is equal, because all opens are complemented; moreover I_b=D_b=Δ_b (I via disjoint complementary rectangles, D via binary covers {a,a^c}). For a finite Alexandrov locale J, its genuine localic relations are I_J=A_J B_J and D_J=B_J A_J.

On b×J, set F_I=Δ_b×I_J, F_D=Δ_b×D_J under reordering of coordinates. F_I is CLOSED and contains diagonal, and F_D FITTED and contains diagonal (as pullbacks/meets of Δ_b and I_J/D_J). Thus I_(b×J)≤F_I and D_(b×J)≤F_D. Conversely F_I⊆A_(b×J)B_(b×J) because each (p,q)∈I_J has a common upper r∈J, with the SAME b-base map; similarly F_D⊆B_(b×J)A_(b×J) via a common lower. Since AB≤I and BA≤D universally, both inclusions are equalities. Disjoint unions preserve the diagonal relations componentwise, proving the theorem.

**Important scope:** finiteness of P is essential to the finite sublocale-join/finite clopen-partition argument. No unrestricted claim for an infinite Alexandrov factor. BOOLEAN BASE is also essential to making every slice clopen; regularity alone does not do it.

**Consequence:** even an ATOMLESS POINTLESS Boolean base cannot be used to refute mixed≤V with finitely many Sierpiński predicates. If a counterexample arises in the finite-predicate family over a regular base, the obstruction is genuinely NON-BOOLEAN GLUING of slice sublocales.

## 5. Two-predicate antichain: actual failure of BOTH semilattice mechanisms but no mixed failure

Let Y=R with usual topology. Let X be the topological sum Q⊔(R\Q), with projection r:X→Y the identity on underlying sets, and let e=Q,f=R\Q (two complementary clopen predicates of X). The map i=(r,e,f):X→Y×S² is a sublocale embedding because its coordinate opens generate O(X).

Put B=RO(R). Define f_Q,g_Z:O(X)→B by regularized traces to the dense Q and Z=irrational components. They have the same base map O(R)→B, while

    f_Q(e)=1, f_Q(f)=0;
    g_Z(e)=0, g_Z(f)=1.

Therefore their pair factors through K=ker r, and produces a NONZERO POINTLESS off-diagonal K-component; X has precisely one ordinary point over each real base point, so K has no offdiagonal ordinary points.

These maps have NO common lower specialization: any h≤f_Q,g_Z would send both e,f to 0, contradicting e∨f=1. Likewise NO common upper: any h≥f_Q,g_Z sends both e,f to 1, contradicting e∧f=0.

Nevertheless X is regular (sum of two metric spaces), so I_X=D_X=Δ_X, V_X=Δ_X, and I_X D_X=V_X. Thus the precise antichain mechanism itself is INSUFFICIENT for a counterexample.

This shows the reduction in §1 is sufficient, not necessary, and a large pointless kernel pair can coexist with trivial I,D.

## 9. GENERAL four-sublocale presentation (no Boolean base assumed)

The proof of §4 actually yields a structural normal form over ANY base Y, provided P finite Alexandrov.

**Theorem (working, algebraic presentation).** Every sublocale X↪Y×P is uniquely determined by its finite family of sliced sublocales

    Y_p = X∧(Y×{p})  (viewed as sublocales of Y).

One has

    X = join_{p∈P}(Y_p×{p}).

Conversely any choice of finitely many sublocales Y_p↪Y defines X by the displayed join; restriction back to Y×{p} recovers Y_p because distinct singleton slices of Y×P meet in 0, and finite distributivity holds in the sublocale lattice.

Frame-theoretic proof: O(Y×P)=Mon(P,O(Y)), and restrictions to the slice Y×{p} are the evaluations ev_p. The ev_p are jointly injective, so the join of all slices is the whole product. Since the sublocale lattice is a coframe (hence finitely distributive), intersecting with X distributes over the finite slice join. Every Y_p is an arbitrary sublocale of Y, NOT necessarily an ordinary subspace or spatial.

Thus the entire two-predicate extension problem reduces to a **four-sublocale configuration** (Y_00,Y_10,Y_01,Y_11) in the base. These need not be disjoint. In fact distinct *spatially disjoint* dense sublocales of a non-Boolean regular Y can have a NONZERO POINTLESS meet (at least the Isbell Booleanization B(Y), contained in every dense sublocale).

For n=2, if r*:O(Y)→O(X) is the base map and e,f are the Sierpinski generators, every x∈O(X) is represented by

    r*(a0) ∨ (r*(a1)∧e) ∨ (r*(a2)∧f) ∨ (r*(a12)∧e∧f)

with coefficients a0≤a1,a2≤a12 in O(Y), interpreted as the join presentation of an open of the product. This is the 2-variable analogue of Banaschewski's quoted singly-generated normal form.

The joint restriction map

    O(X) ↪ ∏_{p∈P}O(Y_p)

is injective. Its image is exactly the tuples of the form (q_p(w(p)))_p, where w:P→O(Y) is monotone and q_p:O(Y)↠O(Y_p) is the frame quotient. This is an explicit subdirect representation that can be used for frame/nucleus calculations, with no need to choose a topological subspace model.

**Caution on composition.** It would be FALSE to assume without proof that the localic image I∘D can be calculated by composing separately in the p,q slices: localic images do not in general commute with arbitrary base change along the nonopen/nonproper slice embeddings. This is precisely why the Boolean case is easier: clopen base patching gives a genuinely stable local decomposition.

## 10. Dense-colouring construction as a *dense sublocale* of Y×P

Let Y=R_usual and partition Y into four dense P_p. Let X carry the initial topology from r:X→Y and the coloring map to P=S² (equivalently, generated by base opens and e,f). The canonical map i:X→Y×P is a **dense sublocale embedding**.

Surjectivity of i*:O(Y×P)→O(X) is true by the very definition of the topology. If a nonzero product open w corresponds to monotone coefficient tuple (w_p)_p, some w_p is nonzero usual open. Since P_p is dense in Y, w_p meets P_p. Hence i*(w)≠0, so i is dense.

However i* is generally NOT injective: distinct usual opens may agree on a dense proper colour stratum (e.g. R and R\{x}, if x is outside that stratum). Thus X is a proper dense sublocale of Y×P rather than isomorphic to it. The graph of a dense coloring is a clean way to engineer nonspatial cross-slice behavior while remaining spatial Hausdorff itself.

All Y_p are dense sublocales of Y; therefore their pairwise meets contain the least dense Boolean sublocale B(Y), even though the ordinary coloured strata P_p are disjoint. The Boolean-valued maps ρ_p of §6 are precisely these common-pointless overlaps. This EXPLAINS rather than merely exhibits the nonspatial I and D in the four-colour test.

## 12. MAJOR STRENGTHENING: REGULAR BASE IMPLIES D=B A FOR EVERY FINITE POSET FIBER

**Theorem (NEW WORKING, localic proof).** Let Y be REGULAR and P FINITE Alexandrov. For any sublocale X↪Y×P:

    **D_X = B_X∘A_X**.

Consequently

    **I_X∘D_X = I_X∘A_X = A_X∘J_X∘A_X ≤ U_X∧V_X**,

and similarly with I,D reversed. Here A_X denotes the canonical specialization-order sublocale and B_X=A_X^smile. The universal inclusion I D≤U is from the preceding segment.

**Dependency facts, to be independently audited:**
(a) Regular locale Y is strongly Hausdorff (specialization equality on all generalized points), F-separated (Δ_Y fitted), and FIT (EVERY sublocale of Y is fitted). Standard regular⇒fit in J. Martínez, CMUC 49 (2008), Def/Remarks 5.1; fit⇔all sublocales fitted (Picado–Pultr, Arrieta).
(b) Finite P gives frame O(Y×P)=Mon(P,O Y) and finite slice-join decomposition of EVERY sublocale, as in §9.
(c) Relational image is left adjoint to inverse image on sublocales and hence preserves arbitrary sublocale joins.

**Step 1: canonical specialization on slices.** Write X=join_p(Y_p×{p}), where Y_p↪Y arbitrary sublocales. Since Y is strongly Hausdorff, any two generalized maps f,g to X that are specialization-comparable must have equal Y-coordinate. A pair with fixed labels p,q and equal Y-coordinate satisfies f≤g iff p≤q: X-open values are inherited from monotone Y×P opens via the quotient i*. Thus A_X on (p,q) is the diagonal-base sublocale Y_p∧Y_q if p≤q, and zero otherwise; B_X is the converse.

**Step 2: calculate the genuine LOCALIC image B A.** Let E=B_{12}∧A_{23}↪X³. It lies over the diagonal base in Y³ and has a finite decomposition into triple label slices (p,t,q). Such a slice is precisely Y_p∧Y_t∧Y_q↪Y for t≤p,q, and zero otherwise. The localic image E→X² preserves joins of these finitely many source slices; on each slice its map to the (p,q) target is the inclusion of that meet sublocale into the base diagonal. Hence its image has (p,q)-component

    R_{pq}=join_{t≤p,q}(Y_p∧Y_q∧Y_t)
           =(Y_p∧Y_q) ∧ (join_{t≤p,q}Y_t).

These equalities are of sublocales of Y, including pointless intersections; NO calculation on ordinary point sets is used.

**Step 3: construct a fitted ambient relation.** Put

    H_{pq}=join_{t≤p,q}Y_t    as sublocale of Y.

Because Y is fit and the lower cones are finite, each H_{pq} is fitted (finite join of fitted sublocales). The H_{pq} increase as p,q increase, so their tuple defines a FITTED sublocale H↪Y×P².

**Finite fitted tuple lemma:** For any finite poset Q and any increasing tuple (H_s)_{s∈Q} of FITTED sublocales of Y, the join_s(H_s×{s}) is FITTED in Y×Q. Proof: given s and an open a∈O(Y) with H_s≤O(a), define an ambient monotone open w^s_a:Q→O(Y) by w(t)=a for t≤s and w(t)=1 otherwise. Then this open contains every slice H_t; intersecting all such opens over s,a recovers H_s on each slice, hence the original relation.
