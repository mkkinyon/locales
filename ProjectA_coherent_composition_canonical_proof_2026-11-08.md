# Project A — Canonical proof of the coherent proconstructible composition calculus

**8 November 2026 · Consolidated foundational theorem note**

**Status and provenance.** This is a corrected consolidation of the Project A coherent-composition technical packet (8 November), the independent blind referee report (*Referee report — Project A coherent proconstructible composition calculus*, 8 November), and the subsequent internal reconciliation. The principal localic composition theorem and the identifications of the canonical relations were **independently externally audited**; the intrinsic compact-quotient criterion, compact-fitted-hull lemma, and uniform hull formulas were supplied or sharpened by that referee and checked internally. This document replaces *the proofs*, not the historical audit record. No literature priority is asserted. The exposition is **classical**, using the prime ideal theorem when passing to points; an unproved constructive strengthening is not claimed.

**Scope.** This note concerns *finite* compositions of **proconstructible spectral sublocales of coherent locales**. It does **not** establish associativity of arbitrary image-defined localic relations. The Project A relation \(\mathcal U\) is used below only through the independently established coherent theorem \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)\), which is **not reproved here**.

## 0. Notation, conventions, and principal results

For a locale \(X\), put \(\mathcal O(X)=L\). A **coherent** locale means that \(L\) is compact, algebraic, and its compact elements are closed under finite meets. Then \(K(X)\), the compact elements of \(L\), is a **bounded** distributive lattice (in particular \(1\) is compact), and

\[
\mathcal O(X)\cong\operatorname{Idl}(K(X)).
\]

The category \(\mathbf{DLat}\) consists of bounded distributive lattices and bounded homomorphisms; \(\mathbf{Frm}\) consists of frames and frame homomorphisms. A locale map \(f:X\to Y\) between coherent locales is called **spectral** if \(f^*\) preserves compact elements. Under classical spectral duality, \(X\) has spectral point space \(P_X=\operatorname{pt}(X)\), whose patch topology is compact Hausdorff. Compact opens are patch-clopen; the patch topology is generated by them and their complements. We use

\[
x\leq y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\overline{\{y\}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\forall U\in\mathcal O(P_X))(x\in U\Rightarrow y\in U).
\]

Thus opens are specialization **upsets**; \(y\geq x\) is a generization of \(x\).

For sublocales \(R\hookrightarrow X\times Y\), \(S\hookrightarrow Y\times Z\), define **diagrammatic** localic composition (first \(R\), then \(S\)) by

\[
R\circ S=\operatorname{im}_{\mathbf{Loc}}
\bigl(R\times_Y S\longrightarrow X\times Z\bigr).
\tag{0.1}
\]

All pullbacks and images are in the entire category \(\mathbf{Loc}=\mathbf{Frm}^{\mathrm{op}}\), not merely in the coherent subcategory. We sometimes omit \(\circ\) **only after** proving associativity in the specified class. Sublocale order is inclusion, \(\vee\) is sublocale join, and \(\wedge\) is sublocale meet.

A **proconstructible spectral sublocale** \(S\hookrightarrow Z\) of a coherent locale is the sublocale associated with a patch-closed subset of \(P_Z\), equipped with its *spectral subspace topology*. This is a restricted class of sublocales: equal ordinary point sets do not identify arbitrary sublocales.

**Theorem A (intrinsic characterization).** For a sublocale \(S\hookrightarrow Z\) of a coherent locale and its quotient \(q:\mathcal O(Z)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(S)\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(S\) is proconstructible spectral.
2. \(q\) takes compact elements to compact elements.
3. Under \(\mathcal O(Z)=\operatorname{Idl}K(Z)\), the quotient is \(\operatorname{Idl}(h)\) for a surjective bounded lattice homomorphism \(h:K(Z)\twoheadrightarrow K'\).

**Theorem B (actual localic composition).** If \(X,Y,Z\) are coherent and \(R\hookrightarrow X\times Y\), \(S\hookrightarrow Y\times Z\) are proconstructible spectral, then \(R\circ S\) is proconstructible spectral and, on points,

\[
\operatorname{pt}(R\circ S)=
\{(x,z):\exists y\ ((x,y)\in\operatorname{pt}(R),\ (y,z)\in\operatorname{pt}(S))\}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Within this class, localic composition **associates**, the diagonal is its identity, converse reverses composition, and composition distributes over **finite** sublocale joins.

**Theorem C (hulls).** If \(S\) is any **compact sublocale** of a coherent locale \(Z\), then \(\operatorname{fit}(S)\) is proconstructible spectral (even if \(S\) is not spatial). For a proconstructible spectral \(R\hookrightarrow X^2\), both its closure and its fitting stay in the class and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cl}(R)=\mathcal A\circ R\circ\mathcal B,
\qquad \operatorname{fit}(R)=\mathcal B\circ R\circ\mathcal A,}
\tag{0.3}
\]

where \(\mathcal A\) is the canonical specialization sublocale and \(\mathcal B=\mathcal A^{\smile}\).

**Corollary D (the named relations).** For coherent \(X\), with \(\Delta\) the diagonal, \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}\Delta\), \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta\), and \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{fit}\mathcal I\), the following hold as **genuine sublocale equalities**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I&=\mathcal A\mathcal B,&\mathcal D&=\mathcal B\mathcal A,\\
\mathcal I\mathcal D&=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A,&
\mathcal D\mathcal I&=\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B,\\
\mathcal I^2&=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B,&
\mathcal V=\mathcal D^2&=\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.4}
\]

Using the **separate previously audited coherent identity** \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)\), (0.3) additionally yields \(\mathcal U=\mathcal I^2=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B\). Precise finite comparability-word identities are given in §7.

## 1. Ideal completion and genuine localic pullbacks

For a bounded distributive lattice \(K\), \(\operatorname{Idl}K\) is its frame of ideals, with principal ideals \(\downarrow k\). There is a natural adjunction

\[
\mathbf{Frm}(\operatorname{Idl}K,M)
\cong \mathbf{DLat}(K,U(M)),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where \(U(M)\) is the underlying bounded distributive lattice of a frame. Explicitly, a bounded lattice homomorphism \(h:K\to U(M)\) has the unique extension

\[
\widehat h(J)=\bigvee_{j\in J}h(j).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The expression preserves arbitrary joins because ideal joins are generated by finite joins; it preserves binary meets by distributivity, since

\[
\widehat h(J)\wedge\widehat h(J')
=\bigvee_{j\in J,j'\in J'}h(j\wedge j')
=\bigvee_{k\in J\cap J'}h(k).
\]

It preserves the top because \(h(1)=1\), and uniquely extends \(h\) because an ideal is the join of its principal ideals. Consequently \(\operatorname{Idl}:\mathbf{DLat}\to\mathbf{Frm}\) is a **left adjoint** and preserves all colimits, notably lattice coproducts and pushouts.

Every compact-preserving frame homomorphism between coherent frames is the ideal completion of its restriction to compact elements: write each ideal as the join of its principal ideals.

**Pullback lemma.** If \(f:X\to Z\) and \(g:Y\to Z\) are spectral maps of coherent locales, then

\[
\mathcal O(X\times_ZY)
\cong\operatorname{Idl}\bigl(K(X)\amalg_{K(Z)}K(Y)\bigr),
\tag{1.3}
\]

where \(\amalg\) is the pushout in \(\mathbf{DLat}\). This is an **actual frame pushout**, by (1.1), and hence an actual pullback in **all** of \(\mathbf{Loc}\); there is no restriction on the locale against which its universal property is tested.

Classically, bounded lattice maps from the pushout to \(2\) are exactly pairs of prime-filter points agreeing on \(K(Z)\). The compact-open generators give the subspace topology of the usual spectral-space fiber product. In particular, finite products of coherent locales are coherent and their point spaces are the corresponding spectral-space products. The patch topology of a finite spectral product is the product of the respective patch topologies.

We also need the elementary fact that \(\operatorname{Idl}\) preserves **injective** and **surjective** bounded lattice maps. In the injective case, \(h:K\hookrightarrow K'\) is an order embedding, so distinct ideals are distinguished by the inverse images of their generated ideals; in the surjective case every ideal of \(K'\) is generated by images of elements of \(K\). Accordingly the image factorization of a spectral map can be computed in bounded lattices and then transported to frames.

## 2. The intrinsic characterization and patch-closed subspaces

Let \(Z\) be coherent, \(K=K(Z)\), and \(q:\operatorname{Idl}K\twoheadrightarrow M\) be a sublocale quotient.

**Proof of Theorem A, (2)\(\Rightarrow\)(3).** If \(q\) preserves compact elements, every element of \(M\) is a join of elements \(q(k)\), \(k\in K\), because \(q\) is a frame surjection and the compact elements generate \(\operatorname{Idl}K\). A compact element of \(M\) is below a finite join of such \(q(k)\)'s, and that finite join is itself a \(q(k)\); hence every compact element is in \(q[K]\). The latter is closed under finite joins and meets and contains \(0,1\). Thus \(M\) is coherent, \(K(M)=q[K]\), and \(q=\operatorname{Idl}(q|_K)\) with \(q|_K:K\twoheadrightarrow q[K]\).

**Patch-closed realization.** Let \(S\subseteq P_Z\) be patch closed. Its patch topology is compact Hausdorff. The traces \(k\cap S\), \(k\in K\), are compact open in the spectral subspace topology: patch compactness implies compactness for that coarser topology. They form a basis; every compact open of \(S\) is a finite union of such traces, hence another trace. Thus

\[
K\twoheadrightarrow K(S),\qquad k\longmapsto k\cap S
\tag{2.1}
\]

is a surjective bounded lattice map. We must justify that its spectral spectrum has exactly the points of \(S\), not merely some points with the same specialization order. A prime filter \(F_p\) on \(K\) for \(p\notin S\) is not saturated for the kernel of (2.1): since \(S\) is patch closed, there are compact opens \(c,d\) with \(p\in c\setminus d\) and \((c\setminus d)\cap S=\varnothing\); then \(c\cap S=(c\wedge d)\cap S\), but \(c\in F_p\) and \(c\wedge d\notin F_p\). Conversely points of \(S\) plainly satisfy every congruence of (2.1). This identifies the spectrum of \(K(S)\) with \(S\), carrying its spectral subspace topology. The quotient \(\operatorname{Idl}(K)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Idl}(K(S))\) is the inclusion of a genuine sublocale.

**Conversely**, if \(q=\operatorname{Idl}(h)\) for surjective \(h:K\twoheadrightarrow K'\), its spectral points are exactly the prime filters of \(K\) saturated under \(\ker h\). This is a patch-closed subset: for each pair \(a\equiv_{\ker h}b\), the condition \(a\in F\iff b\in F\) is patch closed, and take the intersection over all such pairs. Its induced spectral topology is the spectrum of \(K'\). Therefore (3)\(\Rightarrow\)(1) and, by (2.1), (1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2). This completes Theorem A.

**Consequence.** Proconstructible spectral sublocales of \(Z\) correspond to bounded lattice congruences on \(K(Z)\), with their inclusion order reverse to congruence inclusion. In particular, **these** sublocales are determined by their patch-closed point sets; this assertion is not made for arbitrary sublocales.

Some useful closure properties follow. Intersections of proconstructible sublocales are again proconstructible (the corresponding congruences are joined in the lattice of congruences); finite joins are represented by finite unions of patch-closed subsets. To verify the finite-join statement at the level of localic images, note that the image of a coproduct map \([f_1,f_2]:S_1\sqcup S_2\to Z\) is the join of the images: a sublocale contains it iff both \(f_1,f_2\) factor through that sublocale. Its factorization through the spectral subspace on \(S_1\cup S_2\) is a locale surjection, since it is onto the points of that spatial target. **Arbitrary sublocale joins need not remain in the class.**

## 3. Spectral images and the relation-composition theorem

**Spectral image lemma.** Let \(f:T\to Z\) be a spectral map between coherent locales, with spectral point map also written \(f\). The subset \(C=f(P_T)\subseteq P_Z\) is patch closed: spectral maps are patch-continuous, and the image of compact \(P_T^{\mathrm{patch}}\) in Hausdorff \(P_Z^{\mathrm{patch}}\) is closed. By §2, \(C\) defines a spectral sublocale \(C\hookrightarrow Z\).

The factorization \(T\to C\hookrightarrow Z\) is the **localic image**. Indeed, its first map is onto the ordinary points of the *spatial target* \(C\), so \(\mathcal O(C)\to\mathcal O(T)\) is injective: different opens of \(C\) are distinguished by a point and then by a point of \(T\) over it. Thus the first factor is a locale surjection. Uniqueness of the surjection–sublocale factorization identifies \(C\) with the actual image. This argument never claims that an arbitrary localic image has exactly the ordinary image points of its source.

Now let \(R\hookrightarrow X\times Y\), \(S\hookrightarrow Y\times Z\) be proconstructible spectral relations. The actual pullback \(R\times_Y S\) is the actual meet

\[
\pi_{12}^{-1}(R)\wedge\pi_{23}^{-1}(S)
\hookrightarrow X\times Y\times Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

To see (3.1) directly, a map into the triple product factors through the meet iff its first two coordinates factor through \(R\) and its last two coordinates factor through \(S\). The two factorizations agree on \(Y\), and this gives precisely the universal property of the pullback. The argument works in any finitely complete category with subobjects.

All inclusions and projections here are spectral: §2 presents each proconstructible inclusion as an \(\operatorname{Idl}\) of a lattice quotient, and §1 handles the products. The pullback is therefore the proconstructible spectral sublocale on the patch-closed set

\[
E_{R,S}=\{(x,y,z):(x,y)\in R_{\mathrm{pt}},\ (y,z)\in S_{\mathrm{pt}}\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For readers wanting a strictly algebraic embedding check, the map from the compact-open lattice of \(X\times Y\times Z\) to the lattice pushout \(K(R)\amalg_{K(Y)}K(S)\) is **surjective**: the pushout is generated by the images of \(K(R),K(S)\), which in turn are generated by the compact opens of the coordinate products. Hence the map \(R\times_Y S\to X\times Y\times Z\) is a spectral sublocale embedding, not merely a spectral map with those points.

The endpoint projection \(E_{R,S}\to X\times Z\) is spectral. Applying the spectral image lemma yields the proconstructible sublocale whose point set is (0.2). This proves the binary part of **Theorem B**.

For three such relations, both parentheses consist of two successive applications of the binary theorem and are proconstructible with the same point set of four-coordinate paths. They are therefore equal by Theorem A. The diagonal is the image of the spectral diagonal map and is the identity under (0.2); coordinate swap gives converse; finite joins are finite point-set unions, so ordinary finite distributivity gives finite sublocale distributivity. **Theorem B is proved.**

*Categorical boundary.* There is no appeal here to global regularity of \(\mathbf{Loc}\). Outside the spectral/proconstructible class the image surjections used in relation composition may not be pullback-stable, so reassociation can fail even for endorelations of one locale. The present calculation certifies only finite words that stay in the class.

## 4. The specialization sublocale is genuinely spectral

For any locale \(X\), define the canonical specialization sublocale \(\mathcal A\hookrightarrow X^2\) by requiring

\[
\pi_1^*(u)\le\pi_2^*(u)\quad(u\in\mathcal O(X)).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For opens \(a,b\) of a locale, the largest sublocale satisfying \(a\le b\) is

\[
[a\le b]=\mathfrak c(a)\vee\mathfrak o(b).
\tag{4.2}
\]

One direct verification uses the nucleus \(j(t)=(a\vee t)\wedge(b\to t)\) for (4.2): a locale map factors through it precisely when its inverse images of \(a,b\) satisfy the inequality. Hence \(\mathcal A=\bigwedge_u[u_1\le u_2]\), and its generalized points classify specialization pairs. Put \(\mathcal B=\mathcal A^{\smile}\).

Assume \(X\) coherent. It suffices to impose (4.1) for compact \(k\in K(X)\), because every open is a join of compact opens and inverse-image frame maps preserve joins. Let \(M=K(X\times X)\), the bounded lattice coproduct of the two copies of \(K(X)\). Form the lattice quotient

\[
h:M\twoheadrightarrow M'
\tag{4.3}
\]

by the congruence generated by \(k_1=k_1\wedge k_2\) for all compact \(k\), where \(k_i\) is the copy of \(k\) in coordinate \(i\). By (1.1), maps from \(\operatorname{Idl}(M')\) into **arbitrary frames** correspond exactly to maps from \(\operatorname{Idl}(M)\) satisfying (4.1). Therefore the **presented** \(\mathcal A\) has quotient frame map \(\operatorname{Idl}(h)\). It preserves compact elements and belongs to the proconstructible class by Theorem A.

A pair of spectral points \((x,y)\) factors through this quotient iff every compact open containing \(x\) contains \(y\), equivalently \(x\le y\). Thus \(\mathcal A\) is **the actual proconstructible spectral sublocale** on the graph of the specialization order. Likewise \(\mathcal B\) is the opposite graph.

There is no unsupported inference “same points therefore same sublocale”: compact preservation of the *presented* quotient is the indispensable additional step. Since \(\le\) is reflexive and transitive on spectral points, Theorem B gives

\[
\mathcal A^2=\mathcal A,\qquad
\mathcal B^2=\mathcal B,\qquad
\Delta\subseteq\mathcal A,\mathcal B.
\tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. The compact-open-filter lemma for fitted hulls

This section supplies the key formerly missing proof that \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta\) is a *spectral sublocale*, rather than relying only on its points.

**Lemma 5.1 (compact fitted hull).** Let \(Z\) be coherent, \(K=K(Z)\), and \(S\hookrightarrow Z\) a **compact sublocale**, not necessarily spatial. Define the filter

\[
\Phi_S=\{k\in K:S\subseteq\mathfrak o(k)\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then \(\operatorname{fit}(S)\) is the spectral sublocale whose compact-open lattice is \(K/\equiv_{\Phi_S}\), where

\[
a\equiv_{\Phi_S}b
\iff (\exists f\in\Phi_S)\; a\wedge f=b\wedge f.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Here \(\mathfrak o(k)\) denotes the open sublocale determined by \(k\).

*Proof, neighborhood reduction.* \(\Phi_S\) contains \(1\), is upward closed in \(K\), and is closed under finite meets because intersections of open sublocales correspond to meets of opens. Every open neighborhood \(\mathfrak o(u)\) of \(S\) contains an open \(\mathfrak o(k)\) with \(k\in\Phi_S\) and \(k\le u\): write \(u\) as a join of compact opens and use compactness of the top of \(\mathcal O(S)\) to reduce its pullback to a **finite** join. Hence

\[
\operatorname{fit}(S)=\bigwedge_{k\in\Phi_S}\mathfrak o(k).
\tag{5.3}
\]

*Proof, congruences.* Let \(\theta_k\) be the **frame congruence** defining \(\mathfrak o(k)\):

\[
u\,\theta_k\,v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\wedge k=v\wedge k.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The meet of the sublocales in (5.3) corresponds to the join \(\Theta=\bigvee_{k\in\Phi_S}\theta_k\) of these frame congruences. Formula (5.2) defines a bounded lattice congruence: reflexivity takes \(f=1\); transitivity combines witnesses by their meet; finite lattice operations preserve the witnessed equality. Let \(\Theta'\) be the frame congruence generated by (5.2). We prove \(\Theta=\Theta'\).

**First inclusion \(\Theta'\subseteq\Theta\).** If \(a\equiv_{\Phi_S}b\), a witness \(f\in\Phi_S\) gives \(a\wedge f=b\wedge f\), so \((a,b)\in\theta_f\subseteq\Theta\). This covers the lattice generators and hence the frame congruence they generate.

**Reverse inclusion \(\Theta\subseteq\Theta'\).** Fix \(k\in\Phi_S\) and opens \(u,v\) satisfying \(u\wedge k=v\wedge k\). Given compact \(a\le u\), use algebraicity to write \(v=\bigvee_{b\le v,\ b\in K} b\). Since \(a\wedge k\) is compact and

\[
a\wedge k\le v\wedge k
=\bigvee_{b\le v,\ b\in K}(b\wedge k),
\]

there is a compact \(b\le v\) such that \(a\wedge k\le b\wedge k\). Thus

\[
a\wedge k=(a\wedge b)\wedge k,
\]

so \(a\equiv_{\Phi_S}a\wedge b\) and hence \(a\,\Theta'\,(a\wedge b)\), where \(a\wedge b\le u\wedge v\). Joining these equalities over all compact \(a\le u\) yields \(u\,\Theta'\,w\) for some \(w\le u\wedge v\). Congruence classes of a lattice/frame are order-convex (alternatively, take the meet with \(u\wedge v\)); because \(w\le u\wedge v\le u\), we get \(u\,\Theta'\,(u\wedge v)\). Symmetrically \(v\,\Theta'\,(u\wedge v)\), so \(u\,\Theta'\,v\). Thus \(\theta_k\subseteq\Theta'\) for every \(k\) and \(\Theta\subseteq\Theta'\).

We have shown \(\Theta=\Theta'\). By the ideal-completion adjunction, the frame quotient of \(\operatorname{Idl}(K)\) by \(\Theta'\) is \(\operatorname{Idl}(K/\equiv_{\Phi_S})\), with quotient map the ideal completion of the lattice quotient. It preserves compact elements. Theorem A therefore shows that \(\operatorname{fit}(S)\) is proconstructible spectral. **Lemma 5.1 is proved.**

*Point description when appropriate.* A prime filter \(F\subseteq K\) descends to \(K/\equiv_{\Phi_S}\) iff it contains \(\Phi_S\). Necessity follows by comparing \(f\in\Phi_S\) with \(1\) in (5.2), and sufficiency follows by meeting both sides of each witnessed equality with its witness \(f\in F\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{pt}(\operatorname{fit}S)
=\bigcap_{k\in\Phi_S}k.
\tag{5.5}
\]

If \(S\) is itself proconstructible spectral with patch-closed point set \(T\), (5.5) is the **upward specialization saturation** \(\uparrow T\): the intersection of all open neighborhoods of a set is its saturation; compactness of \(S\) allows compact-open neighborhoods to suffice. For a general compact **nonspatial** \(S\), no description of its fitted hull solely in terms of points of \(S\) is asserted.

**Corollary 5.2.** If \(X\) is coherent, the diagonal \(\Delta\hookrightarrow X^2\) is compact, because it is isomorphic to the compact locale \(X\). Thus \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta\) is proconstructible spectral; its points are

\[
\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal D)
=\{(x,y):\exists z\;(z\le x\ \text{and}\ z\le y)\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Similarly, \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}\Delta\) is a **closed** sublocale of the coherent compact locale \(X^2\), hence compact; Lemma 5.1 applies to \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{fit}\mathcal I\) as well. Its point set is \(\uparrow\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal I)\).

## 6. Closure, fitting, and the two-sided hull formulas

**Lemma 6.1 (closure of a patch-closed subset).** If \(T\) is patch closed in the spectral point space \(P_Z\) of coherent \(Z\), then the point set of the localic closure of its associated sublocale is

\[
\operatorname{pt}(\operatorname{cl}T)=\downarrow T
:=\{p:\exists t\in T,\ p\le t\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

*Proof.* Since \(p\le t\) means every open containing \(p\) contains \(t\), \(\downarrow T\) lies in the ordinary closure of \(T\). Conversely suppose \(p\notin\downarrow T\). For every \(t\in T\), \(p\not\le t\); choose a compact open \(U_t\ni p\) with \(t\notin U_t\) (compact opens determine specialization). The patch-open complements \(P_Z\setminus U_t\) cover compact \(T\); choose finitely many. Their associated intersection \(U=\bigcap U_{t_i}\) is an open neighborhood of \(p\) disjoint from \(T\). Therefore \(p\) is not in the ordinary closure. Since \(T\) is a spectral sublocale in a spatial ambient locale, its localic closure is the closed sublocale on precisely this ordinary closure. ∎

A closed sublocale \(\mathfrak c(a)\) of a coherent locale is itself proconstructible spectral: its quotient \(u\mapsto u\vee a\) sends compact \(k\) to an element compact in the quotient frame \(\uparrow a\). Indeed, if \(k\vee a\) lies below a directed join of elements \(\ge a\), compactness of \(k\) already gives one directed member above \(k\vee a\). This uses Theorem A.

**Lemma 6.2 (fitting of a proconstructible sublocale).** If \(T\) is patch closed, its associated sublocale is coherent and hence compact. By Lemma 5.1 its fitting is spectral, and (5.5) gives

\[
\operatorname{pt}(\operatorname{fit}T)=\uparrow T
:=\{p:\exists t\in T,\ t\le p\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Here \(\uparrow T\) is patch closed: it is the projection of the patch-closed subset \(\{(t,p):t\in T,\ t\le p\}\) of the compact patch product, because specialization is patch closed. Likewise \(\downarrow T\) is patch closed.

Now specialize to \(Z=X^2\), with \(\mathcal A\) the graph of \(\le\), \(\mathcal B=\mathcal A^\smile\). If \(R\) has patch-closed point set \(T\), then by pointwise composition and the specialization convention

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal A\circ R\circ\mathcal B)
&=\{(x,y):\exists(x',y')\in T,\ x\le x',\ y\le y'\}
=\downarrow T,\\
\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal B\circ R\circ\mathcal A)
&=\{(x,y):\exists(x',y')\in T,\ x'\le x,\ y'\le y\}
=\uparrow T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

All four sublocales are members of the proconstructible class, by Theorem B and Lemmas 6.1–6.2. Equality of their patch-closed point sets therefore **does** imply sublocale equality by Theorem A. This proves (0.3) and hence **Theorem C**.

## 7. Applications to Project A: diagonal hulls and finite words

Since \(\Delta\) is proconstructible (it is the image of the spectral diagonal \(X\to X^2\)), the two hull formulas give

\[
\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}\Delta
=\mathcal A\Delta\mathcal B=\mathcal A\mathcal B,
\qquad
\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta
=\mathcal B\Delta\mathcal A=\mathcal B\mathcal A.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The relevant point descriptions, now justified at the sublocale level, are

\[
\begin{aligned}
(x,y)\in\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal I)
&\iff\exists z\ (x\le z\ \&\ y\le z)
&&\text{(common generization)},\\
(x,y)\in\operatorname{pt}(\mathcal D)
&\iff\exists z\ (z\le x\ \&\ z\le y)
&&\text{(common specialization)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is also a direct check of the first equality: \(\operatorname{cl}\Delta\) has the ordinary closure of the diagonal as its point set; \((x,y)\) belongs to that closure precisely when every pair of compact-open neighborhoods meets, so the associated filters generate a proper filter and admit a common prime-filter extension \(z\), giving \(x,y\le z\).

Applying (0.3) again gives

\[
\mathcal V=\operatorname{fit}(\mathcal I)
=\mathcal B(\mathcal A\mathcal B)\mathcal A
=\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A
=\mathcal D^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Likewise,

\[
\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)
=\mathcal A(\mathcal B\mathcal A)\mathcal B
=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B
=\mathcal I^2.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Equation (7.4) is a theorem about **the closure of \(\mathcal D\)**, established here. To replace the left-hand side by Project A's independently defined Urysohn relation \(\mathcal U\), invoke the **separate coherent result** \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D\). We then recover \(\mathcal U=\mathcal I^2\) without any global associativity assumption in \(\mathbf{Loc}\).

By Theorem B and idempotence (4.4),

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\mathcal I\mathcal D=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A,&
\mathcal D\mathcal I=\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B,&
\mathcal I^2=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B,\\
\mathcal D^2=\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A,&
\mathcal I\vee\mathcal D=\mathcal A\mathcal B\vee\mathcal B\mathcal A.&
\end{array}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Let \(\mathcal C=\mathcal A\vee\mathcal B\) and \(\mathcal J=\mathcal I\vee\mathcal D\). Because \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\) are reflexive and idempotent, their finite words can be shortened by \(\mathcal A^2=\mathcal A\), \(\mathcal B^2=\mathcal B\), and shorter alternating words can be padded by reflexivity. Finite distributivity gives

\[
\mathcal C^2=\mathcal A\mathcal B\vee\mathcal B\mathcal A=\mathcal J,
\quad
\mathcal C^3=\mathcal A\mathcal B\mathcal A\vee\mathcal B\mathcal A\mathcal B,
\tag{7.6}
\]

\[
\mathcal C^4=\mathcal I^2\vee\mathcal D^2,
\quad
\mathcal J^2=\mathcal C^4=\mathcal I^2\vee\mathcal D^2.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus, using the separate relation identification \(\mathcal U=\mathcal I^2\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C^4=\mathcal J^2=\mathcal U\vee\mathcal V.}
\tag{7.8}
\]

For every finite \(m\ge1\), let \(w_{\mathcal A}(m)\) and \(w_{\mathcal B}(m)\) be the length-\(m\) fully alternating words starting respectively with \(\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal B\). The same cancellation/padding argument gives the general exact identity

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C^m=w_{\mathcal A}(m)\vee w_{\mathcal B}(m).}
\tag{7.9}
\]

For even \(m=2n\), this is \((\mathcal A\mathcal B)^n\vee(\mathcal B\mathcal A)^n=\mathcal I^n\vee\mathcal D^n\). The formula is confined to **finite** powers. It does not assert that a limit-stage union is itself a proconstructible sublocale.

**A word about word equality.** Cancelling \(\mathcal A^2\) and \(\mathcal B^2\) proves formal equalities. It is not an *if and only if* criterion for equality in a particular coherent locale: for a one-point locale \(\mathcal A=\mathcal B=\Delta\), so formally different words coincide.

## 8. Boundaries, examples, and dependencies

1. **Arbitrary \(\mathbf{Loc}\).** Relation composition by localic image of pullback is not universally associative; the project has an explicit same-locale symmetric reflexive counterexample in `A-relational-composition-associativity-audit-I-2026-11-07.md`. The coherent theorem works because it computes the pullbacks and images of a restricted class. A general closed or fitted relation on a noncoherent locale is not automatically in that class.
2. **Infinite joins.** The patch-closed class is closed under finite unions and arbitrary intersections of point sets, but not arbitrary unions. For a Stone space \(\omega+1\), the sublocale join of its isolated point sublocales can be the open sublocale of \(\omega\), whereas their patch closure also contains the limit point. Therefore a transfinite hull recursion needs an explicit limit-stage argument; it cannot rely on (7.9) beyond finite stages.
3. **Noncompact sources.** Patch-closed spectral images are compact in the patch topology. In noncompact spectral-like spaces the projected set need not be patch closed, so our spectral image lemma does not extend simply by deleting compactness from coherence.
4. **Compact fitted hull, not arbitrary fitted hull.** Lemma 5.1 assumes \(S\) compact. It makes no assertion that the fitting of an arbitrary noncompact sublocale is proconstructible.
5. **Constructivity.** The proof of pointwise formulas uses classical spectral representation and the prime ideal theorem. The referee proposed a separate route through regularity of \(\mathbf{DLat}^{op}\) for a constructive extension, but its required constructive input was not verified in this audit. **No constructive version of Theorem B is claimed here.**
6. **Beyond coherence.** The referee's possible stably compact/general proper-map extension and allegory reformulation are recorded in their report, but are not incorporated as proved assertions. They should not enlarge the current foundation without a separate check.
7. **Provenance of \(\mathcal U\).** Within this note the identity \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)\) is an established **external input** from Project A's earlier coherent research. The localic image calculus itself gives \(\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)=\mathcal I^2\) directly.
