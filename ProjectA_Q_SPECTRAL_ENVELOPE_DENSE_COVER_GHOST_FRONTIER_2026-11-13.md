# Project A — finite-cover spectral envelope and the nowhere-dense ghost boundary

**13 November 2026 (America/Denver); NEW INTERNAL proof/interpretation, NOT externally audited.** This follows the [coverwise shrinkability / relative-tail diagnostic](ProjectA_Q_COVERWISE_SHRINKABILITY_AND_RELATIVE_TAIL_BAIRE_2026-11-13.md). The point is to put finite-cover fitting and the infinitary density gap in the same **compact coherent envelope**, retaining the distinction between ordinary locale points and prime-filter ghosts. No claim to originality of the ideal-completion construction or prime ideal theorem; **universal Q/Q′ remain OPEN**.

## 0. Standing definitions

Let \(X\) be a locale, \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), and \(P=L\oplus L\). For each cover \(C\subseteq L\), \(\bigvee C=1\), let
\[
w_C=\bigvee_{c\in C}c\boxtimes c,\qquad
D(X)=\bigwedge_{C\text{ cover}}\mathfrak o(w_C),\qquad
D_2(X)=\bigwedge_{F\text{ finite cover}}\mathfrak o(w_F).
\]
The meet is in the **sublocale lattice**. Their closed hulls are \(W=\operatorname{cl}D\) and \(W_2=\operatorname{cl}D_2\). The Q′ problem is whether \(W=W_2\). A *finite-compatible pair* is a pair of frame maps \(h,k:L\to M\) into a target frame M such that \(\bigvee_{c\in F}h(c)\wedge k(c)=1_M\) for all finite L-covers F.

## 1. Canonical compact coherent envelope

Let \(L_{\mathrm{lat}}\) be the bounded distributive-lattice reduct of L. Put
\[
\widehat L=\operatorname{Idl}(L_{\mathrm{lat}}),
\]
the lattice of lower, finite-join-closed ideals of \(L_{\mathrm{lat}}\). The compact elements of this frame are precisely the principal ideals \(\downarrow a\), \(a\in L\), and they are closed under finite meets. Hence \(\widehat X=\operatorname{Loc}(\widehat L)\) is **compact coherent**.

**Proposition 1.1 (canonical dense embedding).** The map
\[
j:\widehat L\to L,\qquad j(I)=\bigvee_{a\in I}a \tag{1.1}
\]
is a surjective frame homomorphism, reflecting zero. Thus the corresponding locale map
\[
i:X\hookrightarrow\widehat X \tag{1.2}
\]
is a **dense sublocale embedding**.

*Proof.* A join of ideals is generated by finite joins of members of their union, so j preserves arbitrary joins. For ideals I,J,
\[
j(I)\wedge j(J)=\bigvee_{a\in I,b\in J}(a\wedge b)=\bigvee(I\cap J)=j(I\cap J).
\]
Surjectivity follows from \(j(\downarrow a)=a\). If \(j(I)=0\), all members of I are zero, hence \(I=\downarrow0\); this is exactly density of i. □

For any X-cover C put
\[
\widehat u_C=\bigvee_{c\in C}\downarrow c\in\widehat L. \tag{1.3}
\]

**Proposition 1.2 (infinitary covers become proper dense opens).** The open \(\widehat u_C\) is always dense in \(\widehat X\), and
\[
\boxed{\widehat u_C=1_{\widehat L}\iff C\text{ has a finite subcover of }X.} \tag{1.4}
\]

*Proof.* Its restriction along i is \(j(\widehat u_C)=\bigvee C=1_L\). A nonzero open of \(\widehat X\) disjoint from \(\widehat u_C\) would restrict to zero on dense X, impossible; so \(\widehat u_C\) is dense. The top ideal \(\downarrow1=L\) equals the ideal generated by C iff 1 is below a finite join of C-members. □

In particular a genuinely infinitary X-cover becomes a **proper dense open** of a compact coherent locale. Its closed complement \(\mathfrak c_{\widehat X}(\widehat u_C)\) is **nowhere dense**. This is an exact source-side category picture, not yet the necessary density *inside \(W_2\)*.

## 2. Exact finite-diagonal pullback

**Theorem 2.1 (coherent-envelope presentation).**
\[
\boxed{D_2(X)=(i\times i)^{-1}D(\widehat X)} \tag{2.1}
\]
as sublocales of \(X^2\).

*Proof by arbitrary generalized pairs.* For a locale T and f,g:T→X, write \(h=f^*,k=g^*:L→\mathcal O(T)\). Their composites with i have inverse-image maps
\[
\widehat h(I)=\bigvee_{a\in I}h(a)=h(j(I)),\qquad
\widehat k(I)=\bigvee_{a\in I}k(a).
\]
If the h,k pair respects all finite X-covers, let \(\mathscr U\) be any cover of \(\widehat X\). By compactness it has a finite subcover, and by coherence it is refined by a **finite** family of compact basic opens \(\downarrow a_j\) covering \(\widehat X\). Thus \(\bigvee_j a_j=1_L\). The matching value on \(\mathscr U\) dominates \(\bigvee_j h(a_j)\wedge k(a_j)=1\). Hence \(\widehat h,\widehat k\) respect every \(\widehat X\)-cover.

Conversely, a finite X-cover \(\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) gives a finite \(\widehat X\)-cover \(\{\downarrow a_1,\ldots,\downarrow a_n\}\). Full compatibility of the composites implies finite compatibility of h,k. This equivalence for every T is the pullback assertion (2.1). □

**Caution.** (2.1) is about the **entire fitted sublocale**, not an equality of its ordinary-point sets. Neither \(D_2\) nor its pullback is assumed spatial. It does **not** give \(D(X)=D_2(X)\) or \(W=W_2\).

## 3. Binary-cover reduction and common prime predecessors

A useful auxiliary fact is that **binary covers already test finite compatibility**.

**Lemma 3.1.** A pair of frame maps h,k into any target frame M respects every finite L-cover iff it respects every binary L-cover.

*Proof sketch.* A failure for \(F=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) has a nonzero closed quotient of M on which all \(h(a_i)\wedge k(a_i)\) vanish. Pass to its nonzero Booleanization and choose an ordinary Stone ultrafilter. Evaluations are bounded-distributive-lattice homomorphisms to 2, with nonempty index sets \(I_h=\{i:h(a_i)=1\}\) and \(I_k=\{i:k(a_i)=1\}\), but \(I_h\cap I_k=\varnothing\). Group F into the binary cover
\[
u=\bigvee_{i\in I_h}a_i,\qquad v=\bigvee_{i\notin I_h}a_i.
\]
At the chosen Stone ultrafilter the h-coordinate takes (u,v) to (1,0), the k-coordinate to (0,1), so binary matching fails. This contradicts the hypothesis. The reduction is purely finite; no ordinary points of X or spatial-square assumption are involved. □

Now let P,Q be **prime filters of the bounded distributive lattice** \(L_{\mathrm{lat}}\), not necessarily *completely* prime. Set \(I=L\setminus P\) and \(J=L\setminus Q\), prime ideals.

**Proposition 3.2 (prime predecessor criterion; classical prime ideal theorem).** The following are equivalent:
1. for every finite L-cover F, \(F\cap P\cap Q\ne\varnothing\);
2. no \(a\in I,b\in J\) satisfy \(a\vee b=1\);
3. the ideal generated by \(I\cup J\) is proper;
4. there exists a **prime filter** \(R\subseteq P\cap Q\).

*Proof.* Failure of (1) partitions a finite cover among I and J; joining terms in each group gives \(a\in I,b\in J\) with \(a\vee b=1\). Conversely such a,b form a bad binary cover. This proves (1)⇔(2)⇔(3). If the join ideal is proper, extend it to a prime ideal K by the prime ideal theorem, and put \(R=L\setminus K\). Conversely any R as in (4) has its complementary prime ideal containing I,J, so their join is proper. □

Such prime filters are **ordinary points of the spectral envelope** \(\widehat X\). A pair of completely prime filters (ordinary locale points of X) cannot fail an arbitrary X-cover if finite-compatible: if C had no common member, partition C into terms belonging to the first completely prime filter and its complement. Their two joins form a binary X-cover separating the pair. This recovers the earlier fact that **ordinary points never witness a full-vs-finite fitted-diagonal defect**.

**Corollary 3.3 (ghost predecessor of a weak C-defect).** Suppose a Boolean target frame M has h,k:L→M that are finite-compatible but satisfy \(\bigvee_{c\in C}h(c)\wedge k(c)=0\). At every Stone ultrafilter \(\eta\) of M, the induced lattice-prime filters \(P_\eta,Q_\eta\) have a common prime predecessor \(R_\eta\) by Proposition 3.2. Every such predecessor avoids *all* \(c\in C\), hence corresponds to a spectral point in
\[
\boxed{\widehat X\setminus\widehat u_C,} \tag{3.1}
\]
the **closed nowhere-dense ghost boundary**. It cannot be completely prime, since \(\bigvee C=1_L\).

*Proof.* Since each \(h(c)\wedge k(c)=0\), no c belongs to both Pη,Qη; thus none belongs to a common subfilter. If such a filter were completely prime, it would contain some c of the frame cover C. □

This is a **pointwise spectral representation** of Boolean weak defects. It does **not** assert a locale-level existential-image/effective-descent theorem for the ternary incidence locale of predecessor triples; such a claim requires separate proof and is related to the project's earlier composition/triquotiency cautions.

## 4. Adversarial category check

It is tempting to conclude from “\(\widehat u_C\) dense in \(\widehat X\)” that the matching-square union \(\bigvee_{c\in C}(\downarrow c)\boxtimes(\downarrow c)\) is dense in \(D(\widehat X)\). **This does not follow from density alone.**

Finite countercontrol: let V be the three-point upper-Alexandrov space with a least point p below two incomparable maximal points x,y. Its nonempty open \(\{x,y\}\) is dense. The singleton opens \(\{x\},\{y\}\) cover that dense open, but their square union misses the nonzero open rectangle \(\{x\}\times\{y\}\). Every cover of V includes V itself (to cover p), so \(D(V)=V^2\). Therefore the matching-square union is **not dense in \(D(V)\)**. This is only a negative test of the naive general assertion: the dense open here is **not** being claimed to arise as the canonical \(\widehat u_C\) for an X-cover under the specific embedding from Proposition 1.1.

For Q′ the remaining question is substantially sharper: do these special, canonically induced nowhere-dense ghost boundaries admit **relatively open images in the closed hull of \(D_2(X)\)**, or does the canonical product/relation geometry force their contribution to remain nowhere dense? No general category-preservation statement for the predecessor-incidence projection has been established.

The completed Scott space demonstrates that ghost predecessors and finite/full defects can exist while **W=W₂**; the Arrieta controls likewise must be accounted for by any proposed universal argument.

## 5. Decision / status

The formalization (1.1)–(2.1) is a useful *compact-coherent normal form* for the finite theory; (3.1) locates bad-cover prime predecessors in a **nowhere-dense boundary of the envelope**. This gives a principled interface between the referee's “density inside \(D_2\)” program and the older spectral/ordered-spectral tools.

It does **not** solve the needed **relative** category-transfer step, nor justify replacing a localic relation by its ordinary points or the image of a ternary witness relation. Avoid treating these as hidden lemmas.
