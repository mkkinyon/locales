# Independent blind referee request — Project A, zero-dimensional two-sheet specialization

**Please read this prompt first, then the separate packet.** This is an independent mathematical audit. The authors have not supplied the internal audit; please evaluate the packet as if no internal checking had occurred.

## Background and motivation

Project A studies localic analogues of separation graphs by treating the canonical **oriented** specialization relation \(\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X\times X\) as a localic relation, even in spatial sober \(T_1\) spaces. Ordinary specialization on points can be equality while a nonzero pointless cross-sheet relation survives.

An earlier independent referee confirmed a compact locally compact sober \(T_1\) Cantor-gluing example where every point sublocale is fitted but \(\mathcal A(X)\ne\Delta_X\). A later follow-up identified its cross-sheet relation with a nonzero pointless sublocale. The new packet develops a broader structural result: for a class of two-sheet zero-dimensional gluings, one application of \(\mathcal A\)-saturation allegedly produces the fitting of **every sublocale**.

The theorem matters because it would delimit a large family in which open/closed-interface pathologies do not create multi-step saturation ranks, even though the canonical relation can remain nonspatial. We are at a deliberate research checkpoint and will use the audit to decide what can be consolidated.

## Scope of the review

Please check **every** assertion in the packet, including optional remarks and small examples. In particular:

1. Reconstruct the cross-sheet calculation using maps from **arbitrary test locales**, not ordinary points. Verify every direction of the generalized-point characterization and every use of clopen complements.
2. Verify that the stated hypotheses really imply all diagonal opens \((c,c)\), and that the globalization identity for \((c,v)\) stays in the gluing frame.
3. Verify the fitting calculation for an **arbitrary**, possibly pointless/nonclosed sublocale \(H\) of the first sheet. Look for covert use of point-set topology or invalid interchange of infinite lattice operations.
4. Check all ambient-vs-relative claims: open and closed sheets, fitting of a sublocale of the open sheet in the ambient locale, and decomposition of an arbitrary sublocale along the two sheets.

6. Try to falsify the theorem with a concrete small or nonspatial locale satisfying its precise hypotheses. If a claim fails, give the smallest clear counterexample or identify the exact unproved implication.
7. Report unclear notation, missing hypotheses, and opportunities to simplify, even if your verdict is positive.

Please distinguish **proved**, **repairable**, **unproved**, and **false** items. Feel free to propose a stronger statement or a more conceptual proof, but clearly separate such suggestions from your correctness verdict. No literature-priority assessment is required, though a relevant known theorem is welcome.
