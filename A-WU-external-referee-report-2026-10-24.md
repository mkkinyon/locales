**Date:** 24 October 2026. **Protocol:** cold read only. I did not consult any prior audit, reconciliation note, referee report, or the live repository. I did not consult the Arrieta–Picado–Pultr paper either; attributions to it below are therefore *unverified* and flagged as such.

Notation follows the packet: \(Q=L\oplus L\), \(\mathcal U=C(d_U)\), \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}(\Delta)\), \(\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D=C(d_W)\), \(\mathcal D_2,\mathcal W_2=C(d_{W_2})\), \(N=\uparrow d_U\), \(\beta:N\to B(N)\). I write \(\mathfrak B(Y)\) for the least dense sublocale (Booleanization) of a locale \(Y\), and \(\Phi_2(q):=\bigvee_{\mathscr C\ \text{finite cover}}(w_{\mathscr C}\to q)\).

## 1. Executive verdict

Every labelled claim A1–G4 is **correct** as stated; I found **no fatal and no major mathematical error**. Several proofs can be shortened, a few statements should be sharpened, two remarks about the examples need correction, and the \(\Psi\)-caveat in §4 can be removed entirely. The focal implication (Q) is **not settled** by the packet and I could not settle it either; what I can add is (i) a clean reformulation of both \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) and (Q) in terms of Booleanizations, (ii) a natural strengthening (Q′) that is free of \(\mathcal U\), (iii) an obstruction lemma explaining why the Scott/Cantor mechanism cannot be upgraded to a counterexample, which locates where a counterexample (if any) must come from, and (iv) a simple new control example with \(\mathcal I\subsetneq\mathcal W\subsetneq\mathcal U\).

## 2. Findings

### Fatal
None.

### Major (mathematically correct, but the packet's framing needs changing)

**M1. §4: \(\Psi\) can be replaced by a genuine pre-nucleus; the caveat is unnecessary.**
Let \(\mathscr B_2\subseteq Q\) be the filter generated by the binary neighbourhoods \(v(a,b)\). By B2, \(w_{\mathscr C}=\bigwedge_S v(a_S,b_S)\) for every finite cover \(\mathscr C\); conversely \(v(a,b)\wedge v(a',b')\ge w_{\{a\wedge a',a\wedge b',b\wedge a',b\wedge b'\}}\). Hence \(\mathscr B_2=\{w:\ w\ge w_{\mathscr C}\text{ for some finite cover }\mathscr C\}\), i.e. **\(\mathcal D_2=\bigcap_{\mathscr C\ \text{finite}}O(w_{\mathscr C})\) is the "finite-cover fitting" of \(\Delta\)**. Since \(\mathscr B_2\) is a filter (closed under finite meets), \(\Phi_2(q)=\bigvee_{w\in\mathscr B_2}(w\to q)\) is a directed join of open nuclei and is a pre-nucleus by exactly the argument that makes \(\Phi\) one. Its fixed points are \(\mathcal D_2\), \(\Phi_2(0)=d_U\) (because \(\mathscr B_2\subseteq\mathscr B_\Delta\) and \(\Phi_2(0)\ge\Psi(0)=d_U\)), and \(\Phi_2^\infty(0)=d_{W_2}\). All statements made about \(\Psi\) (C1, D1, G2, G4) go through verbatim for \(\Phi_2\), and the sentence "the binary family is not known to be closed under finite meets" should be replaced by "the filter it generates is the finite-square-cover filter". This also makes the pair \((\Phi,\Phi_2)\) perfectly symmetric, which is the right setting for (Q).

**M2. §9 is not a "nontrivial control"; it is explained by E2, exactly like §10.**
For any finite space (or any Alexandrov space in which every element of a closed set lies below a maximal element) every closed set is regular closed: closed sets are down-sets, the interior of a down-set contains its maximal elements, and the closure of the maximal elements is the whole set. So \(\mathcal U(P)\) is regular closed for every finite \(P\), and the "finite coherent result" \(\overline{\operatorname{Cm}(P)}=\operatorname{pt}\mathcal U(P)\) is just E2. For \(X=S\times P\) one has \(R_U=S^2\times\operatorname{pt}\mathcal U(P)\), \(\operatorname{int}R_U=S^2\times\operatorname{int}\mathcal U(P)\), and \(\overline{\operatorname{int}R_U}=S^2\times\overline{\operatorname{int}\mathcal U(P)}=R_U\). Hence **\(G(S\times P)=0\)**, and F3 holds by E2 alone. The packet's own closing sentence of §10 ("cannot witness failure of (Q) because \(G=0\)") applies verbatim to §9 as well. Both examples therefore say nothing about the only sector in which (Q) can fail. (The claim \(\mathcal D(X)\subsetneq\mathcal D_2(X)\) in F3 is correct and unaffected.)

**M3. D2's hypothesis is essentially never satisfied in countably-based situations.**
If \(N\) has a countable base (e.g. \(L\) second countable, hence \(Q\) and the quotient \(N\) countably based), then \(B(N)\cong\mathcal P(A)\times B_0\) with \(B_0\) atomless and carrying a countable dense subset, so \(B_0\) is the Cohen algebra \(\operatorname{RO}(2^{\mathbb N})\), which is not \((\omega,2)\)-distributive (as the packet itself notes in §8). So in that setting D2 gives nothing beyond C3/E4 (atomic case). \((\omega,2)\)-distributive atomless complete Boolean algebras do exist (completions of \(\sigma\)-closed posets, e.g. \(\operatorname{Fn}(\omega_1,2,\omega_1)\)), so D2 is not vacuous, but its range is non-separable. This should be stated so that readers do not over-estimate D2.

### Minor

**m1. B2 (finite-cut identity).** The prime-filter transport is legitimate: the sublattice of \(Q\) generated by \(\iota_1(c_i),\iota_2(c_i)\) is a homomorphic image of the distributive-lattice coproduct \(D\oplus_{\mathbf{DLat}}D\) (\(D\) the sublattice of \(L\) generated by the \(c_i\)), whose prime filters are pairs of prime filters of \(D\); an identity valid there survives to any quotient. But a direct proof avoids the issue: expand \(\bigwedge_S v(a_S,b_S)\) by distributivity into \(\bigvee_f y_f\boxtimes y_f\) with \(y_f=\bigwedge_{S}x_S\), \(x_S\in\{a_S,b_S\}\); expand each \(y_f=\bigvee_g\bigwedge_S c_{g(S)}\) in \(L\); a term \((\bigwedge_S c_{g(S)})\boxtimes(\bigwedge_S c_{g'(S)})\) with \(\operatorname{im}g\cap\operatorname{im}g'=\varnothing\) is impossible (look at the index \(S=\operatorname{im}g\): whichever of \(a_S,b_S\) was chosen, one of \(g(S),g'(S)\) lands in the wrong half), and every other term lies below some \(c_i\boxtimes c_i\). Only frame distributivity and \((p\boxtimes q)\wedge(p'\boxtimes q')=(p\wedge p')\boxtimes(q\wedge q')\) are used.

**m2. D1 is Isbell's density theorem in disguise.** The canonical pair \((h,k)\) is the composite \(L\rightrightarrows Q\twoheadrightarrow N\twoheadrightarrow B(N)\), i.e. the inclusion \(\mathfrak B(\mathcal U)\hookrightarrow\mathcal U\hookrightarrow X\times X\). Hence D1 says: \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\iff\mathcal D\) is dense in \(\mathcal U\iff\mathfrak B(\mathcal U)\subseteq\mathcal D\), and \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\iff\mathfrak B(\mathcal U)\subseteq\mathcal D_2\). Stating it this way removes the computation with \(\beta(d_U\vee w_{\mathscr C})\).

**m3. E1/E3/E4: a sharper picture of the sector \(G\).** With \(d=d_U\): \(d^{**}\in\mathcal D\) (this is the content of E1). Since sublocales are closed under \(y\mapsto y\to r\) for \(r\) in them, every element \((x\to d^{**})\to d^{**}\) lies in \(\mathcal D\); these are exactly the elements of \(\mathfrak B(O(d^*))=\mathfrak B(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)\). Moreover \(d^*\to d=d^{**}\) and \((d^{**}\to d)\to d=d^{**}\), so in \(B(N)\) the elements \(\beta(d^{**})\) and \(\beta(d\vee d^*)\) are complementary and
\[
\mathfrak B(\mathcal U)=\mathfrak B(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)\ \sqcup\ \mathfrak B(G),\qquad \mathfrak B(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)\subseteq\mathcal D\ \text{always}.
\]
So **\(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\iff\mathfrak B(G)\subseteq\mathcal D\)** and **\(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\iff\mathfrak B(G)\subseteq\mathcal D_2\)**. This is E3+E4 with the "closure commutes with restriction" step made explicit; the step itself is the coframe identity \(\operatorname{cl}(S)\cap O(u)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S\cap O(u))\), valid for every sublocale \(S\) and open \(O(u)\) in any locale (reduce to \(\operatorname{cl}S\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S\cap O(u))\vee C(u)\), whose right side is closed). The relative-frame conventions in E4 are correct: \(\mathcal O(G)\cong[d_U,d_U^{**}]\) and \(B(\mathcal O(G))=B(N){\downarrow}\beta(d_U^{**})\).

**m4. C3 and G1/G4.** The last sentence of C3 (equality of point sets) holds for every frame (take \(M=2\)); G1's "\(\operatorname{pt}\mathcal D_2=\operatorname{pt}\mathcal D\)" and the same statement in G4 therefore need no spatial-square hypothesis. (The identification with \(\operatorname{Cm}(X)\) does need it.)

**m5. G3.** The proof should say explicitly: a rectangle \(p\boxtimes q\le d_W\le d_I\) is disjoint because \(p\wedge q=\delta^*(p\boxtimes q)\le\delta^*(d_I)=0\). As written ("rectangular generation and \(d_I=\dots\) yield") the reader might try to extract a disjoint rectangle from \(d_I\) directly, which does not give \(a\boxtimes b\le d_W\).

**m6. A2 attribution.** That \(\Phi=\bigvee_{w\in\mathscr B_\Delta}(w\to-)\) is a pre-nucleus is immediate from \(\mathscr B_\Delta\) being a filter (directed join of open nuclei). I cannot confirm from the cold read that this is "Lemma 5.4" of Arrieta–Picado–Pultr, nor that "Lemma 4.4" is the cover-compatibility criterion; please check numbering against the published version (247–263) rather than the preprint.

**m7. §8 presentation claim.** Correct: the free frame on countably many generators is \(\mathcal O(\mathcal P(\mathbb N),\text{Scott})\) with generators \(\{A:n\in A\}\), and adding \(\bigvee_nc_n=1\) is passage to the open sublocale \(\mathcal P(\mathbb N)\setminus\{\varnothing\}\). Worth adding one line: the map \(f=[h,k]:\mathfrak B(2^{\mathbb N})\to S\times S\) is **dense onto** the closed sublocale \(Z=C(w_{\mathscr C})\) of disjoint pairs (\(f(r)=0\iff r\le w_{\mathscr C}\) on basic rectangles), so its image is \(\mathfrak B(Z)\) and \(B(\mathcal O(Z))\cong\operatorname{RO}(2^{\mathbb N})\). The packet's witness is thus exactly \(\mathfrak B(Z)\subseteq\mathcal D_2\setminus\mathcal D\), sitting inside a closed nowhere-dense sublocale of \(S^2\). This is the key to Section 5 below.

**m8. §10.** Verified in full (compactness, \(T_1\), local compactness, sobriety: closed sets missing \(p\) are exactly the finite sets, closed sets containing \(p\) are \(\{p\}\cup C'\) with \(C'\) closed in \(Y\sqcup Z\), and \(T_1\)-regularity of \(Y\sqcup Z\) splits any such set with \(|C'|\ge2\)). The nonzero pointless sublocales \(\mathcal D\wedge O(A\boxtimes B)\) are explained by m3: \(\mathcal D\supseteq\mathfrak B(Y\times Y)\).

## 4. Status of the focal implication (Q)

**Open.** The packet's positive cases all have the same shape: (Q) holds whenever "finite-cover compatible ⇒ cover compatible" holds for pairs \(L\to B(\mathcal O(G))\), and the only two known ways to force this are (a) \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) spatial = atomic (C3/E4) and (b) \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) \((\omega,2)\)-distributive with countable covers sufficing (D2/E4). In forcing language both say "forcing with \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) adds no new reals"; the Scott/Cantor example shows that when new reals *are* added, "finite ⇒ all" can fail for *some* pair. What is missing is any argument that the **canonical** pair (the generic point of \(\mathfrak B(G)\)) is better behaved than an arbitrary pair, or any example where it is not.

**Reformulations that may help.**

1. (Q) \(\iff\) \(\big[\mathfrak B(G)\subseteq\mathcal D_2\Rightarrow\mathfrak B(G)\subseteq\mathcal D\big]\). The regular core is irrelevant (m3).

2. **(Q′): \(\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D_2)=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D)\) for every frame**, equivalently \(d_{W_2}\in\mathcal D\), equivalently \(\mathfrak B(\mathcal W_2)\subseteq\mathcal D\), equivalently \(\Phi(d_{W_2})=d_{W_2}\). (Q′) ⇒ (Q) trivially, (Q′) mentions neither \(\mathcal U\) nor Urysohn separation, and it is symmetric in \((\Phi,\Phi_2)\). All packet examples satisfy (Q′) (both closures equal \(\mathcal U\), or both miss the same isolated point as in Section 6). I recommend attacking (Q′) first: it is the natural "universal" question, and a counterexample to (Q′) is likely easier to find than one to (Q) because it does not require arranging \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\).

3. **Second-countable test case.** If \(L\) is second countable then every cover has a countable subcover, and the atomless part of \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) is the Cohen algebra. So (Q) for second-countable \(L\) is a concrete statement about a Cohen-generic pair \((\dot x,\dot y)\) of points of \(L\): *if every ground-model finite open cover has a member containing both \(\dot x\) and \(\dot y\), must every ground-model countable cover?* This is a sharply posed set-theoretic question and a good place to look for either a proof (using properties of Cohen reals) or a counterexample.

## 5. Why the present techniques cannot produce a counterexample, and where one would have to come from

**Obstruction lemma.** *If \(a\in L\) and \(\downarrow a\) is hyperconnected (any two nonzero elements below \(a\) meet), then \(u^*=a^*\) for every \(0\neq u\le a\).* Proof: \(x\wedge u=0\Rightarrow(x\wedge a)\wedge u=0\Rightarrow x\wedge a=0\Rightarrow x\le a^*\).

Consequences. (i) For \(0\ne u\le a\): \(u\boxtimes v\le d_U\iff a^*\vee v^*=1\), so \(\mathcal U\cap O(a\boxtimes b)\) is "constant along the \(a\)-coordinate". (ii) The Scott/Cantor witness \(\mathfrak B(Z)\) lives in the closed nowhere-dense sublocale \(Z=C(w_{\mathscr C})\) of \(S^2\) (m7). To turn it into a G4-type witness one needs a frame in which, on some open rectangle \(a\boxtimes b\), \(\mathcal U\cap O(a\boxtimes b)\subseteq C(w_{\mathscr C})\), i.e. \(c_n^*\vee c_n'^*=1\) but \(c_n^*\vee c_m'^*\neq1\) for \(n\neq m\) (coordinate pieces whose generators have *distinct* pseudocomplements). By (i) this is impossible whenever the coordinate pieces are hyperconnected — and \(\mathcal O(S)\) is hyperconnected. I verified this concretely: every attempt to glue two Scott pieces through mediating points either makes \(\operatorname{Cm}\) dense again (because mediators acquire common specializations) or separates the pieces completely.

So a counterexample to (Q) or (Q′) needs a **new source of \(\mathcal D_2\neq\mathcal D\)** in a non-hyperconnected frame, not derived from the free-frame/Scott mechanism via a map \(S\to X\). Equivalently it needs a finite-cover-compatible, non-cover-compatible pair \((h,k):L\to B\) for which the generators of a failing cover have distinct pseudocomplements. **A characterization of the frames with \(\mathcal D_2\neq\mathcal D\)** is therefore the most useful intermediate target. In \(V^B\)-terms: \(F_h\cap F_k\) is a filter on \(\check L\) meeting every finite (ground) cover; the question is which such filters miss a ground cover, i.e. what "finitely cover-prime but not cover-prime" filters look like generically. Conversely, if one could show that in the *canonical* situation the filter \(F_h\cap F_k\) is forced to be \(V\)-completely prime whenever it meets all finite covers, (Q) would follow.

Two further structural facts worth recording:

- The gap between finite and infinite covers is exactly the "both coordinates in infinitely many members" phenomenon: for an infinite cover \(\mathscr C\), \(\bigwedge_{\mathscr C'\subseteq_{fin}\mathscr C}v(\bigvee\mathscr C',\bigvee(\mathscr C\setminus\mathscr C'))\) is strictly larger than \(w_{\mathscr C}\) in general (pointwise already in \(S^2\)); B2 says the two agree iff \(\mathscr C\) is finite.
- Under \(\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\), G4's region \(Y=\mathcal U\cap O(r)\subseteq C(w_{\mathscr C})\) carries opens \(o_{c,c'}=O(c\boxtimes c')\cap Y\) with \(o_{c,c}=0\), \(\bigvee o_{c,c'}=Y\), and \(\bigvee_j\bigvee_{c\neq c'\in\mathscr C_j}o_{c,c'}\) dense for every finite partition \(\mathscr C=\bigsqcup_j\mathscr C_j\). The Cohen model \(Y=\mathfrak B(Z)\), \(o_{m,n}=A_m\wedge B_n\) satisfies all of this, so no contradiction is available at this level of generality; the contradiction (if there is one) must use that \(Y\) is open in \(\mathcal U\), i.e. must use the pseudocomplement structure.

## 6. A simple new control example (\(\mathcal I\subsetneq\mathcal W\subsetneq\mathcal U\), \(G\neq0\))

Let \(X=\mathbb R\sqcup\{p,q\}\), with \(\mathbb R\) open with its usual topology, basic neighbourhoods of \(p\) the sets \(\{p\}\cup((0,\infty)\setminus F)\), and of \(q\) the sets \(\{q\}\cup((-\infty,0)\setminus F)\), \(F\) finite. Then \(X\) is \(T_1\), sober, locally compact, non-compact, and the square is spatial. The closure of \(\{p\}\cup((0,\infty)\setminus F)\) contains \([0,\infty)\cup\{p\}\), that of \(\{q\}\cup((-\infty,0)\setminus F)\) contains \((-\infty,0]\cup\{q\}\); they meet at \(0\), so \((p,q)\in R_U\), while \(p,q\) have disjoint neighbourhoods, so \((p,q)\notin\mathcal I\). Moreover \((N_p\times N_q)\cap R_U=\{(p,q)\}\) (for \(x>0>y\) the closures \([x\pm\varepsilon]\cup\{p\}\) and \([y\pm\varepsilon]\cup\{q\}\) are disjoint, and likewise for \((p,y)\), \((x,q)\)). So \((p,q)\) is an **isolated point of \(R_U\)** not in \(\overline{\operatorname{int}R_U}\supseteq\overline{(0,\infty)^2\cup(-\infty,0)^2}\): it lies in the sector \(G\). Since \(X\) is \(T_1\), \(\operatorname{pt}\mathcal D=\Delta\), so \(\mathcal D\cap O(N_p\boxtimes N_q)=0\) (it is a sublocale of the one-point sublocale \(\mathcal U\cap O(N_p\boxtimes N_q)\)), hence \(\mathcal W\cap O(N_p\boxtimes N_q)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D\cap O(N_p\boxtimes N_q))=0\): \((p,q)\notin\mathcal W\). The same applies to \(\mathcal D_2\), so \(\mathcal W_2\neq\mathcal U\) too, as (Q) predicts; here \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) is atomic (E4). Since \((0,x)\in\overline{(0,\infty)^2}\subseteq K\subseteq\mathcal W\) but \((0,x)\notin\mathcal I\), we get \(\mathcal I\subsetneq\mathcal W\subsetneq\mathcal U\). The packet currently asserts that \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) fails for some compact non-coherent frame without exhibiting one; this example (or its one-point compactification) can serve.

Products \(S\times X\) with this \(X\) give examples where additionally \(\mathcal D_2\neq\mathcal D\) and \(G\neq0\), still with atomic \(B(\mathcal O(G))\); and \(S\times\mathbb R\) gives \(G=\mathcal U\) with \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) the Cohen algebra and nevertheless \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) — showing that E4's hypotheses are far from necessary.

## 7. Assessment of the general \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) programme

What survives, and is clean enough to be the backbone of a paper:

1. \(\mathcal U=\bigcap_{w\in\mathscr B_\Delta}\operatorname{cl}O(w)\) and \(\mathcal D=\bigcap_{w\in\mathscr B_\Delta}O(w)\): \(\mathcal U\) is the "closure-wise fitting" of the diagonal. E1 is the general lemma relating the two for any family of opens, and it squeezes \(\mathcal W\) between \(\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}\mathcal U\) and \(\mathcal U\).
2. \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\iff\mathfrak B(\mathcal U)\subseteq\mathcal D\iff\mathfrak B(G)\subseteq\mathcal D\): the question is purely about the Boolean part of the non-regular boundary of \(\mathcal U\), and the fitted diagonal always swallows \(\mathfrak B(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)\) — which is why \(\mathcal D\) is "pointlessly fat" in compact examples like §10.
3. The functorial test C2 and the identification \(\mathcal D_2=\) finite-cover fitting make the binary/finite/arbitrary hierarchy conceptually transparent; \(\mathcal D_2\neq\mathcal D\) is a genuinely non-spatial phenomenon with a concrete Cohen-algebra model.
4. Sufficient conditions in decreasing generality: \(\mathcal I=\mathcal U\); \(\mathcal U\) regular closed; \(B(\mathcal O(G))\) atomic; Lindelöf + \((\omega,2)\)-distributive. The first two cover regular, finite/Alexandrov-type, and the packet's product examples; the third covers scattered boundaries.

What does not yet exist: any technique that distinguishes the canonical pair from an arbitrary pair into a Boolean target. Until one does, (Q) and (Q′) will remain open, and I would present them as conjectures with the obstruction of Section 5 as evidence that a counterexample must be genuinely new.

## 8. Bibliographic and priority cautions

- The pre-nucleus \(\Phi\) and the arbitrary-cover compatibility criterion are, per the packet, due to Arrieta–Picado–Pultr (2022). I did not consult the paper; lemma numbers should be checked against the journal version. The packet correctly makes no novelty claim for them.
- E1 is an elementary lemma about closures of intersections of opens in a locale; I would expect it to be folklore (Picado–Pultr's book treats fitting and closure side by side) and would present it as "presumably known".
