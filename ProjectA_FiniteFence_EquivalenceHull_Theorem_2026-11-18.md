# Project A — finite-fence quotient and the four-step equivalence hull

**Working theorem note, 18 November 2026.**  This consolidates the proposed
argument (60)–(67) of Session 9.  The final equality has **not** yet received an
independent external audit.  In particular, the support/Cantor-tree argument,
the dense-strip lemma, and the kernel-pair support bound below should be read
as proof obligations rather than as results established independently.

## 1. Statement and notation

Let \(P=[0,1]\) with its usual compact Hausdorff topology.  Choose three
disjoint ordered Cantor sets

\[
 K<M<L\subseteq(0,1),
\]

disjoint countable dense subsets \(A=\{a_n:n\ge1\}\) and
\(B=\{b_n:n\ge1\}\) of \(M\), and homeomorphisms
\(f:M\to K\), \(g:M\to L\).  We assume the enumerations use distinct points.

For each \(n\ge1\) choose an **even-length** polygonal zigzag fence \(P_n\)
from \(f(a_n)\) to \(a_n\) and an even-length fence \(Q_n\) from \(b_n\) to
\(g(b_n)\).  Every edge has Euclidean length less than \(1/n\).  The fence
vertices are otherwise disjoint from \(K\cup M\cup L\), and vertices of
different fences do not coincide.  All even-position vertices, including
endpoints, are minimal; all odd-position vertices are maximal.  Order exactly
the endpoints of the edges and the diagonal.  Thus every strict edge runs from
a minimal to a maximal vertex, and **there is no strict chain of length two**.
The fences may be chosen by subdividing each interval sufficiently finely,
then perturbing finitely many interior vertices to meet the disjointness
requirements.  The set of strict order pairs is closed away from the
diagonal, and lengths of edges in the tail tend to zero.  Consequently the
order is a closed partial order on \(P\).

Let \(X\) denote the locale of the **upper topology** of this compact pospace;
its patch locale is \(P\), with proper surjective patch counit
\(p:P\to X\).  All comparability components are finite fence-vertex sets or
singleton sets.  Set

\[
 E_P=\bigcup_n V(P_n),\qquad
 E_Q=\bigcup_n V(Q_n),\qquad E=E_P\cup E_Q.
\]

Let \(E_G\subseteq\operatorname{pt}(X)^2\) be the equivalence relation of
membership in a common comparability component (the components of the
undirected fence graph).  Its spatial sublocale in \(X\times X\) is

\[
 H_G=[E_G].
\]

Here \([S]\) denotes the spatial sublocale generated by the ordinary points
of \(S\); it is **not** necessarily a closed or fitted sublocale.
Define \(q:X\to Q\) by the frame of upper opens which are unions of whole
comparability components.  It coequalizes the two projections of \(H_G\).
Write

\[
 T:=H_G\circ H_G,\qquad
 \mathcal K_q:=X\times_QX,\qquad
 R_*:=\operatorname{EqHull}(H_G),
\]

where \(\circ\) is image-based **localic** relational composition.  We never
invoke associativity of arbitrary localic composition; \(T\circ T\) has the
fixed bracketing \((H_G\circ H_G)\circ(H_G\circ H_G)\).

**Proposed theorem (four-step effectiveness).**  For the finite-fence
compact-pospace construction,

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_q=T\circ T=R_*}. \tag{Thm}
\]

In particular, the locally generated equivalence hull of \(H_G\) is effective
and its quotient map is \(q\).  The qualifier *proposed* is important until the
external check of the lemmas below.

## 2. Finite-fence forcing and the generic quotient point

The following observation governs the additional, nonordinary fibre of \(q\).

**Fence-forcing lemma.**  If a Euclidean-closed, component-saturated subset
\(S\subseteq P\) contains infinitely many different fence components, then
it contains every \(P_n\) and \(Q_n\), hence all of \(E\).

**Proof.**  Suppose first that infinitely many \(P_n\)'s occur.  For any point
\(t\) in the fixed open interval between \(K\) and \(M\), each sufficiently
fine \(P_n\) running from \(K\) to \(M\) has a vertex at distance at most
\(1/n\) from \(t\).  Closedness of \(S\) yields the entire gap.  Every \(P_m\)
has a vertex in the gap, so saturation brings all \(P_m\) into \(S\).  Since
their endpoints \(a_m\) are dense in \(M\), closedness then brings \(M\), and
in particular every \(b_m\), into \(S\); saturation brings all \(Q_m\).
Starting instead with infinitely many \(Q_n\)'s is symmetric.  Among
infinitely many fence components one of the two families is infinite. ∎

An upper open \(U\) is component-saturated exactly when its Euclidean-closed
complement is component-saturated.  Hence a saturated upper open either meets
no fence vertex, or contains all but finitely many fence components.  There is
therefore a completely prime filter

\[
 p_\infty=\{U\in\mathcal O(Q):U\cap E\ne\varnothing\}
\]

on the saturated-open frame.  For any ordinary \(x\in P\), the component
\(C_x\) is Euclidean closed and saturated, and its complement is a saturated
upper open.  The ordinary point \(q(x)\) has component fibre \(C_x\); it is
different from \(p_\infty\).

Write \([p_\infty]\) for the one-point **sublocale** of \(Q\), not the
topological closure of that point, and set \(G=q^{-1}([p_\infty])\).

## 3. The patch cofinite-tail fibre: (60)

For a patch-closed \(D\subseteq P\), let \(\mathfrak o_P(P\setminus D)\)
denote the corresponding open sublocale of the regular locale \(P\).  Define

\[
 \Lambda_E=
 \bigwedge_{\substack{D\subseteq P\text{ closed}\\D\cap E\text{ finite}}}
 \mathfrak o_P(P\setminus D). \tag{59}
\]

This is a nonzero, pointless cofinite-tail sublocale contained in the spatial
sublocale \([E]_P\).  The central fibre calculation is

\[
 \boxed{p^{-1}(G)=\Lambda_E}. \tag{60}
\]

**Proof route.**  If \(D\) is patch closed and \(D\cap E\) finite, adjoining
the finitely many fence components meeting \(D\) produces a closed saturated
set \(D^+\).  (The components are finite and are closed lower and upper
sets.)  Its upper-open complement descends to a \(p_\infty\)-neighborhood
whose pullback misses \(D\).  This gives the inclusion of the left side into
every open sublocale in (59).  Conversely, the closed complement of a
\(p_\infty\)-neighborhood is closed saturated and meets \(E\) only finitely;
the corresponding pulled-back neighborhood therefore contains
\(\Lambda_E\).  If a quotient open does **not** contain \(p_\infty\), it
misses \(E\) completely, and its patch inverse image has zero meet with
\(\Lambda_E\le[E]_P\).  The neighborhood and non-neighborhood tests
establish the claimed inverse image of the point sublocale, not merely an
intersection-of-neighborhoods estimate. ∎

It follows, since products of inverse images commute with pullbacks, that

\[
 (p\times p)^{-1}(G\times G)=\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E. \tag{61}
\]

## 4. Two support lemmas on the patch

We use \(\mathcal B(Z)\) for the **least dense sublocale** (Booleanization)
of a regular locale \(Z\).  All closed sets and supports in this section are
Euclidean/patch closed.  The patch locale \(P\times P\) is compact Hausdorff
and regular, so closed-sublocale annihilators detect inclusion between fitted
sublocales.

**Antichain-support lemma (the corrected Cantor-tree version).**  Suppose

\[
 0\ne S\le [E]_P\times[E]_P
\]

has zero meet with every one-point vertical and horizontal fibre.  If \(C\)
is its smallest closed support, there exists a compact graph
\(\Gamma\subseteq C\) of a Cantor set such that:

1. The coordinate projections \(D_1,D_2\) of \(\Gamma\) are compact antichains
   for the pospace order, and each projection is homeomorphic to a Cantor set.
2. For some colours \(\alpha,\beta\in\{P,Q\}\), both
   \(D_1\cap E_\alpha\) and \(D_2\cap E_\beta\) are dense in their
   respective Cantor sets.
3. Its Booleanized graph \(\mathcal B(\Gamma)\) is nonzero and lies in
   \(\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E\).

**Proposed proof.**  Any relatively open nonempty part of \(C\) meets \(S\)
nontrivially, by minimality of support.  The products \([E]_P\times P\)
and \(P\times[E]_P\) are spatial: the other factor \(P\) is compact
Hausdorff and locally compact.  It follows that selected first-coordinate
points and selected second-coordinate points are separately dense in \(C\).
Point-fibre vanishing similarly implies that every vertical and horizontal
point section has empty interior in \(C\).  Since \(E=E_P\cup E_Q\), a finite
Baire-category localization gives a **nonempty relatively open** subset
\(W\subseteq C\) on which one fixed family is dense in each coordinate.

There is no reason for \(W\) to be relatively clopen.  Work instead with sufficiently small closed Euclidean
rectangles having nonempty relative interiors contained in \(W\).
At a finite tree stage, a comparability component of a chosen coordinate
is finite.  Its coordinate fibre is thus a finite union of nowhere-dense
sections of \(C\).  Choose within each current rectangle (i) an anchor,
(ii) a new point with first coordinate in \(E_\alpha\), and (iii) a new
point with second coordinate in \(E_\beta\), avoiding every previously used
coordinate component in **both** factors.  The order is patch-closed, so
coordinate neighborhoods of these anchors can be shrunk to pairwise
order-incomparable closed intervals.  Choose child rectangles disjoint in
each projection and of rapidly decreasing diameter, and preserve each
anchor down a designated infinite descendant branch.  The resulting ternary
Cantor limit is \(\Gamma\), has injective projections, and its two
projections are antichains; preserved selected anchors are dense.

Finally, a patch-compact antichain in a compact pospace has its patch
topology as the subspace upper topology.  Indeed, for \(x\) in the antichain
and a compact subset \(F\) of it avoiding \(x\), the closed sets
\(\uparrow x\) and \(\downarrow F\) are disjoint; Nachbin order normality
separates them by an upper-open neighborhood of \(x\).  This legitimizes
the use of the spatial Cantor graph locale in \(X\times X\).
The cofinite-tail definition (59), together with perfectness and density
of selected points on the projections, gives
\(\mathcal B(\Gamma)\le\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E\). ∎

**Binary generation of the generic fibre.**  Let \(\mathscr G_E\) be all
Cantor graphs arising from the support lemma, with both projections compact
antichains and selected-colour traces dense.  Then

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E
       =\bigvee_{\Gamma\in\mathscr G_E}\mathcal B(\Gamma)}. \tag{62}
\]

The inequality from right to left was just noted.  For the opposite
inequality, let a closed patch set \(F\) meet the generic product
nontrivially.  The sublocale \(S=F\wedge(\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E)\)
has no vertical or horizontal ordinary point fibres, as \(\Lambda_E\) is
pointless.  The support lemma gives a \(\Gamma\subseteq F\) and a nonzero
\(\mathcal B(\Gamma)\le S\), so \(F\) also meets the join on the right.
Thus both fitted sublocales have identical closed annihilators, proving
(62).  This step uses the stated regular-locale closed-annihilator
principle; it must not be replaced by mere point-set density.

## 5. Dense gap mediators and four-step inclusion

**Existence of fixed gap mediators.**  In the open geometric gap between
\(K\) and \(M\), choose an ordinary point \(u_P\) in a singleton comparability
component and a small Euclidean interval \(I_P\) around it inside that gap.
Since \(\uparrow u_P\) and \(\downarrow(P\setminus I_P)\) are disjoint
patch-closed sets, order normality gives a nonempty **upper-open**
\(U_P\subseteq I_P\) containing \(u_P\).  Similarly obtain \(U_Q\) inside
the gap between \(M\) and \(L\).  Every sufficiently fine fence of the
corresponding family has vertices arbitrarily close to every point of the
fixed gap, and therefore visits its chosen \(U_\alpha\).  This explicit
upper-open construction is essential; Euclidean openness in a gap would not suffice.

**Dense-strip lemma (principal localic step).**  Let \(D\) be a compact
antichain of \(P\).  If \(D\cap E_P\) is dense in \(D\), then

\[
 \mathcal B(D\times M)\le T,\qquad
 \mathcal B(M\times D)\le T. \tag{DS-P}
\]

If \(D\cap E_Q\) is dense, the same holds with \(Q\)-fences and \(U_Q\).
Here products on the left are the ordinary Hausdorff compact product
locales, included as closed antichain-supported sublocales of the square.

**Proposed mechanism.**  The two maps into \(H_G\) across each selected
family, restricted respectively to \(D\times U_\alpha\) and
\(U_\alpha\times M\), give sublocales with dense spatial traces.
The first trace meets every relative rectangle because every nonempty
open subset of the perfect \(D\) contains infinitely many selected vertices
from distinct fences, and the mesh tends to zero.  The second trace meets
every relative rectangle by the density of the appropriate endpoints in
\(M\) and the fixed gap-crossing property.  In each open middle rectangle,
the two induced dense sublocales have a nonzero meet (both contain the
double-negation sublocale of the middle open locale).  Their localic
pullback over the middle coordinate consequently has nonzero image in every
rectangle of \(D\times M\).  Applied relative to *all* open and closed
rectangles and using the open projection of overt middle locales, the
dense-bridge argument gives the stronger Booleanization inclusion
(DS-P), not just density of point support.  **The last upgrade is a primary
external-audit target**: a referee should verify the precise localic
image/Booleanization argument, including the direction of inclusion.

Take \(\Gamma\in\mathscr G_E\) with antichain projections \(D_1,D_2\).
Apply the appropriate versions of the dense-strip lemma to obtain

\[
 \mathcal B(D_1\times M)\le T,
 \qquad\mathcal B(M\times D_2)\le T.
\]

The common middle \(M\) supplies a composable witness; the Booleanization
of \(\Gamma\) maps into the localic pullback of the two strips.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal B(\Gamma)\le T\circ T}. \tag{63}
\]

By (62), patch base change, and the proper-surjection descent principle,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (p\times p)^{-1}(G\times G)
   &=\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E\\
   &\le(p\times p)^{-1}(T\circ T),\\
 \boxed{G\times G&\le T\circ T}. \tag{64}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The last step requires that the patch counit and its square are **proper
surjections**, and that proper surjections are effective descent morphisms
for sublocales.  An arbitrary surjective locale map need not reflect the
relevant sublocale inclusion after pullback.

## 6. The entire patch kernel pair: (65)

Let \(R_P=\operatorname{Eq}(q\circ p)\), viewed as a sublocale of the regular
compact patch square \(P^2\).  The proposed exact description is

\[
 \boxed{R_P=[E_G]_P\vee(\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E)}. \tag{65}
\]

**Lower bound.**  Ordinary points in one component have the same \(q\)-image,
so their spatial relation \([E_G]_P\) is in \(R_P\).  By (60), the generic
product \(\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E\) maps constantly to the point
\(p_\infty\) and is in \(R_P\).

**Proposed upper bound (saturated-avoidance argument).**  Test the putative
inclusion against an arbitrary Euclidean-closed set \(F\subseteq P^2\)
annihilating the right side.  If \(F\wedge R_P\ne0\), first note that
\(F\cap E_G=\varnothing\), including \(F\cap\Delta=\varnothing\).
Compactness permits restriction to a closed rectangle \(D_1\times D_2\)
meeting \(F\wedge R_P\) nontrivially and satisfying
\(D_1\cap D_2=\varnothing\).

If a Euclidean-closed subset \(D\subseteq D_1\) misses \(E\), then every
point of \(D\) is a singleton comparability component.  Hence \(D\) is
component-saturated and its complement is a saturated upper open.  That
quotient open separates \(q(D)\) from \(q(D_2)\), because
\(D\cap D_2=\varnothing\).  Using such tests and the spatiality of
\([E\cap D_1]_P\times D_2\) (the second factor is compact Hausdorff), one
obtains the **support restriction**

\[
 R_P\wedge(D_1\times D_2)
 \ \le\ [E\cap D_1]_P\times[E\cap D_2]_P. \tag{65a}
\]

There are no one-point coordinate fibres in the nonzero sublocale
\(S=F\wedge R_P\wedge(D_1\times D_2)\): if one coordinate is a point \(x\),
its counterpart in the equalizer must lie in the finite component \(C_x\),
whereas \(F\) misses all of \(E_G\).  Apply the antichain-support lemma to
\(S\).  It yields a closed Cantor graph \(\Gamma\subseteq F\), with
\(0\ne\mathcal B(\Gamma)\le\Lambda_E\times\Lambda_E\), contradicting
the annihilator assumption on \(F\).  The closed-annihilator criterion in
the regular locale \(P^2\) gives (65).

The passage from the saturated-avoidance test to (65a), especially for
nonspatial sublocales and after restriction to a closed rectangle, is a
second primary external-audit target.

## 7. Proper descent and the effectivity conclusion

The patch pullback of the kernel pair is its ordinary base change:

\[
 (p\times p)^{-1}(\mathcal K_q)=R_P.
\]

The spatial patch component relation \([E_G]_P\) lies in
\((p\times p)^{-1}H_G\), since the map from each ordinary point pair factors
through \(H_G\).  By (62)–(63), its generic companion in (65) lies in
\((p\times p)^{-1}(T\circ T)\).  Consequently

\[
 (p\times p)^{-1}(\mathcal K_q)
 \le(p\times p)^{-1}(T\circ T).
\]

Use effective descent of the proper surjection \(p\times p\) to obtain
\(\mathcal K_q\le T\circ T\).  The reverse inequality follows immediately
from \(H_G\le\mathcal K_q\) and transitivity of the kernel pair:

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_q
   =(H_G\circ H_G)\circ(H_G\circ H_G)}. \tag{66}
\]

Because \(H_G\) is reflexive and symmetric, its equivalence hull \(R_*\)
lies in every equivalence relation containing \(H_G\), in particular
\(R_*\le\mathcal K_q\).  Conversely (66) puts a finite composite of
\(H_G\) into \(R_*\), yielding

\[
 \boxed{R_*=\mathcal K_q}. \tag{67}
\]

Thus the proposed localic equivalence hull is **effective despite the
failure of ordinary pointwise reasoning to detect its extra pairs**.
The equality does not assert \(H_G\circ H_G\le H_G\), which is false in the
same example; nor does it rely on associativity of \(\circ\).

## 8. Status and independent-audit questions

The argument remains provisional.  Points to scrutinize independently:

- The exact construction and nonzero nature of \(\Lambda_E\), and its
  identification with a **point** fibre rather than a closure fibre.
- The Cantor tree's selected-coordinate density and the implications of
  nonspatial support in patch regular locales; the use of relatively open
  localization and nested closed rectangles.
- The dense-strip lemma's upgrade from intersecting dense mediator traces
  to containment of \(\mathcal B(D\times M)\) in the *image* \(T\).
- The existence and use of **upper-open** fixed gap mediators; Euclidean-open
  intervals need not be upper-open.
- The saturated-avoidance estimate (65a) on nonspatial equalizer pieces.
- The properness and order-reflecting descent of \(p\times p\).
- The use of explicit fixed bracketing, given nonassociativity of general
  localic relational composition.

These are unresolved audit obligations, not assumptions to be imposed on a
referee.
