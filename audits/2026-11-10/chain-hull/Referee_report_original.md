### I1. B2 lower proof: wrong carrier simplex

The proof says that, for nested chain-faces σ₀ ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ σ\_k, the vertices selected by both maps lie in Φ(σ₀). If "r ∘ inclusion after barycentric subdivision" means the subdivided map σ ↦ r(σ), this is false.

**Counterexample.** P = {x < y < m₁, x < m₂} with m(x) = m₂ and m(y) = m₁. Take σ₀ = {y} ⊂ σ₁ = {x, y}. Then Φ(σ₀) = {m₁}, but r(σ₁) = {m₁, m₂}.

**Repair, either of:**

- Compose with the last-vertex map sd Ord(P) → Ord(P), σ ↦ max σ, which is homotopic to the canonical homeomorphism. Then m(max σ\_i) ∈ Φ(σ\_i) ⊆ Φ(σ₀), and the barycentres of the Φ(σ\_i) lie in the closed simplex Φ(σ₀). This may be what the author meant.
- Keep the subdivided map and use the simplex Φ({min σ\_k}). It contains r(σ\_k) and Φ(σ₀), because min σ\_k is a common lower bound of both.

In both versions the two maps |sd Ord(P)| → |N\_M| are carried by a common simplex on every simplex of the source, hence are linearly homotopic. Two-out-of-three then finishes as stated.

### I2. C3: the "iff" must not pass through cofibres

A map with contractible cofibre need not be an equivalence (any acyclic, non-contractible space mapping to a point). The packet's order of sentences suggests "cofibres agree, thus one map is an equivalence iff the other is". Reorder: the arrow equivalence gives the iff directly, and the cofibre statement is a second corollary.

### I3. Site prerequisites used but not stated

- **Finite coproducts.** The Čech nerves of finite families, the tensor with Δ\[1\] in C2, and "the empty intersection (initial)" in F1 need h to preserve finite coproducts and the initial object. This holds once the empty family covers ∅ and finite coproduct decompositions are covers. Say so.
- **Constant objects.** "underline(S¹) is not terminal" and "SNC ⇔ Ord(P) contractible for finite P" need the constant-object functor to be faithful on homotopy types. It is: every covering sieve of the point is trivial, so evaluation at the point preserves colimits and is a left inverse.
- **Size.** Each statement holds for a fixed κ large enough for the X in question. Independence of κ is not addressed. A strong limit κ, as in condensed mathematics, is the usual way to obtain it.

### I4. Which example shows F2 is strictly stronger than E3

G3 proves that X\_T is SNC without SZC. That separates two properties of a locale, and the finite poset F(T) already does so. It does not separate the criteria: the facet cover of X\_T in G2 passes D2 alone.

H2 does separate them, and for every cover. Let X be the H2 space and take any finite closed cover.

1. A member containing both p and q has several minimal points and no upper bound of {p, q}. It fails every alternative. So p and q lie in different members W₁, W₂, with q ∉ W₁ and p ∉ W₂.
2. W₁ ∩ W₂ contains P and has no least point. UB forces a point above all facets of T, hence p or q, so W₁ ∩ W₂ is P, P ∪ {f} or P ∪ {e}.
3. P has no SZC (G3). P ∪ {e} and P ∪ {f} are order-disconnected, so they have none either.

Hence no finite closed cover of the H2 space satisfies E3, while the packet's cover satisfies F2. The same argument on H1 shows that no finite closed cover of the H1 space satisfies D2, while E3 applies. So D2 ⊊ E3 ⊊ F2 as criteria on spaces, which is stronger than the packet's cautious remark in H2.

### I5. C3: what the "standard edgewise theorem" gives

The classical theorem gives a homeomorphism |sd₂ X| ≅ |X| induced by a diagonal embedding of simplices, not by a or b. That a and b are equivalences needs one more step. Two options:

- Cite Brown–Chan–Galatius–Payne, Prop. 3.8 and Cor. 3.9, which treat exactly these two block maps.
- Argue directly. The functor ε is cofinal on Δ^op, because sd₂ of a simplex is contractible. The two block inclusions \[n\] → \[n\]∗\[n\] are natural transformations id ⇒ ε. For any cocone the induced map equals the canonical cofinality map. So |a| ≃ |b| and both are equivalences, in any ∞-category with realisations.

The second route needs neither objectwise presheaf arguments nor C2.

### I6. Linking Claim B to I(P) and D(P)

For finite P, I\_n(P) is the set of (n+1)-tuples whose support is a face of K\_up(P). This ordered-tuple simplicial set realises to |K\_up(P)|, and the nerve of P sits inside it compatibly with Ord(P) ↪ K\_up(P) (order the vertices by a linear extension). The packet uses this silently when it treats §3 as the finite case of §4–§5. One sentence and a reference suffice.

### I11. Cameron–Kumar

I have now read the paper's conventions, Dowker preliminaries and homotopy section in the arXiv v1 source. The overlap with Claim B is substantial at the homotopy-type level and absent at the canonical-map level.

Their specialization order is the opposite of the packet's, so their open points are the packet's maximal points. The dictionary:

| Cameron–Kumar | Packet |
| --- | --- |
| Inseparability complex Nins(X) | K\_up(P) |
| Closure-meeting complex Ncm(X) | K\_low(P) |
| Dowker complex Dow(X) on the open points | N\_M |
| F\_S, an intersection of closures of open points | B\_S |
| Order complex Δ(X) | Ord(P) |

- **B1.** The equivalences K\_up ≃ N\_M ≃ K\_low are part (a) of their homotopy theorem, proved by three Dowker dualities. The packet's strong collapse of K\_low onto N\_M is not in their paper.
- **B2, hypothesis.** Identical: every nonempty F\_S has a contractible order complex. Their abstract calls this an acyclicity hypothesis; the theorem itself assumes contractibility.
- **B2, conclusion.** Their part (b) obtains an equivalence between the order complex and Dow(X) from the nerve lemma, hence abstract equivalences with both complexes. It does not say that the inclusions of the order complex are equivalences. The canonical-map statement is the packet's addition.
- **Examples.** The packet's four-point control is their pseudo-circle example. The face-poset remark matches their corollary, applied to the opposite face poset: they take vertices as open points, the packet takes facets.
- **Citation.** The homotopy section is the ninth section of v1, not §8.

**Repair.** In §3, attribute the type-level statements of B1 and B2 to Cameron–Kumar, and present the strong collapse and the canonical-map formulation as what is added. Appendix B's phrase "original graph/witness-complex motivation" understates the overlap. I make no priority judgement; I report only what each text contains.

### Suspected issues that turned out harmless

- **Direction of hulls.** Closure giving upper witnesses looked backwards at first. It is right for the stated order, where x ≤ y means x ∈ cl{y}.
- **Pointwise equality.** It is used only between proconstructible sublocales, or to factor maps out of spatial sources. Both uses are legitimate.
- **Relation composition.** Never used. E\_{p,q} is defined directly as an intersection.
- **Hyperdescent.** Only Čech descent along effective epimorphisms is used, so hypercompleteness is irrelevant.
- **D-side descent.** A tuple with a common lower witness need not lie in one downset Z\_i, so the lower hulls do not localise. The packet never claims they do; all D-side results come through C3.
- **Constant maps.** const\_g is spectral and patch-continuous, so the extra degeneracies in D2 are maps in the site.
- **Realisation and inverse limits.** Never commuted. Section 6 shows they cannot be.

The absence of beat points holds for every triangulation of the dunce hat. In a face poset no face is a down beat point, and a face is an up beat point exactly when it is a free face.

**No SZC, without citing Stong.** Let Q be a finite poset with no beat points and g a monotone map with g ≤ id. Induct upward: if g fixes everything below x and g(x) < x, then g(x) is the largest element below x, so x is a down beat point. Hence g = id; dually for g ≥ id. In a zigzag from id, each map is therefore id, so the last one is constant only if Q is a point.

**Retract.** The map X\_T → P sending all of A to v is monotone, and the preimage of v is the clopen set {v} ∪ A. It is a spectral retraction. Conjugating a zigzag on X\_T by it gives a zigzag on P, which is impossible. G3 stands.

## 6. Scope: patched sheaf realisations versus intrinsic shape

The packet proves statements about three objects of the Stone sheaf ∞-topos H attached to a coherent locale. It proves nothing about the shape of the locale, and one example below shows that it could not without new ideas.

### What is proved about patched realisations

- **Arrows.** For every coherent X, the canonical maps C(X) → I(X) and C(X) → D(X) are equivalent as objects under C(X). No hypothesis on X is needed.
- **Descent.** For a finite closed cover of X, C(X) is the realisation of the Čech diagram of the C(Z\_S), and I(X) that of the ambient hulls of the Z\_S, compatibly with the canonical maps.
- **Certificates.** If every nonempty Z\_S passes one of the local tests, then C(X) ≃ I(X) ≃ D(X) through the canonical maps. If all Z\_S are SNC, all three are the constant object on the cover nerve.
- **Underlying homotopy type.** Evaluation at the point preserves colimits. So the global sections of C(X) are the classifying space of the abstract poset of points of X, with the patch topology forgotten. The topology survives only in the sheaf structure.

### What is not proved, and what the objects are not

- **Not shape.** For a Stone space X the order is trivial, so C(X) = I(X) = D(X) = h(X). This is the space itself as a sheaf, not a homotopy type. No Stone space with two points is SNC.
- **Not determined by finite stages.** See the example W below.
- **No non-constant computation.** F1 can only output constant objects. Every infinite example in the packet is a finite poset plus a clopen convergent sequence of maximal points, and retracts onto its finite part. The sheaf-theoretic content is never exercised.
- **No direct D-side descent.** Lower-witness tuples do not localise on downset covers. Every conclusion about D(X) is inherited through C3.
- **No unpatched statement.** Nothing is claimed or shown for realisations in Loc, for effective descent of the unpatched maps, or for a model structure. The packet says so in §10, correctly.

### Example W: SNC is not visible at finite stages

Let W = {x₀, y₀, x₁, y₁, …} ∪ {∞}, the one-point compactification of the discrete fence x\_n < y\_n > x\_{n+1}, with ∞ incomparable to everything.

- **W is Priestley.** The order is closed, since comparable pairs converge only to (∞, ∞). The sets ↑x\_n and {y\_n} are finite clopen upsets. The tails U\_N = {y\_{N−1}, x\_N, y\_N, …, ∞} are clopen upsets around ∞.
- **Finite stages are contractible.** The lattices K\_N of clopen upsets that contain or miss U\_N are cofinal among finite sublattices. Spec K\_N is the finite fence x₀ < y₀ > ⋯ > x\_{N−1} < t, with U\_N collapsed to t. Its order complex is an arc.
- **W is not SNC.** The poset of points is an infinite fence plus the isolated point ∞. Its classifying space has two components. By the previous subsection this is the underlying homotopy type of C(W), so C(W) is not terminal.

So a cofinal family of finite stages can be contractible while the locale is not SNC. This confirms the caveat after A2: realisation does not commute with the finite-poset limit, and SNC is a property of the sheaf object, not of the pro-homotopy type of the stages. The connected components of C(W) form a two-point sheaf that no map to a finite discrete space can separate, a sheaf-level phenomenon with no counterpart at any finite stage.

## 7. Simpler or stronger routes

Six simplifications or strengthenings are available; none requires new machinery. Items 3 to 6 are my own derivations and should be checked like the packet's claims.

1. **Cofinality instead of C2 in C3.** The functor ε is cofinal and the block inclusions are natural transformations id ⇒ ε. So |a| ≃ |b| and both are equivalences in any ∞-category with geometric realisations (I5). C2 stays true but is no longer needed.
2. **C2 holds for every simplicial object.** The homotopy is H\_n(y, θ) = Y(f\_θ)(y) with f\_θ(i) = i + (n+1)θ(i). Nothing about chains is used.
3. **B2-lower from B2-upper.** For finite P, C3 with I6 says Ord(P) ↪ K\_up(P) and Ord(P) ↪ K\_low(P) are equivalent arrows under Ord(P), with no hypothesis. So one is an equivalence iff the other is, and the Quillen-fibre argument for the lower inclusion can be dropped. The exhaustive test in Section 5 is consistent with this.
4. **Descent for all finite closed covers.** D1 and F1 never use compactness of the u\_i. For any opens u\_i with meet 0, the Z\_i are patch-closed downsets, so their chain and hull pieces still form finite jointly surjective families of Stone spaces with the same intersections. Compact complements are needed only to phrase the tests in the intervals \[v\_S, 1\].
5. **UB is exactly the hull condition.** UB holds for Z if and only if every finite set of points of Z has an upper bound in X, if and only if H\_n^X(Z) = Z^{n+1} for all n. The middle condition implies UB by choosing one point in each k\_i. So E1 is sharp: UB is necessary and sufficient for the ambient hull to be the full Čech nerve.
6. **Strict chain of criteria.** D2 ⊊ E3 ⊊ F2, with H1 and H2 as witnesses for every finite closed cover (I4).

A more economical way to state the core of §5 and §7 is one theorem: C(−) and the realised ambient hull satisfy Čech codescent for finite closed covers. D1, F1 and all criteria are then corollaries, and the role of the ambient hull, as opposed to the intrinsic one, is visible in the statement.

Two smaller remarks. For finite Z, SNC is contractibility of the order complex, so in H2 "SNC by F1" can be replaced by |sd T| ≃ ∗. And the two endpoint tests of D2 are the cases where Z\_S is irreducible (generic point) or local (closed point).

## 9. Notes on first reading

These points slowed me down or looked wrong before I resolved them. All are resolved unless marked.

- **Which hull is which.** Closure of the diagonal giving common upper witnesses felt inverted. It is right: opens are upsets, so closure adds specializations of diagonal points, which are tuples below a common point.
- **"Witness" language.** "Upper witness", "generization", "upper bound" and "ambient upper bound" are used interchangeably. A single term would help.
- **Overloaded letters.** C, H, T, K, A, F each carry three meanings (I8). H\_n in C2 next to the topos H and the hull H\_n^X(Z) was the worst case.
- **"Nonzero Z\_S".** It means nonempty, that is v\_S ≠ 1. I first read it as a condition on the interval.
- **esd₂ Ch × Δ\[1\].** This is the tensor with a simplicial set, a finite coproduct of locales in each degree. That needs h to preserve finite coproducts (I3).
- **"The standard edgewise-subdivision theorem".** I expected the homeomorphism, which does not concern a or b. The cited source does cover the block maps (I5).
- **"After barycentric subdivision" in B2.** Ambiguous between the subdivided map and the last-vertex composite. Only the second fits the stated simplex (I1).
- **"Quillen lower fibers".** The fibre over S is taken in the opposite order on faces of N\_M, so it is {σ : Φ(σ) ⊇ S}. Correct, but the op is easy to miss.
- **D2, "a Čech nerve over a point".** It means the Čech nerve of Z → point. It realises to the point because Z is nonempty.
- **Why H^X and not I(Z).** The reason, that intrinsic hulls do not intersect correctly, is stated only as an inequality. One line of explanation would help readers.
- **Where Claim B is used.** Nowhere later. It reads as a prerequisite but is a stand-alone finite result, and its link to I(P) and D(P) is implicit (I6).
- **G3's last sentence.** I first took it as a claim about the criteria and looked for a cover of X\_T failing E3. There is none; the claim belongs to H2 (I4).
- **"SNC by Claim F1" in H2.** F1 is not needed for a finite poset.
- **Role of the sequence A.** I looked for a place where the convergence matters homotopically. It does not; it only makes the locale infinite. Unresolved as a question of intent: an example where the patch topology changes the answer, such as W in Section 6, would test the theory more.
- **underline(−).** That constant objects detect homotopy types is used but never said (I3).

### Machine computations

Three short Python scripts, standard library only. Homology is computed as ranks of boundary matrices over prime fields.

- **Exhaustive posets.** All transitive subrelations of the natural order on ≤ 6 points (5,231 posets). Tests: homology of K\_up, K\_low, N\_M; relative homology of both canonical inclusions; the B2 hypothesis.
- **Point descriptions.** All posets on ≤ 4 points, tuples of length 2 and 3, against all open covers and all neighbourhood rectangles.
- **Examples.** The C2 identity; the four-point core; the H1 table, nerve and truncation; my dunce hat (f-vector, edge degrees, homology, fundamental group, beat points); truncations of X\_T for three choices of T.

### Limitations

- **Homology, not homotopy.** Machine tests compare Betti numbers over F₂ and F₃ (up to F₁₃ for the dunce hat). "Contractible" in the B2 hypothesis was tested as acyclic, which is adequate at this size but is a proxy.
- **Truncations.** Replacing the sequence A by three points tests the order structure only. Closedness, separation, compactness and the retract were checked by hand.
- **Not re-derived.** Standard ∞-topos facts and the classical theorems listed in Section 2 were not re-proved or re-read.
- **Cameron–Kumar read in part.** I read the sections bearing on Claim B, not its graph-theoretic sections.
- **My own additions** (Section 7 items 3 to 6, the argument in I4, example W) have had no second reader.
