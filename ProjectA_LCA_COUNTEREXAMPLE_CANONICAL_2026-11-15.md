# Project A — Negative (LC-A): a compact sober T1 counterexample

**Canonical theorem note · 15 November 2026.** The explicit counterexample and its original proof received an **independent blind referee verdict of correct** (all supporting lemmas checked; no mathematical repairs required). The shorter generalized-point proof below was proposed in the referee report and checked internally after the audit. The literature priority of the example is **not established**.

**Context:** [positive (A-SC) theorem](ProjectA_ASC_CANONICAL_2026-11-15.md); [historical LC frontier](ProjectA_ASC_LOCALLY_COMPACT_FRONTIER_2026-11-15.md); [external-audit reconciliation](ProjectA_LCA_EXTERNAL_REFEREE_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-15.md); [archive](project-a/audits/2026-11-15/LCA/README.md).

## 1. Statement

For any locale X, let \(\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X^2\) be presented by
\[
u_1\le u_2\qquad(u\in\mathcal O(X)).
\]
Equivalently, \((f,g):Z\to X^2\) factors through \(\mathcal A(X)\) iff \(f^*(u)\le g^*(u)\) for every open u. Its ordinary points are pairs \(x\le_X y\) in the specialization order.

**Theorem (negative LC-A).** There is a **compact, locally compact, sober, T1** space X such that
\[
\mathcal A(X)\supsetneq\Delta_X=[G_{\le_X}].
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal A(X)\) is not spatial. Its locale is not stably locally compact.

The positive (A-SC) theorem remains intact: **stable** local compactness implies spatiality of \(\mathcal A\). The example shows that ordinary local compactness alone does not suffice.

## 2. The space

Let \(K=[0,1]\) with its Euclidean topology; adjoin a point \(p\). The opens of \(X=K\sqcup\{p\}\) are precisely:

1. ordinary opens \(V\subseteq K\);
2. \(U_F=\{p\}\cup(K\setminus F)\), for finite \(F\subseteq K\).

The identities \(U_F\cap U_G=U_{F\cup G}\), \(U_F\cap V=V\setminus F\), and \(U_F\cup V=U_{F\setminus V}\) verify the topology; arbitrary unions containing p again have finite complement in K.

- **Compact and T1:** any cover has a member \(U_F\) covering all but finitely many points; \(\{p\}\) has open complement K and \(\{x\}\) has open complement \(U_{\{x\}}\).
- **Locally compact:** each \(U_F\) is compact open at p. At \(x\in K\), every neighborhood contains \(V\ni x\) lying in a compact \(C\subseteq K\) inside the neighborhood. Thus the frame of opens is continuous; this is localic/core-compact local compactness, not merely a weak pointwise claim.
- **Sober:** a nonempty irreducible closed subset meets K in at most one point (disjoint Euclidean neighborhoods separate any two); it cannot contain both p and a point x of K (the relatively open sets from K and \(U_{\{x\}}\) separate them). It is therefore a singleton.
- **Not stably locally compact:** for \(x\in K\), both K and \(U_{\{x\}}\) are compact saturated, while their intersection \(K\setminus\{x\}\) is noncompact. Equivalently, \(a=K\setminus\{x\}\) satisfies \(a\ll K\), \(a\ll U_{\{x\}}\), but \(a\not\ll a\). This witnesses failure of multiplicativity of \(\ll\) independently of compactness conventions.

The space is not Hausdorff: every neighborhood of p meets every nonempty open V of K.

## 3. A short generalized-point proof

Let \(\beta_K\hookrightarrow K\) be the double-negation (Booleanization) sublocale. It is nonzero. If \(W\subseteq K\) is dense open, \(\beta_K\le O_K(W)\). In particular, for every finite \(F\subseteq K\),
\[
\beta_K\le O_K(K\setminus F).
\]
Let \(z:\beta_K\to K\hookrightarrow X\) be the inclusion, and \(p!:\beta_K\to X\) the constant map at p.

For each open u of X:
- if \(p\notin u\), \((p!)^*u=0\le z^*u\);
- if \(p\in u\), \(u=U_F\) and \((p!)^*u=1=z^*u\).

Thus \((p!,z)\) factors through \(\mathcal A(X)\).

It cannot factor through \(\Delta_X\), since that would give \(p!=z\), but
\[
(p!)^*(K)=0,\qquad z^*(K)=1
\]
in the nonzero frame \(\mathcal O(\beta_K)\). Therefore \(\Delta_X\subsetneq\mathcal A(X)\).

Since X is T1, the point graph \(G_{\le_X}\) is the ordinary diagonal; since X is sober/spatial, the diagonal sublocale is generated by those points. Consequently \(\mathcal A(X)\) is not spatial. This proof is classical and makes no constructivist claim.

## 4. The fitted point fiber: structural proof

For any locale X and ordinary point \(p:1\to X\), the fiber of the first projection \(\rho_1:\mathcal A(X)\to X\) is canonically
\[
\mathcal A(X)\times_{X,\rho_1}[p]\ \cong\ \operatorname{fit}_X([p])
=\bigwedge_{p\in u}O_X(u).
\]
Indeed, \((p!,z)\) factors through \(\mathcal A\) iff \(z^*u=1\) for every \(u\ni p\). The dual calculation gives the fiber of the *second* projection:
\[
\mathcal A(X)\times_{X,\rho_2}[p]\ \cong\ \operatorname{cl}_X([p])
=\bigwedge_{p\notin u}C_X(u).
\]
For spatial X, the latter is spatial, because it is closed in X; the spatiality claim must not be extended indiscriminately to nonspatial X.

For the constructed space, the first fiber contains the nonzero pointless open part
\[
E=\operatorname{fit}_X([p])\wedge O_X(K).
\]
Indeed \(\beta_X\le E\) since K and all \(U_F\) are dense open, whereas the ordinary points common to all \(U_F\) consist only of p, outside K. Because [p] is closed, its first-projection fiber is closed in \(\mathcal A(X)\), with E open therein. If \(\mathcal A(X)\) were spatial, this **locally closed** part would be spatial, contradiction. This is the independently audited original proof; the shorter proof in §3 needs no fiber analysis.

## 5. Totally unordered spaces and scope

The condition \(\mathcal A(X)=\Delta_X\) is exactly Isbell's **totally unordered** axiom \((T_U)\): any two locale maps \(f\le g:Z\to X\) coincide. This identification follows immediately from the universal property of \(\mathcal A\); it is discussed in Johnstone, *Stone Spaces*, III.1.5, and Arrieta, *Localic separation and the duality between closedness and fittedness*, §2.3.

Arrieta's \((pt\text{-}fit)\) axiom requires every point sublocale to be fitted, and Proposition 3.10 states \((T_U)\Rightarrow(pt\text{-}fit)\). The present X fails both at p. Whether they coincide for **locally compact sober T1 spaces** is open here: [the active questions](ProjectA_LCA_FOLLOWUP_AND_A_DUAL_QUESTIONS_2026-11-15.md).

The referee also suggested that for a general locally compact Hausdorff K and its cofinite-point extension X, \(\mathcal A(X)\) is spatial iff K is scattered. This is recorded as a **referee sketch, internally rechecked but not independently blind-audited**; it is not part of the boxed theorem. The explicit \([0,1]\) example and its direct extension to K without isolated points were blind-audited correct.

## 6. Provenance and priority caution

The blind referee checked all seven sections of the original [packet](project-a/audits/2026-11-15/LCA/Blind_packet.md), including the optional generalization, and found no mathematical error. Suggested editorial clarifications include: §6 is independent of the fiber proof, not an independent Booleanization; the \(\ll\)-witness avoids convention issues; and the diagonal is not closed in this non-Hausdorff X.

The referee's [addendum](project-a/audits/2026-11-15/LCA/Referee_addendum_original.md) supplied the \((T_U)\), \((pt\text{-}fit)\), and known Johnstone-example connections. **No search has established that the locally compact counterexample itself is new.** Do not cite it as novel without a targeted literature check.
