# Scott-frame depth-two collapse: chain and downward-directed continuous dcpos

A depth-three witness would disprove only a universal depth-two bound, not a higher finite uniform bound; to disprove every finite bound requires bars of arbitrarily large minimal depth, and to force transfinite rank for an individual bar is stronger still.

Let L=O_sigma(P) for a continuous dcpo P which is downward directed (any x,y admit r<=x,y). For any rectangular bar B the diagonal pullback yields join_{(a,b)in B}(a meet b)=1. For each x choose z way-below x, and let U_z={t:z way-below t}, a Scott-open neighborhood of x contained in upper(z). Fix y in P; choose r<=z,y. Diagonal coverage gives (a,b) in B with r in a meet b. Since a,b upper, upper(z) subset a and y in b. Hence for the fixed U_z, all second opens b from B rectangles with U_z subset a cover P. Thus (U_z,1) has a vertical one-step leaf cover. The U_z cover P by continuity; use them as root horizontal branches. Every bar has depth <=2. No compactness, regularity, local compactness, or completely join-prime basis hypothesis is used.

Specific test P=(0,infinity] with Scott upper-ray opens: noncompact, nonregular, no nonzero completely join-prime opens; complete-chain case also follows from direct order argument. P=(0,infinity]^2 is continuous, downward directed, non-chain and likewise depth two. Therefore both are excluded as higher-depth candidates.

Future strict-depth candidates: Scott domains failing downward directedness in a genuinely interacting manner, beyond mere disjoint sums of components. An explicit frame/bar must still fail both algebraic depth-two criteria while satisfying C-ideal barhood. No such example is established here.
