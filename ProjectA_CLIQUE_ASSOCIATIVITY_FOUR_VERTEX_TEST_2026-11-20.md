### Statement

For the Stone-locale clique relation H_2 on the **four** ordinary component supports U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3 arranged as the localic overlap path 0–1–2–3, let
\[
B_{\le2}=\bigvee_{|i-j|\le2} U_i\times U_j.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{(H_2\circ H_2)\circ H_2=H_2\circ(H_2\circ H_2)=B_{\le2}.}\tag{1}
\]
This is equality of **localic sublocales**, not only their point sets. It is consistent with the referee's fourfold all-bracketing completion H_2^4=G_2²: B_{\le2} is proper because it misses the private point pair (a_0,b_2).

### Lemma: one-sided compact-envelope absorption

Let C be compact Hausdorff spatial, Y any spatial locale, and A,B≤C spatial sublocales whose point sets cover the points of C (e.g. the rational/irrational Cantor partition), so A∨B=C. Write
\[
P_{A,Y}=\bigvee_{a\in\mathrm{pt}(A),y\in\mathrm{pt}(Y)}\{(a,y)\}\le A\times Y.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{A\times Y=P_{A,Y}\vee((A\wedge B)\times Y).}\tag{2}
\]
There is a transpose version for Y×A.

**Proof.** The localic product C×Y is spatial (a locally compact spatial factor is a spatial multiplier). Its point pairs are exhausted by those with first coordinate in A or B. Thus
\[
C\times Y=P_{A,Y}\vee P_{B,Y}\le P_{A,Y}\vee(B\times Y).
\]
Meet with A×Y; finite distributivity of sublocales, P_{A,Y}≤A×Y and the product-meet identity give (2). No spatiality of A×Y is assumed. The lemma uses the *compact envelope* C×Y, not a purported spatial localic product of two rational/irrational sublocales.

### Common lower and upper bounds

Let H=H_2=∨_{i=0}^3 P_i with P_i=[E_i×E_i] and U_i=[E_i], as in the canonical note. The overlap graph is the path 0–1–2–3. By finite expansion and endpoint support, every triple word vanishes unless its consecutive index labels differ by ≤1; therefore
\[
(H\circ H)\circ H,\ H\circ(H\circ H)\ \le B_{\le2}.\tag{3}
\]

Both bracketings contain
\[
B_{\le1}=\bigvee_{|i-j|\le1}U_i\times U_j.\tag{4}
\]
For i=j, P_i∘P_i=U_i² and reflexivity pads to length 3. If |i−j|=1, the two-factors P_i∘P_j contain all point-pair edges, hence all one-point-legged slabs by spatiality of U_i,U_j. The two threefold words
\[
P_i\circ(P_i\circ P_j)=U_i\times U_j,\qquad
(P_i\circ P_j)\circ P_j=U_i\times U_j
\]
supply the full neighboring rectangle, respectively, in the right and left global bracketings. Reverse orientations are handled by exchanging i,j (not by assuming a bracketing symmetric).

The referee's all-binary-tree induction also implies that both triple bracketings of H contain **every ordinary point pair** from E_i to E_j when |i−j|≤2. Consequently, each contains the spatial point-generated sublocale P_{ij}=[E_i×E_j] for these endpoint indices.

### The two distance-two blocks

The ordinary components for n=2 are
\[
E_0=\{a_0\}\cup Q_0,\quad
E_1=I_0\cup\{b_0,a_1\}\cup Q_1,\quad
E_2=I_1\cup\{b_1,a_2\}\cup Q_2,\quad
E_3=I_2\cup\{b_2\}.
\]
All displayed unions are over disjoint **clopen ambient components**, except that Q_i,I_i partition the same Cantor component by ordinary points.

Let M_i=Q_i∧I_i≠0.

First, U_0×U_2. Its a_0×U_2 slab is generated by ordinary point pairs and hence is present in both bracketings. Apply (2) with C=C_0, A=Q_0, B=I_0, Y=U_2:
\[
Q_0\times U_2=P_{Q_0,U_2}\vee(M_0\times U_2).
\]
The first summand is present from all-bracketing point pairs, and the second lies in U_1×U_2≤B_{\le1}. Hence both bracketings contain U_0×U_2. Transposition gives U_2×U_0, using the full common neighboring rectangle U_2×U_1.

Second, U_1×U_3. Its U_1×b_2 slab is present. Apply the transposed (2) with C=C_2, A=I_2, B=Q_2, Y=U_1:
\[
U_1\times I_2=P_{U_1,I_2}\vee(U_1\times M_2).
\]
The first summand is present by point-pair induction, and the second lies in U_1×U_2≤B_{\le1}. Thus U_1×U_3 is present in both bracketings; transpose similarly gives U_3×U_1. Along with (4), these are **all** index pairs with distance≤2, proving the reverse inequality to (3). This establishes (1).

### General boundary observation

The same proof shows for **any** n≥2 that both triple bracketings of H_n contain the *full* rectangles
\[
U_0\times U_2,\ U_2\times U_0,\quad
U_{n-1}\times U_{n+1},\ U_{n+1}\times U_{n-1}.
\]
For n=2 these exhaust the distance-two possibilities. For n≥3, **interior** distance-two component pairs remain to be tested.

### The next first-open test: five overlap vertices, n=3

For H_3 on U_0–U_1–U_2–U_3–U_4, all distance≤1 rectangles and boundary distance-two rectangles are full. The sole unresolved distance-two pair (up to reversal) is U_1×U_3.

Break it into clopen ambient blocks. The general point-pair induction and the compact-envelope absorption lemma settle all nonisolated blocks except
\[
\boxed{I_0\times Q_3\le C_0\times C_3.}\tag{5}
\]
Indeed Q_1×U_3 is controlled by the source overlap Q_1∧I_1≤U_2, and U_1×I_2 is controlled by the target overlap Q_2∧I_2≤U_2. Any isolated-endpoint slab is spatial and already present.

On the **clopen** C_0×C_3 sector, the baseline B_{\le1} and all other indexed three-factor words vanish by support; only the consecutive component-index word (1,2,3) can contribute. Restricting its three factors to the necessary clopen intermediate blocks gives
\[
R=[I_0\times Q_1]_{C_0\times C_1},\quad
S=[I_1\times Q_2]_{C_1\times C_2},\quad
T=[I_2\times Q_3]_{C_2\times C_3},
\]
where each is the **point-generated spatial sublocale**, not necessarily the full localic product. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
((H_3\circ H_3)\circ H_3)\wedge(C_0\times C_3)
 &= (R\circ S)\circ T,\\
(H_3\circ(H_3\circ H_3))\wedge(C_0\times C_3)
 &= R\circ(S\circ T).
\end{aligned}\tag{6}
\]
The right-hand products are taken with the evident typed successive middle Cantor blocks. This is the **first unmasked candidate** for a clique associator: both sides contain all ordinary point-pair edges from I_0 to Q_3, while I_0×Q_3 itself can be nonspatial. A difference is possible but **not proved**.

**No inference from nonspatiality alone.** The known nonspatiality of the localic product of rational/irrational spaces only marks where an associator could hide; it does not show that either composite in (6) is proper or that they differ.
