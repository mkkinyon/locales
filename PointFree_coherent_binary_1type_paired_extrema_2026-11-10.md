# Binary feedback V — Coherent binary reconstruction of chain 1-types via compact paired extrema

**10 November 2026. New internally checked working theorem; NOT externally audited.** Completes the bounded segment requested after [Binary feedback IV](PointFree_binary_reconstruction_fixed_hulls_unbounded_obstructions_2026-11-10.md). The obstruction there was noncompact minimal/maximal point loci. We avoid it by taking the closure of pairs of corresponding extrema of the two orders. This proves the coherent version of binary reconstruction of chain 1-types, including simultaneous compatibility with both canonical comparisons. It is the last research segment before the proposed next independent audit. No priority, intrinsic shape, site-size independence, or arbitrary-locale assertion.

## 1. Statement and preliminaries

**Theorem.** Let X,Y be coherent locales and suppose a homeomorphism of their Priestley patch spaces identifies their two binary common-witness relations I and D. Identify the patched spaces with a common Stone space V and write the two specialization orders ≤_X, ≤_Y. In the fixed sufficiently large Stone-sheaf infinity-topos of the previous audited work, there is an equivalence
\[
\tau_{\le1}\mathbf C(X)\simeq\tau_{\le1}\mathbf C(Y)
\]
compatible with the vertex maps from h(V) and simultaneously with the two canonical comparison arrows to the common 1-truncated patched hulls. Equivalently the internal edge-path fundamental groupoids on the common vertex sheaf h(V) are isomorphic, compatibly with both hull functors. In particular the coherent binary pair determines the equivalence class of the chain 1-type and its two comparison arrows.

The common patched hull simplicial objects are identified using the binary-reconstruction theorem in [Binary feedback IV](PointFree_binary_reconstruction_fixed_hulls_unbounded_obstructions_2026-11-10.md): distinct closed neighborhoods of graph-simplicial vertices of I are precisely the maximal-point downsets, dually for D. This uses classical Zorn/compactness to extend each point to a maximal/minimal one. Hence every maximal p of X matches a unique maximal q of Y with ↓_X p=↓_Y q, bijectively; similarly for minimal points. We use the established proconstructible coherent witness descriptions. If V is empty the claim is immediate, so assume V nonempty.

Here an internal edge-path groupoid of a simplicial Stone chain object is constructed objectwise over every Stone test T: take the ordinary groupoid with vertices the T-sections of the vertex object and arrows freely generated by the T-sections of edges, modulo degeneracy and triangle relations, then sheafify its arrows. Left exactness gives an internal groupoid in sheaves of sets; its classifying object is the 1-truncation of the sheaf realization (objectwise nerve groupoid completion computes 1-truncation, then sheafification commutes with colimits, finite limits and truncation). In particular maps of edge generators preserving triangle/degeneracy relations define internal functors; locally agreeing path classes descend uniquely between fixed endpoints. We use a site containing the finite products, closed subspaces, and finite coproducts of the Stone objects occurring here. No hypercompleteness assumption.

## 2. Compactification of matched extrema

Let
\[
U=\overline{\{(p,q): p\in\operatorname{Max}(X),q\in\operatorname{Max}(Y),\ \downarrow_Xp=\downarrow_Yq\}}^{\,V^2},
\]
\[
L=\overline{\{(a,b): a\in\operatorname{Min}(X),b\in\operatorname{Min}(Y),\ \uparrow_Xa=\uparrow_Yb\}}^{\,V^2}.
\]
Both are closed Stone subspaces of V². All (p,q)∈U satisfy
\[
q\le_Xp,\quad p\le_Yq,
\]
and all (a,b)∈L satisfy
\[
a\le_Xb,\quad b\le_Ya.
\]
Indeed these inequalities hold on the original matched pairs by equality of the indicated downsets/upsets, and are closed conditions. The added limit pairs need not be extremal and need not have matching principal cones; **neither is required**. In particular we do not claim the set of extremal points, the graph of their matching, or the equality-of-cones locus is closed.

Build the Stone space R=L⊔V⊔U with disjoint summands. For λ=(a,b)∈L, middle x∈V and μ=(p,q)∈U, put
\[
\lambda<x\iff a\le_X x\ \land\ b\le_Y x,\qquad
x<\mu\iff x\le_X p\ \land\ x\le_Y q.
\]
Put λ<μ iff there exists x∈V with λ<x<μ. Add reflexivity, and no other strict comparisons. This is a partial order of height at most three. Its graph is closed: adjacent-layer incidence conditions are closed, and the lower-to-upper incidence is a projection of a closed subset of L×V×U, hence compact/closed. All finite chain spaces are therefore closed Stone subsets of finite powers. We do NOT need R to be Priestley, and make no assertion that it defines a coherent locale.

Coordinate projections on L and U and identity on V give continuous order-preserving maps f_X:R→V_X and f_Y:R→V_Y. Their order preservation for lower-to-upper edges follows by the intermediate x. Write G_R,G_X,G_Y for the resulting internal edge-path groupoids. The maps induce functors F_X:G_R→G_X and F_Y:G_R→G_Y.

## 3. A key local witness property

For every X-chain tuple x_0≤_X···≤_X x_n (including n=0 and degenerate chains), there exists μ∈U above every x_i in R, and λ∈L below every x_i in R. Indeed choose an actual maximal X-point p above x_n, its matched Y-maximal q; equality of their downsets implies x_i≤_Y q as well. Choose actual minimal a below x_0 and its matched Y-minimal b; equality of their upsets gives b≤_Y x_i. These are actual pairs in U,L. The same argument works for every Y-chain.

Crucially this pointwise existence gives **local sheaf sections** over any Stone T, not a claimed global continuous choice. For example the space of X-chain tuples together with a simultaneous incident upper pair is a closed subset of Ch_n(X)_patch×U, hence compact Stone, and the projection to Ch_n(X)_patch is onto by the argument above. It is a cover in the Stone finite-surjection topology. Pull it back along a T-section. All lower witnesses and simultaneous selections of finitely many witnesses are handled likewise. The lower-to-upper incidence λ<μ itself has an analogous surjective Stone space of intermediate points, by definition and compactness. These are precisely the local choices used below.

## 4. Explicit inverse groupoid functors

Construct G_X→G_R, denoted J_X, on vertices by the inclusion of V as the middle summand. For an X-chain edge x≤_X y, choose locally a simultaneous upper witness μ and assign the R-path
\[
J_X([x,y])=[x,\mu][y,\mu]^{-1}.
\]
Paths are concatenated in traversal order. A simultaneous lower witness λ exists locally and the R-triangles λ<x<μ, λ<y<μ imply
\[
[x,\mu][y,\mu]^{-1}=[\lambda,x]^{-1}[\lambda,y].
\]
Thus the assignment is independent of μ, since the same lower witness works for any two upper witnesses, and equivalently can be computed using any lower witness. Equality on overlaps descends to a unique arrow sheaf section; the assignment is natural in T. It preserves degeneracies. For a chain triangle x≤_X y≤_X z choose a simultaneous upper witness for all three, which gives the triangle relation by cancellation. This defines the functor J_X. The analogous J_Y exists.

**First composite:** F_X J_X=id_G_X, on objects and arrows: the assigned path after projection is [x,p][y,p]^{-1} for p≥_X y, equal to the original [x,y] by an X-chain triangle. Same for Y.

**Second composite:** J_X F_X is naturally isomorphic to id_G_R. Define a path η_r:r→J_X F_X(r) as follows:
- for middle x use identity;
- for lower λ=(a,b), use [λ,a], an edge since a≤_X a and b≤_Y a;
- for upper μ=(p,q), use [p,μ]^{-1}, an inverse edge since p≤_X p and p≤_Y q.

These are morphisms of sheaves because the pair projections and edge incidences are continuous. To verify naturality, for an edge e:r→s show η_r (J_X F_X)(e)=e η_s.
- For x→μ, use μ itself as simultaneous upper witness for the X-edge x≤_X p. Then J_X F_X([x,μ])=[x,μ][p,μ]^{-1}, giving the equality.
- For λ→x, choose a simultaneous upper witness ν for a≤_X x. Both λ<a<ν and λ<x<ν are R-triangles, since a and x are both incident to λ and ν. Thus
\[
[\lambda,a][a,\nu][x,\nu]^{-1}
=[\lambda,\nu][x,\nu]^{-1}
=[\lambda,x].
\]
- For λ→μ, choose a middle point x locally using the incidence witness cover. The R-triangle λ<x<μ factors the edge, so naturality follows from the preceding two cases and descent.
- Degenerate edges are immediate.
These check every generating edge and hence every path. The same proof for Y uses b and q in place of a and p.

Thus F_X,F_Y are equivalences of internal groupoids. The composite F_Y J_X:G_X→G_Y has identity object map on the common h(V) and is an equivalence (indeed its inverse F_X J_Y is inverse on arrows: a natural isomorphism on these composites with identity components at middle points follows from η). This establishes chain 1-type reconstruction without any compactness claim for actual extremal point loci.

## 5. Simultaneous compatibility with both canonical comparisons

Identify the patched I and D nerves by the preceding binary reconstruction result; denote their internal edge-path groupoids G_I,G_D. Let c_X^I,c_Y^I,c_X^D,c_Y^D be the four canonical groupoid functors induced by the chain inclusions.

For an X-edge x≤_X y, choose a simultaneous upper pair μ=(p,q). The comparison of F_Y J_X([x,y]) in G_I is the path [x,q][y,q]^{-1}. Since q is a common Y-upper witness, the Y-upper-hull triangle (x,y,q) identifies this path with the direct edge (x,y) in the common patched I nerve. This is exactly c_X^I([x,y]). For the lower hull, compute J_X via a simultaneous lower pair λ=(a,b). After F_Y the path is [b,x]^{-1}[b,y], and the common D triangle (b,x,y) identifies it with (x,y). This is c_X^D([x,y]). These identities are local and descend as identities of arrow sheaf maps. The object maps agree on V. Hence
\[
c_Y^I F_Y J_X=c_X^I,\qquad c_Y^D F_Y J_X=c_X^D
\]
on the internal groupoids. Taking classifying objects yields the claimed equivalence of both arrows on 1-truncations, compatibly with the common vertex map. QED.

**Precision about reconstruction:** this establishes invariance of the chain 1-type and comparison diagram under isomorphism of the patched binary pair. The compact auxiliary R is built from the *two* realizations of that pair, not directly from the binary data of a single structure. No reconstruction functor on arbitrary binary graph morphisms is claimed, nor recovery of the spectral topologies or of chain 2-types. The six-point example and fixed Cantor middle-layer construction in Binary feedback IV rule out unrestricted higher reconstruction. The paired-extrema proof also recovers the finite result without assuming compactness of a varying extremal locus.
