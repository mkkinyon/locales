# Exact calculation of V in Claude Fable's almost-normal two-chain space

23 October 2026. **New working theorem, independently proved after the external referee's report; request separate audit before promotion.**

## Statement

Let X be the countable T0 almost-normal space constructed in §2.1 of the independent report *Referee report — Project A, the relation V* (23 October 2026): isolated points S={s_t:t∈Q_rat}, two chains Z={z_k:k∈N}, Y={y_l:l∈N}, and the nowhere-dense-robust rational trace filters at distinct irrational limits α_k and β_l. A neighborhood containing z_k contains z_0,...,z_k; similarly for y_l. The filters may be those in the report, with α and β each dense and mutually disjoint.

Put L=O(X), Q=L⊕L, d_I=⋁_{a∧b=0}a⊠b, I=C(d_I), V=fit(I), and V_n the fitted rank≤n approximation. Then

    I = V = V_3  ⊊  V_2 = U = F.

Thus I itself is fitted, and the exact rank-three strip bar from the referee eliminates the entire rank-two fitted defect. The strictly intermediate case I⊊V⊊U does NOT occur in this example.

The frame nucleus of V is therefore exactly

    j_V(q) = q ∨ d_I,    for every q∈Q.

In addition, I has a fully explicit open-neighborhood presentation generated by the rank≤3 bars listed in §5 below.

## 1. Topology of the boundary and scatteredness

Each isolated singleton {s_t} is CLOPEN: it is open by construction, and X\{s_t} is open because for every chain point one can choose small real intervals around its finitely many relevant irrational attachment coordinates avoiding the rational t. Let e=S be the open discrete set and B=X\e=Z⊔Y the closed remainder.

In the subspace topology of Z the opens are ∅, finite initial segments P_k={z_0,...,z_k}, and Z. Similarly for Y. Each P_k is compact because finite, and it is an open neighborhood of z_k. Each chain is sober: its nonempty irreducible closed sets are precisely tails {z_j:j≥k}, with generic point z_k. Thus B is locally compact, sober and spatial.

X is sober as well: any irreducible closed subset meeting a clopen singleton {s_t} must equal that singleton; otherwise it is contained in B, where it is a tail in one of the two clopen chain components.

X is scattered. Any nonempty subset containing an s_t has an isolated point. Otherwise it is a subset of B; choosing its least-indexed z_k (if the subset meets Z), or least-indexed y_l, isolates that point by an ambient open whose chain trace is the corresponding finite prefix. Every finite product of scattered spaces is scattered: isolate a first-coordinate value in the projection of an arbitrary nonempty subset, then an isolated point in the corresponding fiber. Hence ordinary X×X is scattered.

## 2. The *localic* square is spatial

This step is essential and cannot be omitted because X itself is not assumed locally compact.

Let Q=O(X)⊕O(X). Its open sublocale O(e⊠e) is isomorphic to O(S×S) since S is discrete, and is spatial.

The complementary closed sublocale decomposes as

    C(e⊠e) = C(e⊠1) ∨ C(1⊠e)
            ≅ (B×_loc X) ∨ (X×_loc B)

(where the second display is a description of the two summands, not an isomorphism of the join with a coproduct). Each factor B×_loc X and X×_loc B is spatial by the standard locally compact sober product theorem: B is locally compact and sober, and X is spatial/sober.

Finite joins of spatial sublocales are spatial: for sublocales R_i with nuclei j_i, the nucleus of their join is ⋀_i j_i computed pointwise (finite or arbitrary meets of nuclei). If the join is T, then O(T) embeds via the restrictions into ∏_i O(R_i) since j_T(q)=⋀_i j_i(q). An unequal pair in O(T) differs in some spatial quotient, giving a separating point. Thus C(e⊠e) is spatial.

An open sublocale and its complementary closed sublocale join to the whole locale. Hence Q itself is a finite join of spatial sublocales, and is spatial. Its points are pairs of points of X, and rectangle opens generate the ordinary product topology, so

    Q ≅ O(X×_top X).

## 3. Any nonzero sublocale of this square has an ordinary point

General lemma: if T is spatial and scattered, every nonzero sublocale R of O(T) has a point.

Proof: The closure cl(R) is a nonzero closed spatial sublocale corresponding to some nonempty closed F⊆T. Choose an isolated p∈F. Its singleton {p} is a nonzero open sublocale of F. Since R is dense in its closure, R meets that open sublocale nontrivially; the only nonzero sublocale of a one-point locale is the whole point, so p is a point of R.

This is the elementary relevant consequence of Simmons' scattered-space sublocale theorem. We only require the lemma, not the stronger classification of all sublocales.

Apply to T=X×X, which is scattered by §1 and represents Q by §2.

## 4. Point set of I

In any spatial square the complement of d_I is the set of pairs having no disjoint open neighborhoods. In the present X:

- Any pair (z_k,z_l) is inseparable because all neighborhoods contain z_0.
- Any pair (y_k,y_l) is inseparable because all neighborhoods contain y_0.
- Every (s_t,s_t) is inseparable.
- Distinct s_t,s_u are separated by their clopen singletons.
- Every s_t and any chain point are separated by {s_t} and its open complement.
- A z_k and a y_l have disjoint opens: take disjoint small real intervals around the finite α_0,...,α_k and β_0,...,β_l and use them as neighborhood traces.

Therefore the point set of I is exactly

    F = (Z×Z) ∪ (Y×Y) ∪ Δ_S,
    Δ_S={(s_t,s_t):t∈Q_rat}.

In particular d_I is the topological open (X×X)\F, and F is the ordinary closure of the diagonal in X×X. The latter equality is about the point set of the closed inseparability sublocale; it does not identify I with the actual diagonal locale.

## 5. Rank≤3 bars eliminate every off-I point

For t∈Q_rat let c_t={s_t} (clopen) and e_t=X\c_t. Define

    v_t=(e_t⊠1)∨(1⊠c_t).

This has an explicit H→V tree of edge depth ≤2:
- Root first-coordinate cover 1=e_t∨c_t.
- (e_t,1) is a v_t leaf.
- Below (c_t,1), vertical cover 1=e_t∨c_t; (c_t,e_t) is a D leaf (disjoint), and (c_t,c_t) is a v_t leaf.

Consequently v_t is a bar and excludes every pair (s_t,x) with x≠s_t. Its transpose τv_t excludes every (x,s_t) with x≠s_t.

Now take the external referee's independently checked rank-three bar

    w=(e_Z⊠1)∨(1⊠e_Y),
    e_Z=X\Z, e_Y=X\Y.

Its H→V→H tree is given explicitly in the referee's §2.1(d). It excludes the entire ordinary block Z×Y. Its transpose τw is another rank≤3 bar and excludes Y×Z.

Every point outside F is in one of these excluded cases. All the v_t, τv_t, w, τw are bars and therefore contain the closed sublocale I. Define

    W = O(w) ∧ O(τw) ∧ ⋀_{t∈Q_rat}(O(v_t)∧O(τv_t)).

Then I⊆W, and the point set of W is exactly F.

If W ≠ I, since I=C(d_I) is complemented by O(d_I), finite distributivity in the sublocale coframe shows that W∧O(d_I) is nonzero. By §3 this nonzero sublocale has a point, which would be a point of W outside F, contradiction. Thus W=I.

As all displayed bars have rank ≤3, V_3⊆W=I; but I⊆V⊆V_3 always. Therefore

    I = V = V_3 = W.

The previously established and externally audited almost-normal rank-two theorem yields V_2=U=F. The referee's pair (z_0,y_0) belongs to U but is excluded by w, proving V_2⊋V_3. This gives the full stated chain.

## 6. Exact description

The frame nucleus is

    j_V(q)=q∨d_I.

Under Q≅O(X×X), this is the closed subspace nucleus

    j_V(q)=q ∪ ((X×X)\F).

The sublocale V is the closed sublocale supported on the subspace F=(Z×Z)∪(Y×Y)∪Δ_S with its inherited topology. Do not interpret this union as a topological coproduct: Δ_S can accumulate at points in the chain-square blocks.

The countable rank≤3 neighborhood formula is

    V=I = O(w)∧O(τw)∧⋀_t(O(v_t)∧O(τv_t)).

This is an explicit non-tautological fitted description for THIS example, not a general intrinsic dual formula for arbitrary locales.
