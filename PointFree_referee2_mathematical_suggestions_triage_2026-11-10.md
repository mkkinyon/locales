# Point-Free Topology — Second referee's mathematical suggestions: triage and priorities

**10 November 2026.** Source: user-uploaded \`claude_ideas_2.txt\`, supplied *after* the independent blind coherent chain/higher-hull report. The referee states expressly that **none of the eight suggestions had a second reader**. They remain **referee-origin ideas**, not claims covered by the original blind audit. Our assessments below are preliminary checks, not an external verification. The earlier referee's independently generated suggestions are registered at [first referee suggestions](PointFree_referee_post_audit_suggestions_priorities_2026-11-10.md). ## Ranked assessment (rank = current usefulness, not depth or novelty)

| Current rank | Referee item | Assessment and disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **2. Nonconstant suspension test.** Take Priestley X on a Stone set S plus two isolated maxima p,q above every s∈S. | **Keep; top priority for the next research segment.** The two closed downsets ↓p, ↓q have contractible chain nerves and intersection S with trivial specialization; Čech codescent gives \(\mathbf C(X)\simeq 1\amalg_{h(S)}1=\Sigma_{\rm unred}h(S)\). Each relative upper hull over the intersection is the full Čech nerve of S→1, so \(\mathbf I(X)\simeq1\); the canonical arrow comparison gives \(\mathbf D(X)\simeq1\). For finite discrete S this recovers the four-point control; Cantor S is a genuinely nonconstant *candidate*, deserving a proof that its suspended sheaf is not a constant object (e.g. via its augmented free-abelian sheaf). **Do not yet call the nonconstancy proved in the canonical ledger.** Also check how this differs from a trivial product S×P. |
| **2** | **1. Mirrored D-side codescent for finite compact-open covers.** | **Keep; strong and readily testable.** If \(U_i\) are a finite cover by compact opens (patch-clopen upsets), a chain is captured by a U_i containing its first vertex, and a tuple with a common *lower* witness z is captured by any U_i containing z. Thus Čech descent should hold for \(\mathbf C\) and **relative** D-hulls \(D_n^X(U_S)=D_n(X)\wedge U_S^{n+1}\). This is not the *unpatched* descent of witness projections \(s_q\), which remains false in general. Reverse-order Priestley duality explains the result, but the localization argument is independent and should be preferred. The referee's expression “reversing chains is an automorphism of Δ” needs precise bookkeeping: the order reversal \([n]\mapsto[n]^{op}\), identified with \([n]\), acts by conjugation on simplicial operators. |
| **3** | **3. Products.** | **Keep as useful structural lemma.** Degreewise chain/higher closed/higher fitted hulls commute with finite products of coherent locales; Patch and realization preserve the relevant products, so \(\mathbf C,\mathbf I,\mathbf D\) and the *canonical arrows* are multiplicative. This proves SNC and chain→I equivalence classes closed under finite products. Examples \(P\times S\) are nonconstant when S is nontrivial Stone but the dependence on S is a trivial external product, so not a compelling main example of *genuinely new* sheaf-level homotopy. |
| **4** | **6. Order adjunctions.** | **Keep as short proof tool.** If spectral maps f:P→Q and g:Q→P satisfy \(gf\ge id_P\) and \(fg\le id_Q\), the comparisons give natural transformations on the internal specialization categories. Hence C(f), C(g) are mutually inverse equivalences. This generalizes finite comparable-endomorphism zigzags. Source and target and order orientation must be made explicit. |
| **5** | **5. Fiberwise certificates with continuous choices.** | **Defer; plausible but underspecified.** For an order-family over a Stone base S, a *patch-continuous internal* terminal/initial selection yields an extra-degeneracy contraction **over** h(S). Merely checking each ordinary fibre is contractible is not sufficient without a descent/continuity argument in the sheaf ∞-topos. Define the parametrized object and morphism category before claiming a theorem. This may become more valuable after investigating item 2. |
| **6** | **7. Finite-stage comparison.** | **Keep as a distinct open problem, but correct its motivation.** Spectral maps X→P_λ induce a canonical comparison \(\mathbf C(X)\to\lim_\lambda\underline{|Ord(P_\lambda)|}\), under suitable site and diagram conventions. Referee-origin fence W shows naive equivalence fails. The question “when is this an equivalence?” is worth studying, but **is not itself a formulation or computation of intrinsic localic shape**. It compares our patched invariant with a limit of constant objects. Separate possible pro-homotopy from actual sheaf limits and maintain size qualifications. |
| **7** | **4. Acyclic noncontractible B under two maxima.** | **Keep as an instructive counterexample, but decline an open-ended homological generalization for now.** Given a finite poset B whose order complex is connected, acyclic, and noncontractible (e.g. the face poset of a finite acyclic non-simply-connected CW/simplicial complex), adjoining two maxima above B produces a suspension of Ord(B), which is contractible by simple connectivity plus acyclicity/Whitehead. The two witness complexes are contractible, but B_S=B violates the B2 criterion. Thus B2 is sufficient, not necessary. **Do not assert Cameron–Kumar's informal phrase is literally false without quoting and checking the precise original text.** Their *theorem* remains correct. A homological test alone would not establish homotopy equivalence; “plus a π₁ statement” needs formulation. |
| **8** | **8. Countably based Stone site.** | **Reject as a general replacement for size bookkeeping.** Many examples constructed so far have countable compact-open lattices, so a smaller test site may be useful **for those examples**. But the results quantify over arbitrary coherent frames, often with uncountable K; restricting representables to countably based Stone spaces changes the ambient theory and does not automatically resolve full faithfulness/site independence. Keep as a later technical option, not a substantive mathematical research line. |

## Comparison with first referee and what to do next

**First referee, 10 Nov:** Highest largely unpursued original direction was Čech–Vietoris refinement pro-systems; the chain-nerve and finite-poset suggestions were subsequently pursued and audited. Classification of unpatched s_q descent remains deferred, and the human/formal non-descent verification remains outstanding.

**Second referee, 10 Nov:** Lead **item 2**, the suspension \(\Sigma h(S)\), directly targets the newly articulated problem of *genuinely nonconstant sheaf homotopy*. Item **1**, the open-cover dual, supplies an immediate complementary tool for that investigation. Structural lemmas **3** and **6** should be folded into the canonical mathematics when checked, but do not require a separate research campaign. Items **4,5,7** are parked carefully, and **8** is not a general solution.

**Combined short active queue after repository consolidation:**

1. Prove/disprove sheaf-nonconstancy of \(\Sigma h(S)\) for a concrete infinite Stone S (Cantor); calculate actual sheaf homology/π₁ and distinguish it from \(\underline{\Sigma|S_{\rm set}|}\).
2. Verify and add the *relative* finite compact-open **D-side codescent theorem**; do not confuse with unpatched lower-witness descent.
3. Use both finite closed/open codescent, products and adjunctions to find a natural genuinely varying coherent family with computable \(\mathbf C\).
4. Return to first referee's refinement/pro-homotopy perspective, now informed by fence W and the failure of naïve limits.
5. Later: Esakia/co-Esakia classification of s_q, fibrewise/parametrized contractibility, and adequate homological weakening of the finite crosscut hypothesis.

**Provenance:** The eight numbered suggestions and the original suggested priority “first two” belong to the **second referee**; all ranking and cautionary qualifications above are ours. This document is a follow-up register, not an amendment to the independent blind report.
