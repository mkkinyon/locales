## 2. Repairs classified by severity

### R1 — Important: \((G')\) and RC evidence provenance

The compact-Hausdorff **full sublocale generation** (G′) is explicitly **“Theorem 3.1 (G′, referee-origin)”** in the corrected RC canonical. The original blind submission was compact-metric clique associativity. A separate post-report referee developed the closed-support theorem and mixed rectangles; a later independent structural review tested mixed RC. The new synthesis says “the separately checked full-sublocale generation” without explaining exactly which part was blind and which was post-verdict. **Repair:** label G′ *referee-origin/post-verdict, subsequently internally reconciled and used in independently reviewed mixed RC*, instead of letting readers infer original-blind certification for the stronger G′. This is evidence bookkeeping, not a flaw in the proof.

### R2 — Moderate: display the actual second half of the \((G')\) proof

Showing every qualified \(\mathcal B(K)\le[A]\times[D]\) is easy by density; **showing those terms generate the entire product** needs compact Hausdorff **fitness**. If their join \(J\) is proper below \(q(A,D)\), find an ambient open \(O\ge J\) not containing \(q\), take its closed complement \(F\), and let \(W=F\wedge q\ne0\). The least closed support \(K'\subseteq F\) of \(W\) is qualified, forcing \(0\ne\mathcal B(K')\le J\wedge F=0\). The synthesis should include this succinct contradiction; otherwise a reader may mistake **dense support detection** for a completed generation theorem.

### R3 — Moderate: avoid \(\mathcal B\) symbol collision

The manuscript sets \(\mathcal B=\mathcal A^\smile\) as the converse specialization relation but later writes \(\mathcal B(K)\) for **Isbell Booleanization**. The function argument technically disambiguates them, yet this is avoidable cognitive risk in the principal proof narrative. **Repair:** write simply \(\mathcal A^\smile\) for converse specialization; reserve \(\mathcal B(K)\) for Booleanization.

### R4 — Moderate: put witnesses and scope on “strict \(\mathcal V<\mathcal V_{\mathrm{fin}}\)”

The expression denotes **an example**, not a universal strict inequality. The canonical [Vfin strictness proof](A-Vfin-negative-consolidated-proof-2026-10-23.md) constructs explicit spaces. State: “there exists a countable first-countable \(T_1\) example with \(\mathcal V\subsetneq\mathcal V_{\rm fin}\),” while the later sober \(T_0\) simplification has a different post-referee status. Retain that finite-rank bar strictness is not just an observation of unbounded finite ranks.

### R5 — Minor but mathematical: reinforce finite and empty-case conventions

In the 5-point example include the explicit \(|\mathcal I|=15,|\mathcal D|=17,|\mathcal I^2|=23\) count, or another direct proof that only \((m,n),(n,m)\) fail. In the six-point example include the two f-vectors and remind the reader that an explicit finite cone proves **sharpness**, not the general 2-connectivity theorem. The proof-guided text already makes the latter restriction; supplying the counts makes it more independently readable. For the rectangle-unit theorem, the empty \(G\) case is “unital” only under the **one-element zero-algebra convention**; the statement in the account should include that parenthetical.

### R6 — Editorial: signpost site/conditional status and archive roles

The account correctly says higher results live in a chosen patched Stone-sheaf topos, but typography should keep the site restriction immediately next to every central higher theorem. Continue to retain original fourth-audit P1–P6 imported premises, and avoid a blanket claim that a single outside reader checked the whole 47-group ledger. The repository's original A/B/C ledger stays unmodified because this is an **exposition-level** audit, not theorem-number reclassification. No need to re-open broad TWIST or product-spatiality classification in this pass.

## 3. Important negative findings

The following **tempting stronger conclusions are *not* warranted** by the audited account and have *not* been asserted in its current repaired design:
- All localic relation composition is associative; MAT*/MUL* is binary, FINMAT* is a **subalgebra** theorem.
- The topology on a scattered index disappears; the sublocale lattice decomposes, but the ambient locale need not be a discrete coproduct and index-open constraints remain.
- Finite-join distributivity implies arbitrary infinite-join distributivity; Plewe explicitly distinguishes the latter.
- Plewe/Isbell spatial multiplier criteria and the *method* of closed supports were independently invented in this project; their published antecedents are acknowledged.
- \(\mathbf C\to\mathbf M\) is a 2-type equivalence, or patched Stone-sheaf realizations are already intrinsic localic shape.
- Finite \(\mathcal V\) proof-tree rank, \(\omega+m\) hull-iteration rank, and localic relational-composition rank are one common invariant.
