## 0. Notation and finite compatibility

Let L be an ARBITRARY frame (hence a bounded distributive lattice if arbitrary joins are forgotten), M a nonzero COMPLETE BOOLEAN ALGEBRA, and h,k:L→M FRAME maps. Finite-cover compatibility is
\[
\boxed{\bigvee_{i=1}^n h(c_i)\wedge k(c_i)=1_M
\quad\text{whenever }c_1\vee\cdots\vee c_n=1_L.}
\tag{0.1}
\]
The joins in (0.1) are finite. Let \(S=\mathrm{Stone}(M)\) be the compact Hausdorff Stone space of M, extremally disconnected because M is complete.

Let \(X_{\mathrm{fin}}=\operatorname{Spec}_{\rm lat}(L)\) be the **prime-filter spectrum of the bounded DISTRIBUTIVE LATTICE underlying L**, with compact-open basis \(\widehat a=\{P:a\in P\}\) for \(a\in L\). This spectrum is spectral compact with patch topology compact Hausdorff and specialization order \(P\preceq Q\iff P\subseteq Q\).

**IMPORTANT:** \(X_{\rm fin}\) is NOT in general the original locale Y=Loc(L). Its points are FINITELY PRIME filters of L, not necessarily COMPLETELY prime frame filters. In particular \(\mathrm{Stone}(M)\) uses ordinary Boolean ultrafilters, not completely prime points of the noncompact Boolean locale with frame M. Conflating these is exactly the missing infinitary step.

## 1. The universal bounded-lattice selection theorem

**Theorem 1.1 (Boolean prime predecessor selection; NEW INTERNAL).** For every L,M,h,k satisfying (0.1), there exists a BOUNDED DISTRIBUTIVE-LATTICE homomorphism
\[
\boxed{p_{\rm fin}:L\to M,\qquad
p_{\rm fin}(a)\le h(a)\wedge k(a)\quad(\forall a\in L).}
\tag{1.1}
\]
It preserves finite joins, finite meets, bottom and top. It is NOT claimed to preserve arbitrary joins.

*Proof.* Restrict h,k to their bounded-lattice structures. Under Stone/Priestley duality they determine PATCH-continuous maps \(\widehat h,\widehat k:S\to X_{\rm fin,patch}\), specified by
\[
a\in\widehat h(s)\iff h(a)\in s,\quad
a\in\widehat k(s)\iff k(a)\in s.
\]
The specialization order on X_fin is CLOSED in the patch product: violation \(P\not\subseteq Q\) is witnessed by some a with \(a\in P\), \(a\notin Q\), which is a clopen patch condition.

**Pointwise common lower prime filter.** For any s∈S, let H_s,K_s be the two prime filters. Put F_s=H_s∩K_s, a proper filter. Every finite join cover \(\bigvee_i c_i=1_L\) contains some c_i∈F_s: otherwise each \(h(c_i)\wedge k(c_i)\notin s\), contradicting (0.1) and finite primeness of the Boolean ultrafilter s. Hence the IDEAL generated by \(L\setminus F_s\) is proper: if it contained 1, some FINITE join of elements OUTSIDE F_s would equal 1, contradiction. Extend this proper ideal to a PRIME ideal by the distributive lattice prime ideal theorem (classical choice). Its complement P_s is a prime filter contained in F_s, i.e. \(P_s\preceq H_s,K_s\). This is also the topological fact that the closures of the two spectral points meet.

Define
\[
R=\{(s,P)\in S\times X_{\rm fin,patch}:
P\preceq\widehat h(s),\ P\preceq\widehat k(s)\}.
\]
The order is patch-closed, so R is compact Hausdorff. Its projection onto S is surjective by the pointwise argument. By GLEASON projectivity of the Stonean S, the projection has a CONTINUOUS section. Compose with the second projection to obtain a PATCH-continuous \(\widehat p:S\to X_{\rm fin,patch}\) with \(\widehat p(s)\preceq\widehat h(s),\widehat k(s)\).

For each a∈L, the pullback of the patch-CLOPEN compact-open \(\widehat a\subset X_{\rm fin}\) is clopen in S and hence corresponds to an element \(p_{\rm fin}(a)\in\operatorname{Clop}(S)=M\). These values form a BOUNDED LATTICE homomorphism because \(\widehat{a\vee b}=\widehat a\cup\widehat b\) and \(\widehat{a\wedge b}=\widehat a\cap\widehat b\). From the pointwise specialization inequalities follows \(p_{\rm fin}(a)\le h(a)\wedge k(a)\) for all a. QED.

**Adversarial check:** The proof NEVER asserts the selected prime filters are completely prime. They need not be. The Stone ultrafilters do NOT preserve infinite Boolean joins, and the lattice spectrum of L need not represent the frame locale. Those omissions are deliberate and precisely account for failure of frame-level selection in noncoherent locales.

## 2. An exact marked infinite-join defect for any Boolean fitting witness

**Corollary 2.1 (selected prime predecessor must break the marked cover).** Suppose in addition that C⊆L is ANY (possibly infinite) frame cover with
\[
\boxed{\bigvee_{c\in C}h(c)\wedge k(c)=0_M.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Then EVERY bounded-lattice predecessor \(p_{\rm fin}\le h,k\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm fin}(1)=1_M,\qquad
p_{\rm fin}(c)=0_M\quad\forall c\in C,\qquad
\bigvee_{c\in C}p_{\rm fin}(c)=0_M<1_M
=p_{\rm fin}\Bigl(\bigvee_{c\in C}c\Bigr).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
In particular p_fin cannot be a FRAME map. The same holds on EVERY nonzero Boolean condition m by restricting to the interval \(\downarrow m\) and applying Theorem 1.1 there.

*Proof.* Each matching term h(c)∧k(c)=0 because their join is zero. Thus p_fin(c)=0. But frame cover C has join 1, and any bounded lattice hom sends 1 to 1. Restriction to a nonzero Boolean m preserves finite compatibility and the marked zero cover, and the theorem applies with M=↓m. QED.

**Application to Q′/Q canonical holes:** A nonzero canonical relatively OPEN hole E_C⊆W₂ has complete Boolean frame M=O(B(E_C)) and coordinate h,k finite compatible with marked C matching 0. Hence (2.2) applies. The result gives an entirely algebraic version of "noncompact infinitary self-similarity": the hole forces a continuously selected Boolean-valued **prime predecessor** which is NOT a genuine locale point because it annihilates an actual cover.

**Important NO-GO:** Exactly the SAME conclusion (2.2) follows for the weak fitting-defect Boolean witness on the completed branching Scott locale, where W=W₂=U. Thus (2.2) alone does NOT distinguish a genuine relatively OPEN Q/Q′ hole from weak D₂>D. Relative openness remains the indispensable missing hypothesis.

## 3. Coherence is exactly where the selected predecessor can be upgraded in this proof

When L is coherent with compact-element lattice K, apply the Gleason construction to \(\operatorname{Spec}(K)\), not the oversized spectrum \(\operatorname{Spec}_{lat}(L)\). Every compact c∈K is genuinely compact relative to infinite frame joins, and L=Idl(K). The resulting bounded lattice p_K:K→M extends as
\[
\boxed{p(a)=\bigvee_{c\in K,\ c\le a}p_K(c)}
\]
to a FRAME homomorphism ≤h∧k, as fully checked in [the coherent selection note](ProjectA_Q_COHERENT_BOOLEAN_PREDECESSOR_GLEASON_PATCH_SELECTION_2026-11-12.md). The key is finite generation of each compact c whenever \(c\le\bigvee_i a_i\).

This demonstrates why a proof of Q purely by finite Boolean selection will be insufficient: **finite lattice selection is ALWAYS possible**, even in bad cover cases. Any genuine advance must force the selected p to preserve a specific infinitary cover join, using the canonical hole's relatively OPEN position or a strong structural hypothesis on L.

## 4. Exact relation to the referee's nowhere-distributivity obstruction

The independently reviewed referee lemma shows that a finite-compatible Boolean witness with C matching zero has the fixed family of Boolean predicates \(\{h(c):c\in C\}\) NOWHERE deciding a complete binary partition below any nonzero Boolean condition; it witnesses failure of \((|C|,2)\)-distributivity throughout M.

The universal lattice predecessor p_fin produces a COMPLEMENTARY formulation of the same infinitary phenomenon:
- the h(c),k(c) Boolean truth values split indefinitely on every condition;
- nevertheless a continuous Stonean **prime-filter** selection p_fin exists pointwise in the compact patch spectrum;
- the selected prime filters all *miss* at least the marked infinite cover C, so NONE is a completely prime frame point of Y.

Thus the obstruction is not the absence of a selectable common lower prime filter. Rather, it is the inability to select one which RESPECTS THE FRAME'S infinitary joins. The regular-closed or finite-rank Q-hypotheses may constrain this failure further, but no theorem is proved here.
