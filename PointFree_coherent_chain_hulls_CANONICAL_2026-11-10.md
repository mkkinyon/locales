## 0. Conventions, site, and mathematical scope

All locales below are classical. A **coherent locale** \(X\) has frame \(\mathcal O(X)=\operatorname{Idl}K(X)\) for its bounded distributive lattice \(K(X)\) of compact opens. Spectral points are prime filters of \(K(X)\), and the specialization order is
\[
x\le y\iff(\forall k\in K(X))\,[x\in k\Rightarrow y\in k].
\]
Thus spectral opens are **upsets**, and closed subspaces are **downsets**. We use the classical prime-filter theorem, not constructive point-freeness, when identifying proconstructible spectral sublocales by their Priestley point sets.

Write \(A_X\hookrightarrow X^2\) for the actual coherent localic specialization relation, and
\[
\operatorname{Ch}_n(X)=\{(x_0,\ldots,x_n):x_0\le\cdots\le x_n\}
\]
for its localic simplicial nerve, defined by finite intersections of pullbacks of \(A_X\). The higher diagonal hulls are
\[
I_n(X)=\operatorname{cl}(\Delta_{n+1}),\qquad D_n(X)=\operatorname{fit}(\Delta_{n+1}).
\]
**Important:** the subscripts \(n\) on \(I_n,D_n\) mean *arity \(n+1\)*. For coherent \(X\), their proconstructible spectral points are exactly tuples having, respectively, an upper bound and a lower bound **in \(X\)**. There are natural canonical simplicial inclusions \(i_I:\operatorname{Ch}_\bullet(X)\hookrightarrow I_\bullet(X)\) and \(i_D:\operatorname{Ch}_\bullet(X)\hookrightarrow D_\bullet(X)\).

Let \(\mathscr H_\kappa\) be the \(\infty\)-topos of sheaves of spaces on a sufficiently large essentially small site of \(\kappa\)-small Stone locales closed under the relevant finite limits and coproducts, with finite jointly surjective families as covers, **including the empty family covering the empty locale**. Taking \(\kappa\) sufficiently large for each \(X\) avoids a false size-independent assertion; independence of the chosen \(\kappa\) has not been proved. Representables are sheaves, finite jointly surjective families are effective epimorphisms, and sheafified Yoneda \(h\) preserves finite limits, finite coproducts, and the initial object. The constant-object functor \(\underline{(-)}:\mathcal S\to\mathscr H_\kappa\) is fully faithful here: evaluation at the terminal Stone point preserves colimits because every nonempty Stone covering of the point admits a section, and is a left inverse of the constant functor. This is enough to distinguish \(\underline{S^1}\) from \(1\).

The **Patch** construction is understood on coherent locales and spectral maps; it preserves the finite limits used here, and converts the proconstructible coherent sublocales into Stone sublocales. We form three sheaf-homotopy objects:
\[
\mathbf C(X)=\left|h(\operatorname{Patch}\operatorname{Ch}_\bullet(X))\right|,\quad
\mathbf I(X)=\left|h(\operatorname{Patch} I_\bullet(X))\right|,\quad
\mathbf D(X)=\left|h(\operatorname{Patch} D_\bullet(X))\right|.
\]
Geometric realization is colimit in \(\mathscr H_\kappa\). **These are not asserted to compute the intrinsic shape of \(X\)**, nor do our results assert equivalences of unpatched simplicial locales.

The actual coherent image lemma used throughout: spectral-image subsets of proconstructible spectral sublocales are again proconstructible spectral; if a spatial spectral source map has point image contained in a proconstructible sublocale, the map factors through that sublocale. The **source's** spatiality as well as the target's is used for pointwise factorization. This claim is classical/spectral, not a blanket assertion that arbitrary sublocales are point-determined.

## 1. Coherent lattice presentations and finite approximations (Claims A1–A2)

Let \(K=K(X)\) and \(T_n(K)=\coprod_{j=0}^n K\) in bounded distributive lattices, writing \(k^{(j)}\) for the \(j\)-th copy.

The compact-open lattices of the actual coherent sublocales above are the quotients of \(T_n(K)\) generated by:

1. **Chain:** \(k^{(j)}\le k^{(j+1)}\) for all \(k\in K\), \(0\le j<n\).
2. **Closed higher hull:** \(\bigwedge_{j=0}^n k_j^{(j)}=0\) whenever \(\bigwedge_j k_j=0\) in \(K\).
3. **Fitted higher hull:** for each finite cover \(U\subseteq K\) with \(\bigvee U=1\),
\[
\bigvee_{u\in U}\bigwedge_{j=0}^n u^{(j)}=1.
\]

For arbitrary locales the diagonal-hull presentations involve all opens and all (possibly infinite) covers; compact-open finitarization holds **because \(X\) is coherent**. For the fitted hull, joins of congruences present meets of the corresponding open sublocales; the equations above are actual quotient equations, not just conditions on points.

As \(K\) is the directed union of its finitely generated finite bounded sublattices \(K_\lambda\), the quotient presentations give simplicial coherent inverse limits, in every degree:
\[
\operatorname{Ch}_n(X)\cong\varprojlim_\lambda\operatorname{Ch}_n(P_\lambda),
\quad I_n(X)\cong\varprojlim_\lambda I_n(P_\lambda),\quad D_n(X)\cong\varprojlim_\lambda D_n(P_\lambda),
\]
where \(P_\lambda=\operatorname{Spec}(K_\lambda)\). The bonding maps need not remain surjective after restriction to chain sublocales. **No realization/inverse-limit interchange follows**; §8 exhibits a referee-origin counterexample.

## 2. Finite-poset backdrop, with correct Cameron–Kumar attribution

For a finite poset \(P\), let \(K_{\rm up}(P)\) and \(K_{\rm low}(P)\) be the simplicial complexes of nonempty finite subsets with a common upper or lower bound. Let \(M\) be the maximal elements of \(P\) and \(B_S=\bigcap_{m\in S}\downarrow m\). The maximal crosscut complex is \(N_M=\{S\subseteq M:B_S\ne\varnothing\}\).

**Known theorem, attribution:** Cameron–Kumar, *Graphs on topological spaces*, arXiv:2610.14783v1, **§9** (their opposite specialization convention) already show \(K_{\rm up}\simeq K_{\rm low}\simeq N_M\), and under contractibility of every nonempty \(\operatorname{Ord}(B_S)\), an abstract homotopy equivalence with \(\operatorname{Ord}(P)\). Their \(\mathcal N_{\rm ins},\mathcal N_{\rm cm},\operatorname{Dow}\) correspond to our \(K_{\rm up},K_{\rm low},N_M\). The four-point pseudo-circle control and face-poset corollary also appear in their work in corresponding convention. **Neither the type-level theorem nor its contractible-intersection hypothesis is claimed here as new.**

**Additional canonical-map formulation (blind-refereed):** Under that intersection hypothesis, the actual canonical maps
\[
\operatorname{Ord}(P)\to K_{\rm up}(P),\qquad
\operatorname{Ord}(P)\to K_{\rm low}(P)
\]
are homotopy equivalences. There is also a strong simplicial collapse of \(K_{\rm low}\) onto its maximal-element crosscut subcomplex; this precise collapse is not stated in Cameron–Kumar.

Proof route: cover \(\operatorname{Ord}(P)\) and \(K_{\rm up}\) by the matching \(\operatorname{Ord}(\downarrow m)\) and \(\Delta(\downarrow m)\), respectively. The finite nerve lemma **for the canonical inclusion of covers** gives the upper map. The lower map follows automatically from the canonical edgewise comparison in §3. An older independent Quillen-fibre proof is sound **after stating the last-vertex map explicitly**: on barycentric subdivisions use \(\sigma\mapsto\max\sigma\), compare by a common carrier simplex \(\Phi(\sigma_0)\), not by an incorrectly described literal subdivided map. Historical note: [finite-poset comparison](PointFree_finite_poset_crosscut_chain_witness_comparison_2026-11-10.md).

For finite \(P\), the simplicial sets \(I_\bullet(P)\) and \(D_\bullet(P)\) are the **ordered-tuple** models of \(K_{\rm up}(P),K_{\rm low}(P)\). Their realizations identify with these ordinary complexes, and the localic chain inclusions recover the canonical order-complex inclusions (up to the conventional ordering/linear-extension equivalence). This supplies the missing explicit bridge between finite combinatorics and the coherent localic constructions.

## 3. Patched rectangle and the canonical edgewise arrow theorem (Claims C1–C3)

Define
\[
E_{p,q}(A_X)=\{(x_0,\ldots,x_p;y_0,\ldots,y_q):x_i\le y_j\quad\forall i,j\}.
\]
These are intersections of pullbacks of the actual specialization relation, not image-composed relations. For coherent \(X\), horizontal/vertical simplicial directions are **strict Čech nerves** of the upper/lower witness maps; after Patch the relevant Stone maps are surjective and their Čech realizations recover their targets. Iterated realization equals diagonal realization in the sheaf \(\infty\)-topos. Let
\[
\mathbf E(X)=\left|h(\operatorname{Patch}\operatorname{diag}E_{\bullet,\bullet}(A_X))\right|.
\]
The two projections give equivalences \(\mathbf E(X)\overset{\sim}\to\mathbf I(X)\) and \(\mathbf E(X)\overset{\sim}\to\mathbf D(X)\).

Let \(\epsilon:\Delta\to\Delta\) be the ordinal-sum functor \([n]\mapsto[n]*[n]=[2n+1]\). The strict localic simplicial map
\[
F_n:\operatorname{Ch}_{2n+1}(X)\to E_{n,n}(A_X)
\]
splits a long chain into its first and second \((n+1)\)-blocks. Transitivity forces all cross-block comparisons. The block projections \(a,b:\epsilon^*\operatorname{Ch}_\bullet\to\operatorname{Ch}_\bullet\) realize to equivalences, and to homotopic maps.

**Most economical proof of the last claim:** the edgewise functor \(\epsilon^{op}\) is cofinal on \(\Delta^{op}\) (the subdivision of each simplex is contractible); the two natural block inclusions \(\mathrm{id}\Rightarrow\epsilon\) induce the canonical cofinal colimit map. This works in any \(\infty\)-category admitting realizations. One can alternatively use Brown–Chan–Galatius–Payne, Prop. 3.8 and Cor. 3.9, **not** merely the usual geometric-realization homeomorphism, which by itself does not identify the block projections.

**Explicit homotopy (still valid and optional):** for monotone \(\theta:[n]\to[1]\),
\[
H_n((x_0,\dots,x_{2n+1}),\theta)
=(x_{i+(n+1)\theta(i)})_{i=0}^n.
\]
All selected indices increase. Naturality in \(\Delta\) gives a simplicial homotopy \(a\simeq b\) already before patching. In fact this formula works for **every simplicial object**, not only chain nerves; the tensor with \(\Delta[1]\) uses finite coproducts, already included in §0.

The composites \(\epsilon^*\operatorname{Ch}\to E\to I,D\) are \(i_I\circ a\) and \(i_D\circ b\). After realization, the two projections \(\mathbf E\to\mathbf I,\mathbf D\) identify the two arrows from \(\mathbf C\). We obtain the central theorem:
\[
\boxed{(\mathbf C(X)\to\mathbf I(X))\simeq(\mathbf C(X)\to\mathbf D(X))
\quad\text{in }\mathbf C(X)/\mathscr H_\kappa.}
\]
Hence **one canonical map is an equivalence iff the other is**. The equivalence of their homotopy cofibres \(\mathbf I\amalg_{\mathbf C}1\simeq\mathbf D\amalg_{\mathbf C}1\) is a *separate consequent*; do **not** infer equivalence of arrows from cofibres (contractible cofibre does not imply equivalence). The statement does not establish unpatched D-side witness descent; the prior independently verified non-descent obstruction remains.

## 4. One finite closed-cover Čech codescent theorem (Claims D1 and F1)

Let \(Z_i=C(u_i)\hookrightarrow X\) be any **finite closed-sublocale cover** with \(\bigwedge_i u_i=0\), and for \(S\ne\varnothing\), \(Z_S=\bigcap_{i\in S}Z_i=C(\bigvee_{i\in S}u_i)\).

**Referee-origin extension:** \(u_i\) need not be compact. For coherent \(X\), each closed \(Z_i\) is spectral, proconstructible/patch-closed, and specialization-downward-closed, so the finite patched Stone covers and intersection identities remain valid. Compactness matters only for the interval-algebra certificates of §5.

Define **relative ambient upper hull**
\[
H_n^{X,\uparrow}(Z)=I_n(X)\cap Z^{n+1};
\]
this is **not** generally \(I_n(Z)\), since an upper witness can lie in \(X\setminus Z\).

Then, simultaneously in every simplicial degree, the finite families \(\operatorname{Ch}_n(Z_i)\to\operatorname{Ch}_n(X)\) and \(H_n^{X,\uparrow}(Z_i)\to I_n(X)\) are jointly surjective **after Patch**: choose a cover member containing the chain's last vertex, respectively the common upper witness. Their Čech intersections are the corresponding \(Z_S\) chain and ambient-hull objects.

The two resulting Čech diagrams are compatible with the canonical map. Therefore:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf C(X)&\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[r]}
\mathbf C(Z_S)\quad\text{(Čech/alternating intersection diagram, with appropriate multiplicities)},\\
\mathbf I(X)&\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[r]}
\left|h(\operatorname{Patch} H_\bullet^{X,\uparrow}(Z_S))\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]
**Interpret the displayed colimits as the full Čech nerve of the finite family**, not an unqualified colimit of a naive poset diagram. Whenever every relative local inclusion \(\mathbf C(Z_S)\to|h\operatorname{Patch}H_\bullet^{X,\uparrow}(Z_S)|\) is an equivalence, the **canonical** \(\mathbf C(X)\to\mathbf I(X)\) is an equivalence; §3 gives the D-side conclusion.

**Good-cover nerve formula:** If every nonempty \(Z_S\) is specialization-nerve contractible (§5), the chain realization is
\[
\mathbf C(X)\simeq\underline{|N(\{Z_i\})|},
\]
where \(N\) consists of nonempty S with \(Z_S\ne0\). Empty intersections contribute initial objects, nonempty SNC intersections terminal ones, and the residual Čech simplicial object is the ordered-tuple nerve of \(N\). No continuous choices of local contractions are needed.

## 5. Frame-theoretic certificates (Claims D2, E1–E3, F2)

For compact \(v\in K(X)\) and \(Z=C(v)\), \(K(Z)\cong[v,1]_{K(X)}\). Empty \(Z\) means \(v=1\).

- **Prime-endpoint condition D2:** A meet-prime bottom \(v\) in this interval yields a greatest specialization point \(g\in Z\); a join-prime top \(1\) yields a least \(\ell\in Z\). Greatest \(g\) gives \(H_n^{X,\uparrow}(Z)=Z^{n+1}\) and a terminal chain object; least \(\ell\) conically contracts the chain nerve and the relative hull via insertion of \(\ell\).
- **Ambient upper-bound condition UB (E1):** Put \(T_v=\{k\in K(X):k\nleq v\}\). Require that every finite nonempty family from \(T_v\) have nonzero meet *in \(K(X)\)*. By prime-filter extension this is equivalent to existence of some \(g\in X\) above **all** points of \(Z\), which need not itself belong to \(Z\). Then \(H_n^{X,\uparrow}(Z)=Z^{n+1}\) for all n, hence its patched realization is terminal (nonempty Z→1 effective epi).
- **Referee-origin sharpening:** UB is **equivalent** to \(H_n^{X,\uparrow}(Z)=Z^{n+1}\) for **every** n. Conversely, choose one point of \(Z\) from each compact open \(k_i\nleq v\). The full ambient higher hull supplies a common upper witness for that tuple, forcing \(\bigwedge_i k_i\ne0\). Thus UB is exact for the full Čech-hull property (not merely sufficient).
- **Spectral specialization-zigzag contraction SZC (E2–E3):** Suppose spectral endomorphisms \(f_0=\mathrm{id}_Z,f_1,\ldots,f_m=\mathrm{const}_z\) are pointwise specialization-comparable consecutively (alternating directions permitted). Their compact-open inverse-image maps are comparable bounded lattice endomorphisms. Adjacent comparisons are internal natural transformations, so the realized chain nerve is contractible. This is sufficient, not necessary, for SNC.
- **Specialization-nerve contractibility SNC (F1–F2):** Define \(\mathbf C(Z)\simeq1\). It is an invariant under **spectral order-homotopy equivalence**; no stronger vague “homotopy invariance” is asserted. A finite closed good cover with SNC nonempty intersections and contractible abstract nerve proves SNC recursively.

**Relative criterion:** For a finite closed cover \(Z_i=C(u_i)\) with compact \(u_i\), suppose each nonempty intersection \(Z_S\) either (a) has a least specialization point, or (b) satisfies ambient UB and is SNC. Then all the local relative inclusions are equivalences, so \(\mathbf C(X)\to\mathbf I(X),\mathbf C(X)\to\mathbf D(X)\) are canonical equivalences. Earlier sufficient criteria replace (b) by SZC, or both alternatives by prime-endpoint conditions.

**Strictness of existence criteria (referee-origin strengthening):**
\[
\boxed{\text{prime-endpoint (D2)}\ \subsetneq\ \text{UB+SZC (E3)}\ \subsetneq\ \text{UB+SNC (F2)},}
\]
where criteria mean that **some finite closed cover** passes the tests. The H1/H2 spaces in §7 separate the criteria even after allowing **all alternative finite closed covers**, not just the displayed one. The referee proves this by examining closed members containing incomparable maxima p and q: no cover member can contain both, intersections of separate members contain the essential four-point/face-poset core, and UB leaves no possible SZC or endpoint contraction there. This stronger “no alternative cover” assertion is referee-origin, separately checked in the finite H1 core but not part of original blind packet.

## 6. Coherent realizations and limits (Claims G1–G3)

For any nonempty finite simplicial complex \(T\), let \(P=F(T)\) be its nonempty-face poset, choose a vertex \(v\), and adjoin a Stone convergent sequence \(a_n\to a_\infty\) of new **maximal** specialization points above v only. The finite face points are patch-isolated. The resulting ordered compact Stone space is Priestley: the order is closed and its clopen upsets separate noncomparable pairs. It defines an infinite coherent locale \(X_T\).

The finite patch-clopen closed-downset cover consists of all principal downsets \(\downarrow F\) of maximal faces F and \(Z_A=\{v\}\cup\{a_n,a_\infty\}\). Each nonempty intersection has an extremal point. The cover nerve is the facet-intersection nerve, with one cone attached along a simplex on facets containing v. By the classical nerve lemma, that nerve has type \(|T|\). Thus:
\[
\boxed{\mathbf C(X_T)\simeq\mathbf I(X_T)\simeq\mathbf D(X_T)\simeq\underline{|T|}}
\]
and the equivalences between canonical maps follow from the cover criterion plus §3. The construction is correct but the convergent sequence **does not play an essential role in the computed homotopy type**: it makes X genuinely infinite rather than creating nonconstant sheaf-homotopy structure. These realizations should not be advertised as evidence for intrinsic localic shape.

For a finite triangulation \(T\) of a dunce hat with no free faces, \(P=F(T)\) has a contractible order complex but **no beat points**. Any finite comparable-endomorphism zigzag from id on P is stationary, hence cannot reach a constant; the spectral retraction \(X_T\to P\) sending all new maxima to v transfers this obstruction to \(X_T\). Thus SNC **does not imply** SZC, but ***this example alone does not separate the finite-cover criteria***: its facet cover satisfies D2.

## 7. Separating examples H1/H2

Let \(Z=\{a,b,c,d\}\) ordered \(a<c,\ b<c,\ b<d\). It has neither least nor greatest point, but the monotone map \(f(a)=c,\ f(b)=b,\ f(c)=c,\ f(d)=d\) gives \(\mathrm{id}\le f\ge\mathrm{const}_b\). Hence SZC holds.

**H1:** adjoining isolated e,f and incomparable upper points p,q,r with each element of Z below p,q, and e<q,r, f<r,p; also adjoin a Stone convergent sequence of maximal points above a only. The finite patch-clopen downset cover \(\downarrow p,\downarrow q,\downarrow r,Z_A\) has nonempty intersection \(Z_p\cap Z_q=Z\), and other nonempty intersections are singleton. The cover nerve is a hollow triangle pqr and a filled triangle pqA; it has homotopy type S¹. Every local intersection satisfies endpoint or UB+SZC. Therefore \(\mathbf C\simeq\mathbf I\simeq\mathbf D\simeq\underline{S^1}\), with **canonical** equivalences, and H1 separates the first two existence criteria.

**H2:** Replace finite Z by P=face poset of a dunce-hat triangulation with no free faces and choose its vertex a; keep p,q above P, e,f,r as above and the convergent maximal sequence above a. The intersection \(Z_p\cap Z_q=P\) has UB (via p) and SNC but no SZC. The same nerve computation yields \(\mathbf C\simeq\mathbf I\simeq\mathbf D\simeq\underline{S^1}\). The referee's new universal-cover obstruction shows H2 separates E3 from F2 as **existence** criteria, not only for the displayed cover.

## 8. Referee-origin counterexample W to naive finite-stage homotopy continuity

**Status:** Added by the external referee **after** independently checking Claims A–H. The core obstruction is independently reasoned in the accompanying [reconciliation](PointFree_chain_hulls_EXTERNAL_referee_reconciliation_2026-11-10.md); the further claim about the precise component sheaf is retained as a working observation requiring targeted verification.

Let W be the one-point patch compactification of the infinite fence \(x_0<y_0>x_1<y_1>x_2<\cdots\), with the new limit \(\infty\) incomparable to every fence point. The order is patch-closed and has enough clopen upsets (principal finite upsets and clopen tails containing ∞) to be Priestley. A cofinal directed family of finite compact-open sublattices that cannot distinguish sufficiently late tails gives finite quotient posets \(P_N\) which are finite fences with the tail collapsed into a terminal end point, so \(\mathbf C(P_N)\simeq1\) for all N.

But the **underlying abstract poset of points of W** has two components: the infinite fence and the isolated, incomparable ∞. Evaluation of \(\mathbf C(W)\) at the terminal Stone point preserves colimits and gives the classifying space of that abstract poset, which has two π₀ components. Therefore \(\mathbf C(W)\not\simeq1\), even though a cofinal family of finite stage chain realizations are terminal.

**Consequences:** Finitary quotient presentations reconstruct the simplicial locales degreewise but do **not** imply commutation of realization with inverse limits. In particular the natural map from \(\mathbf C(W)\) to the limit of the constant finite-stage realizations cannot be an equivalence. The further observation that the sheaf of components may have two point-values but no continuous global separation into a discrete two-point object is a promising *sheaf-level* issue, not yet a certified theorem.

## 9. Editorial status, literature, future direction

**Directly external-blind-checked:** coherent quotient presentations, finite-stage degreewise limits, finite-poset canonical comparison (after B2 lower carrier repair), edgewise/arrow comparison, finite closed-cover codescent with compact complements, endpoint/UB/SZC/SNC sufficiency, the infinite X_T and H1/H2 constructions.

**Referee-origin additions** after the blind review: a proof simplification for B2 lower via C3; dropping compactness in closed-cover codescent; exactness of UB for all higher arities; existential strictness D2⊊E3⊊F2 with no alternate cover; the infinite fence W. These have mathematical arguments and independently checked portions, but do not retroactively become externally blind-verified claims.

**Not claimed:** intrinsic localic shape, unpatched lower-witness effective descent, an invariant independent of the choice of Stone test-site size, a new proof of classical crosscut/Dowker topology, or literature priority for the canonical arrow-level formulations. Relevant literature: Cameron–Kumar arXiv:2610.14783v1 **§9**; Brun–Fosse–Salbu, *Dowker duality for relations of categories*, arXiv:2303.16032; Brun–Salbu, *The rectangle complex of a relation*, arXiv:2207.02018; Brown–Chan–Galatius–Payne, arXiv:2405.11528 §3.1; McCord/Stong/Quillen/Barmak on finite spaces and posets.

**Next research question (not resolved here):** Can \(\mathbf C(X)\) have essential, computable **nonconstant sheaf-theoretic homotopy structure**, rather than only constant realizations of classical finite complexes? The second referee's after-audit [ideas register](PointFree_referee2_mathematical_suggestions_triage_2026-11-10.md) suggests an internal suspension of \(h(S)\) and a complementary **D-side** codescent for compact-open upsets. Treat these as research leads, not as already incorporated results.
