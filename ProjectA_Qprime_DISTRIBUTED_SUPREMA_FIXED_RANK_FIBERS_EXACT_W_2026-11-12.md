# Project A — Distributed boundary suprema and finite-rank forcing fibres: exact Q′-positive no-go

**12 November 2026 · NEW INTERNAL mathematical investigation; not independently externally audited, no literature-priority assertion.** This is the requested test of *distributed* rather than one common boundary supremum, following [the non-product Baire-completion trap](ProjectA_Qprime_NONPRODUCT_BAIRE_BRIDGE_COMPLETION_TRAP_2026-11-11.md). We construct a non-product, noncompact, **locally compact sober noncontinuous Scott dcpo**, with an algebraic but noncoherent open frame, a real \(\mathcal D<\mathcal D_2\) gap, a real nonregular Urysohn boundary, and **\(\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2<\mathcal U}\)**. The two defects coexist and the forcing sits in the same connected order construction; nevertheless **finite bridge rank cuts** eliminate \(\mathcal D_2\) on both mixed wings. An abstract two-wing exhaustion criterion explains why. Universal Q and Q′ remain OPEN.

The **closed forcing core** K below the left bridges is essential for an independent D₂>D certificate; individual open bridge fibres, being above \(x_0\), cannot themselves witness a full-versus-finite cover gap through restriction, because every cover has a member containing \(x_0\) and hence the entire fibre.

## 0. Notation

Let \(w_{\mathcal C}=\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}c\boxtimes c\) in \(Q=\mathcal O(P)\oplus\mathcal O(P)\). Define
\[
\mathcal D=\bigwedge_{\mathcal C\ \text{cover}}\mathfrak o(w_{\mathcal C}),\quad
\mathcal D_2=\bigwedge_{F\ \text{finite cover}}\mathfrak o(w_F),\quad
\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D,\quad
\mathcal W_2=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D_2,\quad
\mathcal U=C(d_{\mathcal U}).
\]
The localic product is identified with the ordinary product **only after** local compactness and sobriety are established.

Let S be the algebraic dcpo of all nonempty **finite or infinite** partial functions \(\omega\rightharpoonup2\), ordered by extension. Finite nonempty partial functions form a compact basis.

## 1. Distributed-supremum order

P has the following elements:
- x-chain \(x_0<x_1<\dots<x\) and y-chain \(y_0<y_1<\dots<y\);
- for each \(k\in\omega\), two independent chains \(u^k_0<u^k_1<\dots<z_k\) and \(v^k_0<v^k_1<\dots<z_k\) with **shared** supremum \(z_k\), but \(z_k\) incomparable with \(z_\ell\) for \(k\ne\ell\);
- one **closed core** K consisting of \(K_s\), \(s\in S\);
- for each \(k,m\in\omega\), two **open bridge fibres** \(A_{k,m}=\{A_{k,m,s}:s\in S\}\) and \(B_{k,m}=\{B_{k,m,s}:s\in S\}\).

The order is generated by: extension order within each copy of S; \(x_i,u_i^k\le A_{k,m,s}\) for \(i\le m\); \(y_i,v_i^k\le B_{k,m,s}\) for \(i\le m\); and \(K_s\le A_{k,m,t}\) whenever \(s\subseteq t\). **No other comparisons** except those forced transitively. In particular NO \(z_k\) lies below a bridge point and NO element lies below a bridge in both left and right wings. The bridge rank \(m\) is FIXED under directed extension of its forcing label.

**Lemma 1.1 (dcpo).** Any directed family meeting a bridge fibre has a cofinal subfamily inside that same fibre, since nothing lies above points of that fibre except its own extensions. Its supremum is the union of the partial-function labels. A directed family containing both an element of K and a left bridge point also has its supremum in that left fibre. A directed family contained entirely in K has its supremum in K. The remaining directed families are eventually in one of the countable chains or have a greatest element, so their suprema are x,y, or an appropriate z_k. Directed families cannot combine distinct bridge fibres, or left/right wings, without a common upper element in the family (none exists). Thus P is a dcpo.

**Lemma 1.2 (not continuous).** For each k, the directed u^k- and v^k-chains have the same supremum z_k and no point lies below a member of each chain. Therefore **no** \(p\ll z_k\) exists. P is noncontinuous; removing the infinite forcing labels would fail directed completeness, so they are genuine domain points.

## 2. Scott basis, local compactness, sobriety, noncompactness

Write \(A=\coprod_{k,m}A_{k,m}\), \(B=\coprod_{k,m}B_{k,m}\), and
\[
X_n=\uparrow x_n=\{x\}\cup\{x_i:i\ge n\}\cup\bigcup_{k,m\ge n}A_{k,m},
\]
\[
Y_n=\uparrow y_n=\{y\}\cup\{y_i:i\ge n\}\cup\bigcup_{k,m\ge n}B_{k,m},
\]
\[
Z^k_{p,q}=\{z_k\}\cup\{u_i^k:i\ge p\}\cup\{v_j^k:j\ge q\}
\cup\bigcup_{m\ge p}A_{k,m}\cup\bigcup_{m\ge q}B_{k,m}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Use additionally the principal opens \(\uparrow K_f,\uparrow A_{k,m,f},\uparrow B_{k,m,f}\), with finite nonempty partial-function conditions f. These form a **Scott-open basis**: all are upward closed; Scott inaccessibility follows from the classification of directed suprema in §1, finite-condition compactness, and the necessity of both chain tails at z_k. Conversely every Scott-open neighborhood of a chain limit x,y,z_k contains one of these basics, and the finite-condition principal opens form bases on K and each bridge fibre. The **entire** bridge fibre \(A_{k,m}\), resp. \(B_{k,m}\), is itself Scott-open.

**Compact opens.** Each \(X_n=\uparrow x_n\), \(Y_n=\uparrow y_n\), and each finite-label principal open is **supercompact** (any cover member containing the generating point contains its entire upset). Each \(Z^k_{p,q}\) is compact: a cover member containing \(u_p^k\) covers its whole upset (the u-tail, z_k, and A-fibres of rank≥p), and a member containing \(v_q^k\) covers the v-tail and B-fibres of rank≥q. Those TWO members together cover Z. Thus P is locally compact and \(\mathcal O(P)\) an **algebraic frame**.

**Sobriety, using open/closed stratification.** Let O=A∪B, a disjoint topological sum of open sober algebraic-domain Scott spaces (copies of S); hence O is sober. The closed complement T=P\O is a topological disjoint sum of K, the two single-chain domains \(\omega+1\) (x/y), and for each k the two-chain fan with common top z_k. These are **clopen** components within T: restrict respectively the principal K basics, X_n/Y_n and Z^k_{p,q}. K and the chain components are sober, and each two-chain fan is sober (an irreducible closed subset containing z_k is its principal closure; one not containing z_k is a finite initial segment in one branch). Hence T is sober. If F⊂P is nonempty irreducible closed and meets O, then its nonempty relatively open F∩O is irreducible, sober with generic p; because a nonempty open in an irreducible space is dense, F=cl_P{p}. Otherwise F lies in T and has a generic point there. Thus P is sober.

**Noncompact.** The Scott-open family \(\{L,R\}\cup\{Z^k_{0,0}:k\in\omega\}\), where
\[
\boxed{L=K\cup X_0,\qquad R=Y_0}
\tag{2.2}
\]
covers P, but no finite subfamily covers all z_k (each z_k belongs only to its own indexed Z-open). L is Scott-open since \(L=X_0\cup\bigcup_f\uparrow K_f\); R is Scott-open. They are disjoint.

**Noncoherent.** Both X_0 and Z^k_{0,0} are compact opens, but
\[
X_0\cap Z^k_{0,0}=\coprod_{m\in\omega}A_{k,m}
\]
is a noncompact open (covered by the disjoint Scott-open individual fibres with no finite subcover). Therefore compact elements of \(\mathcal O(P)\) are not meet-stable.

Since P is locally compact sober, its **localic square** is represented by the ordinary topological square P×P. Closed sublocales of that square are determined by their closed point sets. The arguments below nevertheless use the **actual sublocale** D₂ for the difficult infinite meets.

## 3. Distributed finite covers eliminate BOTH mixed wing rectangles

Put \(Z_{p,q}=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}Z^k_{p,q}\), an (in general noncompact) Scott-open. Let
\[
A_{\ge n}=\coprod_{k,m\ge n}A_{k,m},\qquad
B_{\ge n}=\coprod_{k,m\ge n}B_{k,m}.
\]
The cover
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F_n=\{L,R,Z_{0,n},Z_{n,0}\}}
\tag{3.1}
\]
is a **finite** Scott-open cover for every n: L covers K and the entire x-wing A, R covers the entire y-wing B, Z_{0,n} covers every u-chain, and Z_{n,0} every v-chain; both Z-opens cover all z_k.

Because \(L\cap R=0\), \(L\cap Z_{0,n}=A\), \(R\cap Z_{0,n}=B_{\ge n}\), and with left/right reversed for \(Z_{n,0}\), the square-cover trace is **exactly**
\[
\boxed{
w_{\mathcal F_n}\wedge(L\boxtimes R)
=(A\boxtimes B_{\ge n})\vee(A_{\ge n}\boxtimes B).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
At n=0 this is \(A\boxtimes B\). The open fibre products \(A_{k,m}\boxtimes B_{\ell,j}\) cover \(A\boxtimes B\), and for each fixed k,m,ℓ,j the right side in (3.2) vanishes on that product as soon as n>max(m,j). Therefore the full meet of the OPEN SUBLOCALES \(\mathfrak o(w_{\mathcal F_n})\), when restricted to the mixed wing, is zero. This argument does NOT assume arbitrary intersections of opens agree with frame meets; it uses that the sublocale already lies in the **open cover by fibre products**, and is zero when restricted to each member.

Thus
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_2(P)\wedge\mathfrak o(L\boxtimes R)=0,\qquad
\mathcal D_2(P)\wedge\mathfrak o(R\boxtimes L)=0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Closure commutes with **restriction to an open sublocale**, so
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_2(P)\le
\mathfrak c\big((L\boxtimes R)\vee(R\boxtimes L)\big).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
This is the central distributed-suprema **finite-rank exhaustion** no-go. It works even though bridge fibres are themselves noncompact, non-discrete algebraic domains, and even though there is NO single common boundary supremum z.

## 4. Exact W and W₂ via two dense same-side relations

The key new bonus is that (3.4) is SHARP.

**Lemma 4.1 (closures of bridge opens).**
\[
\boxed{\overline A=P\setminus R,\qquad \overline B=P\setminus L.}
\tag{4.1}
\]
*Proof.* R is open and disjoint from A, so \(\bar A\subseteq P\setminus R\). Every K-point has Scott neighborhoods meeting A (a finite principal ↑K_f meets many A-fibres), every x-chain point and x has neighborhoods meeting A, and every u/v-chain point or z_k has neighborhoods meeting A: at v_i^k, any Scott-open containing it also contains z_k and then a tail of the *other* u^k chain, hence A-fibres; at z_k the two tails are already required. Every A-point of course lies in \(\bar A\). These exhaust P\R. Symmetrically, no neighborhood of a K- or x-wing point needs to meet B, while all y-/z-side points and B-points have neighborhoods meeting B, proving \(\bar B=P\setminus L\). □

Any two ordinary A-points have common specialization predecessor x₀; any two B-points have common predecessor y₀. Thus **every ordinary pair in A×A or B×B belongs to the full fitted diagonal D(P)**: every open cover member containing the common predecessor contains both endpoints. The closed relation W contains their topological closures:
\[
\mathcal W\ \supseteq\ \overline{A\times A}\cup\overline{B\times B}
=(P\setminus R)^2\cup(P\setminus L)^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]
The right side is exactly the **closed complement** of \((L\times R)\cup(R\times L)\), because L,R are disjoint. This is an equality of **closed localic subrelations** since the square is spatial.

Combine (4.2), \(\mathcal W\le\mathcal W_2\) and (3.4):
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W(P)=\mathcal W_2(P)=
\mathfrak c\big((L\boxtimes R)\vee(R\boxtimes L)\big).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
This is an **exact global computation**, not merely equality on the targeted mixed rectangle. In particular Q′ HOLDS for P despite a strict full-versus-finite fitted-diagonal gap (next section).

## 5. U is strictly larger and NOT regular closed

For any Scott-open neighborhoods U of x and V of y, choose n,m with \(X_n\subseteq U\), \(Y_m\subseteq V\). For every fixed k, every Scott-open neighborhood of z_k contains u^k- and v^k-tails, hence meets U through A_{k,j} for j sufficiently large and V through B_{k,j} for j sufficiently large. Thus
\[
z_k\in\overline U\cap\overline V
\quad\text{for every }k,\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{(x,y)\in\operatorname{pt}\mathcal U(P)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
But x∈L,y∈R, so by (4.3) \((x,y)\notin\operatorname{pt}\mathcal W_2\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2<\mathcal U.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

To check nonregular-closedness **localically**, observe that every fixed-rank basic left bridge open \(\uparrow A_{k,m,f}\) and fixed-rank right bridge open \(\uparrow B_{\ell,j,g}\) have **disjoint closures** in P. The former has possible limit predecessors only in K, the x-chain and an initial segment of the u^k-chain, while the latter has possible predecessors only in the y-chain and an initial segment of the v^ℓ-chain. Neither accumulates at a z-index because z_k has neighborhoods using bridge ranks larger than m or j. Thus their closures are disjoint, and their product rectangles are generators of \(d_{\mathcal U}\). They cover A×B, so
\[
A\boxtimes B\le d_{\mathcal U}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
By Lemma 4.1, A×B is dense in the **open** L×R. Hence d_U is dense in L×R; \(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U\) and its closure both miss this open rectangle. But \(\mathcal U\) meets it at (x,y). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{int}\mathcal U)<\mathcal U.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This shows that the regular-core argument for continuous Scott dcpos has genuinely failed, while Q′ itself survives through a DIFFERENT mechanism.

## 6. The CLOSED forcing core gives a genuine D₂>D gap

K is a **Scott-closed** lower sub-dcpo of P: every element below a K_s is K_t with t⊆s, and directed suprema of K-points remain in K. Its relative Scott topology is the usual completed partial-function domain S (restrictions of ↑K_f form a basis).

Recall the two standard frame maps
\[
h_\pm:\mathcal O(K)\to B_0=\operatorname{RO}(2^\omega)
\]
defined, for Scott-open U⊆K, by applying regular-open completion to the union of Cantor cylinders \([f]\) (positive orientation) or their coordinatewise complementary cylinders \([\bar f]\) (negative orientation), over all finite nonempty f with \(\uparrow f\subseteq U\).

These preserve arbitrary joins and finite meets. For any finite Scott-open cover of K, one member contains infinitely many **singleton partial functions at distinct coordinates**. Both orientations of that member have dense unions of coordinate cylinders, so its images under h₊ and h₋ are both 1. Thus the pair is **finite-cover-compatible**.

For the infinite literal cover
\[
\mathcal L=\{\uparrow\{n\mapsto b\}:n\in\omega,b\in2\},
\tag{6.1}
\]
each c has \(h_+(c)\wedge h_-(c)=0\), proving \(\mathcal D(K)<\mathcal D_2(K)\).

Compose h± with the surjective restriction \(\mathcal O(P)\to\mathcal O(K)\), since K is closed. Every finite P-cover restricts to a finite K-cover. The P-cover
\[
\mathcal C^\star=
\{P\setminus K\}\ \cup\
\{\uparrow K_{\{n\mapsto b\}}:n\in\omega,b\in2\}
\tag{6.2}
\]
restricts exactly to the incompatible literal cover on K (the complement term restricts to zero). Hence the extended pair is NOT fully cover-compatible:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D(P)<\mathcal D_2(P).}
\tag{6.3}
\]
The strict gap is genuinely point-free (Boolean-valued), not visible on ordinary point-pairs.

## 7. Abstract two-wing finite-exhaustion criterion

**Theorem 7.1 (generalized distributed-suprema no-go).** Let X be a locale with a spatial localic square (e.g. X locally compact sober). Let L,R⊆X be disjoint opens. Suppose there are opens A⊆L,B⊆R such that
\[
\overline A=X\setminus R,\qquad \overline B=X\setminus L,
\]
every ordinary pair of points in A×A and B×B factors through D(X), and A,B have open coverings \(A=\bigvee_i A_i\), \(B=\bigvee_j B_j\). Assume there are **finite** X-covers F_n with
\[
w_{F_0}\wedge (L\boxtimes R)\le A\boxtimes B,
\]
and for each i,j some n satisfies
\[
w_{F_n}\wedge(A_i\boxtimes B_j)=0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W(X)=\mathcal W_2(X)=
\mathfrak c((L\boxtimes R)\vee(R\boxtimes L)).}
\tag{7.1}
\]
*Proof.* Since D₂ lies in every square-cover open, its restriction to L×R lies inside A×B, and is zero on every component A_i×B_j. Those components cover A×B, so D₂ vanishes on L×R; symmetry uses the same square covers. Thus W₂ is inside the closed complement of the two mixed rectangles. On the other side, pointwise D on A×A and B×B forces W to contain their closed hulls, which by the two density hypotheses join to exactly that complement. The closed hull comparison chain forces all equalities. □

**No local compactness needed** for the finite-cover elimination direction; spatiality of the square is only used to infer the closed relation from ordinary D-point density. A purely locale-theoretic variant could replace pointwise density with suitable dense sublocale inclusions.

### 7.2 Variable-rank tails: the closed-frontier localization

The fixed-rank argument has a useful extension which makes the *remaining* design window precise.

**Proposition 7.2 (frontier localization, new internal).** Let L,R be disjoint opens of any locale X, with open sublocales A⊆L, B⊆R. Suppose there are finite covers F_n of X and descending families of opens \(T_n^A\subseteq A\), \(T_n^B\subseteq B\) such that
\[
w_{F_0}\wedge(L\boxtimes R)\le A\boxtimes B
\quad\text{and}\quad
w_{F_n}\wedge(A\boxtimes B)
\le (T_n^A\boxtimes B)\vee(A\boxtimes T_n^B).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Define the **closed accumulation frontiers** (closed sublocales of A and B)
\[
H_A=\bigwedge_n\operatorname{cl}_A\mathfrak o(T_n^A),\qquad
H_B=\bigwedge_n\operatorname{cl}_B\mathfrak o(T_n^B).
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then the actual finite-cover fitted relation restricted to the mixed open satisfies
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_2(X)\wedge\mathfrak o(L\boxtimes R)
\ \le\ (H_A\times B)\vee(A\times H_B),}
\tag{7.4}
\]
where the right side is a sublocale of the open \(A\times B\hookrightarrow L\times R\). In particular if **both frontiers vanish**, then \(\mathcal D_2\) and its closure \(\mathcal W_2\) vanish on \(L\times R\).

*Proof.* The first cover confines the sublocale to A×B. The complement in A×B of the closed union on the right is the open product
\[
(A\setminus H_A)\times(B\setminus H_B).
\]
It has an open cover by products U×V where U is disjoint from \(\operatorname{cl}_A(T_n^A)\) for some n and V from \(\operatorname{cl}_B(T_m^B)\) for some m. Since the T-families are descending, for N=max(n,m) the right side of (7.2) restricts to zero on U×V, so \(\mathcal D_2\) does as well. These rectangles cover the complement, proving (7.4). With H_A=H_B=0 the finite relation vanishes on the entire mixed open, and so does its closure (closure commutes with restriction to opens). □

**Baire consequence.** If A is a **spatial Baire space** and the descending opens \(T_n^A\) have empty ordinary intersection, then \(H_A\) has **empty interior** in A. Otherwise some nonempty open U lies inside all \(\overline{T_n^A}\), so every \(T_n^A\cap U\) is dense and open in U; the Baire theorem would give a point in their intersection. Similarly for B. Thus absent actual *infinite-rank* completion points, any nonzero frontier is **nowhere dense**. Nonzero frontiers can still exist: in ordinary \(\mathbb R\), let C be a nowhere-dense perfect Cantor set and
\[
T_n=\{x:0<\operatorname{dist}(x,C)<1/(n+1)\}.
\]
Then the \(T_n\) are descending opens with empty intersection but \(\bigcap_n\overline{T_n}=C\), a perfect nowhere-dense frontier. This ordinary metric example merely verifies the combinatorics of the frontier; **regularity of \(\mathbb R\) rules out Q′ there**.

For our fixed-rank bridge construction, \(A=\coprod_{k,m}A_{k,m}\), \(T_n^A=A_{\ge n}\), and similarly B. Each \(T_n^A\) is **clopen in A**, and \(\bigcap_n T_n^A=\emptyset\) because every fibre has finite rank. Thus \(H_A=H_B=0\), and Proposition 7.2 recovers the finite-cut vanishing.

**Prospective escape, not a claimed construction:** allow variable rank so that \(H_A\) or \(H_B\) is a nonzero **nowhere-dense, potentially atomless** boundary inside a bridge wing. Then the finite-cover relation could be supported in these thin fronts without any ordinary infinite-rank common-supremum points. To obtain a genuine Q′ gap one would still need (i) an actual nonzero finite-cover sublocale there, (ii) full D absent on a relatively open part of its closure, and (iii) survival of the earlier nonregular Urysohn boundary. These remain demanding independent conditions.

## 8. Status and research implications

This is a **fully distributed countable family** of separate limit suprema z_k, with arbitrary completed forcing labels within each fixed-rank bridge fibre. It is non-product, noncompact, locally compact, sober, algebraic-frame and noncoherent. Its \(\mathcal U\) boundary is nonregular closed and contains (x,y) outside W. Its D₂>D defect lives on a **closed core**, yet W₂=W *globally*.

Two mutually exclusive earlier traps are now seen to have a common generalization:
1. Making bridge **ranks cofinal inside individual directed forcing completions** produces points above a common z_k, filling the mixed square with FULL D (Baire-completion rescue).
2. Keeping each forcing fibre at a **fixed finite rank** avoids such completion, but uniform finite covers across ALL distributed z_k cut D₂ out of the mixed rectangle (finite-rank exhaustion).

A real Q′ counterexample must defeat BOTH mechanisms in **one actual open residual**. Merely distributing suprema over infinitely many indices does not suffice because unions of all indexed Scott-open neighborhoods let one form **four global cover members**, with the same rank-cut formula.

The project-level open questions Q, Q′ remain OPEN in general. No claim of originality. For the next bounded investigation one should test either (a) a genuinely non-uniform rank structure in which the global \(Z_{0,n},Z_{n,0}\) fails to be Scott-open/usable as a finite cover, or (b) indexed boundary domains whose tails are non-Baire and cannot be completed into common-predecessor points. Avoid treating the addition of ordinary forcing fibres as evidence for Q′ before explicitly computing its localic square-cover traces.
