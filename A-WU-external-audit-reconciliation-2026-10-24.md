## 2. Accepted useful improvements

**Finite-cover filter.** B2 (finite-cut) implies that the filter B2 generated by binary square neighborhoods is precisely the filter of all elements lying above some square neighborhood w_C from a FINITE cover C. Thus D2=intersection_{C finite} O(w_C). The operator

    Phi2(q)=join_{C finite}(w_C → q)
           =join_{w in B2}(w → q)

is a genuine pre-nucleus. It has Fix(Phi2)=D2, Phi2(0)=d_U, and W2=U iff Phi2(d_U)=d_U. It is preferable to the earlier operator Psi, which was monotone/inflationary but not proven a pre-nucleus.

**Isbell density reduction.** The least dense sublocale B(U) exists by Isbell's theorem. Therefore

    W=U iff B(U)≤D,
    W2=U iff B(U)≤D2.

For the canonical nonregular boundary G=U∧O(d_U**), the original regular-core theorem plus the Boolean part of the interior gives

    W=U iff B(G)≤D,
    W2=U iff B(G)≤D2.

The referee also supplies a Boolean splitting B(U)=B(int U) sqcup B(G), with B(int U) always contained in D. The splitting deserves standard notation checks but is compatible with our frame calculations.

**Further useful qualifications.** The report points out that the Lindenloef/countable-distributivity theorem adds no new second-countable cases beyond atomicity, since a countably based atomless complete Boolean algebra admits a countable dense atomless part (Cohen algebra). We take this as a research heuristic pending separate source/precision check, rather than promoting every structural assertion about Boolean algebras as audited.

**Source/prior art.** The full operator Phi is the kappa_L of Arrieta–Picado–Pultr (2022), Lemma 5.4 of the authors' preprint; their Lemma 4.4 covers arbitrary-cover-compatible homomorphisms. Isbell's least dense sublocale is standard. Do not claim these named constructions as new.


## 3. Correction of referee M2: arbitrary finite closed sets need NOT be regular closed

The report (§2 M2) asserts that every closed set of a finite upper-Alexandrov space is regular closed. This is FALSE. In the Sierpinski chain 0<1 with upper topology, the closed subset {0} has empty interior, so is not regular closed. More generally, maximal points *of a closed down-set* need not be maximal in the ambient poset.

The **specific** conclusion that U(P) is regular closed for a finite upper-Alexandrov space P is nevertheless TRUE, for a different reason. If (x,y)∈U(P), the closures of the minimal open neighborhoods ↑x,↑y meet. Hence there exist z and u≥x,v≥y with z≤u,v. Extend u and v to ambient maximal elements m,n. Then z≤m,n, so (m,n)∈U(P). Because (m,n) is ambient maximal in P×P, it belongs to int U(P), and (x,y) is in its closure. Therefore U(P)=cl(int U(P)).

For the finite six-point zigzag P, this establishes that U(P) is regular closed without using the false blanket assertion. Since the Scott factor S in the product example has U(S)=S², the product relation U(S×P)=S²×U(P) is also regular closed. Hence the report's narrower conclusion G(S×P)=0 holds; the stated justification should be replaced.

## 4. Correction of referee §5: hyperconnected pseudocomplements do NOT imply the proposed rectangle criterion

The report's elementary lemma is valid: if ↓a is hyperconnected and 0<u≤a, then u*=a*. But the report infers

    u⊠v ≤ d_U  iff  a*∨v*=1.

This equivalence is FALSE. Being contained in d_U, a JOIN of far rectangles, does NOT require the rectangle itself to be far.

**Concrete counterexample.** Take X={z,t}⊔N, with t and every n∈N isolated, and neighborhoods of z given by

    {z,t} ∪ (N\F),  F finite.

It is a compact sober T0 locally compact space. In L=O(X), let a={t} and v=N. The subframe ↓a is two-element and thus hyperconnected. We have

    cl(a)={z,t},   cl(v)={z}∪N,
    a*=N,          v*={t},
    a*∨v*=X\{z}<1.

But for EVERY n∈N the singleton {n} is clopen; a and {n} form a far pair:

    a*∨{n}*=N∨(X\{n})=1.

Therefore, in the genuine LOCALIC square,

    a⊠v = ⋁_{n∈N} a⊠{n} ≤ d_U.

So a⊠v≤d_U yet a*∨v*≠1, even though ↓a is hyperconnected.

**Consequence for the referee's advice.** The rest of §5, particularly the purported requirement that a successful counterexample have cover-generators with distinct pseudocomplements and the alleged impossibility of upgrading a hyperconnected-coordinate mechanism, is NOT established by the stated argument. We should not adopt it as a necessary obstruction. The generic open/closed boundary and binary-descent certificates from our original packet remain valid and are sufficient for planning future work.


## 5. Accepted and strengthened: the two-apex boundary control

The referee's new space X=R⊔{p,q}, with basic p-neighborhoods {p}∪((0,∞)\F) and q-neighborhoods {q}∪((−∞,0)\F), is a locally compact sober T1 noncompact space with spatial localic square. Put

    A_+=[0,∞)∪{p},   A_-=(−∞,0]∪{q}.

A more precise point-relation calculation gives

    pt U = A_+² ∪ A_-² ∪ {(p,q),(q,p)}.

The two rectangles A_+²,A_-² are closed and regular closed in X²: their open cores ((0,∞)∪{p})² and ((−∞,0)∪{q})² are dense in them. Hence

    K=cl(int U)=A_+² ∪ A_-²
    G=U∩O(d_U**)=the discrete two-point sublocale {(p,q),(q,p)}.

The latter pair of points is relatively isolated in U. Since X is T1, neither lies in pt(D) nor pt(D2). An open neighborhood of each isolates that point inside U, so D,D2 restrict to the zero sublocale there. Thus W and W2 omit both points of G. But K≤W≤W2≤U universally. Therefore the example actually satisfies the STRONGER exact chain

    I < W = W2 = K < U,

with G a nonzero atomic boundary. The strict I<K is witnessed by (0,x), x>0: these points admit disjoint neighborhoods, yet lie in the closure of the positive-square interior of U.

This is a very useful SIMPLE example of an actual strict W<U with a nonregular boundary and a precise binary obstruction. It does not refute (Q) or (Q'), and unlike the report's proposed compactification remark, the calculation above makes no claim about modifications without further checks.
