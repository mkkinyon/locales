# Project A — W as a Boolean-valued cover-compatibility relation

**9 November 2026. New working research segment, not independently audited or priority checked.** This begins the study of \(\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{fit}\Delta)\) **in its own right**, with Q/Q′ now deferred. The Booleanization and basic cross-hull formalism use established localic facts and earlier Project A notes; do not claim those ideas as original. The three-point restriction examples and categorical organization here are internally checked.

Let \(X\) be a locale, \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), \(Q=L\oplus L=\mathcal O(X\times X)\). Denote the diagonal sublocale by \(\Delta\) and set
\[
\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}\Delta
=\bigcap_{\mathcal C\text{ cover of }1_L}O(w_{\mathcal C}),
\quad w_{\mathcal C}=\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}c\boxtimes c,\quad
\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}\mathcal D=C(d_{\mathcal W}).
\]
The known hierarchy includes \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}\Delta\le\mathcal W\le\mathcal U\), \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{fit}\mathcal I\le\mathcal H\), \(\mathcal W\le\mathcal H\), where \(\mathcal H\) is the least simultaneously closed and fitted hull of \(\Delta\). Since \(\mathcal W\) is closed and \(\mathcal V\) fitted,
\[
\mathcal W=\mathcal H\iff \mathcal W\text{ fitted},\qquad
\mathcal V=\mathcal H\iff\mathcal V\text{ closed},
\]
and \(\mathcal W=\mathcal V\iff\mathcal W=\mathcal V=\mathcal H\).

## 1. Boolean-valued cover-coincidence semantics

**Theorem 1 (working, for all locales).** For nonzero \(a,b\in L\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(\mathcal W\wedge O(a\boxtimes b)\ne0\) (equivalently \(a\boxtimes b\not\le d_{\mathcal W}\)).
2. \(\mathcal D\wedge O(a\boxtimes b)\ne0\).
3. There is a **nontrivial complete Boolean algebra/frame** \(B\) and frame homomorphisms \(h,k:L\to B\) such that
   \[
   \bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}h(c)\wedge k(c)=1_B
   \quad\text{for EVERY open cover }\mathcal C\text{ of }1_L,
   \tag{1.1}
   \]
   and \(h(a)\wedge k(b)\ne0\).
4. There is such a Boolean frame and compatible pair with the stronger property \(h(a)=k(b)=1_B\).

**Proof.** (1) iff (2): \(\mathcal D\) is dense in its closure \(\mathcal W\), and closure commutes with restriction to the open sublocale \(O(a\boxtimes b)\). Equivalently, \(O(r)\wedge S=0\iff r\le j_S(0)\), and \(j_{\mathcal D}(0)=d_{\mathcal W}\). (2) ⇒ (4): Let \(S=\mathcal D\wedge O(a\boxtimes b)\ne0\). The canonical frame quotient \(Q\to\mathcal O(S)\) sends \(a\boxtimes b\) to top because \(S\le O(a\boxtimes b)\), and sends every \(w_{\mathcal C}\) to top because \(S\le\mathcal D\). Compose with the double-negation quotient \(\mathcal O(S)\to B(\mathcal O(S))\), a **nonzero complete Boolean frame**, and restrict the resulting \(Q\to B\) to the two coproduct injections \(L\to Q\). Images of a and b both equal 1, proving (4). (4) ⇒ (3) is immediate. (3) ⇒ (2): the induced map \(Q\to B\) factors through \(\mathcal D\) by (1.1). Restrict \(B\) to its nonzero complemented open \(O(h(a)\wedge k(b))\) to obtain a nonzero locale map that factors also through \(O(a\boxtimes b)\), witnessing \(S\ne0\). □

**Conceptual form.** If \(B(\mathcal D)\hookrightarrow\mathcal D\) denotes Isbell's least dense Boolean sublocale, then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}_{X\times X}B(\mathcal D).}
\tag{1.2}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal W\) is the closed relation generated by **all Boolean-valued, arbitrary-cover-compatible pairs** of coordinate maps. This is not the closure merely of the ordinary two-valued points of \(\mathcal D\); \(\mathcal D\) can have a dense pointless component. The construction supplies an intrinsic interpretation of W that makes no reference to the deferred finite-cover approximation W₂.

For a sober T1 space Y with \(\Delta_Y\) closed, all ordinary D-points are diagonal (binary cover by complements of two distinct points). Yet in Arrieta's 2024 **spatial strongly Hausdorff anti-Urysohn non-F-separated example**, \(\mathcal I_Y=\Delta_Y\), while \(d_{\mathcal U}=0\) and the universal regular-core theorem gives \(\mathcal W_Y=\mathcal U_Y=Y^2\). Thus two-valued points alone do not test \(\mathcal W\), even for a spatial strongly Hausdorff locale. Reference: Igor Arrieta, *Localic separation and the duality between closedness and fittedness*, Topology Appl. 342 (2024), 108785; see the internal derivation in [associativity audit IV](A-relational-composition-associativity-audit-IV-D-cube-high-low-2026-11-07.md), §5. Treat the detailed application as working, with the published input explicitly attributed.

## 2. Universal lax naturality

**Theorem 2 (working).** Let \(f:Y\to X\) be **any** locale map, and \(g=f\times f:Y^2\to X^2\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_Y\le g^{-1}\mathcal D_X,\qquad
\mathcal W_Y\le g^{-1}\mathcal W_X.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** For every cover \(\mathcal C\) of 1 in \(\mathcal O(X)\), its inverse image \(f^*\mathcal C\) covers 1 in \(\mathcal O(Y)\), and
\[
g^*(w_{\mathcal C})=w_{f^*\mathcal C}.
\]
The map \(g\circ(\mathcal D_Y\hookrightarrow Y^2)\) therefore factors through every diagonal-neighborhood open \(O(w_{\mathcal C})\) of \(X^2\). It factors through their intersection \(\mathcal D_X\), giving the first inclusion. Since \(\mathcal W_X\) is closed and contains \(\mathcal D_X\), its pullback \(g^{-1}\mathcal W_X\) is a **closed** sublocale containing \(\mathcal D_Y\). It therefore contains the least closed sublocale above \(\mathcal D_Y\), namely \(\mathcal W_Y\). □

This is *lax* naturality: general maps preserve W-relatedness in the forward direction, but the pullback of W need not equal intrinsic W.

## 3. Open restriction can be strict in a 3-point coherent locale

Let \(P_\wedge=\{0,p,q\}\) with \(0<p\), \(0<q\), p and q incomparable, and give it the upper Alexandrov topology. Every open cover contains the entire open \(P_\wedge\), since any open containing 0 is all of P. Thus every square cover neighborhood is top,
\[
\mathcal D(P_\wedge)=\mathcal W(P_\wedge)=P_\wedge^2.
\]
But \(U=\{p,q\}\) is an **open**, two-point discrete sublocale. Therefore
\[
\mathcal W(U)=\Delta_U\subsetneq U^2
=\mathcal W(P_\wedge)\wedge O(U\times U).
\tag{3.1}
\]
This is an entirely finite/coherent counterexample to equality in the open-embedding case of (2.1).

## 4. Closed restriction can also be strict in the dual 3-point locale

Let \(P_\vee=\{p,q,1\}\) with \(p<1\), \(q<1\), p and q incomparable; equip it with the upper Alexandrov topology. Every two nonempty opens contain 1, so \(\mathcal I(P_\vee)=P_\vee^2\) and therefore
\(\mathcal W(P_\vee)=P_\vee^2\).

The subset \(F=\{p,q\}\) is **closed** (a lower set), and its subspace topology is discrete. Thus
\[
\mathcal W(F)=\Delta_F\subsetneq F^2
=\mathcal W(P_\vee)|_{F\times F}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The lax inclusion can therefore be strict for **both open and closed sublocale embeddings**, already among finite coherent locales. The examples arise by dualizing the two 3-point forks, but the reasons W is total differ: full diagonal fitting in §3 and full inseparability in §4.

These are failures of **restriction equality for the relation**, not counterexamples to heredity of a class such as \(\{\mathcal W=\mathcal U\}\).

## 5. Exactness for clopen sums

**Proposition 3 (working).** For an arbitrary localic disjoint sum \(X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_i\), \(\mathcal D(X)\) and \(\mathcal W(X)\) have no mixed-component blocks and restrict on \(X_i^2\) to \(\mathcal D(X_i)\), \(\mathcal W(X_i)\), respectively.

**Proof.** Let \(e_i\) be the complemented open representing the ith component. The family \(\{e_i\}_{i\in I}\) covers 1, so the diagonal cover square \(w_{\{e_i\}}=\bigvee_i e_i\boxtimes e_i\) contains D(X) and excludes all off-diagonal blocks \(X_i\times X_j\), i≠j. Any cover of X restricts to a cover of X_i. Conversely any cover \(\mathcal C_i\) of X_i extends to a cover of X by its component-supported opens together with \(e_j\), j≠i. The full cover-compatibility test therefore gives the exact equality \(D(X)|_{X_i^2}=D(X_i)\). Closure commutes with restriction to open sublocales, and each \(X_i^2\) is clopen in \(X^2\), so W has the asserted blocks. □

Thus \(\mathcal W\) respects clopen decomposition *exactly*, although it need not respect arbitrary open or closed restrictions. This offers a first structural boundary for a systematic study of W under localic constructions.

Our first interpretative result is that \(\mathcal W\) is a **Boolean-valued cover-coincidence relation**: the closure of the universal full-cover-compatible Boolean core. The functoriality theorem and 3-point counterexamples make precise its **ambient dependence**, while clopen block decomposition is an exact positive counterpart.

## 7. Semantic tests for the two extremal equalities

The Boolean witness theorem provides a useful pair of tests that no longer mention nuclei or W₂. Let \(\mathrm{Comp}_B(L)\) denote all fully cover-compatible pairs \(h,k:L\to B\) where B ranges over nonzero complete Boolean frames, and let \(r=a\boxtimes b\).

**Proposition 4 (working).**

**(a) W=I** iff for **all** \((h,k)\in\mathrm{Comp}_B(L)\) and **all disjoint opens** \(a\wedge b=0\), one has \(h(a)\wedge k(b)=0\).

Proof: \(\mathcal I=C(d_{\mathcal I})\), with \(d_{\mathcal I}=\bigvee_{a\wedge b=0}a\boxtimes b\). Since \(\mathcal I\le\mathcal W\), equality is equivalent to each disjoint rectangle being disjoint from W, which by Theorem 1 is exactly the displayed universal Boolean condition.

**(b) W=U** iff for **every** rectangle \(a\boxtimes b\not\le d_{\mathcal U}\), there exists a full-cover-compatible Boolean pair \(h,k:L\to B\) with \(h(a)\wedge k(b)\ne0\).

Proof: by Theorem 1 the latter says that every rectangle meeting U also meets W. Since W≤U is closed, equality follows because rectangles join-generate Q and test equality of their defining closed complements. Conversely W=U supplies the Boolean witness for each such rectangle.

These tests distinguish \(\mathcal W\) from both adjoining closed relations: \(\mathcal W=\mathcal I\) means **no new Boolean cover-compatible correlations on disjoint opens**, whereas \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) means **every rectangle not already U-separated has a full-cover-compatible Boolean correlation**. Both are logical rephrasings of earlier inequalities and the new witness interpretation; no claim of an easier algorithm or a new separation axiom is intended.
