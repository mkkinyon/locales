# Project A — W=H beyond coherence: fitness inside the functional envelope

**9 November 2026 · Working internal mathematics; not externally audited or priority-checked.** Q/Q′ remain deferred. This note develops noncoherent sufficient conditions for \(\mathcal W=\mathcal H\) and isolates a pointfree obstruction. Input from the October independently audited post-V hierarchy: \(\Delta\le \mathcal W\le \mathcal H\le \mathcal F\), \(\mathcal F\) is closed and fitted, and the compact scattered \(T_1\) \(\alpha\)-\(\beta\)-\(\gamma\) construction has \(\mathcal W=\mathcal H=\mathcal I<\mathcal U=\mathcal F\).

Let \(Q=L\oplus L=\mathcal O(X^2)\). Write
\[
\mathcal W=C(d),\quad \mathcal H=C(e),\quad \mathcal F=C(f),
\qquad f\le e\le d.
\]
For any frame \(A\), an element \(a\in A\), and the corresponding closed sublocale \(C(a)\), open neighborhoods of \(C(a)\) are exactly \(O(w)\) with \(a\vee w=1\).

## 1. The exact restricted frame-fit test

**Proposition 1 (general fact, specialized here).** For every frame A and every a∈A,
\[
\boxed{C(a)\text{ fitted}\iff
\forall b\in A,\ a\not\le b\Rightarrow
\exists w\in A:\ a\vee w=1,\ w\to b\ne b.}
\tag{1.1}
\]
This is the standard first-order *fit* axiom restricted to the single element a. It is a reformulation of fittedness, NOT a new separation axiom.

**Proof.** The fitted hull of \(C(a)\) is the intersection of the open sublocales \(O(w)\) for which \(a\vee w=1\). Its underlying frame is the set of \(b\in A\) fixed by every open nucleus \(w\to(-)\) with \(w\vee a=1\). The frame of \(C(a)\) is \(\uparrow a\). These two fixed-point sets are equal iff (1.1) holds. Note \(b\le w\to b\), so \(\ne b\) and \(\not\le b\) are equivalent for \(w\to b\). □

Applied to \(A=Q,a=d=d_{\mathcal W}\), this gives a single-element test for \(W=H\), with no reference to W₂.

## 2. Absolute rather-below generation is too strong

For any \(a\in A\), define its relative regular core
\[
\rho_A(a)=\bigvee\{c\in A:c\prec a\},\qquad
c\prec a\iff c^*\vee a=1.
\]
**Lemma 2.1.** If \(\rho_A(a)=a\), then \(C(a)\) is fitted. Proof: if \(a\not\le b\), some \(c\prec a\) has \(c\not\le b\). Take \(w=c^*\); then \(w\vee a=1\) and \(c\le c^{**}=w^*\le w\to b\), whence \(w\to b\ne b\). □

More generally,
\[
\bigvee_{w\vee a=1}w^*=\rho_A(a).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Indeed \(w^*\prec a\) when \(w\vee a=1\), and conversely \(c\prec a\) gives \(w=c^*\), \(w\vee a=1\) and \(w^*=c^{**}\ge c\).

**Lemma 2.2 (diagonal obstruction).** Suppose \(S=C(s)\) is *any closed sublocale of \(X^2\) containing the diagonal*. Then
\[
\boxed{\rho_Q(s)\le d_{\mathcal U}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Proof: if \(c\prec s\), then \(c^*\vee s=1\) and hence \(S\le O(c^*)\). In particular \(\Delta\le O(c^*)\), so \(c^*\) is a diagonal neighborhood. The already established identity
\[
d_{\mathcal U}
=\bigvee_{\Delta\le O(w)} w^*
\]
then gives \(c\le c^{**}\le d_{\mathcal U}\). Join over c. □

For \(S=\mathcal W\), \(d_{\mathcal U}\le d_{\mathcal W}\), so
\[
\rho_Q(d_{\mathcal W})=d_{\mathcal W}\Longrightarrow
\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal U=\mathcal H}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
The equality with H uses Lemma 2.1, not just the U equality. **Therefore absolute rather-below generation of \(d_{\mathcal W}\) cannot explain the noncoherent W=H<U examples.** In fact, in any such example \(\rho_Q(d_{\mathcal W})<d_{\mathcal W}\), even if Q itself is a fit frame. This is a substantive limitation on the naive regularity approach.

For comparison, \(d_{\mathcal F}\) *is* absolutely rather-below generated: \(\mathcal F\) is the intersection of pullbacks of diagonals of the regular locale \(\mathbb R\); the complement of the real diagonal is rather-below generated, inverse images preserve rather-below, and joins of such regular-element generators are again generated by elements rather below the resulting join. This re-proves fittedness of F by a stronger one-stage mechanism, but it cannot apply unchanged to W=H<U.

## 3. A fitted-envelope principle

**Theorem 3.1 (general localic lemma).** Suppose \(S\le T\le Z\), the ambient inclusion \(T\hookrightarrow Z\) is **fitted**, and the locale T is **fit** (all of its sublocales are fitted intrinsically). Then S is fitted as a sublocale of Z.

**Proof.** Every open sublocale of T is the intersection \(T\wedge O_Z(u)\) with some open sublocale of Z (the frame map \(\mathcal O(Z)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O(T)\) is surjective). Since T is fit, S is an intersection of these relative open sublocales, so \(S=T\wedge\bigwedge_i O_Z(u_i)\). As T itself is an intersection of open sublocales of Z, S is an intersection of open sublocales of Z. □

**Lemma 3.2 (fitting commutes with restriction to fitted envelopes).** If T is fitted in Z and contains S, then
\[
\operatorname{fit}_T(S)
=T\wedge\operatorname{fit}_Z(S)
=\operatorname{fit}_Z(S),
\tag{3.1}
\]
regarding both sides as sublocales of Z. The first equality comes from restricting ambient opens; the second because \(\operatorname{fit}_Z(S)\le T\) by minimality, as T is fitted and contains S.

Since W≤H≤F and both H and F are fitted in \(X^2\), this yields the EXACT reductions
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal H
\iff \mathcal W\text{ is fitted intrinsically in }\mathcal H
\iff \mathcal W\text{ is fitted intrinsically in }\mathcal F.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
In particular, either
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H\text{ is a fit locale}\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathcal F\text{ is a fit locale}}
\tag{3.3}
\]
is a sufficient condition for W=H. The whole square need not be fit. No implication W=U or H=F is asserted in arbitrary locales.

**Functional-envelope element test.** Let \(f=d_{\mathcal F}\le d=d_{\mathcal W}\). The frame of F is \(\uparrow f\), with Heyting implication inherited from Q for elements \(c,b\ge f\). Proposition 1 in this smaller frame and (3.2) give
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W=\mathcal H\iff
\forall b\in Q,\ b\ge f,\ d\not\le b\Rightarrow
\exists w\in Q:\ w\vee d=1,\ w\to b\ne b.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Indeed every element of \(\uparrow f\) representing a relative neighborhood can be written \(f\vee w\). Because \(f\le d,b\), one has \(f\vee w\vee d=w\vee d\), and
\((f\vee w)\to b=(f\to b)\wedge(w\to b)=w\to b\). This test is restricted to the functional envelope, not the entire square.

## 4. Relative regularity and relative clopenness, without W=U

In the frame \(\uparrow f=\mathcal O(\mathcal F)\), write
\[
c^{*_{\mathcal F}}=c\to f,\qquad
c\prec_{\mathcal F}d\iff(c\to f)\vee d=1.
\]
**Theorem 4 (working sufficient conditions).** Each of the following is sufficient for \(\mathcal W=\mathcal H\):

1. \(d=d_{\mathcal W}\) is **regularly generated inside** \(\mathcal O(\mathcal F)\):
\[
\boxed{d=\bigvee\{c\in Q:f\le c,\ (c\to f)\vee d=1\}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]
2. \(d\) is **complemented inside** \(\uparrow f\): there exists \(e\in Q\) with
\[
\boxed{e\ge f,\quad e\vee d=1,\quad e\wedge d=f.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
3. \(\mathcal W\) is an **open sublocale of \(\mathcal F\)** (it is already closed there); equivalently (4.2).

**Proof.** (1) is Lemma 2.1 applied to the smaller frame \(\uparrow f\), followed by (3.2). Condition (2) implies (1) because d itself is rather below d relative to f. Condition (2) is equivalent to W=C_F(d) being clopen in F (hence open and fitted), proving (3). □

**IMPORTANT:** Neither relative condition implies \(\mathcal W=\mathcal U\) in general. The diagonal obstruction (2.2) applies to the **absolute** rather-below relation on Q; in (4.1) the relative pseudocomplement is \(c\to f\), not \(c^*\). This is precisely the distinction needed to capture W=H<U.

## 5. The compact scattered alpha–beta–gamma example revisited

Recall the externally checked October example X consisting of disjoint infinite discrete sets \(N_1,N_2\) and three T₁ limit points \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Neighborhoods of α contain cofinally all of N₁; of β cofinally all of N₁ and N₂; of γ cofinally all of N₂. Its localic square is spatial and scattered and the audited relations are
\[
\mathcal W=\mathcal H=\mathcal I
<\mathcal U=\mathcal F.
\]
Inside the actual functional relation F, **the relative complement \(R=\mathcal F\wedge O(d_{\mathcal W})\) consists precisely of two points**, \((\alpha,\gamma)\) and \((\gamma,\alpha)\), because U\I has exactly these two cross pairs. They are relatively isolated: a product of an α-neighborhood (tracing only N₁) and γ-neighborhood (tracing only N₂) meets F only at \((\alpha,\gamma)\), since isolated sheet points are functionally separated and the remaining boundary points are excluded by these neighborhoods. Likewise for the transpose. The square is T₁, so these relative isolated points are closed in F as well; their union is a clopen sublocale of F. Hence W is **clopen inside F**, satisfying (4.2) and giving a direct alternative explanation of W=H that does *not* rely on the whole square being fit.

This matters: the relative criterion succeeds where (2.3) provably fails. Here W<U, so \(\rho_Q(d_{\mathcal W})<d_{\mathcal W}\); but \(d_{\mathcal W}\) is complemented in \(\mathcal O(\mathcal F)\). The distinction is real, not just terminological.

For an example where the ambient square is **not** fit but W=H<U nevertheless, take the localic disjoint sum of this X with the 3-point Alexandrov lower fork \(0<p,\ 0<q\). The fork has W=H=whole square by its least open neighborhood at 0, and componentwise clopen decomposition gives W=H on the sum, while W<U persists on the X block. The enlarged square is **not fit**: its clopen fork-square sublocale is nonfit, e.g. the closed one-point bottom \(\{(0,0)\}\) is not fitted there. This separate example only demonstrates that whole-square fitness is not necessary, not any new connected construction.

## 6. T₁ squares: nonfitted W forces a pointless functional residual

**Theorem 6 (working).** Assume the localic square \(Z=X^2\) is **spatial and T₁** (e.g. X is locally compact sober and T₁). Put
\[
R=\mathcal F\wedge O(d_{\mathcal W}),
\qquad
E=\operatorname{fit}_Z(\mathcal W)\wedge O(d_{\mathcal W}).
\]
Then:

(a) \(\mathcal W\ne\mathcal H\) iff \(E\ne0\).

(b) If \(E\ne0\), then E is a **nonzero pointless sublocale of R**.

(c) Hence if **every nonzero sublocale of R has a point** (in particular, if R is a discrete locale / complete atomic Boolean frame, or if R is totally spatial), then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W=\mathcal H.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(\mathcal F\) is fitted and contains W, \(\operatorname{fit}_Z(W)\le F\), hence \(E\le R\). The open and closed sublocales \(O(d),C(d)=W\) are complementary, and fitting W contains W. Thus \(\operatorname{fit}(W)=W\) iff its open restriction E vanishes, proving (a).

For (b), suppose p is any ordinary point of Z outside W. Because Z is T₁, \(w=Z\setminus\{p\}\) is open. Since W=C(d) is closed and p lies in O(d), d∨w=1 as an equality of opens in the **spatial** Z. Hence W≤O(w), so \(\operatorname{fit}(W)\le O(w)\) and does not contain p. Therefore every ordinary point of \(\operatorname{fit}(W)\) lies in W. But E lies in O(d), disjoint from W, so E has no ordinary points. If E≠0 this is a nonzero pointless sublocale of R.

If every nonzero sublocale of R has a point, then E cannot be nonzero; (c) follows. Note the hypothesis is **stronger** than mere spatiality of R: a spatial locale can contain a nonzero pointless Booleanization. □

**Interpretation.** A failure W<H on a spatial T₁ square cannot be witnessed by an ordinary point, even though coherent finite-poset failures can be witnessed by ordinary points (those posets are not T₁). Such failure requires a genuinely pointfree sublocale of the *functional residual* \(F\setminus W\), a stricter localization than merely looking somewhere in the ambient square. This is conceptually parallel to the earlier Q′ pointlessness obstruction, but is an **independent problem** and uses no W₂.
