# Adversarial audit of dense-bridge Cantor multigraph counterexample
22 October 2026. Internal independent proof-check, not an external blind referee report.

## Verdict
**Main strict-inclusion argument survives audit, subject to minor proof clarifications.** I found no fatal gap in the asserted V⊊W_bgr for the strengthened countable Hausdorff rational bridge locale. Critical proof is localic, not point-set product identification.

## Verification of the construction
Let K be standard Cantor set and C=K∩Q. Endpoints of complementary Cantor intervals are rational and dense in K, so C is dense in K and has no isolated points. C is closed as subset of Q. Enumerate basic nonempty real rectangles I_n×J_n. The sets Q−K and Q+K are countable unions of compact nowhere dense translates of K. At each stage choose α_n in I_n avoiding Q−K, earlier α_i and β_i−K, then β_n in J_n avoiding Q+K, earlier β_i and α_i+K (including α_n+K); all forbidden sets are meagre. This ensures α,β irrational, pairwise distinct on each side, β_m−α_n∉K for all m,n and dense pairs (α_n,β_n). Rational sequences A_n→α_n and B_n→β_n can be chosen pairwise disjoint by selecting them in tiny mutually disjoint real neighborhoods of the individual limit points, inductively; previously chosen countably many sequences may cause trouble if chosen naively, so explicitly require each sequence be closed/discrete in Q and avoid previous chosen points, and choose recursively (finite previous sequences are closed nowhere dense in R when including their real irrational limits). Defining neighborhoods at z_n as z_n plus open L/R sets containing tails is coherent. X is Hausdorff, L,R open, Z closed discrete.

## Closedness of ordinary F_C
F_C={(ℓ_t,r_s):s−t∈C}; inside L×R, use difference map Q²→Q and closed C. For boundary pairs (z_n,r_s), the compact set K is disjoint from s−α_n by α_n∉Q−K, giving neighborhoods around α_n,s such that differences miss K. Same at (ℓ_t,z_m) using β_m−t∉K; at (z_n,z_m) use β_m−α_n∉K. A_n,B_m eventually lie in these real neighborhoods, so the z-neighborhoods can be chosen with L/R components inside desired rational intervals; other components arbitrary. These yield open product neighborhoods missing F_C. Other quadrants immediately excluded. Thus F_C is ordinary closed and disjoint diagonal.

## Localic q_C∨d_I=1
Map η:⊔_{c∈C}Q→X×loc X via graphs; q_C is largest open killed by η*, equals meet q_c in frame T, and is proper. e=L∨R, e⊠e≤q_C∨d_I from quadrant decomposition. Each ordinary point with Z coordinate lies outside ordinary closed F_C, so an open rectangle around it misses all graph branches, giving generator ≤q_C. Apply Claude's frame boundary lemma for closed discrete Z. Therefore q_C∨d_I=1 in the NONSPATIAL locale coproduct. Do not replace with mere pointwise argument.

## Inseparable projections
Any nonempty relatively open G⊂F_C contains graph point (ℓ_t,r_{t+c}) and a relatively open rectangular slice based on open rational intervals I,J. Shrink I with I+c⊂J and t∈I. Dense (α_n,β_n) in R² gives α_n∈I and β_n∈I+c. A_n eventually in I, giving ℓ_{a} and r_{a+c} in G; B_n eventually in I+c, giving ℓ_{b−c} and r_b in G. Thus z_n lies in closures of both projections. IMPORTANT: the witnesses use two different sets of rational parameter values; their equality is not needed.

## Strip nuclei
Suppose a frame element t satisfies t∧s≤q_C but t≰q_C. Since T is join-generated by rectangles, some a⊠b≤t has η*(a⊠b)≠0; choose graph point in its ordinary rectangle, giving nonempty relative-open G=F_C∩(a×b). The inequality implies every point G lies outside ordinary strip s, hence projections lie in closed complements X\k,X\h, so common bridge point z_n is in both. Diagonal point (z_n,z_n) annihilates s and d_I, contradicting d_I∨s=1. Hence s→q_C=q_C, entirely as a frame inequality.

## Single-valued graph nuclei
For ANY partial continuous locale map φ:U_open→X, ordinary graph closure intersects the open set U×X exactly in its graph, because X is Hausdorff. On a relative-open slice G=F_C∩(I×J), choose a smaller nonempty rational interval I'⊂I around a graph parameter t0 with I'+c0⊂J. Since C has no isolated points, for each t∈I' there are at least TWO shifts c in C with t+c∈J (choose c near c0). If I'∩U=∅, the open cylinder I'×X misses graph closure. If I'∩U≠∅ choose t in it; closure over this interior-domain coordinate cannot contain two distinct outputs in G. Thus G cannot lie in the closed graph image. Same for reverse graph by using second-coordinate interval J' with two first-coordinate fiber values.

If t∧q_φ≤q_C but t≰q_C, use rectangle r≤t with graph point in F_C, choose point of relative-open F_C∩r outside ordinary closure of φ graph, then shrink to an ordinary rectangular neighborhood inside r disjoint from graph. The resulting frame rectangle lies below t∧q_φ but has nonzero η*-image, contradiction. This proves q_φ→q_C=q_C in either orientation. No assumption that locale product is spatial is used: ordinary points only produce nonzero rectangular pullbacks.

## Conclusion
All strip and bi-graph open nuclei fix proper q_C; their nucleus join fixes q_C (since it is the least nucleus dominating them and q_C is already a common fixed point: apply closure or use iterative prenucleus). But q_C∨d_I=1 implies j_V(q_C)=1. Thus strict V⊊W_bgr, confirming that the collectively étale primitive O(q_C) strictly strengthens the symmetric bi-graph scheme in this constructed X.

## Recommended edits
(1) Explicitly choose α_n,β_n pairwise distinct on both sides and all cross K-avoidance by recursion.
(2) Emphasize topological ordinary F_C closedness versus localic q_C cover via boundary lemma.
(3) In graph-closure lemma, state the relative closed-graph property over open domain, and separately cover transposed orientation.
(4) Do not assert étale COMPLETE: only étale strictly stronger on this example.
(5) Get a fresh external blind audit for the new construction before treating as publication-ready.
