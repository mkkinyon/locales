# Depth-two bar collapse via Lindelof P-spaces

For any spatial locale X whose space is Lindelof and a P-space (countable intersections of opens are open), every rectangular bar B of L=O(X) admits a depth-two horizontal-then-vertical separation tree.

Proof: Put S=D union B, with D disjointness pairs. The bar equation join_{(a,b) in S}(a box b)=1, evaluated at each product point (x,y), yields that the b with x in a cover X. Lindelof yields a countable subcover (a_n,b_n) with x in all a_n and join_n b_n=1. The countable intersection a_x=intersection_n a_n is open by the P-space property and contains x. Thus (a_x,1) splits vertically to leaves (a_x,b_n) under S. The a_x cover X as x varies. This constructs depth two. The proof never assumes localic-product spatiality.

Example: any uncountable set with cocountable topology. It is Lindelof: one nonempty member of a cover omits only countably many points, covered by countably more members. It is a P-space: countable intersection of complements of countable sets remains cocountable. It is noncompact (take increasing complements of a decreasing nonempty sequence of countable tails, exhausting X), hyperconnected and nonregular, and it lacks a nonzero completely join-prime basis. Thus another noncompact nonregular class collapses.

General cardinal version: If X has the property that every open cover admits a subcover of cardinality at most kappa, and intersections of at most kappa opens remain open, the same proof yields depth two. Intersections must themselves be open, not merely have nonempty interior at each x.

Still unknown: whether arbitrary frames exhibit strict depth-two separation-tree rank. All these positive classes do not establish the universal statement.
