# Project A — Missing-center forcing gluing and the finite-flip obstruction

**9 November 2026 · Internal working stress test. Not externally audited; no originality or priority claim.** Focus: Q′, the unresolved question whether \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2\) for every frame. This note **does not** resolve Q′. It explains why a stronger two-sheet/atomless-boundary construction still has \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_2=\mathcal U\) locally.

## 1. Framework

For \(L=\mathcal O(X)\), let \(Q=L\oplus L\), \(w_{\mathcal C}=\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}c\boxtimes c\) for a cover \(\mathcal C\) of \(1\),
\[
\mathcal D=\bigcap_{\mathcal C\text{ any cover}}O(w_{\mathcal C}),\quad
\mathcal D_2=\bigcap_{F\text{ finite cover}}O(w_F),\quad
\mathcal W=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D),\quad
\mathcal W_2=\operatorname{cl}(\mathcal D_2),\quad
\mathcal U=C(d_{\mathcal U}).
\]
An induced frame homomorphism \(Q\to M\) determined by \(h,k:L\to M\) factors through \(\mathcal D\) iff
\(\bigvee_{c\in\mathcal C}h(c)\wedge k(c)=1_M\) for every cover, and through \(\mathcal D_2\) iff the condition holds for every finite cover.

Let \(R=\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q\) (the irrational numbers) with the subspace topology. Fix an enumeration \((q_n)_{n\ge1}\) of \(\mathbb Q\). Let
\[
P=\operatorname{Fn}(\mathbb N,2)\setminus\{\varnothing\}
\]
be the set of finite nonempty partial binary functions, ordered by extension, with upper Alexandrov topology. Write \(\uparrow s\) for the principal open generated by a finite condition \(s\), and \(\ell_n=\{n\mapsto0\}\). The poset is **not** hyperconnected: incompatible conditions have disjoint principal opens.

Take two disjoint open sheets \(A,B\), each a copy of \(R\times P\). Introduce new boundary points \(t_n\), one for each rational center \(q_n\). Set \(X=A\sqcup B\sqcup\{t_n:n\ge1\}\). Besides the sheet product opens, use the basic neighborhoods
\[
N_{n,I}=\{t_n\}\cup((I\cap R)\times\uparrow\ell_n)^A
                       \cup((I\cap R)\times\uparrow\ell_n)^B,
\quad q_n\in I,
\tag{1.1}
\]
where \(I\) is an open interval in \(\mathbb R\) (allow \(I=\mathbb R\)). Intersections of two such opens with distinct boundary points are unions of sheet-open rectangles; for the same boundary point their intersection has the same form with interval \(I\cap J\). Intersections with sheet basics are open by construction. So these sets and the sheet basics form a basis. The space is second-countable; its closed remainder \(T=X\setminus(A\cup B)\) is countably infinite and discrete, thus noncompact.

For each fixed n, \(\bigcap_{I\ni q_n}(N_{n,I}\cap A)=\bigcap_{I\ni q_n}(N_{n,I}\cap B)=\varnothing\), since \(q_n\notin R\). There is **no ordinary cross-sheet pair** satisfying every cover: for any sheet point \(x\) with irrational parameter \(r\), choose for each n an interval \(I_n\ni q_n\) avoiding \(r\). The cover \(\{A,B\}\cup\{N_{n,I_n}:n\ge1\}\) has no member containing both \(x\in A\) and any \(y\in B\). Since frame points of the sheet locales \(R\times P\) have actual irrational parameter, the same excludes cross-sheet points of \(\mathcal D\) in their sober representations.

## 2. Boolean-valued finite-cover defect

Let \(K=2^{\mathbb N}\), \(M=\operatorname{RO}(R\times K)\), and denote by \([s]\subseteq K\) the nonempty clopen cylinder of infinite sequences extending a finite condition \(s\). Write \(\overline s\) for the condition obtained by changing each bit of \(s\). Let \(j:\mathcal O(R\times K)\to M\) be double-negation regularization.

For a sheet-open \(U\subseteq R\times P\), set \(U_s=\{r\in R:(r,s)\in U\}\) and define
\[
H_+(U)=j\Bigl(\bigcup_{s\in P}U_s\times[s]\Bigr),\qquad
H_-(U)=j\Bigl(\bigcup_{s\in P}U_s\times[\overline s]\Bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]
They are frame maps: arbitrary joins are immediate, finite meets follow because compatible finite conditions have common extensions, incompatible cylinders are disjoint, and \(j\) is a frame quotient. Both maps send top to top.

Restrict an open of X to A or B and apply \(H_+\) or \(H_-\):
\[
h(U)=H_+(U\cap A),\qquad k(U)=H_-(U\cap B).
\]
Then \(h(A)=k(B)=1\), \(h(B)=k(A)=0\), and
\[
h(N_{n,I})=j((I\cap R)\times[n=0]),\quad
k(N_{n,I})=j((I\cap R)\times[n=1]).
\tag{2.2}
\]
These two images have zero meet.

**Finite-cover compatibility.** Let \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\) cover X. For each n, select \(j(n)\) such that \(t_n\in U_{j(n)}\), and choose \(I_n\ni q_n\) with \(N_{n,I_n}\subseteq U_{j(n)}\). Fix a nonempty basic open \(O\times[s]\subseteq R\times K\), with \(O=J\cap R\) for a real interval J. For all but finitely many n, n is outside \(\operatorname{dom}(s)\). Since the rational centers with these indices are dense in J, one color class \(E=\{q_n:j(n)=j\}\) has closure containing a nonempty open interval \(J'\subseteq J\) (finite union of nowhere-dense closed sets cannot cover J). Choose n of this class with \(q_n\in J'\) and n outside \(\operatorname{dom}(s)\); then choose a distinct n′ of the same class, also outside that finite domain, with \(q_{n'}\in I_n\cap J'\). Their intervals overlap in a nonempty real open interval. The set
\[
(I_n\cap I_{n'}\cap J'\cap R)\times([s]\cap[n=0]\cap[n'=1])
\]
is nonempty open and lies in \(h(U_j)\cap k(U_j)\). Thus \(\bigvee_jh(U_j)\wedge k(U_j)=1_M\). So the pair factors through \(\mathcal D_2\).

But the infinite cover
\[
\mathcal C=\{A,B\}\cup\{N_{n,\mathbb R}:n\ge1\}
\tag{2.3}
\]
is not compatible: every \(h(c)\wedge k(c)\) vanishes. This proves \(\mathcal D\ne\mathcal D_2\), and the witness is supported in the disjoint mixed rectangle
\(\rho=A\boxtimes B\), where \(h(A)\wedge k(B)=1\).

The closed interface T is noncompact. For (2.3), the cover-dependent rather-below localization \(t_{\mathcal C}=0\): any nonzero sheet open contains \(J\times\uparrow s\), and its closure meets \(t_n\) for infinitely many n with \(q_n\in J\) and n outside \(\operatorname{dom}(s)\); the same is true for every nonzero open of X. A finite subunion of (2.3) contains at most finitely many t_n, so no nonzero open is rather below it.

## 3. The Urysohn boundary on the mixed rectangle

Every nonzero rectangle \(u\boxtimes v\le\rho\) contains a nonzero subrectangle whose two rational parameters lie in disjoint real intervals with disjoint closures. Such a subrectangle is **far**: its sheet closures can meet only at boundary centers \(q_n\), and no rational center belongs to both separated interval closures. Hence \(d_{\mathcal U}\wedge\rho\) is dense in \(\rho\), so \(\rho\le d_{\mathcal U}^{**}\). The Urysohn relation restricted to \(\rho\) lies in the nonregular residual \(G=\mathcal U\wedge O(d_{\mathcal U}^{**})\).

For ordinary cross-sheet points \(((r,s)^A,(r',t)^B)\), the Urysohn relation holds **iff \(r=r'\)**. If \(r\ne r'\), separated parameter neighborhoods are far. If \(r=r'\), any two product neighborhoods contain parameter intervals overlapping in a nonempty real interval, which contains a rational \(q_n\) outside the finite domains of s and t. The two opens both accumulate at \(t_n\), and hence are not far.

In particular \(\mathcal U\wedge O(\rho)\ne0\), and its Booleanization is atomless: every nonzero relative open admits two disjoint nonzero parameter localizations. The finite-cover witness (2.1) therefore lies inside a nonzero atomless \(\mathcal H_2=\mathcal W_2\wedge G\) part.

## 4. The unexpected rescue: finite-coordinate flips

The partial-function forcing P has **no common upper bound for incompatible conditions**, unlike the earlier finite-subsets poset, and the projection \(U\mapsto\pi_R(U)\) fails to preserve finite meets. Ordinary cross-sheet \(\mathcal D\)-points are absent (§1). Nonetheless, \(\mathcal D\) is dense in \(\mathcal U\) on the mixed rectangle because of a different family of Boolean-valued maps.

For every finite \(E\subseteq\mathbb N\), let \(\gamma_E\) be the automorphism of M induced by flipping precisely the coordinates in E on K. Define
\[
\theta_A(U)=H_+(U\cap A),\qquad
\theta_B^E(U)=\gamma_E(H_+(U\cap B)).
\tag{4.1}
\]
**Claim 4.2.** Every pair \((\theta_A,\theta_B^E)\) is *fully cover compatible*.

Given any cover \(\mathcal V\) of X, for each n choose \(V_n\in\mathcal V\) containing t_n, hence some \(N_{n,I_n}\subseteq V_n\). For n outside E,
\[
\theta_A(V_n)\wedge\theta_B^E(V_n)
\ \ge\ j((I_n\cap R)\times[n=0]).
\]
These opens, as n ranges outside finite E, have **dense union** in \(R\times K\): every nonempty basic \(O\times[s]\) meets one by choosing \(q_n\) in the underlying real interval for O with n outside \(E\cup\operatorname{dom}(s)\). Therefore the join of the intersections over \(\mathcal V\) is 1 in M. This proves the claim.

Now let \(u\le A,v\le B\) be nonzero and suppose their irrational-parameter projections overlap. Choose a common irrational parameter r and basic subopens
\[
(J\cap R)\times\uparrow s\subseteq u,\qquad
(J'\cap R)\times\uparrow t\subseteq v
\]
with r∈J∩J′. There is a **finite** set E of conflicting coordinates so that the cylinders \([s]\) and \(\gamma_E([t])\) are compatible; take E to consist of coordinates where s and t prescribe opposite bits. Thus
\[
\theta_A(u)\wedge\theta_B^E(v)\ne0.
\]
Since \((\theta_A,\theta_B^E)\) factors through the **full** fitted diagonal \(\mathcal D\), this gives \(\mathcal D\wedge O(u\boxtimes v)\ne0\).

If the parameter projections of u,v are disjoint, then \(u\boxtimes v\le d_{\mathcal U}\), **in the actual frame coproduct**: first express u as a join of basic sheet rectangles with parameter intervals J whose closures lie inside \(\pi(u)\). For each such fixed basic rectangle, express v as a join of sheet rectangles with parameter intervals K having \(\overline K\cap\overline J=\varnothing\); this is possible because every parameter of v lies outside \(\overline J\subseteq\pi(u)\), and the irrational line is regular. Each resulting cross rectangle is far, since the only potential common closure points lie at rational boundary centers, and disjoint parameter closures preclude those. Applying coordinatewise join distributivity **twice**, rather than invoking ordinary product points, proves the inequality. Therefore **every nonzero relatively open part of** \(\mathcal U\wedge O(\rho)\) meets \(\mathcal D\). We conclude
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W\wedge O(\rho)
=\mathcal W_2\wedge O(\rho)
=\mathcal U\wedge O(\rho).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
This is a **local** equality; no global W=W2 assertion for this X is needed or established.

Notably, (4.3) holds despite there being **no ordinary cross-sheet points of** \(\mathcal D\). The dense sublocale is witnessed by truly nonspatial complete Boolean-valued points, and the maps (4.1) are *different* from the finite-cover-defective pair (h,k) of §2.

## 5. Disposition

The new example simultaneously has: two disjoint open sheets, noncompact closed interface, nonprincipal filters with empty sheet intersections, no ordinary cross-sheet \(\mathcal D\)-points, a genuine Cantor-valued finite-cover defect supported on the mixed rectangle, \(t_{\mathcal C}=0\), and a nonzero atomless nonregular \(\mathcal H_2\) part. Yet the full fitted diagonal is dense in that part.

The **finite-support symmetry obstruction** is the mechanism: for every pair of basic finite forcing conditions, a finite coordinate flip makes their Boolean images compatible, without changing the infinitely many boundary labels needed for full-cover compatibility. In the prior finite-subset model density came from common upper bounds; in the rational nonprincipal model it came from dense-projection regularization; in this model neither explanation applies. Tail-stable finite-coordinate automorphisms are a third, distinct source of density.

To beat this mechanism using forcing-like sheets, one needs a nonhomogeneous or rigid Boolean completion in which no family of modifications supported on finitely many labels can connect arbitrary relevant basic opens while leaving a cofinal family of boundary labels unchanged. That is a proposed construction constraint, **not** a formulated new theorem or a claim Q′ is false.

**Q′ and Q remain open.** No external review is requested at this stage.
