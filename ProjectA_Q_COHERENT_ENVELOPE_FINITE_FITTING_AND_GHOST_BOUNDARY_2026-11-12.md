# Project A — Canonical coherent envelope: finite fitting is a full fitting trace; prime predecessors live in a disjoint spectral remainder

**12 Nov 2026 — bounded internal algebraic segment; no external audit or literature priority claim.** This revisits the preceding universal Boolean prime-predecessor argument, testing whether the relative OPEN position of a hypothetical rank-one Q-hole forces a new infinitary frame law. The direct lift does NOT establish that: the prime selection always exists in a **canonical compact coherent envelope**, and a bad cover forces every selected predecessor into a closed remainder DISJOINT from the original locale. This is a precise geometric diagnosis; a branching Scott weak-fitting defect exhibits the same external-anchor pattern while Q holds. Both Q and Q′ remain OPEN.

The ideal completion of a bounded distributive lattice and its spectral prime-filter space are STANDARD constructions; no originality claim is made for them. The exact fitted-diagonal trace identity and its rank-one interpretation are recorded here as working deductions, not claims of literature novelty.

## 0. The coherent envelope of an arbitrary frame

Let L be ANY frame. Denote its underlying bounded distributive lattice by \(L_{\mathrm{lat}}\), and form
\[
\widehat L=\mathrm{Idl}(L_{\mathrm{lat}}).
\]
Elements are NONEMPTY lower sets directed under finite joins, including 0; principal ideals are \(\downarrow a=\{x:x\le a\}\). The frame \(\widehat L\) is COHERENT, with compact generators precisely the principal ideals \(\downarrow a\); its top \(\downarrow1=L\) is compact. Its locale \(\widehat X=\mathrm{Loc}(\widehat L)\) is the SPECTRAL space of ordinary finitely prime filters of the lattice \(L_{\rm lat}\), and is therefore SPATIAL and COMPACT even if \(X=\mathrm{Loc}(L)\) is entirely POINTLESS.

Define
\[
\varepsilon:\widehat L\to L,\qquad
\varepsilon(J)=\bigvee_{a\in J}a.
\tag{0.1}
\]
This preserves arbitrary joins (the join of ideals is generated by finite joins of the union), finite meets (for ideals I,J, \((\vee I)\wedge(\vee J)=\vee_{i\in I,j\in J}(i\wedge j)=\vee(I\cap J)\)), and top; it is SURJECTIVE because \(\varepsilon(\downarrow a)=a\). Thus
\[
\boxed{i:X\hookrightarrow\widehat X}
\tag{0.2}
\]
is a locale SUBLOCALE inclusion, not necessarily OPEN or CLOSED; and \(\widehat X\) is a spectral compactification in the broad locale-sublocale sense. Do NOT confuse the literal point set \(\mathrm{pt}(X)\) with all finitely prime filters of \(L\).

### 0.1 The adjunction: ALL infinitary defects originate in the principal-ideal inclusion

Define
\[
j:L_{\rm lat}\hookrightarrow\widehat L,\qquad j(a)=\downarrow a.
\]
This is a BOUNDED DISTRIBUTIVE-LATTICE embedding, preserving 0,1, finite joins/meets, but not necessarily arbitrary frame joins. Crucially
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon\dashv j,\quad
\varepsilon(J)\le a\iff J\le j(a),\quad
\varepsilon\circ j=\operatorname{id}_L.}
\tag{0.3}
\]
Thus a frame cover C produces the exact infinitary discrepancy
\[
\boxed{
\bigvee_{c\in C}j(c)=\widehat u_C\le j(1)=1_{\widehat L},
\qquad
\widehat u_C<1_{\widehat L}\iff C\text{ has no finite subcover}.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

The ideal completion has the universal property that EVERY bounded lattice homomorphism \(p_{\rm fin}:L_{\rm lat}\to M\), with M a frame, extends uniquely to a FRAME homomorphism
\[
\widehat p(J)=\bigvee_{a\in J}p_{\rm fin}(a),
\qquad
\boxed{p_{\rm fin}=\widehat p\circ j.}
\tag{0.5}
\]
This includes the prime predecessor from the previous Gleason argument. Consequently the failed infinite join of p_fin is simply the frame map \(\widehat p\) **detecting the failure of j to preserve that infinite join**. The closed remainder R_C from §2 is the geometric complement of precisely the missing open \(\widehat u_C\). This adjunction explains the full-versus-finite gap at its simplest algebraic level; it does not constrain the RELATIVELY OPEN position of a strong Q-hole.

## 1. Exact finite/full fitted-diagonal trace

Write \(D_2(X)=\bigwedge_{F\text{ finite X-cover}}\mathfrak o_X(w_F)\) and \(\mathcal D(\widehat X)=\bigwedge_{G\text{ any }\widehat X\text{-cover}}\mathfrak o_{\widehat X}(w_G)\), using open rectangles in the appropriate square.

**Theorem 1.1 (coherent-envelope trace identity).** For EVERY locale X,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_2(X)
=(i\times i)^{-1}\mathcal D(\widehat X)
=\mathcal D(\widehat X)\wedge X^2
}
\tag{1.1}
\]
as ACTUAL sublocales of X², without spatiality, compactness or frame coherence of X.

*Proof.* Every finite L-cover \(F=\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}\), with \(\bigvee a_j=1_L\), lifts to a finite \(\widehat L\)-cover \(\{\downarrow a_1,\dots,\downarrow a_n\}\), because
\[
\bigvee_{\widehat L,j}\downarrow a_j
=\downarrow\bigvee_{L,j}a_j=\widehat L.
\]
The pullback under i×i of its diagonal square is exactly \(w_F^X\). Conversely, for ANY finite \(\widehat L\)-cover G, its image \(\varepsilon(G)\) is a finite L-cover and its diagonal square pulls back to \(w_{\varepsilon(G)}^X\). Thus the finite diagonal-square cover families agree exactly after restriction. Taking their arbitrary SUBLOCALE meets gives
\[
\mathcal D_2(X)=(i\times i)^{-1}\mathcal D_2(\widehat X).
\]
Because \(\widehat X\) is compact, \(\mathcal D_2(\widehat X)=\mathcal D(\widehat X)\). QED.

**Equivalence for generalized pairs with ANY target.** A generalized pair h,k:L→M (arbitrary frame M) is finite-cover-compatible on X iff the extended pair
\[
\widehat h=h\circ\varepsilon,\qquad
\widehat k=k\circ\varepsilon:\widehat L\to M
\tag{1.2}
\]
is FULL-cover-compatible on the coherent COMPACT envelope. This also proves (1.1) directly by the universal characterization of fitted diagonal sublocales. No Booleanization or Gleason theorem is needed for this equivalence.

**Corollary 1.2 (rank-one as a density problem after restriction).** In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_2(X)
=\operatorname{cl}_{X^2}\bigl(\mathcal D(\widehat X)\wedge X^2\bigr).
}
\tag{1.3}
\]
Since \(\widehat X\) is coherent, its OWN closed full fitted hull satisfies \(\mathcal W(\widehat X)=\mathcal U(\widehat X)\). But it is NOT legitimate to exchange the closure in (1.3) with arbitrary non-open restriction to X², and generally U(X) is only contained in \(\mathcal U(\widehat X)\wedge X²\) by far naturality (equality NOT asserted). Thus (1.3) is a geometric restatement of finite fitted density, not a proof of Q.

## 2. Every infinitary cover has an exact CLOSED remainder in the envelope

For an arbitrary X-open cover C (so \(\bigvee_{c\in C}c=1_L\)), put
\[
\boxed{
\widehat u_C=\bigvee_{c\in C}\downarrow c
=\{a\in L:\exists F\subseteq C\text{ finite with }a\le\bigvee F\}\in\widehat L.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is the lattice ideal generated by C, generally strictly below top \(\downarrow1\).

Let
\[
\boxed{
R_C=\mathfrak c_{\widehat X}(\widehat u_C)
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
be its CLOSED complementary sublocale, whose ordinary points are prime filters of L containing NONE of the c∈C.

**Lemma 2.1 (canonical closed cover boundary).**
\[
\boxed{
R_C\ne0\iff C\text{ has NO finite subcover},
\qquad R_C\wedge X=0.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
*Proof.* Since top \(\widehat L\) is the principal ideal ↓1, \(\widehat u_C=1_{\widehat L}\) iff \(1_L\) is bounded by a finite join from C, i.e. C has a finite subcover. Thus the closed complement is nonzero exactly without a finite subcover. On the other hand \(\varepsilon(\widehat u_C)=\bigvee C=1_L\), so the open \(\widehat u_C\) contains the sublocale X; hence its closed complement meets X in ZERO. QED.

In particular every cover without a finite subcover induces a NONZERO closed spectral remainder disjoint from X; the existence of such a remainder is equivalent to an ordinary FAILURE OF COMPACTNESS and is NOT a Q/Q′ obstruction by itself.

## 3. The prime predecessor forced into the same closed remainder

Let M be a nonzero COMPLETE BOOLEAN ALGEBRA and h,k:L→M finite-cover-compatible. The previous universal Gleason–patch theorem supplies a bounded distributive-lattice predecessor \(p_{\rm fin}:L\to M\) with
\[
p_{\rm fin}(a)\le h(a)\wedge k(a)\quad(\forall a).
\]
Extend p_fin to a frame map on the coherent envelope:
\[
\boxed{
\widehat p:\widehat L\to M,\qquad
\widehat p(J)=\bigvee_{a\in J}p_{\rm fin}(a).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This preserves arbitrary joins (ideal generation), finite meets (use \(i\wedge j\in I\cap J\)), and top because \(p_{\rm fin}(1)=1\). It is therefore a genuine LOCALE MAP from the Boolean locale with frame M INTO \(\widehat X\), with \(\widehat p\preceq \widehat h,\widehat k\) pointwise on \(\widehat L\).

**Theorem 3.1 (ghost predecessor factorization).** Suppose a cover C of X has ZERO matching value on M:
\[
\bigvee_{c\in C}h(c)\wedge k(c)=0_M.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Then EVERY bounded-lattice predecessor p_fin ≤h∧k (not only one Gleason-selected predecessor) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\widehat p(\widehat u_C)=\bigvee_{c\in C}p_{\rm fin}(c)=0_M,
\qquad
\widehat p:\operatorname{Loc}(M)\longrightarrow R_C\hookrightarrow\widehat X.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Moreover R_C is nonzero and disjoint from X.

*Proof.* Since each h(c)∧k(c)=0, each p_fin(c)=0. Because p_fin preserves finite joins, all members of the ideal \(\widehat u_C\) have p_fin-value zero. Thus \(\widehat p(\widehat u_C)=0\), and the map factors through the closed sublocale R_C. Nonzero-ness and disjointness follow from Lemma 2.1. □

**Strong limitation:** The target R_C is EXTERNAL to X as a sublocale. This map is NOT a generalized predecessor into the original locale X. That failure is EXACTLY the infinite-join law \(p_{\rm fin}(\bigvee C)\ne\bigvee_Cp_{\rm fin}(c)\), expressed geometrically. The existence of such a ghost is not special to Q: the same lift exists for every weak D₂>D Boolean witness.

## 4. All common prime predecessors avoid compact frame elements

Let K(L) denote the compact elements of L. No algebraicity assumption is needed for this lemma.

**Corollary 4.1 (cover-independent compact-open corona).** Under Theorem 3.1, for every compact frame element b∈K(L),
\[
\boxed{p_{\rm fin}(b)=0_M.}
\tag{4.1}
\]
*Proof.* Because b≤∨C and b is compact, choose finite F⊆C with b≤∨F. Since p_fin is monotone and finite-join-preserving,
\[
p_{\rm fin}(b)\le\bigvee_{c\in F}p_{\rm fin}(c)=0.
\]
QED.

Let
\[
J_{\rm cpt}=\{a\in L:a\le b\text{ for SOME compact }b\in K(L)\}
\]
be the lattice ideal generated by all compact frame elements, and put \(R_{\rm cpt}=\mathfrak c_{\widehat X}(J_{\rm cpt})\). Then every ghost predecessor \(\widehat p\) as above factors through the SAME closed sublocale \(R_{\rm cpt}\), independently of the bad cover C; in fact \(R_C\le R_{\rm cpt}\), because every compact b lies below a finite join of members of C, so \(J_{\rm cpt}\subseteq\widehat u_C\).

If L is ALGEBRAIC (compact elements join-generate 1), then \(R_{\rm cpt}\wedge X=0\), but it can be nonzero when L is noncompact. It is the explicit “prime-at-infinity” boundary, consisting of finitely prime filters that contain NO compact frame element. Since every completely prime filter of an algebraic frame meets some compact element, this boundary has **NO ordinary points of X**, even though it may contain many prime-filter points of \(\widehat X\).

**No-go:** For NONCOMPACT algebraic frames, R_cpt is nonempty by the prime ideal theorem (the compact elements have no finite join equal to 1). Hence the existence of the boundary cannot itself contradict rank-one fixedness: it is present for any noncompact algebraic frame, including Q-positive Scott examples.

## 5. Two controls: free ultrafilters with no defect; Scott ghost with weak defect

**Control A (discrete countable X).** Let L=P(N), the powerset Boolean frame (opens of infinite discrete N). Then
\[
\widehat X=\mathrm{Spec}_{lat}(P(N))=\beta N
\]
as an ordinary Stone space. The embedding X=N↪βN is the usual OPEN DENSE inclusion. For the infinite singleton cover C={{n}:n∈N}, \(\widehat u_C=\mathrm{Fin}(N)\) and
\[
R_C=\beta N\setminus N\ne0.
\]
Nevertheless **D₂(X)=D(X)=Δ_X**: every open is complemented, so finite-cover-compatible coordinate maps agree on all opens (using each \{a,a^*\} cover). Thus there is NO fitting defect. The external remainder exists merely because X is noncompact; it does not predict Q failure.

**Control B (branching Scott positive Q).** Let X be the upper-Alexandrov locale of nonempty finite partial binary functions, with the singleton-literal cover C used in the earlier branching example. The Boolean maps h,k into RO(Cantor) are finite-compatible and C-matching zero, although W=W₂=U=X². By Theorem 3.1 their selected prime predecessor lands entirely in the closed \(\widehat X\) remainder R_C; by Corollary 4.1 it avoids EVERY compact principal Scott open. This supplies an explicit weak-fitting nonframe ghost despite Q and Q′ both holding.

This is the decisive **negative control** for treating spectral ghost predecessors as Q evidence. A hypothetical Q-hole's Boolean witness would have precisely the same remainder pattern, plus the independent fact of being dense in a NONZERO relatively OPEN portion of U. The latter geometric condition was NOT used in Theorem 3.1.

## 6. Exact open-hole reformulation in the coherent envelope

The externally reviewed prenucleus support theorem proved, for \(d_0=d_{W₂(X)}\) and any X-cover C,
\[
E_C

\operatorname{cl}_{X²}\bigl(D₂(X)\wedge\mathfrak c_{X²}(w_C)\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]
(This is an EQUALITY OF SUBLOCALES, not a statement about the ordinary point sets; see [prenucleus external-vs-internal reconciliation](project-a/audits/2026-11-12/Qprime-prenucleus-internal/ProjectA_Qprime_PRENUCLEUS_EXTERNAL_VS_INTERNAL_RECONCILIATION_2026-11-12.md).)

Substituting Theorem 1.1 yields the exact coherent-envelope formula
\[
\boxed{
E_C=
\operatorname{int}_{W₂(X)}
\operatorname{cl}_{X²}\left(
\bigl(D(\widehat X)\wedge X²\bigr)\wedge
\mathfrak c_{X²}(w_C)
\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Thus a Q′ failure is precisely a cover for which the **weak defect obtained by restricting the full fitted diagonal of the compact coherent envelope** has closure with NONZERO INTERIOR inside the closed fitted hull W₂(X). Mere existence of the external spectral boundary R_C (Lemma 2.1) is NOT sufficient: discrete X=N has \(R_C=\beta N\setminus N\ne0\) but NO weak fitting defect; branching Scott X has a weak defect but (6.2) is ZERO on all covers because W=W₂. A Q failure additionally requires W₂(X)=U(X).

This places the independently audited **relatively OPEN strong-versus-weak distinction** inside one universal compact coherent envelope. In particular, the missing implication is not a statement merely about point selection in the external remainder R_C. It asks whether a particular restricted fitted relation has a weak full-cover defect that is **somewhere dense in the rank-one Urysohn hull**.

## 7. What this segment decides (and what it does not)

**Proved exact geometry:** finite fitting on arbitrary X is the trace of full fitting on one canonical compact coherent \(\widehat X\), and every C-bad Boolean prime predecessor is a genuine frame map into the cover's closed spectral remainder \(R_C\subseteq\widehat X\) disjoint from X. In algebraic frames, it even lies in the cover-independent compact-open corona R_cpt.

**Negative resolution of proposed direct upgrade:** Without bringing in the canonical hole's RELATIVE OPENNESS, the Gleason-selected predecessor is guaranteed to fall outside X whenever the cover is bad. The coherent envelope turns the failed infinitary join into an honest missing sublocale, not into a contradiction. The missing mathematical step is whether the marked Urysohn open-hole geometry can force ANY full-compatible predecessor into X, despite this external boundary, or can exclude the existence of the open hole while all selected lower primes live outside. We have no proof of that.
