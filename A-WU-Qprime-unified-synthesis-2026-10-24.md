**General research goal:** characterize frames L for which W=cl(fit(Delta)) equals U, the Urysohn relation.

**Focused questions:**
- (Q) Does W2=U always imply W=U?
- (Q') Does W2=W always hold? (Q') implies (Q).
- Stronger equality (A): D2=D.

## 1. Common notation and the three equality levels

Q=L⊕L, w_C=join_{c∈C} c⊠c for a cover C of 1; the finite-cover approximation D2=intersection_{C finite}O(w_C), and full fitted diagonal D=intersection_{C any}O(w_C). Closed hulls W2=cl D2=C(d_W2), W=cl D=C(d_W). The Urysohn relation U=C(d_U), where d_U=join_{a*∨b*=1}a⊠b.

Always:

    D ⊆ D2 ⊆ U,
    I ⊆ W ⊆ W2 ⊆ U,

with reversal of inequalities for defining opens:

    d_U ≤ d_W2 ≤ d_W ≤ d_I,
    and more sharply d_W≤d_U**.

Logical implications:
- D=D2 ⇒ W=W2.
- W=U ⇒ W=W2=U.
- W=W2 does NOT imply either D=D2 (Scott/Cantor and branching examples) or W=U (compact two-apex example).
- Q' universally ⇒ Q universally; both remain unresolved.

## 2. One pre-nucleus setup

The open filter for ALL diagonal neighborhoods is the filter generated by {w_C:C any cover}; the FINITE filter is generated by {w_C:C finite cover}, equivalently by binary square neighborhoods v(a,b)=(a⊠a)∨(b⊠b) with a∨b=1.

Let

    Phi(q)  = join_{C any}(w_C→q),
    Phi2(q) = join_{C finite}(w_C→q).

These are pre-nuclei (the families of square neighborhoods are filters; for the finite family closure under meets follows by refining the two finite covers with pairwise meets). Their fixed point frames are O(D),O(D2); their transfinite fixed-point closures satisfy

    Phi(0)=Phi2(0)=d_U,
    Phi^∞(0)=d_W,
    Phi2^∞(0)=d_W2.

Hence

    W=U iff Phi(d_U)=d_U,
    W2=U iff Phi2(d_U)=d_U,
    W=W2 iff Phi(d_W2)=d_W2.

The first equality Phi(0)=d_U arises from the rectangle far/square-cover identity; full Phi is already the literature operator kappa_L of Arrieta–Picado–Pultr (2022). Do NOT ascribe novelty to it.

## 3. Isbell density, exact boundary, and one canonical Boolean test

Write B(T) for the least dense Boolean sublocale of a locale T (Isbell). For any S⊆T, cl_T(S)=T iff B(T)⊆S.

The canonical Boolean criteria are

    W=U iff B(U)⊆D,
    W2=U iff B(U)⊆D2,
    W=W2 iff B(W2)⊆D.

Put K=cl(int U)=C(d_U**), G=U∧O(d_U**). We have K≤W≤W2≤U and U=K∨G. Define the *finite-cover residual boundary*

    H2=W2∧G.

Since H2 is relatively open in W2, W2=K∨H2, and closure of D commutes with restriction to H2, we have the exact result

    **W=W2 iff B(H2)⊆D.**

Moreover D2 is dense in W2 and hence in its relatively open H2, so

    **B(H2)⊆D2 ALWAYS.**

Thus the universal Q' asks whether this automatically upgrades to B(H2)⊆D. Under the hypothesis W2=U, one has H2=G, recovering the focused question Q: is B(G) necessarily inside D?

Equivalently, let h,k:L→O(B(H2)) be the two coordinate maps restricted to the Booleanization of H2. Their induced map factors through D2 automatically. The equality W=W2 holds iff this ONE CANONICAL PAIR is compatible with all covers. Any Q' counterexample must fail a cover on precisely this Booleanized residual.

If B(O(H2)) is atomic/spatial, equality follows because all points of D2 are points of D (finite binary versus arbitrary cover compatibility agrees for 2-valued maps). Atomicity of B(W2) or B(G) is stronger than necessary. Nonzero atomlessness of H2 is necessary but NOT sufficient for strict W<W2.

## 4. Three positive proof mechanisms and compact remainders

(A) **Finite-cover patching (pointwise complemented).** Finite-cover-compatible h,k agree on every complemented e, by compatibility with {e,e^c}. For a given cover C, if complemented e_i cover 1 and each e_i≤join of finitely many C-members, then compatibility with {e_i^c} plus those finite members implies compatibility with C. No pairwise disjointness is necessary. If this patching condition holds for every C, D=D2.

Working NEW strengthening: if Y is ZERO-DIMENSIONAL (every open a join of complemented elements) and K is COMPACT, then D(Y×K)=D2(Y×K). A compact-fiber/tube argument gives a cover of Y by opens v_j such that v_j×K has a finite subcover from any given cover of Y×K. Refine the v_j by complemented opens e_i (not necessarily disjoint) and invoke patching. The earlier ultraparacompact base hypothesis was unnecessarily strong. The trivial compact case uses e=1; zero-dimensional L itself gives D=D2=Delta.

(B) **Compact closed neighborhood strips.** Let h_cc=join{k*:C(k) compact closed sublocale}. On O(h_cc⊠1∨1⊠h_cc), D=D2. If the closed remainder C(h_cc) is compact, a finite cover modulo h_cc proves global D=D2. These results do not require complemented local opens.

(C) **Hyperconnected strips (Claude follow-up).** Let h_hc=join of all nonzero hyperconnected opens. On O(h_hc⊠1∨1⊠h_hc), W=W2. If the closed remainder C(h_hc) is compact, W=W2 globally. This need NOT force D=D2; the Scott/Cantor example proves the difference. More generally, set e=h_hc∨h_cc; W,W2 agree on o_e=e⊠1∨1⊠e, so if C(e) is compact, W=W2.

Separate overarching mechanism: if U is REGULAR CLOSED, i.e. d_U=d_U**, then W=W2=U directly by the universal regular-core inclusion. In particular every Alexandrov locale has U=cl Cm(P), with Cm(P) open, so W=W2=U, even without compactness or hyperconnected opens.

**Logical summary:** (A) and (B) prove the stronger fitting equality; (C) and regular-closedness prove closed-hull equality. They should not be combined under a single fictitious separation axiom.

## 7. Exact disjoint-rectangle certificate for failure of Q'

This criterion is stronger than merely requiring a noncompact nonregular boundary and is the recommended target for any constructive attack.

**Proposition (working, direct consequence of the pre-nucleus fixed-point test).** W<W2 iff there exist an open cover C of 1, with NO finite subcover, and nonzero a,b∈L such that

    a∧b=0,
    r=a⊠b not≤d_W2,
    r∧w_C≤d_W2.

Proof. Write d0=d_W2, so Phi2(d0)=d0. If W<W2 then d_W>d0 and Phi(d0)>d0, because Phi(d0)=d0 would make d0 a full Phi fixed point and force d_W=d0. Thus for some full cover C, (w_C→d0)not≤d0. Extract a nonzero rectangle r=a⊠b below (w_C→d0) but not below d0, since rectangles join-generate Q. Then r∧w_C≤d0 and C cannot have a finite subcover: if it had finite F⊆C, w_F≤w_C would give r≤(w_F→d0)≤Phi2(d0)=d0, contradiction.

Also (w_C→d0)≤Phi(d0)≤d_W≤d_I, where the last relation is I≤W. Hence r≤d_I. Applying diagonal inverse image δ*:Q→L yields a∧b=δ*(r)≤δ*(d_I)=0. The converse: if such C,r exist then r≤w_C→d0 but rnot≤d0, so Phi(d0)>d0 and W<W2.

Furthermore D∧O(r)=0, since D≤O(w_C) and D≤W2=C(d0), whereas r∧w_C≤d0; meanwhile W2∧O(r)≠0 and D2∧O(r) is dense there. Since pt(D2)=pt(D) for EVERY locale, the nonzero dense sublocale D2∧O(r) has NO locale points.

In words: a counterexample requires a **genuinely infinite diagonal square cover**, a **disjoint pair of coordinate opens**, and a **dense pointless finite-cover remnant on an open part of W2**, not merely a strict D2>D somewhere invisible under closure.

This exact certificate, the Boolean residual test B(H2)⊆D, and the three local-positive mechanisms should be used together. The first is suited to explicit constructions; the second to abstract proof; the third to eliminate degenerate attempts.
