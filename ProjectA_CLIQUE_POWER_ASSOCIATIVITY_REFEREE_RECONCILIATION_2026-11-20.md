## 1. Verdict and source boundary

The independent referee's overall verdict was **ACCEPT MATHEMATICALLY, with minor expository revisions and an important strengthening**. No invalid step was found in the submitted compact-metric graph-generation lemma, Cantor fusion, bipartite saturation, clique induction, or the five-vertex calculation.

The referee then supplied a **new, simpler proof** that eliminates the Cantor graph machinery from the principal theorem and replaces compact **metric** by compact **Hausdorff**. This is **referee-origin mathematics**, not a claim that the original authors submitted those weaker hypotheses. The later referee addendum extends the same method beyond cliques to arbitrary **finite joins of point-generated and full bipartite rectangles**, proves associativity of that finite-join algebra, and derives directed-walk composition formulas. The addendum explicitly supersedes the original report's cautious statement that unrelated bipartite relations were outside scope.

The former metric graph-generation theorem remains a valid and separately interesting **explicit Cantor-graph refinement** according to the referee, but is no longer a dependency of the main algebra theorem.

## 3. Corrected dependency map

The recommended primary exposition is:
\[
\text{compact closed-support richness}\Longrightarrow
(G')\Longrightarrow
(B*)\Longrightarrow
(RC)\Longrightarrow
\text{associative finite-join rectangular calculus}\Longrightarrow
\text{directed-walk formula}.
\]
The originally submitted graph-generation (G) is a separate, stronger **geometric description** using Booleanized compact Cantor graphs in metric spaces. It must not be presented as a prerequisite of (G') or (B*).

**Closed-support richness:** For W≤[A]×[D] nonzero, its least ambient closed support K contains ordinary first-A and second-D traces separately dense. A purported open hole missing one trace can be compactly shrunk; its projected compact closed coordinate set is disjoint from that endpoint selected point set, contradicting the density of W. This argument selects **no point of W**.

**(G'):** The full localic product is the join of Booleanizations B(K) of nonempty compact closed supports with separately dense selected-coordinate traces. A fitted ambient open separator for a proposed missing piece produces exactly such a K and contradicts the separation.

**(B*):** For dense middle ordinary B,C, pull the two point-generated bipartite relations to K×Y. The two inverse images contain ordinarily dense sets, hence are dense **sublocales** and share the least dense sublocale. An **open-surjective** projection to K transfers density to B(K), producing the whole endpoint localic product.

**(RC):** For any p(A,B) or q(A,B) followed by any p(C,D) or q(C,D), their composite is 0 when [B]∧[C]=0 and q(A,D) otherwise. The nonzero case is reduced to (B*) on the least closed support of [B]∧[C]. The uniformity with respect to p/q choice is the central reason associativity holds for arbitrarily mixed finite rectangular expressions.

## 4. Algebraic consequence and the crucial finite-join boundary

Write p(A,B)=[A×B], q(A,B)=[A]×[B] for ordinary subsets of compact Hausdorff G. The addendum's class
\[
\mathscr R_\vee(G)=\{\text{finite joins of }0,p(A,B),q(A,B)\}
\]
is closed under localic relational composition, converse, finite joins, and is **associative**. This class is defined using all ordinary subsets A,B, not just the finitely many labels of one clique example. Each product of two generators becomes the **full** endpoint rectangle if and only if the middle localic supports overlap.

For finite generators t_i=p(A_i,B_i) or q(A_i,B_i), put i→j when [B_i]∧[A_j]≠0. For k≥2 every bracketing of k copies of H=∨t_i has the common exact-length directed-walk value
\[
W_k(H)=\bigvee_{i_1\to\cdots\to i_k}q(A_{i_1},B_{i_k}).
\]
Directed walks are **exactly k−1 arrows**; no loop-padding is allowed unless loops actually exist. The usual undirected distance-at-most formula for cliques is the special case with a loop at every vertex.

The positive algebra **does not** extend automatically to arbitrary spatial graph relations [T] by expressing T as an infinite union of singleton rectangles. Localic relation composition is not assumed to distribute over arbitrary joins. This is the most important scope boundary and the leading new research problem.

## 5. Corrections to the submitted document

The referee identifies three necessary editorial changes, all incorporated into the new canonical note:

1. Replace the compact-metric hypothesis in the **main clique theorem** by compact Hausdorff, using (G')/(B*) as the main proof. Do not weaken the metric hypothesis on the explicit Cantor-graph lemma (G).
2. Replace the too-weak disclaimer about generated-subalgebra associativity: the finite-join/composition algebra of cliques **is** associative, and the addendum proves the stronger bipartite finite-rectangle algebra.
3. In asymmetric binary-tree steps, use (B*) on explicit point-generated subrelations and the outer endpoint-support bound; do not cite the candidate's optional monotonicity variant outside its exact middle-support assumptions.

The Cantor fusion remains available for conceptual interpretation of the **geometry of nonspatial products**, rather than as a compulsory step in the main proof.

## 6. Next frontier and preservation protocol

**Priority:** Understand how far the associative calculus extends beyond **finite rectangular joins**. Concrete entry points: ordinary nonrectangular graphs T with finite rectangular coverings or with suitably stable image/closed-support behavior; examine minimal infinite unions where compositional interchange fails. A separate direction is replacing compact Hausdorff with locally compact Hausdorff or proper-coordinate hypotheses.
