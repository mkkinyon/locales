# Project A — The A/A-op join need not be M: four-point diamond

**Internally checked counterexample · 15 November 2026.** This result was found **after** the LC-A blind referee packet and is not externally audited. It closes the newly posed universal question (A-JOIN) **negatively**. No novelty claim.

For any locale X put \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}(\Delta)\), \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}(\Delta)\), \(\mathcal M=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D\). The relation \(\mathcal A\) is universally defined by \(u_1\le u_2\); its transpose \(\mathcal A^{op}\) by \(u_2\le u_1\). The universal inequalities
\[
\Delta\le\mathcal A,\mathcal A^{op}\le\mathcal M
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A^{op}=\Delta
\]
are established independently in the [interval note](ProjectA_LCA_FOLLOWUP_AND_A_DUAL_QUESTIONS_2026-11-15.md).

## Counterexample

Let P be the four-element *diamond* poset
\[
0<a<1,\qquad 0<b<1,\qquad a\not\le b,\quad b\not\le a,
\]
with the Alexandrov topology of **upper sets**. The product locale \(P\times P\) is the locale of the finite product poset.

**Claim 1.** \(\mathcal D(P)=P^2\).

The diagonal contains the least point \((0,0)\). Every open subset of \(P^2\) containing \((0,0)\) is the entire square, because opens are upper sets. Thus the only open sublocale containing the diagonal is \(P^2\) itself; its intersection is \(\operatorname{fit}(\Delta)=P^2\).

**Claim 2.** \(\mathcal I(P)=P^2\).

The diagonal also contains the greatest point \((1,1)\). Its closure is the whole square (every point specializes to \((1,1)\)). Equivalently, any nonempty open of the square contains \((1,1)\) and therefore meets \(\Delta\), so \(\Delta\) is dense.

Consequently \(\mathcal M(P)=\mathcal I(P)\wedge\mathcal D(P)=P^2\).

**Claim 3.** \((a,b)\notin\mathcal A(P)\vee\mathcal A(P)^{op}\).

The ordinary points of \(\mathcal A(P)\) are precisely comparable ordered pairs \((x,y)\) with \(x\le y\), and the points of \(\mathcal A(P)^{op}\) are those with \(y\le x\). The points \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) belong to neither. Both relations are spatial (any sublocale of a finite locale has a finite quotient frame, hence is spatial), and their join is the sublocale generated by the union of their points. Thus the join does not contain either incomparable pair.

It follows that
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(P)\vee\mathcal A(P)^{op}\subsetneq\mathcal M(P)=P^2.}
\]

This is an extremely small, fully spatial counterexample; no nonspatial product or image subtleties are involved.

## Scope

The same argument works for **any finite bounded T0 Alexandrov poset with two incomparable elements**, since its diagonal contains a least and greatest point of the square, forcing I=D=whole, whereas the union of the two order graphs misses incomparable pairs.

**Status:** (A-JOIN) closed **false** for arbitrary locales, internally checked. The distinct user question (A-DUAL)—whether there is a *conceptually closure-side* natural relation between \(\Delta\) and \(\mathcal I\), as opposed to merely transposing \(\mathcal A\)—remains open/deferred.
