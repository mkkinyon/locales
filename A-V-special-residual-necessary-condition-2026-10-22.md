# Special residual pseudocomplement: reduction and individual-good counterexample

Date 2026-10-22. No universal depth-three theorem or counterexample obtained.

Let w be a canonical bar open, h(b)=b*∨r_w(b), Φ(a)=∨{b:a≤h(b)}, G={a:Φ(a)=1}, p=∨G. The special residual inequality (RI) is
  b→p ≤ p∨h(b) for all b.

Taking b=p gives the necessary condition (N): p∨h(p)=1. Because h is antitone, (N) is EQUIVALENT to all instances of (RI) with b≤p. If b∨p=1, then b→p=p, so (RI) holds. If b is complemented, b→p=b*∨p and (RI) holds. Thus only genuinely mixed noncomplemented b remain beyond (N).

WARNING: even a good open g∈G need not satisfy g∨h(g)=1. In original symmetric rational-bridge bar w=q∧τ(q), the open g=L_0 is good (earlier H-V-H certificate has (L_0,1) as its first-root child with V-H subtree). But g∨h(g)<1: every bridge z_n is outside L_0, outside L_0* (since z_n in closure L_0), and outside r_w(L_0) (since the reverse graph sees right rational points in every bridge neighborhood). Therefore g∨g*∨r_w(g) misses every z_n. This does NOT disprove (N) for the special residual p, because for this w, p=1, hence (N) trivial. It does show maximality p=join ALL good opens cannot be weakened to g being an arbitrary good open.

Since p is not necessarily itself good (G is lower but not generally closed under arbitrary joins), an argument using Φ(p)=1 is invalid. The status of (N) and (RI) for the special residual p remains open; a counterexample would require a canonical bar failing universal H-V-H rank three and thereby yield (via symmetric meet) rank≥4.
