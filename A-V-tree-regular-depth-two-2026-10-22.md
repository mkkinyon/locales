# Depth-two theorem for regular frames

Let L be regular, D={(a,b):a meet b=0}, and B any subset of L x L. Then B is a rectangular bar if and only if join_{(a,b) in B}(a meet b)=1, if and only if B has a depth-two horizontal-then-vertical separation tree.

Necessity: diagonal inverse image sends disjointness open d_I to 0, and rectangle (a,b) to a meet b. Apply it to d_I join b_B=1.

Sufficiency: regularity gives u=join_{c well-inside u}c. Since the diagonal overlaps a meet b cover 1, all c well-inside some a meet b cover 1. Horizontally split root (1,1) to (c,1). Choose v with c meet v=0 and (a meet b) join v=1. Vertically split each (c,1) to (c,a meet b), below B, and (c,v), a disjointness leaf. This proves depth 2. No spatiality or compactness assumptions.

Consequence: any counterexample to depth-two must be nonregular and have no join-dense completely join-prime basis. This search has not yet produced a strict depth-two example.
