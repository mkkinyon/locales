## 1. Stabilizing the Scott witness by a finite nontrivial relation

Let S=P(N)\{empty} with its Scott topology, and take the finite upper-Alexandrov six-point poset P with comparabilities

    0<5>1<4>2<3.

Set X=S×P. Both factors are locally compact, sober, and have compact-open bases. Their product is locally compact, sober, algebraic, and noncompact (S is a continuous surjective image of X). The localic square identifies with the ordinary product.

The space S is hyperconnected, with greatest specialization point N belonging to every nonempty open. Thus I(S)=U(S)=S². The usual pointwise product formula for I and U gives

    pt I(X)=S²×pt I(P),   pt U(X)=S²×pt U(P)

(reordering the four coordinates). Because the localic square is spatial and I,U are closed, these point relations determine the localic relations. In P, (0,4)∈U\I: up(0)∩up(4)=empty, while their closures intersect at 1. Conversely (0,3)∉U: cl(up0)={0,1,5} and cl(up3)={2,3} are disjoint. Hence

    I(X) < U(X) < X².

The singleton closures in a product multiply, so Cm(X)=Cm(S)×Cm(P). The relation Cm(S) is DENSE in S² because (N,N) belongs to every nonempty basic product open. Since P is finite coherent, cl Cm(P)=pt U(P), by the audited coherent equality W(P)=U(P). Consequently

    cl_(X²)(Cm(X)) = pt U(X).

The closed sublocale W(X) contains each ordinary Cm point and therefore the closed sublocale generated by their closure, namely U(X). The universal W≤U gives W(X)=U(X).

Now take the prior finite-cover-compatible but not all-cover-compatible frame maps h,k:O(S)→RO(2^N). Choose p∈P and the continuous slice i_p:S→S×P, x↦(x,p). Then h∘i_p* and k∘i_p* are maps O(X)→RO(2^N) preserving finite-cover compatibility, but they fail for the pulled-back cover {c_n×P:n∈N}. Thus D2(X)>D(X).

**Result:**

    I(X) < W(X)=U(X) < X²,     D(X)<D2(X),

for an algebraic locally compact sober NONCOMPACT X. The new product construction is a stress test: D2>D can coexist with nontrivial I,U, yet closure equality survives.

**General transfer:** Whenever a locale Y has a point, a finite-versus-infinite cover-compatible pair on S transfers to S×Y by composing with a point-slice inverse image. This transfer alone says nothing about W<W2.

## 2. Geometric binary criterion for spatial squares

Suppose X is sober and its localic square is O(X×X). Write R_U⊆X² for the ordinary closed relation of U. For a binary cover a∨b=1, let A=X\a and B=X\b. These are disjoint closed sets and

    (X²)\[(a×a)∪(b×b)] = (A×B) ∪ (B×A).

Because U is a closed spatial sublocale, restriction of v(a,b)=a×a∪b×b to U is dense iff the relative closed complement R_U∩[(A×B)∪(B×A)] is nowhere dense in R_U. U is symmetric, and a finite union of two relatively closed nowhere-dense sets is nowhere dense. Therefore:

**THEOREM (working).** Under the spatial-square hypothesis,

    W2=U
      ⇔ for every pair of disjoint closed A,B⊆X,
         int_{R_U}(R_U∩(A×B))=empty.

This is an exact topological test for binary density; no assumption that D or D2 is spatial is needed, only that U is a closed sublocale of the spatial square. The test does NOT characterize W=U without the previously recorded extra hypotheses.

If W2=U but W<U, then some nonzero relatively open portion of R_U is missed by D, although no nonempty relatively open portion of R_U can be contained in A×B for disjoint closed A,B. This excludes straightforward clopen-component and Cartesian product thickening of the compact alpha-beta-gamma counterexample, whose old closed boundary cuts remain binary witnesses.

## 3. Outstanding question

The implication W2=U ⇒ W=U is unresolved. A witness must have an infinite diagonal-cover descent failure localized to U\W, beyond a generic nonspatial excess in D2. A new construction should deliberately avoid both the closure-density mechanism of S×P and the binary closed-cut witness of the compact alpha-beta-gamma space. Further model building is needed; do not report a conjectured counterexample as established.

## 4. Disjoint-open rectangle certificate for W<U

**Lemma (working).** Suppose the localic square is the ordinary spatial product, and W<U. Then there are **disjoint opens** a,b of X for which

    R_U∩(a×b) ≠ empty,
    D∧O(a⊠b)=0, and hence Cm(X)∩(a×b)=empty.

Proof. W=C(d_W), I=C(d_I), U=C(d_U), with I≤W<U, so d_U≤d_W≤d_I and d_W not≤d_U. Since rectangles join-generate Q, choose a rectangle r≤d_W not≤d_U. Expand r=r∧d_I as a join of rectangles r∧(u⊠v) with u∧v=0, because d_I is the join of all such disjoint rectangles. Some r'=a⊠b with a∧b=0 remains not≤d_U; it lies below d_W. Thus D≤W=C(d_W) gives D∧O(r')=0, while U∧O(r')≠0. The latter has an ordinary pair because U is a closed sublocale of a spatial frame. Every Cm pair belongs to D, so none belongs to r'.

Combining with §2: any hypothetical W2=U but W<U example with spatial square must have a nonempty U-relation region in the product of DISJOINT opens a,b, with no Cm points there, while for EVERY pair of disjoint CLOSED sets A,B the U-related points lying in A×B form a nowhere-dense subset of U. This juxtaposition is a construction constraint, not a contradiction (open coordinate separation is weaker than being captured by disjoint closed neighborhoods).

## 5. Scattered-U control

If U is a spatial scattered locale, every nonzero sublocale of U has an ordinary point: take an isolated point of its nonempty closed hull and intersect the sublocale with the corresponding isolated open. Since D2≤U and pt(D2)=pt(D), a nonzero pointless residual D2∧C(w) witnessing D2≠D cannot occur. Thus D2=D, so the binary test is exact. In an ordinary spatial square, U scattered implies X scattered (the diagonal embeds X in U), and then the ordinary square is also scattered; thus this is chiefly a useful sublocale-level restatement rather than a fundamentally new topological class.
