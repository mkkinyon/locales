## 1. A canonical compact coherent envelope for ANY frame

Let L be a frame. Regard its underlying structure A=(L,0,1,finite ∨,finite ∧) merely as a bounded distributive lattice. Put

    E(L)=Idl(A),

the frame of (nonempty, zero-containing) lattice ideals of A. This frame is coherent, its compact elements are the principal ideals ↓a, and 1_E=↓1 is compact. The join map

    q:E(L)→L,   q(J)=∨_L{a:a∈J}

is a surjective FRAME HOMOMORPHISM with q(↓a)=a. For finite meets, distributivity gives q(J∩K)=∨_{a∈J,b∈K}(a∧b)=q(J)∧q(K); the other properties are immediate. Its right adjoint is r(a)=↓a. Thus r∘q is an explicit nucleus j(J)=↓(∨_L J), identifying the locale X of L with a sublocale of the compact coherent locale Y of E(L).

Moreover q is **dense**: q(J)=0 entails J=↓0. The product inclusion i×i:X×X→Y×Y is also dense: every nonzero open of Y² contains a nonzero rectangle J⊠K, and its image q(J)⊠q(K) is nonzero because frame coproducts have nonzero pure rectangles. [The product density is useful context, not needed in the main theorem.]

If L is **normal as a frame**, then A is normal as a bounded distributive lattice, and Y=Idl(A) is compact normal. Proof: if J∨K=1_E, compactness of ↓1 yields a,b∈A with a∈J,b∈K and a∨b=1. Apply normality in A to find c,d with a∨c=1=b∨d, c∧d=0; principal ↓c,↓d witness normality in E(L). Conversely, normality of E(L) on principal covers implies normality of A, by compact extraction. Only the forward implication is used.

## 2. I and U pull back EXACTLY from the coherent envelope

Let Q_Y=E(L)⊕E(L), Q_X=L⊕L, and ̄q=q⊕q:Q_Y→Q_X. This is surjective since the rectangle images (↓a)⊠(↓b) map to a⊠b and these generate Q_X.

Define
    d_I(M)=∨{u⊠v:u∧v=0 in M},
    d_U(M)=∨{u⊠v:u*∨v*=1 in M}.

**THEOREM (all frames, no normality needed):**

    ̄q(d_I(E(L)))=d_I(L),
    ̄q(d_U(E(L)))=d_U(L).

Proof for d_I: any disjoint J,K in E(L) map to disjoint q(J),q(K) because q preserves meets, so the image d_I(E)≤d_I(L). Conversely disjoint a,b∈L lift as disjoint principal ideals ↓a,↓b, giving reverse containment on every generator.

Proof for d_U: if J*,K* cover 1_E, then q(J*)∨q(K*)=1_L, and q(J*)≤q(J)*, q(K*)≤q(K)*; hence q(J),q(K) are far in L. Thus image d_U(E)≤d_U(L). Conversely if a*∨b*=1 in L, then the principal ideals ↓a,↓b are far in E(L), because (↓a)* contains ↓(a*) and (↓b)* contains ↓(b*), whose ideal join is ↓(a*∨b*)=↓1. Thus a⊠b is the image of a far generator in E(L).

Translating complementary opens to closed sublocales, for i:X→Y the dense sublocale embedding,

    I(X)=(i×i)^(-1) I(Y),
    U(X)=(i×i)^(-1) U(Y).

The diagonal also pulls back exactly, because i is a monomorphism and (i×i)^(-1)(Δ_Y)=Δ_X. No analogous unconditional exact pullback for fitting is claimed.

## 3. The normality question becomes an exact base-change problem

Assume L is normal. Then Y is compact normal and prior independently audited project results give

    D(Y)=V(Y)=U(Y)=F(Y).

The pullback identities above and the established arbitrary-frame U=F for normal frames give

    U(X)=(i×i)^(-1) U(Y)=(i×i)^(-1)V(Y)=(i×i)^(-1)D(Y)=F(X).

Since inverse images of sublocales preserve arbitrary meets and take open sublocales to open sublocales, the inverse image of a fitted sublocale is fitted. Hence

    D(X) ⊆ V(X) ⊆ U(X)

and the two still-open conditions are EXACTLY

    V(X)=U(X)
      iff fit_X((i×i)^(-1)I(Y))
          =(i×i)^(-1)(fit_Y(I(Y)));

    D(X)=U(X)
      iff fit_X((i×i)^(-1)Δ_Y)
          =(i×i)^(-1)(fit_Y(Δ_Y)).

Thus both normality questions are instances of **fitting descent along the canonical dense inclusion X→Idl(L_fin)**, tested on I_Y and Δ_Y respectively. This is a concrete common framework, not an assertion that fitting commutes with all dense pullbacks.

The defect is due to imposing arbitrary-join relations: a family C with ∨_L C=1 need not have a finite subcover in A, so the ideal generated by C can be a proper (indeed dense) open of compact Y even though its image under q is 1 in L. This is precisely where an infinitary diagonal bar can appear in X without coming from a bar in Y.

## 4. NO-GO: individual bar lifting is false in the nicest normal example

Let L=P(N), frame of the discrete infinite space N. As a bounded distributive lattice A it is the complete Boolean algebra P(N), and E(L)=Idl(P(N)) is the frame of opens of the Stone space βN (compact Hausdorff). The canonical dense inclusion i:N→βN is the usual dense discrete open embedding.

The diagonal open
    w_N=⋁_{n∈N} {n}⊠{n}
in O(N×N) is a bar since I(N)=Δ_N, and indeed V(N)=U(N)=Δ_N. But NO open v in O(βN×βN) that restricts to w_N can contain Δ_(βN): any product neighborhood of (p,p) for a free ultrafilter p includes distinct n,m∈N and so would restrict to an off-diagonal pair. Thus w_N cannot be lifted to a bar for βN while preserving its restriction.

This does not threaten fitting descent in this example (it holds). It disproves the naive proof strategy 'lift each bar of X to a bar of compact Y'.

## 5. Optional extension: rooted-factor transport is not limited to finite P

The rooted-factor theorem in A-V-normal-pointfree-rooted-factor-transport-2026-10-23.md extends verbatim to **ANY nonempty poset P with a least element 0**, not only finite P. For arbitrary locale Y with frame B,

    O(P×_locY)≅Mon(P,B),   O(P²×_locY²)≅Mon(P²,B⊕B).

An explicit inverse to the coordinate evaluation is W↦∨_{p∈P}(↑p⊠W_p); this preserves arbitrary joins and binary meets because ↑p∩↑q=∨_{t≥p,q}↑t and W_p∧V_q≤W_t∧V_t for common upper t.

The root evaluations of d_I and d_U, and of any bar w, are unchanged from the finite case. Every real-valued frame map from O(R) to Mon(P,B) is constant in the P-coordinate by regularity of O(R) and the pointwise order f_0≤f_p. Hence

    D(P×Y)=(π×π)^(-1)D(Y),
    U(P×Y)=(π×π)^(-1)U(Y),
    V(P×Y)=(π×π)^(-1)V(Y),
    F(P×Y)=(π×π)^(-1)F(Y).

Also normal B ⇒ normal Mon(P,B). As π has a section corresponding to the root 0, the inverse-image operation reflects equality. Thus rooted Alexandrov thickening cannot CREATE or DESTROY the property V=U, or the stronger property D=U, irrespective of cardinality of P.

This extension is supplementary and not the solution to the infinitary base-change problem.

## 6. Consequences and next test

- There is no candidate counterexample inside compact normal locales, normal continuous frames, normal subfit frames, or their arbitrary rooted Alexandrov thickenings.
- For any normal L the whole problem can be studied in a compact normal, coherent, *spatial* envelope Y. The obstruction, if real, is failure of fitting to descend to its canonical dense sublocale X despite EXACT pullback of I and U.
- A negative example normal L⇒V≠U must involve genuinely infinitary joins not recoverable from the finitary distributive lattice A, and its witness cannot be inferred just from a non-bar-lift in Y (the discrete N example rules out that shortcut).
- Look for a criterion for fitting of C(d) to commute with the nucleus J↦↓∨J on ideal completions for the SPECIFIC closed sublocale I(Y); characterize residual using maximal proper elements in the closed quotient U_X. No general result or counterexample is yet in hand.
