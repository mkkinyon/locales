## 3. Detailed audit of the submitted proof

### 3.1. Rectangles, fittedness, and closed annihilators

For sublocales `A,B,C,D` of a common locale `G`, the actual fibre product of the two rectangular relations is

\[
(A\times B)\times_G(C\times D)
\cong A\times(B\wedge C)\times D.
\]

If `B∧C=0`, the image vanishes. Otherwise the projection to `A×D` is a pullback of the map `B∧C→1`, which is an **open surjection in classical locale theory**. Its image therefore is the entire `A×D`. This is a valid localic argument and remains valid when `B∧C` has no points.

For the contested open-separator step of (G), compact Hausdorff `X×Z` is regular, hence *fit*: every sublocale is the intersection of its ambient open supersublocales. If `J` fails to contain `U×V`, one of the ambient opens `O` containing `J` fails to contain `U×V`. Its complementary closed sublocale `F` has

\[
F\wedge(U\times V)\ne0.
\]

Indeed the open and closed complements satisfy `O∨F=1` and `O∧F=0`; the sublocale coframe is a distributive lattice for **finite** joins and meets. Were the displayed meet zero, the rectangle would be contained in `O`. Thus the submitted nonzero closed annihilator is justified. This is not an argument from missing ordinary points.

The least closed support of a nonzero sublocale of compact Hausdorff `X×Z` is an ordinary nonempty compact closed subset. Since the annihilator lies in `F`, its closed support is contained in `F`.

### 3.2. Selected-coordinate richness in a closed support

Here is a reusable formulation of the essential fact. Let `X,Z` be compact Hausdorff, `U=[A]_X`, `V=[D]_Z`, and `0≠W≤U×V`. Let `K` be the least ambient closed support of `W`. Then the two subsets

\[
P_K=\{(x,z)\in K:x\in A\},\qquad
Q_K=\{(x,z)\in K:z\in D\}
\]

are **separately dense** in the ordinary space `K`.

To verify the first assertion, suppose a nonempty relatively open `N⊆K` misses `P_K`. By regularity choose a nonempty relatively open `N'` with compact closure `L=cl_K(N')⊆N`. The projection `T=π_X(L)` is closed in `X` and disjoint from `A`. Since `U` is *point-generated*, `U≤X\setminus T`. Hence `W≤U×V` cannot meet the relative open `N'` of `K` (all of whose first coordinates lie in `T`). This contradicts density of `W` in its least closed support. The `Z`-argument is identical.

**No point of `W` is inferred or selected**. The selected points are ordinary points of the compact closed envelope `K`; the information that `W≤[A]×[D]` is used solely through closed exclusions of `A` and `D`. This proves the delicate step in §4 and also the single-coordinate form used in Lemma 6.1.

### 3.3. Relative nowhere-density of sections in (G)

In Lemma 4.1 the closed separator `F` is disjoint from the point-generated relation `[A×D]`; hence `F` contains no ordinary pair `(a,d)` with `a∈A,d∈D`.

If a vertical section `K∩({x}×Z)` contained a nonempty relatively open `N`, shrink to `N'` with compact closure `L⊆N`.

* If `x∉A`, the singleton `{x}` is closed and disjoint from `A`, so `U≤X\setminus\{x\}` and the allegedly dense `W` cannot meet `N'`.
* If `x∈A`, then `π_Z(L)` is compact closed and disjoint from `D`, since `L⊆F` and `F` has no selected pair. Thus `V≤Z\setminus π_Z(L)`, giving the same contradiction.

The horizontal case is symmetric. Because the sections are closed in `K`, empty relative interior is equivalent to nowhere-density. The proof is valid **even in thin or scattered ambient spaces**. Specifically, the hypotheses force `K` to have no isolated points; therefore a nonempty compact scattered `K` cannot occur at this stage. This is why the fusion does not silently assume the existence of Cantor subsets in arbitrary scattered compacta.

### 3.4. All stages of the two-colour fusion

The proposed three-child tree construction is sound. At a given finite depth, parent rectangles already have pairwise disjoint first-coordinate projections and pairwise disjoint second-coordinate projections. Every inherited anchor is in its parent interior. Registering *all* inherited anchor coordinates at the new level before selecting new points is crucial and is explicitly specified.

Each finite union of vertical and horizontal sections is closed nowhere dense in `K`; hence each parent relative open set still contains points of each dense colour `P` and `Q` avoiding all forbidden coordinates. After **all** anchors have been selected, the finite collection can be enclosed in sufficiently small closed product rectangles whose first-coordinate projections are globally disjoint and whose second-coordinate projections are globally disjoint. This also preserves the parent-interior condition.

The nested-compact/diameter argument gives one limit point per infinite branch. Separation at the first branch split makes the limit points different in **both** coordinates, establishing both injective projections. Persistent `0`-descendants retain each newly selected `P` and `Q` anchor, so every cylinder contains anchors of both colours. Hence the traces are simultaneously dense in the resulting ternary Cantor space, homeomorphic to the usual Cantor space.

I found no countability, shrinking, inheritance, or simultaneous-density gap in this construction. It genuinely uses metrizability through its diameter control, but that construction is not needed for (PA).

### 3.5. Graph generation (G)

For a permitted graph `Γ`, the inverse images of `U=[A]` and `V=[D]` on the *spatial compact* locale `Γ` contain the two selected ordinary dense traces. Each is therefore a dense sublocale of `Γ`, so their intersection contains `B(Γ)`. Thus `B(Γ)≤U×V` in the ambient product. The point-pair term `[A×D]` likewise lies there.

For the opposite inclusion, the fittedness separator yields a nonzero `W=F∧(U×V)` and its compact support `K⊆F`. Section 3.2 proves separate richness, and §3.3 proves nowhere-density of sections. The fusion lemma produces `Γ⊆K⊆F` with the required traces. Consequently

\[
0\ne\mathcal B(\Gamma)\le J\wedge F=0,
\]

which is impossible. This certifies (G), including its genuinely nonspatial conclusion. No internal product of the induced endpoint sublocales is tacitly assumed spatial.

### 3.6. The submitted proof of (B)

The pullbacks to `Γ×Y` are genuine localic inverse images. The first contains the ordinary set

\[
\{\gamma\in\Gamma: \pi_X(\gamma)\in A\}\times B,
\]

and the second contains

\[
\{\gamma\in\Gamma: \pi_Z(\gamma)\in D\}\times C.
\]

Both sets are dense in the **ordinary compact Hausdorff product** `Γ×Y`. Hence the two pullback sublocales are dense, and their meet is dense, containing `B(Γ×Y)`. The meet maps into the *actual* relation pullback `R×_Y S`. The projection `Γ×Y→Γ` is an open surjection (because `Y` is nonempty and overt), so the image of the dense meet is dense in `Γ`, and therefore contains `B(Γ)`. The image of the map to `X×Z` lies in `R∘S`.

Thus the published (B)-argument is valid, not merely an assertion about ordinary composable triples. A shorter proof follows.

## 4. Replacement proof: saturation without graphs or metrizability

I recommend using the following independent argument as the principal proof of the theorem.

### Lemma A (compact Hausdorff closed-support generation)

Let `X,Z` be **compact Hausdorff**, `A⊆X`, `D⊆Z`, and `U=[A]_X`, `V=[D]_Z`. Let `\mathscr K(A,D)` consist of all nonempty closed subsets `K⊆X×Z` for which each of

\[
P_K=K\cap(A\times Z),\qquad Q_K=K\cap(X\times D)
\]

is dense in `K`. Then

\[
\boxed{U\times V=\bigvee_{K\in\mathscr K(A,D)}\mathcal B(K).}\tag{G'}
\]

Here `B(K)` is the double-negation sublocale of the ordinary compact Hausdorff space `K`, regarded as a sublocale of `X×Z`. **Singletons `(a,d)` are among the allowed `K`**, so no separate point-generated term is needed.

**Proof.** For each permitted `K`, the two endpoint inverse-image sublocales of `U,V` on `K` contain the prescribed dense point traces; hence both contain `B(K)`, and `B(K)≤U×V`. Call the join on the right `J`.

If `U×V\not\le J`, fit of compact Hausdorff `X×Z` gives an ambient open `O` with `J≤O` but `U×V\not\le O`. Write `F` for its complementary closed sublocale and set `0≠W=F∧(U×V)`. Its least closed support `K` is nonempty, lies in `F`, and by §3.2 has both selected-coordinate traces dense. Thus `K∈\mathscr K(A,D)` and `0≠B(K)≤J∧F=0`, a contradiction. ∎

This generator theorem is **weaker in geometric specificity** than (G), since its generators need not be Cantor graphs, but **stronger in ambient generality**. It completely replaces (G) in the proof of (PA).

### Lemma B* (compact Hausdorff bipartite saturation)

Let `X,Y,Z` be compact Hausdorff spaces, let `A⊆X`, `D⊆Z` be nonempty, and let `B,C⊆Y` be dense. Put

\[
R=[A\times B]_{X\times Y},\qquad
S=[C\times D]_{Y\times Z}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{R\circ S=[A]_X\times[D]_Z.}\tag{B*}
\]

**Proof.** Write `U=[A]`, `V=[D]`, and `T=R∘S`. Endpoint support gives `T≤U×V`.

Let any `K∈\mathscr K(A,D)` be given. Inside the **ordinary compact Hausdorff product** `K×Y`, consider the map

\[
\Phi:K\times Y\longrightarrow X\times Y\times Z,
\qquad ((x,z),y)\longmapsto(x,y,z).
\]

Let `M_R,M_S` be the inverse images under `Φ` of the first and second relation conditions, and put `M=M_R∧M_S`. The sublocale `M_R` contains all ordinary points of `P_K×B`, and `M_S` contains all ordinary points of `Q_K×C`. These sets are dense in the compact Hausdorff space `K×Y`; hence **both `M_R` and `M_S` are dense sublocales**, and so is their meet `M`.

The meet `M` maps into the genuine composability pullback `R×_Y S`. Projection `p:K×Y→K` is an **open surjection**. Thus `p[M]` is dense in `K`, so

\[
\mathcal B(K)\le p[M]\le T
\]

where the last containment views the image as a sublocale of `X×Z` through `K`. Since this holds for every `K∈\mathscr K(A,D)`, (G') implies `U×V≤T`. Combined with the opposite inclusion, this proves (B*). ∎

**Even shorter version:** Rather than first form (G'), suppose directly that `T<U×V`, choose an open separator, let `K` be the closed support of `F∧(U×V)`, and use exactly the preceding `K×Y` argument to force `0≠B(K)≤T∧F=0`. Either presentation is complete.

**Metrizability is absent from every step:** compact Hausdorff regularity supplies closed shrinking, projections of compact closed sets are closed, and `K×Y` is an ordinary spatial compact Hausdorff product. Nothing requires a countable base, a Cantor subset, or injective coordinate projections.

## 5. Clique composition and every bracketing

### 5.1. (C2), including pointless overlaps

Assume `W=U_i∧U_j≠0`, and let `K=cl(W)` in compact Hausdorff `G`. The selected subsets `E_i∩K` and `E_j∩K` are dense in `K` by §3.2 (or Lemma 2.2). In `G×K` and `K×G`, respectively, form

\[
R=[E_i\times(E_i\cap K)],\qquad
S=[(E_j\cap K)\times E_j].
\]

Their images under the middle closed inclusion `K↪G` lie in `P_i` and `P_j`. By (B*) their composite over `K` is the full `U_i×U_j`; its image is contained in the composite over `G`. The reverse inclusion follows from endpoint support. Hence `P_i∘P_j=U_i×U_j`. If `U_i∧U_j=0`, their entire composability pullback is zero.

The construction uses **ordinary points of `K` selected from two dense sets**, not ordinary points of `W`—the latter need not exist.

### 5.2. Nonzero indexed words, all four tree splits

Proceed by induction on the number of leaves.

* **2+2 (or larger on both sides):** Both children are full outer rectangles by induction; (R) composes them to the full rectangle because their middle endpoint supports overlap.
* **1+at least 2:** The leaf `P_i` contains `[E_i×(E_i∩K)]`, where `K` is the closed support of `U_i∧U_j` and `j` is the first index of the right subtree. The right rectangle `U_j×U_ℓ` contains `[(E_j∩K)×E_ℓ]`. Apply (B*) over `K`; then use the evident outer endpoint upper bound. This supplies the **entire** `U_i×U_ℓ`.
* **At least 2+1:** The preceding argument reversed, using the closed support of the overlap at the last internal boundary.
* **1+1:** Exactly (C2).

In the asymmetric cases it is slightly cleaner to cite (B*) and monotonicity **directly**, rather than invoke the optional “monotonicity variant” of §5 literally: the large subtree rectangle need not satisfy that variant's particular *middle* upper bound by `[B]` or `[C]`, though it always satisfies the *outer* support upper bound needed for the result. This is an editorial clarification, not a defect in the actual §7 argument.

### 5.3. Zero indexed words

For any subtree, regardless of whether it vanishes, its result is supported in the product of the first and last endpoint sublocales of its index segment. If the full word contains a consecutive zero-overlap pair `j_t,j_{t+1}`, the two leaves belong to opposite children at their lowest common ancestor, with exactly those two supports as the meeting boundary. Their pullback is therefore zero by (R) and monotonicity; all larger ancestors remain zero. This works for **every** binary bracketing, including both asymmetric shapes.

### 5.4. Finite-join expansion and graph distances

Since the family is finite, composition may be distributed over the `m` summands of `H` at every node without invoking any unproved infinitary interchange. An indexed word contributes the full `U_{j_1}×U_{j_k}` precisely when every consecutive pair overlaps. Such a word is a length-`k−1` graph walk allowing stationary steps. A walk of that length from `i` to `j` exists exactly when `dist(i,j)≤k−1`: pad a shortest path with repeated indices. Therefore (PA) follows, **for compact Hausdorff `G`**, with exactly the stated formula.

## 6. A stronger algebraic consequence: generated-subalgebra associativity

The final paragraph of §8 says the theorem does *not* establish associativity on a subalgebra generated by the heterogeneous `P_i`. Under the natural interpretation—**the smallest collection containing `P_1,…,P_m` and closed under finite joins and composition**—this disclaimer should be corrected.

**Corollary.** For the compact Hausdorff family above, composition is associative on the subalgebra `\mathcal A` generated by `P_1,…,P_m` under finite joins and composition (with the empty join/zero optionally included).

**Proof.** Every expression in `\mathcal A` is a finite join of evaluations of nonempty indexed words: this follows inductively from finite distributivity of composition. For any `R,S,T∈\mathcal A`, expand `(R∘S)∘T` and `R∘(S∘T)` into finite joins indexed by a word from each of `R,S,T`. Each corresponding summand evaluates the **same concatenated indexed word** with two possibly different bracketings. Its length is at least three. Lemma 7.1 says the value of such a word is determined only by adjacent overlaps and its two endpoint indices, hence is independent of the bracketing. The corresponding summands, and therefore the joins, coincide. ∎

This **does not** establish associativity for arbitrary localic relations, or arbitrary *unrelated* point-generated bipartite relations. It does establish an associative finite-join/composition calculus for **these** component cliques. If “subalgebra” was meant in another sense, the boundary statement needs to specify that sense.

## 7. Five-vertex sector and script

For the specified Cantor blocks, `Q_1,I_1` are both dense in `C_1`, and `Q_2,I_2` are both dense in `C_2`. Direct application of (B*) gives

\[
R\circ S=I_0\times Q_2,\qquad S\circ T=I_1\times Q_3.
\]

For the left bracketing, the first full rectangle contains the point-generated `[I_0×Q_2]`, which composed with `T=[I_2×Q_3]` gives the **full** `I_0×Q_3` by (B*). Endpoint support gives the reverse bound. The right bracketing is symmetric, using `R` and `[I_1×Q_3]` inside the second full rectangle. Hence

\[
\boxed{(R\circ S)\circ T=I_0\times Q_3=R\circ(S\circ T).}
\]

No use of the *particular* two-colour Cantor fusion is necessary in this example; its role is to explain how ordinary point-disjoint neighbouring supports can overlap pointlessly. The equality proved is the equality of whole localic products, as requested.

## 8. Requested changes and further questions

**Required editorial corrections (no theorem repair needed):**

1. Change the compact-*metric* restriction to **compact Hausdorff** in the main clique power theorem, if the authors adopt (G')/(B*) as the proof. Retain metric restrictions on the separate special Cantor-graph theorem (G).
2. Correct §8's statement about subalgebra associativity, or distinguish the stronger associative subalgebra from genuinely arbitrary heterogeneous relations.
3. In the asymmetric induction, invoke the saturation lemma directly for the displayed point-generated subrelations and use outer endpoint support for the reverse containment. The stated monotonicity variant, with its own additional middle-support upper bounds, is not the most transparent citation.
4. Consider leading with (G') and (B*), relegating the elegant but technically longer two-colour Cantor-graph construction to an independent refinement. Its proof is sound, but the main theorem does not depend on it.

**Exact hypotheses established here:** The replacement proof requires classical locale theory, **compact Hausdorff spatial ambient spaces**, ordinary subsets used to generate the endpoint sublocales, nonempty dense middle sets in the saturation lemma, and a **finite** family in the power-associativity theorem. No second countability or metric assumption is needed. The submitted graph-specific generation lemma (G), as formulated with Cantor graphs, still uses compact metrizability.

**Potential future questions** (not claims):

* Can the compact-Hausdorff saturation argument be extended under weaker “closed-coordinate image” or properness conditions, perhaps for locally compact Hausdorff ambient spaces?
* What is the most economical abstract condition ensuring the closed-support generation formula (G') for spatial factor locales?
* Can the generated associative finite-join/composition algebra be described explicitly from the finite overlap graph, retaining the distinction between first-degree point cliques and all later full rectangles? One should account for possible equalities of distinct support products when building such a presentation.
* Does the special Cantor-graph generating family in (G) admit a metrizable weakening (e.g. a perfect-set criterion instead of all metric compacta) or a genuinely nonmetrizable analogue using other compact graph witnesses?
