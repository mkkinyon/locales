## Project A — a bipartite extension and an associative rectangular calculus

**Date:** 20 November 2026

### 1. A genuine further strengthening

On rereading the proof, I noticed that the clique hypothesis is not needed for the **all-bracketing** result. This corrects an unnecessarily cautious sentence in §6 of my original report, which said that its proof did not establish associativity for *arbitrary unrelated point-generated bipartite relations*. In fact, it does establish that result, within the precise rectangular class below.

Let `G` be any compact Hausdorff space, in classical locale theory. For ordinary subsets `A,B ⊆ G`, distinguish

\[
 p(A,B)=[A\times B]_{G\times G}, \qquad
 q(A,B)=[A]_G\times_{\mathrm{Loc}}[B]_G.
\]

The first is the **point-generated bipartite relation**; the second is the **full localic rectangle**. Always `p(A,B) ≤ q(A,B)`. These two may be unequal. Write `V_A=[A]_G`, and allow either sort of rectangle to be zero if a factor is empty.

**Proposition (uniform rectangular composition).** Suppose `A,B,C,D` are nonempty ordinary subsets of `G`, and choose independently

\[
 r\in\{p(A,B),q(A,B)\},\qquad
 s\in\{p(C,D),q(C,D)\}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{r\circ s=
 \begin{cases}
 q(A,D),&V_B\wedge V_C\ne0,\\
 0,&V_B\wedge V_C=0.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** The zero case follows from the vanishing of the actual middle composability pullback; the upper bound in the nonzero case follows from endpoint supports. For `p(A,B)∘p(C,D)`, let `K` be the least closed support of `V_B∧V_C≠0`. By Lemma 2.2 of the submitted text, `B∩K` and `C∩K` are both dense in `K`. Restrict the middle legs to these sets; the resulting point-generated bipartite subrelations satisfy the compact-Hausdorff bipartite saturation lemma (B*) proved in §4 of my report. Consequently

\[
 p(A,B)\circ p(C,D)=q(A,D).
\]

Each `p` lies below the corresponding `q`. Thus the same lower bound holds if either or both `p` factors are replaced with their `q` versions. The endpoint upper bound forces equality. (Alternatively `q∘q` follows directly from rectangular composition (R).) ∎

The important feature of (1) is that **the answer depends only on the four endpoint supports and their middle overlap, not on whether either input was point-generated or full**.

### 2. Associativity on a large, not necessarily finitely generated class

Define

\[
 \mathscr R(G)=\{0\}\cup\{p(A,B),q(A,B):A,B\subseteq G\}.
\]

**Corollary.** `\mathscr R(G)` is closed under relation composition, and composition on it is associative. Its closure `\mathscr R_{\vee}(G)` under **finite joins** is an associative, finite-join-distributive algebra of localic relations, with zero and an involution given by converse. No finite list of initial subsets or relations is required to define this class.

**Proof.** Formula (1) gives closure. For any three nonzero factors with successive source/target support labels `(A,B)`, `(C,D)`, `(E,F)`, both bracketings are zero when either `V_B∧V_C=0` or `V_D∧V_E=0`; otherwise both equal `q(A,F)`. This proves associativity on `\mathscr R(G)`. Composition distributes over finite joins, so the same holds for finite joins of its elements. Finally, converse interchanges `p(A,B)` with `p(B,A)` and `q(A,B)` with `q(B,A)` and preserves finite joins. ∎

This is **not** a claim that composition is associative on all sublocales of `G×G`, or on arbitrary point-generated relations `[T]` with nonrectangular `T⊆G×G`: writing such a relation as an *infinite* join of singleton rectangles would require an unjustified infinitary-distributivity step. Nor is `\mathscr R_{\vee}(G)` asserted to have a multiplicative identity.

Thus §6 of my report can be strengthened considerably: the associative algebra generated by the original point-cliques sits inside a naturally defined, much larger associative finite-join calculus.

### 3. Directed overlap graphs and arbitrary bipartite generators

Take a finite family of nonzero relations

\[
 t_i\in\{p(A_i,B_i),q(A_i,B_i)\},\qquad i=1,\ldots,m,
\]

and put `H=∨_i t_i`. Define a **directed** graph on the index set by

\[
 i\longrightarrow j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [B_i]_G\wedge[A_j]_G\ne0.
\]

Loops need not occur. Applying (1) successively gives, for **every** full bracketing and every `k≥2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 W_k(H)=
 \bigvee_{i_1\to i_2\to\cdots\to i_k}
 q(A_{i_1},B_{i_k}).}
 \tag{2}
\]

The indexing is by directed walks of **exactly `k−1` steps**. The formula is independent of bracketing even when the generators are heterogeneous bipartite relations, even when some are already full rectangles, and even when their middle overlaps have no ordinary points.

When `A_i=B_i=E_i` and `t_i=p(E_i,E_i)`, the graph is the symmetric overlap graph with a loop at each vertex. One may then pad shortest paths with stationary steps, recovering the distance-at-most-`k−1` formula from the original submission. Without loops, that reduction is not valid.

A simple warning about the directed setting is that powers need not stabilize to a fixed value: for the discrete two-point space `G={a,b}`, set `H=p({a},{b})∨p({b},{a})`. Then `H^2` is the diagonal and `H^3=H`; the powers alternate. More generally, the Boolean adjacency matrices of a finite directed graph are eventually periodic, so (2) also shows eventual periodicity of the powers of **every finite heterogeneous rectangular join** (possibly with a smaller period after identifying equal resulting sublocales).

### 4. A suggested next boundary question

The precise positive class above suggests a sharper question than the one in my first report: **how far beyond finite joins of rectangular point-generated relations does associativity survive?** In particular, identify hypotheses on an arbitrary ordinary relation `T⊆G×G` under which its point-generated sublocale `[T]` can enter this associative calculus. The distinction between *finite* rectangular decompositions and arbitrary decompositions is essential; the latter cannot be handled by the existing finite-join argument.

I regard §§1–3 as a concrete additional mathematical result, not merely a speculative suggestion. It requires no new compactness assumption beyond the compact-Hausdorff saturation lemma already proved in my report, and gives a clean reformulation of the original clique theorem as a symmetric, reflexive-index specialization of a directed rectangular calculus.
