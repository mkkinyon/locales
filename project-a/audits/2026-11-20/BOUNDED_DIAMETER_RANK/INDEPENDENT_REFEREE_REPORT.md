## 2. Verdict A: finite rectangles, finite joins, and Isbell density — **verified**

Let `R=A×B`, `S=C×E`, with the four supports sublocales of a fixed locale `G`. The actual pullback of the two relation spans is

\[
R\times_G S\cong A\times(B\wedge C)\times E.
\]

When `B∧C=0` this is zero. Otherwise, in classical mathematics the nonzero locale `W=B∧C` is overt, so `W→1` is an **open surjection**, and its pullback `A×W×E→A×E` is again a surjection. Consequently the image is the *entire* localic rectangle `A×E`, whether or not `W` has points. Formula (5) is therefore correct. Note that this is a **special stable surjection** (an open one), not an appeal to stability of arbitrary surjections.

For clarity, finite-join distributivity can be proved inside `G³`. The composability locale is the meet of the pullbacks of the two factors along the projections `G³→G²`. Sublocale inverse image preserves finite joins, the sublocale lattice is distributive, and image preserves joins. Thus

\[
(R_1\vee R_2)\circ S=(R_1\circ S)\vee(R_2\circ S),\qquad
R\circ(S_1\vee S_2)=(R\circ S_1)\vee(R\circ S_2).
\]

Only **finite** distributivity is required. For a sequence of rectangular factors, each consecutive target–source meet either vanishes or does not; the nonzero result is the rectangle from the initial source to the final target. This rule is independent of parenthesization, and zero factors behave as expected. Since the actual diagonal is a two-sided unit, the finite-join algebra generated by the displayed rectangles and `Δ_G` is associative. Both `D_n` and `D̃_n` belong to it.

For every `i`, the subspace of eventually-zero sequences and its complement are both dense in `C_i`. By Isbell density both induced sublocales contain the least dense (double-negation) sublocale `B(C_i)`, which is nonzero. Hence `Q_i∧I_i≠0`. Their ordinary point sets exhaust Cantor space; since Cantor space is spatial, `Q_i∨I_i=C_i`. There is no identification of their localic intersection with an ordinary point.

**Scope:** This verification uses classical overtness, genuine localic products, and finite joins. It does **not** prove associativity of general image-defined relations, or arbitrary-join distributivity.

## 3. Verdict B: exact diameter-two rank `2n+4` — **verified**

### Upper bound

The two-factor full rectangle `a_i×a_{i+1}` is obtained from `a_i×Q_i` followed by `I_i×a_{i+1}`; the intermediate meet is `Q_i∧I_i≠0`. The complete list of costs used by the candidate is valid:

- From any of `a_i,Q_i,I_i,b_i` to `a_{i+1}` (for `i<n`): at most `2,3,1,1`, respectively.
- From `a_j` to any of `a_j,Q_j,I_j,b_j`: at most `0,1,3,2`, respectively.
- Within one block: at most four, and between distinct blocks `i<j`: at most `3+2(j-i-1)+3=2(j-i)+4`.

All these are costs for **whole rectangles**, not point paths. Reversal supplies the opposite direction. Reflexivity pads shorter words, and the finite joins `Q_i∨I_i=C_i` ensure that the resulting collection of rectangles covers `G_n²`. The upper bound holds independently of bracketing by §2.

### Lower bound

Choose `x∈Q_0` and `y∈I_n` as ordinary points. After expanding a length-`k` word by finite distributivity, a summand containing `(x,y)` must be a nonzero rectangular chain (the pure diagonal cannot contain this distinct pair). Delete all diagonal factors. Its first non-diagonal factor must have source `Q_0`, so is `Q_0×a_0`; its last must have target `I_n`, so is `b_n×I_n`. This remains true when `n=0`.

Use the heights

\[
h(a_i)=2i,\qquad h(Q_i)=h(I_i)=2i+1,\qquad h(b_i)=2i+2.
\]

Every primitive non-diagonal factor has height change of absolute value at most 1, including the new `I_i↔a_{i+1}` edges; the old `b_i↔a_{i+1}` bridge has change 0. Consecutive supports with nonzero meet have equal heights: the only distinct overlapping support labels are `Q_i,I_i`. After the mandatory first factor, the middle part starts at height 0 (`a_0`) and must reach height `2n+2` (`b_n`) before the mandatory last factor. It therefore requires at least `2n+2` factors, making `k≥2n+4`.

A point sublocale pulls a **finite** join back to a finite join in `Sub(1)={0,1}`; hence if no rectangular summand contains `(x,y)`, the whole word does not contain that point. This removes a possible logical gap between a support-chain calculation and exclusion from the localic relation.

Thus `D̃_n` has exact completion length `2n+4` for **all** parenthesizations. The `n=0` case gives 4, as it should.

### Ordinary diameter and quotient

The ordinary graph components are unchanged by the added `I_i↔a_{i+1}` edges. An internal component has the center `a_i` adjacent to both its `Q_i` and preceding `I_{i-1}` points, and to `b_{i-1}`; all pairs are at distance at most two. Boundary components are stars. The ordinary diameter is at most two.

The terminal-coequalizer argument is also correct. Restrictions of a coequalizing map to `Q_i` and `I_i` agree respectively with constants at the images of `a_i,b_i`. Since their meet is nonzero, its map to `1` is surjective, and two global points of any target locale that become equal on the meet must already agree. Thus the two constants coincide. Their join covers `C_i`, so the map is constant on the entire block. The bridge edges identify constants from successive blocks. It follows that the coequalizer is `1` and its kernel pair is `G_n²`.

## 4. Verdict C: point-generated clique completion — **verified, with stronger bounds**

Let the ordinary graph components of `D_n` be `E_0,E_1,…,E_{n+1}`, numbered from the `Q_0,a_0` boundary star through the internal components to the `I_n,b_n` boundary star. Write `U_j` for the **sublocale induced** by the point set `E_j`, and `P_j` for the point-generated complete relation on `E_j`:

\[
P_j=[E_j\times E_j]_{G_n²},\qquad H_n=\bigvee_{j=0}^{n+1}P_j.
\]

The ordinary points of `H_n` are exactly the stipulated same-component pairs: `E_j×E_j` is a subspace of the Hausdorff ambient square, hence Hausdorff and sober; its induced sublocale has precisely those points. In particular, the ordinary components really are cliques. Also `P_j≤U_j×U_j`.

The original clopen-block argument is sound. With `Z_i` as in the submission and

\[
B_r=\bigvee_{|i-j|\le r} Z_i\times Z_j,
\]

one has `H_n≤B_1` and, by the finite-rectangular calculus, `B_r∘B_s≤B_{r+s}` (indeed equality after capping the index range). Thus every bracketing of `k` factors lies in `B_k`. For `k<n`, it misses `Z_0×Z_n`. This proves the stated, albeit weaker, lower bound.

The coequalizer is terminal because `D_n≤H_n` and the coequalizer of `D_n` is terminal. Its kernel pair is `G_n²`. No associativity of `H_n` is needed.

## 5. New sharper bounds for `H_n` — **proved**

I recommend replacing (9) in the main candidate by

\[
\boxed{n+2\le r(H_n)\le 2n+2}\qquad (n\ge0), \tag{R1}
\]

with an explicit explanation that both inequalities apply uniformly to all bracketings. Here is a proof using only facts already accepted in the packet.

### Lower bound `n+2`

The component sublocales obey

\[
U_i\wedge U_j=0\quad\text{if }|i-j|\ge2,
\qquad
U_i\wedge U_{i+1}\supseteq Q_i\wedge I_i\ne0.
\]

The first assertion follows from their supports on disjoint clopen `Z`-blocks; the second is Isbell density in `C_i`.

Expand any `k`-factor bracketing of `H_n=∨P_j` into its **finite** join of bracketed words with individual factors `P_{j_1},…,P_{j_k}`. Each subtree of such a word has source supported in `U_{j_a}` and target supported in `U_{j_b}`, where `a,b` are its first and last leaf positions. If a pair of *adjacent* indices `j_t,j_{t+1}` has `U_{j_t}∧U_{j_{t+1}}=0`, consider the lowest common ancestor of the adjacent leaves `t,t+1`: the two children meet across those precise terminal supports, so their composability pullback is zero, and the entire word is zero. Thus a nonzero summand necessarily satisfies

\[
|j_{t+1}-j_t|\le1\quad\text{for every }t.
\]

Use the particular ordinary endpoint pair `(a_0,b_n)`. Since `a_0` belongs only to `U_0` as a point and `b_n` only to `U_{n+1}`, a summand containing that point must begin with index `0` and end with `n+1`. The previous inequality demands at least `n+1` changes among the `k-1` adjacent gaps; hence `k≥n+2`. As in §3, finite joins cannot introduce this particular point unless a summand already contains it. This proves the lower bound **for every parenthesization**, including when `n=0`.

### Upper bound `2n+2`

Each `U_j` is a spatial, point-generated sublocale. Since `H_n` contains all ordinary edges within `E_j`, for any ordinary `x∈E_j` it contains the **whole rectangles**

\[
\{x\}\times U_j\le H_n,\qquad U_j\times\{x\}\le H_n.
\]

Indeed these rectangles are isomorphic to the spatial locale `U_j` and are generated by their ordinary point-pair sublocales, all of which lie in `H_n`.

Fix ordinary points `x∈E_i` and `y∈E_j` with `i<j`. Choose an ordinary point `p_t∈E_t` for each intermediate component. Form the following `2(j-i)` rectangular factors, each contained in `H_n`:

\[
\underbrace{(\{x\}\times U_i),\ (U_{i+1}\times\{p_{i+1}\})}_{\text{first crossing}},
\underbrace{(\{p_{i+1}\}\times U_{i+1}),\ (U_{i+2}\times\{p_{i+2}\})}_{\text{second crossing}},
\ \ldots,\
(\{p_{j-1}\}\times U_{j-1}),\ (U_j\times\{y\}).
\]

At each crossing the intermediate meet is `U_t∧U_{t+1}≠0`; between crossings the intermediate singleton supports match exactly. By Lemma 3.1 the composite, **in every parenthesization**, is `{x}×{y}`. The case `i>j` is symmetric; if `i=j`, the point edge already lies in `H_n`. Pad any shorter expression to `2n+2` factors using diagonal units.

Consequently *every* `2n+2`-factor bracketing of `H_n` contains **every ordinary point-pair sublocale of `G_n²`**. Since the ambient square `G_n²` is spatial, each such word is the entire `G_n²`. This gives the upper bound. Notice that only spatiality of the ambient square and the one-point-legged rectangles is used, **not** spatiality of `U_i×U_j`.

The bound is exact for `n=0`: `r(H_0)=2`. I do **not** claim that either new bound is generally sharp for `n>0`.

**Conceptual interpretation.** The *overlap graph* of the localic component supports `U_j` is a path of `n+2` vertices, despite the fact that the ordinary components are mutually disjoint as sets of points. One factor per support is necessary to traverse that path; two factors per support transition suffice to generate all ordinary endpoint pairs. This may be a more informative formulation than the original clopen-block band estimate.

## 6. Verdict D: repaired nonassociative gadget — **verified**

### Graph image and point-generation

The graph of `f:N→Q⊂Y` is a discrete (hence spatial) subspace of `X×Y`, since each integer is isolated in `X`. Its image under projection to `Y` is exactly the induced sublocale `Q`: the factorization `N→Q→Y` has surjective first arrow in `Loc` because the inverse image of opens of `Q` along the bijection `N→Q` is injective. It need **not** be an open map; and its product with an arbitrary map need not be a surjection. All listed relation summands are spatial and point-generated, so their finite join `D_*` is point-generated.

### The nonzero associator direction

Let `A=D_*∘D_*`. Compose the graph transpose `Γ_f^smile` with `X×X`, but restrict its composability object to those tuples whose final coordinate is `*`. The restricted object is `N`; its map to the output slice `Y×{*}` has image `Q×{*}`. Therefore

\[
Q\times\{*\}\le A.
\]

This does **not** assert `Q×X≤A` and does not require stability of an arbitrary surjection under product. With `I∧Q≠0`, the rectangle formula gives

\[
(\{b\}\times I)\circ(Q\times\{*\})=\{b\}\times\{*\},
\]

proving `(D_*∘A)` contains the entire point sublocale `(b,*)`.

### The zero associator direction

The `b→X` block of `A` is zero. In a two-edge route from `b`, the only candidate through `Y` to `X` is `b→I` followed by `Γ_f^smile`. Its genuine fibre product is `I×_Y N`, which is zero: `N` is the coproduct of its singleton integers, and the pullback over each `f(n)∈Q` is zero. The new singleton bridge `i_0↔q_0` only permits the new **two-step** endpoint `b→q_0`, not `b→X`. The other intermediate blocks `b,r` have no edges into `X`.

To terminate at `*` in the final `D_*` factor, the intermediate point/sublocale must be in `X`; neither `Γ_f^smile` nor any other summand of `D_*` ends at `*` from outside `X`. Since `A` has no `b→X` block, `(A∘D_*)∧({b}×{*})=0`.

Thus the asserted nonassociativity in (11) is real and remains so **after** the rational–irrational singleton repair. It relies on a nonzero pointless intersection on one bracketing, not on an ordinary three-edge path.

### Connectivity and sixfold completion

Every `q_n` connects to its integer `n`, all integers and `*` lie in the `X` clique, `q_0` connects to `i_0`, every irrational connects to `b`, and `r` connects to `b`. In particular `q_n—n—n_0—q_0—i_0—b—r` has length 6, and no category of pairs is farther apart. Ordinary diameter is at most 6.

The ordinary-path lemma is sound. For a prescribed binary bracketing and a point path of length `d`, induct on the binary tree and split the padded point path at the number of leaves in the left subtree. The two recursively obtained point edges have the *same actual middle point*, producing a point in the genuine fibre product. Hence the output point edge is included. Because `G_*²` is a spatial Stone square, containing every point-pair sublocale forces equality with `G_*²`. All sixfold bracketings of `D_*` are therefore complete. This is an upper bound, not a claim of exact minimal rank.

## 7. Verdict D continued: attachment `F_n` — **verified, diameter sharpened**

The bridge `a_0↔r` is the only edge between gadget and chain. It introduces no two-step `b→X` route: the new two-step route `b→r→a_0` ends in the chain, and the last factor into `*` still originates only in gadget `X`. The nonzero direction of the old associator remains by monotonicity. Consequently the two sides of (13) remain unequal exactly as claimed.

The lax direct-image inequality (14) is correctly oriented. Given `ρ:T_n→G_n`, the composability object of `R,S` maps canonically into that of their image relations under `ρ×ρ`; taking images yields

\[
(\rho\times\rho)_*(R\circ S)
\ \le\
(\rho\times\rho)_*R\circ(\rho\times\rho)_*S.
\]

Induction on any binary tree gives the corresponding inequality for words. Here `ρ` is identity on the chain and sends the gadget constantly to `a_0`, so `(ρ×ρ)_*F_n=D̃_n`. If `W_k(F_n)=T_n²`, its image is `G_n²` (the retraction has a section), so `W_k(D̃_n)=G_n²`; §3 then forces `k≥2n+4`.

For the upper bound, every `m=max(2n+4,6)` factor word contains both full diagonal blocks `G_n²` and `G_*²`, and contains the bridge by reflexivity. In the chosen triple bracketing `(W_m∘W_m)∘W_m`, the rectangle products

\[
G_n²\circ(\{a_0\}\times\{r\})\circ G_*²
=G_n\times G_*
\]

and the reverse orientation fill the cross blocks; the diagonal blocks remain. This justifies `3m` as an upper bound without global associativity. Hence (15) is correct.

**Sharper diameter.** The ordinary component of the diameter-two chain containing `a_0` is actually the boundary star `Q_0∪{a_0}`, in which every point is at distance at most **1 from `a_0`**. In the gadget every point is at distance at most **6 from `r`**. Crossing the sole bridge costs 1. The merged component therefore has diameter at most `1+1+6=8`; all other components have diameter at most 2. In fact a `Q_0` point and a gadget rational `q_n≠q_0` realize distance 8, so the attached component has diameter **exactly 8**. Replace “≤9” by “8” in the claims.

## 8. Verdict E: older diameter-three formula — **verified**

For `D_n`, assign heights `h(a_i)=3i`, `h(Q_i)=h(I_i)=3i+1`, and `h(b_i)=3i+2`. Every primitive factor changes height by at most 1, and composable support labels have matching height. The point-pair `(x,y)` with `x∈Q_0`, `y∈I_n` forces the initial `Q_0×a_0` factor and the final `b_n×I_n` factor; the middle must traverse height difference `3n+2`, giving `k≥3n+4`.

Conversely, `a_i×b_i` and its reverse occur in two factors through `Q_i∧I_i`, and `b_i↔a_{i+1}` is primitive. The resulting hub path from `a_0` to `b_n` has weighted length `3n+2`. Every support label attaches to a hub with cost at most 1, so every support-to-support rectangle is present by `3n+4` factors; their finite joins cover `G_n²`. Thus the exact rank really is `3n+4`, for all bracketings. This verification is independent of the clique-completion improvement.

## 9. Recommendations for the revised manuscript

1. **Replace the clique bound** (9) by (R1) and include the component-overlap proof. This strengthens the key diameter-one example considerably. It keeps the intentional nonassociativity caveat for `H_n`.
2. **Sharpen the attachment diameter** from 9 to 8; the stated rank bounds remain unchanged.
3. **Define two ranks where necessary:** `r_exists(R)` (least length of at least one complete bracketing) and `r_all(R)` (least length at which every bracketing is complete). The precise chain ranks make them equal; the reported bounds for `H_n` hold for both, without asserting equality.
4. **Explain point-generated clique completion:** In the present Hausdorff ambient space, the induced sublocale of the subset `S_n` has exactly the intended ordinary point pairs because the subspace is Hausdorff and sober. This closes a possible worry about inadvertently introduced ordinary edges.
5. **Give a one-paragraph categorical justification** for finite join distributivity in `G³` and for why an overt nonzero middle locale makes the rectangular projection an *open surjection*. These are the central technical moves.
6. **Keep the sliced graph-image argument** in the gadget exactly as written: `Q×{*}≤A` is established, whereas the superficially stronger `Q×X≤A` is not required and should not be asserted without an additional argument.
7. **State explicitly that the supplied Python test is only a finite symbolic consistency check.** Running it gave `(n,rank)` equal to `(0,4),(1,7),…,(5,19)` for the diameter-three chain and `(0,4),(1,6),…,(5,14)` for the diameter-two chain, consistent with the proofs. The numerical run is not used to establish any theorem.

### Standard background independently checked

- Isbell density and the rational–irrational phenomenon are discussed, for example, in [Isbell’s density theorem — *Non-Hausdorff Topology and Domain Theory*](https://projects.lsv.ens-paris-saclay.fr/topology/?page_id=1251), as well as Picado–Pultr’s *Frames and Locales*, Chapter III.
- Finite-join preservation by pullback of sublocales is consistent with the standard coframe-homomorphism formulation; see, for example, [*On infinite variants of De Morgan law in locale theory*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022404920301602).
- The caution about nonspatial localic products is real, not merely hypothetical; for example, the localic product of two rational-subspace locales need not coincide with the spatial product. None of the verified proofs requires such a coincidence.

**Final conclusion:** The submission successfully establishes the proposed bounded-ordinary-diameter/unbounded-localic-rank phenomenon, including the independent nonassociative variant. The most useful revision is the stronger, entirely elementary-in-the-given-calculus clique-completion bound (R1).
