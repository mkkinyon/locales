# Project A — Unrestricted Scott-continuity converse: blind mathematical packet

**Date:** 8 November 2026  
**Status:** *Candidate proof submitted for independent adversarial examination; no correctness verdict supplied.*

## The question and why it matters

For arbitrary locales `X` and `Y` and a **closed** sublocale `E ↪ X×Y`, let `p₁:E→X`, `p₂:E→Y`, and define

`r_E := (p₂)_* p₁^* : O(X) → O(Y)`.

The proposed theorem is

> **L\***: `r_E` preserves all **nonempty directed joins** if and only if `p₂:E→Y` is **proper** (right adjoint Scott-continuous **and** closed Frobenius).

A version with `O(X)` continuous is already motivated by passing to locally compact sober spaces, but the candidate proof below asserts **no continuity, spatiality, compactness, or choice assumptions**. Establishing or refuting L* matters because it would give an intrinsic criterion for proper projections of localic separation relations (including the inseparability relation `I=cl(Δ)`), independently of whether such relations are transitive.

The proposed strategy is to classify `r_E` through the upper powerlocale `P_U X`, construct a universal sublocale `K ↪ X × P_U X`, prove the projection `π_K:K→P_U X` proper, and factor `p₂` as a closed inclusion followed by a pullback of `π_K`.

**The claims below are offered for testing, not as established results.** Some statements labeled B1–B5 are cited as published background; please independently examine whether their exact formulations and hypotheses are sufficient. In particular, the long proof of Lemma 2 and the constructive status of pullback stability deserve scrutiny.

## 1. Setting and conventions

* `O(X)` denotes the frame of opens of a locale `X`; for a locale map `f`, `f^* ⊣ f_*`. "Directed" means inhabited and upward directed. All joins/meets in a frame; `→` is Heyting implication.
* **Proper** (Vermeulen convention): `f_*` preserves directed joins and `f_*(v ∨ f^*w) = f_*(v) ∨ w`.
* `X × Y` is the locale with `O(X×Y) = O(X) ⊕ O(Y)` (frame coproduct), `s ⊕ t := π_1^*(s) ∧ π_2^*(t)`.
* **Upper powerlocale** `W := P_U X`: `O(W)` is the frame presented by generators `□u` (`u ∈ O(X)`) and relations `□1 = 1`, `□(u ∧ u') = □u ∧ □u'`, `□(⋁↑u_k) = ⋁ □u_k`. By definition of a presented frame, frame homomorphisms `O(W) → M` correspond exactly to **preframe homomorphisms** `O(X) → M` (maps preserving finite meets and directed joins). [Johnstone 1985 *Vietoris locales and localic semilattices*; Vickers 1989 *Topology via Logic*; Johnstone–Vickers 1991; Elephant C4.1.]
* `η : X → W` with `η^*(□u) = u` (the identity `O(X) → O(X)` is a preframe hom).
* Every element of `O(W)` is a join of generators (finite meets of generators are generators).

## 2. Published background used (B1–B5)

**B1 (presented frames; coverage theorem).** Johnstone, *Stone Spaces* II.2.11: if `A` is a meet‑semilattice with a meet‑stable coverage `◁`, the frame `Fr⟨A | a = ⋁S for a ◁ S⟩` is the frame of *C‑ideals* (downsets `D ⊆ A` with `S ⊆ D ⇒ a ∈ D` whenever `a ◁ S`), ordered by inclusion, the generator `a` going to the C‑ideal generated by `↓a`; and `a ≤ ⋁_{s∈S} s` in the presented frame iff `a` lies in the C‑ideal generated by `S`. The proof is constructive.

**B2 (coproducts of presented frames).** `Fr⟨G_1|R_1⟩ ⊕ Fr⟨G_2|R_2⟩ ≅ Fr⟨G_1 × G_2 qua ∧‑semilattice | R_1 in the first coordinate, R_2 in the second⟩` (Johnstone, *Stone Spaces* II.2.13 for two frames; the same argument for any presentations by meet‑semilattices). We use it for `O(X) ⊕ O(W)`:

> `O(X × W) = Fr⟨ (s,u) ∈ O(X) × O(X) qua ∧‑semilattice | (⋁_j s_j, u) = ⋁_j (s_j,u) ; (s, ⋁↑u_k) = ⋁_k (s,u_k) ⟩`,  with `(s,u)` standing for `s ⊕ □u`.

The coverage `(⋁s_j,u) ◁ {(s_j,u)}`, `(s,⋁↑u_k) ◁ {(s,u_k)}` is meet‑stable (meeting with `(s',u')` gives again an instance of the same two rules, using frame distributivity in the first coordinate and preframe distributivity `u' ∧ ⋁↑u_k = ⋁↑(u'∧u_k)` in the second). Hence by B1:

> **(C) `O(X × W)` ≅ the set of C‑ideals of `O(X) × O(X)`**: downsets `e` such that (i) `(s_j,u) ∈ e ∀j ⇒ (⋁_j s_j, u) ∈ e` (including the empty join), (ii) `(s,u_k) ∈ e ∀k`, `(u_k)` directed `⇒ (s, ⋁u_k) ∈ e`. Principal downsets `↓(s,u)` are C‑ideals, so `s ⊕ □u ≤ e  ⟺  (s,u) ∈ e`. Meets are intersections; `1 ⊕ □d` is `↓(1,d)`; `u ⊕ □u` is `↓(u,u)`.

A useful repackaging (all elementary): by (i), `e_v := {s : (s,v) ∈ e}` is a principal downset `↓σ_e(v)`; `σ_e : O(X) → O(X)` is antitone, and (ii) says exactly `σ_e(⋁↑v_k) = ⋀_k σ_e(v_k)`. Conversely every antitone `σ` turning directed joins into meets defines a C‑ideal `{(s,v) : s ≤ σ(v)}`. So **`O(X × P_U X)` is the poset of Scott‑continuous maps `O(X) → O(X)^{op}`**, and `(s,v) ∈ e ⟺ s ≤ σ_e(v)`; in particular `(u,u) ∈ e ⟺ u ≤ σ_e(u)`.

**B3 (sublocale calculus).** Standard (Picado–Pultr, *Frames and Locales*, Ch. III; Elephant C1.2): sublocales ↔ nuclei (bigger nucleus = smaller sublocale); `o(s)`, `c(s)` are complementary; the coframe of sublocales is distributive; the image `h[T]` of a map `h : T → Z` is the sublocale with nucleus `h_*h^*`; preimages `h^{-1}(S)` are pullbacks and preserve intersections; the pasting lemma for pullbacks. The following elementary facts are invoked:

* **(B3a)** For `s,t ∈ O(Z)` write `[s ≤ t] := c(s) ∨ o(t)`. Then a sublocale `S ⊆ Z` satisfies `i_S^*(s) ≤ i_S^*(t)` iff `S ⊆ [s ≤ t]`. *Proof.* `i_S^*s ≤ i_S^*t ⟺ S ∩ o(s) ⊆ o(t)`. If `S ⊆ c(s)∨o(t)` then `S ∩ o(s) ⊆ (c(s)∩o(s)) ∨ (o(t)∩o(s)) ⊆ o(t)` by finite distributivity; conversely `S = (S∩c(s)) ∨ (S∩o(s)) ⊆ c(s) ∨ o(t)`. ∎
* **(B3b)** A map `h : T → Z` factors through `[s ≤ t]` iff `h^*(s) ≤ h^*(t)`. *Proof.* `h` factors through a sublocale iff its image does; the image has frame `Fix(h_*h^*)` with quotient map `h_*h^*`, and `h_*h^*s ≤ h_*h^*t ⟺ h^*s ≤ h^*t` (apply `h^*`, resp. `h_*`). Now use B3a. ∎
* **(B3c)** `g^{-1}([s ≤ t]) = [g^*s ≤ g^*t]`: a map `h` factors through the left side iff `gh` factors through `[s≤t]` iff `h^*g^*s ≤ h^*g^*t` (B3b) iff `h` factors through the right side; two sublocales through which the same maps factor coincide.

**B4 (proper maps; Vermeulen 1994, Elephant C3.2).** (a) Composites of proper maps are proper (elementary: `(gf)_* = g_*f_*`, and the Frobenius law composes). (b) Closed sublocale inclusions are proper (`i_*` for `c(a)` is the inclusion `↑a ⊆ O(Z)`, which preserves all joins; Frobenius: `(x ∨ a) ∨ w = x ∨ (a ∨ w)`; cf. Elephant C3.2.9). (c) **Pullbacks of proper maps are proper** (Vermeulen 1994; Elephant C3.2). The pullback-stability statement (c) should be checked in the cited sources, including its constructive scope.

**B5 (Scott continuity on a quotient).** If `q : L → M` is a surjective frame hom with nucleus `j` (so `M ≅ Fix(j)`), a directed family `(x_k)` in `M` lifts to the directed family of largest preimages `e_k := j(e)` for any preimage `e` of `x_k` (if `x_k ≤ x_l` then `q(e_k ∨ e_l) = x_l`, so `e_k ∨ e_l ≤ j(e_l) = e_l`), and `q(⋁e_k) = ⋁x_k`. Hence a monotone `Φ : M → N` preserves directed joins as soon as `Φ ∘ q` does. No choice is used (the largest preimage is canonical).

## 3. The universal family `K` and its nucleus

**Definition.** `K := ⋂_{u ∈ O(X)} [ 1 ⊕ □u ≤ u ⊕ 1 ] ⊆ X × W`, i.e. the largest sublocale on which `π_2^*(□u) ≤ π_1^*(u)` for all `u`. Spatially (for sober `X`, points of `W` being compact saturated sets `Q`): `(x,Q) ∈ K ⟺ (∀u)(Q ⊆ u ⇒ x ∈ u) ⟺ x ∈ Q`. Write `i_K : K ↪ X×W`, `π_K := π_2 ∘ i_K : K → W`, and `j_K` for the nucleus of `K` on `O(X×W)`.

(Equivalently `K` is the equalizer of `π_2` and `m := ∪ ∘ (η × 1) : X × W → W`, `m^*(□u) = u ⊕ □u`; This alternate description is not used in the proof.)

**Lemma 1 (the nucleus of K).** In the C‑ideal description (C):

> `j_K(e) = e^K := { (s,v) : (s ∧ v, v) ∈ e }`,   equivalently `σ_{j_K e}(v) = v → σ_e(v)`.

Consequently, for `u ∈ O(X)` and `e ∈ O(X×W)`:

> `i_K^*(1 ⊕ □u) ≤ i_K^*(e)  ⟺  (u,u) ∈ e  ⟺  u ⊕ □u ≤ e  ⟺  u ≤ σ_e(u)`,

and therefore

> **`(π_K)_*( i_K^*(e) ) = Ψ(e) := ⋁{ □u : u ⊕ □u ≤ e } = ⋁{ □u : u ≤ σ_e(u) }`.**

*Proof.* Step 1: `e^K` is a C‑ideal. Downset: clear. (i): `(s_j∧v, v) ∈ e ∀j ⇒ ((⋁s_j)∧v, v) = (⋁(s_j∧v), v) ∈ e`. (ii): let `(s,u_k) ∈ e^K` with `(u_k)` directed, i.e. `(s∧u_k, u_k) ∈ e`. For any `j,k` pick `l ≥ j,k`; then `(s∧u_j, u_k) ≤ (s∧u_l, u_l) ∈ e`, so `(s∧u_j,u_k) ∈ e` for all `j,k`. By (ii) in `k`: `(s∧u_j, ⋁u_k) ∈ e`; by (i) in `j`: `(s ∧ ⋁u_j, ⋁u_k) ∈ e`, i.e. `(s, ⋁u_k) ∈ e^K`. ∎(Step 1)

Step 2: `k : e ↦ e^K` is a nucleus: inflationary (downset), idempotent (`(s∧v∧v,v) = (s∧v,v)`), preserves binary meets (intersections). Let `S_k` be its sublocale.

Step 3: `S_k ⊆ K`. The quotient map is `k`; `k(↓(1,u)) = {(s,v): v ≤ u} = ↓(1,u)` and `k(↓(u,1)) = {(s,v) : s∧v ≤ u} ⊇ ↓(1,u)`. So `k(1⊕□u) ≤ k(u⊕1)` for all `u`, i.e. `S_k` satisfies the defining inequalities of `K`; by B3a, `S_k ⊆ K`, i.e. `j_K ≤ k`.

Step 4: `k ≤ j_K`, i.e. `i_K^*(e^K) ≤ i_K^*(e)`. For `(s,v)` with `(s∧v,v) ∈ e`: on `K`, `i_K^*(1⊕□v) ≤ i_K^*(v⊕1)`, hence
`i_K^*(s⊕□v) = i_K^*(s⊕1) ∧ i_K^*(1⊕□v) ≤ i_K^*(s⊕1) ∧ i_K^*(v⊕1) ∧ i_K^*(1⊕□v) = i_K^*((s∧v)⊕□v) ≤ i_K^*(e)`. Since `e^K = ⋃ ↓(s,v)` over such pairs, `i_K^*(e^K) ≤ i_K^*(e)`.

Steps 3–4 give `j_K = k`. The displayed equivalences: `i_K^*(1⊕□u) ≤ i_K^*(e) ⟺ ↓(1,u) ⊆ j_K(e) = e^K ⟺ (1∧u,u) = (u,u) ∈ e`. Finally `(π_K)_*(i_K^* e) = ⋁{C : i_K^*(1⊕C) ≤ i_K^*(e)}`; writing `C = ⋁□u_k` the condition holds iff it holds for each `□u_k`, so the join is `⋁{□u : (u,u) ∈ e}`. ∎

*Remark.* Spatially `Ψ(e) = {Q : Q × {Q} ⊆ e}` — the "relative Hofmann–Mislove" statement that the fibre over the generic `Q` is detected by diagonal rectangles. Lemma 1 proves this point‑free with no compactness argument at all: the nucleus of `K` merely shrinks `s` to `s ∧ v`.

## 4. The closure lemma

The proposed proof here uses the preframe relation `□(⋁↑) = ⋁□`.

**Lemma 2 (closure lemma).** Let `G ⊆ O(X) × O(X)` be a downset closed under arbitrary joins in the first coordinate (equivalently `G = {(s,v) : s ≤ τ(v)}` for an antitone `τ`). Let `V ∈ O(W)` satisfy

> `□(s ∧ v) ≤ V` for all `(s,v) ∈ G`.

Then `□(s ∧ v) ≤ V` for all `(s,v)` in the C‑ideal `cl(G)` generated by `G`. In particular `Ψ(cl G) ≤ V`.

*Proof.* Let `C^∞` be the least subset of `O(X)×O(X)` containing `G` and closed under: (down) `(s',v') ≤ (s,v) ∈ C^∞ ⇒ (s',v') ∈ C^∞`; (ii) `(s,v_k) ∈ C^∞` for a directed `(v_k)` `⇒ (s,⋁v_k) ∈ C^∞`; (i‑dir) `(s_j,v) ∈ C^∞` for a directed `(s_j)` `⇒ (⋁s_j, v) ∈ C^∞`. (Impredicatively: the intersection of all such subsets; proofs about `C^∞` are by the induction principle "a subset containing `G` and closed under the three rules contains `C^∞`".)

(a) *`P(s,v) :⟺ □(s∧v) ≤ V` holds on `C^∞`.* It holds on `G` by hypothesis. (down): `□(s'∧v') ≤ □(s∧v)`. (ii): `□(s ∧ ⋁↑v_k) = □(⋁↑(s∧v_k)) = ⋁_k □(s∧v_k) ≤ V`. (i‑dir): `□((⋁↑s_j)∧v) = ⋁_j □(s_j∧v) ≤ V`. Both uses of `□(⋁↑) = ⋁□` are the preframe relation of `O(W)`.

(b) *`C^∞` is closed under binary joins in the first coordinate.* First let `B'' := {(s',v) ∈ C^∞ : ∀v' ≤ v, ∀(s,v') ∈ G : (s∨s', v') ∈ C^∞}`. `G ⊆ B''` because `G` is a downset closed under first‑coordinate joins. `B''` is closed under (down) (if `(s'',v'') ≤ (s',v)` and `v' ≤ v''`, `(s,v') ∈ G`, then `(s∨s',v') ∈ C^∞`, hence `(s∨s'',v') ∈ C^∞`), under (ii) (for `v' ≤ ⋁v_k` and `(s,v') ∈ G`: `(s, v'∧v_k) ∈ G`, so `(s∨s', v'∧v_k) ∈ C^∞` for all `k`, and `(v'∧v_k)` is directed with join `v'`), and under (i‑dir) (directed joins of `(s∨s'_j, v')`). So `B'' = C^∞`. Next let `B := {(s,v) ∈ C^∞ : ∀v' ≤ v, ∀(s',v') ∈ C^∞ : (s∨s',v') ∈ C^∞}`. `G ⊆ B` by `B'' = C^∞` (for `(s,v) ∈ G`, `v' ≤ v`, `(s',v') ∈ C^∞`: `(s,v') ∈ G`, so `(s∨s',v') ∈ C^∞`). Closure of `B` under the three rules is verified exactly as for `B''`. So `B = C^∞`, which is the claim.

(c) By (b), (i‑dir) and `(0,v) ∈ G`, `C^∞` is closed under arbitrary first‑coordinate joins (a join is the directed join of its finite subjoins); with (ii) and (down) it is a C‑ideal containing `G`, so `cl(G) ⊆ C^∞`; and `C^∞ ⊆ cl(G)` since `cl(G)` is closed under the three rules. Hence `cl(G) = C^∞`, and (a) gives the lemma. For the last sentence, `(u,u) ∈ cl G ⇒ □u = □(u∧u) ≤ V`. ∎

*Why the detour through `B'', B` is needed:* the predicate `P` is **not** preserved by non‑directed first‑coordinate joins (`□(s_1∨s_2)` is not controlled by `□s_1, □s_2`), so one cannot induct over the naive generating rules of C‑ideals. Part (b) shows that, starting from a `G` already closed under first‑coordinate joins, the C‑ideal closure never needs to re‑apply binary joins — only directed ones, which `□` preserves.

## 5. Theorem K: the universal family is proper

**Candidate Theorem K.** For every locale `X`, `π_K : K → P_U X` is proper.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1 and B5 it suffices to prove, for `Ψ : O(X×W) → O(W)`, `Ψ(e) = ⋁{□u : (u,u) ∈ e}`:

**(S)** `Ψ(⋁↑_j e_j) = ⋁_j Ψ(e_j)` for every directed family of C‑ideals;
**(F)** `Ψ(e ∨ (1 ⊕ D)) = Ψ(e) ∨ D` for all `e` and all `D ∈ O(W)`.

Indeed, (S) gives Scott continuity of `(π_K)_*` by B5 (as `(π_K)_* ∘ i_K^* = Ψ`), and (F) is the Frobenius law for `π_K`: for `x = i_K^*(e)` and `D ∈ O(W)`, `x ∨ π_K^*D = i_K^*(e ∨ (1⊕D))`, so `(π_K)_*(x ∨ π_K^*D) = Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕D) = Ψ(e) ∨ D = (π_K)_*(x) ∨ D`.

In both (S) and (F) the inequality `≥` is trivial (`Ψ` is monotone; and `D ≤ Ψ(1⊕D)` because `(d,d) ∈ ↓(1,d)` for each generator `□d ≤ D`).

**Proof of (S).** Let `G` be the closure of `⋃_j e_j` under first‑coordinate joins: `G = {(s,v) : s ≤ ⋁_j σ_{e_j}(v)}` (a downset, first‑coordinate‑join‑closed). Then `cl(G) = ⋁↑ e_j`. Put `V := ⋁_j Ψ(e_j)`. For `(s,v) ∈ G`: the family `(σ_{e_j}(v))_j` is directed (the `e_j` are), so `s∧v = ⋁↑_j t_j` with `t_j := s ∧ v ∧ σ_{e_j}(v)`. Since `t_j ≤ v`, antitonicity gives `t_j ≤ σ_{e_j}(v) ≤ σ_{e_j}(t_j)`, i.e. `(t_j,t_j) ∈ e_j`, so `□t_j ≤ Ψ(e_j) ≤ V`. Hence `□(s∧v) = ⋁_j □t_j ≤ V`. Lemma 2 gives `Ψ(⋁↑e_j) = Ψ(cl G) ≤ V`. ∎

**Proof of (F).** *Basic case `D = □d`.* Let `G` be the first‑coordinate‑join closure of `e ∪ ↓(1,d)`: `G = {(s,v) : s ≤ σ_e(v) ∨ ⋁{1 | v ≤ d}}`, so `cl(G) = e ∨ (1⊕□d)`. Put `V := Ψ(e) ∨ □d`. For `(s,v) ∈ G`: `s∧v` is the join of the directed set `{t} ∪ {s∧v | v ≤ d}` where `t := s∧v∧σ_e(v)` (directed because `t ≤ s∧v`). As before `t ≤ σ_e(t)`, so `□t ≤ Ψ(e)`; and whenever `v ≤ d`, `□(s∧v) ≤ □d`. Hence `□(s∧v) = □t ∨ ⋁{□(s∧v) | v ≤ d} ≤ V`. Lemma 2 gives `Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕□d) ≤ Ψ(e) ∨ □d`. (The "join over a truth value" formulation avoids the case split `v ≤ d` / `v ≰ d`, so this step is constructive.)
*Finite joins of generators:* iterate, since the basic case holds for every `e`: `Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕(D_1∨D_2)) = Ψ((e ∨ 1⊕D_1) ∨ 1⊕D_2) = Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕D_1) ∨ D_2 = Ψ(e) ∨ D_1 ∨ D_2`.
*General `D`:* `D` is the directed join of its finite subjoins of generators `D_k`; by (S), `Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕D) = ⋁_k Ψ(e ∨ 1⊕D_k) = ⋁_k (Ψ(e) ∨ D_k) = Ψ(e) ∨ D`. ∎

*Sanity check (spatial).* For sober `X`, `K_Q = Q` is compact and `Ψ(e) = {Q : Q×{Q} ⊆ e}` is open with the expected tube property; and for `X = ℕ` discrete, `W` is the set of finite subsets with the Alexandrov topology of `⊇`, closed sets of `ℕ × W` have `⊆`‑upward‑closed sections, and `π_K(H) = {F : ∃n∈F, F ∈ H_n}` is again upward closed.

## 6. Theorem L\* and its proof

**Candidate Theorem L\*.** Let `X`, `Y` be arbitrary locales, `a ∈ O(X×Y)`, `E := c(a)` with projections `p_1, p_2`, and `r := (p_2)_* p_1^* : O(X) → O(Y)`. Then `r` preserves directed joins iff `p_2` is proper.

*Proof.* (⇐) `(p_2)_*` preserves directed joins by hypothesis and `p_1^*` preserves all joins. This direction needs nothing else.

(⇒) **Step 1 (`r` is a preframe hom).** `p_1^*` preserves finite meets and `(p_2)_*` preserves all meets, so `r` preserves finite meets; with the hypothesis it is a preframe hom. Explicitly, `r(u) = ⋁{w : 1⊕w ≤ (u⊕1) ∨ a}`, since `p_2^*w ≤ p_1^*u` in `O(E) = ↑a` means `(1⊕w) ∨ a ≤ (u⊕1) ∨ a`. By the universal property there is a unique locale map **`φ : Y → W = P_U X` with `φ^*(□u) = r(u)`**.

**Step 2 (the pulled‑back family).** Let `Ẹ := (1_X × φ)^{-1}(K) ⊆ X × Y`. By B3c and preservation of intersections, `Ẹ = ⋂_u [ 1 ⊕ r(u) ≤ u ⊕ 1 ]` in `X × Y`.

**Step 3 (`E ⊆ Ẹ`).** By B3a this means `i_E^*(1⊕r(u)) ≤ i_E^*(u⊕1)` for all `u`, i.e. `(1⊕r(u)) ∨ a ≤ (u⊕1) ∨ a`. This holds because `1 ⊕ r(u) ≤ (u⊕1) ∨ a` by the formula for `r(u)` in Step 1 (the set `{w : 1⊕w ≤ x}` is closed under joins as `π_2^*` preserves them). *This is the only place where the relation `E` enters, and it enters exactly through the definition of `r`.*

**Step 4 (`Ẹ → Y` is a pullback of `π_K`).** The square

```
    X × Y  --(1×φ)-->  X × W
      |                   |
     π_2                 π_2
      v                   v
      Y   ----φ---->      W
```

is a pullback (`(X×W) ×_W Y ≅ X × (W ×_W Y) ≅ X × Y`). Pulling `K ↪ X×W` back along `1×φ` gives `Ẹ`; by the pasting lemma `Ẹ ≅ (X×Y) ×_{X×W} K ≅ Y ×_W K`, and under this isomorphism `Ẹ ↪ X×Y → Y` is the projection `Y ×_W K → Y`. So **`Ẹ → Y` is the pullback of `π_K : K → W` along `φ`**, hence proper by Theorem K and B4(c).

**Step 5 (`E ↪ Ẹ` is a closed inclusion).** `E = c(a) ∩ Ẹ = i_Ẹ^{-1}(c(a)) = c(i_Ẹ^*(a))` is a closed sublocale of `Ẹ`; its inclusion is proper by B4(b).

**Step 6.** `p_2 = (Ẹ → Y) ∘ (E ↪ Ẹ)` is proper by B4(a). ∎

**Dependency distinction.** B1–B5 and the upper-powerlocale universal property are proposed background inputs; Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Theorem K, and the six steps of L* are the candidate proof that must be checked, not assumed.
