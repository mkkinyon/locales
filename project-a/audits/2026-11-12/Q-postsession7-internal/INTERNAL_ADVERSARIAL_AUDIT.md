## 3. Boolean predecessor and Gleason-patch audit (A conditional on classical Gleason)

**Pointwise existence.** Fix an ordinary Boolean ultrafilter s of complete M. Its associated finite-prime L-filters H_s,K_s obey: for every finite L-cover, some member lies in H_s∩K_s. Thus the ideal generated by the *complement* of H_s∩K_s is proper, so a prime ideal extension yields a prime filter P_s contained in both. This is exactly the use of the finite-cover hypothesis; it is not a disguised assertion that s respects arbitrary joins.

**Closed relation and selection.** Finite-prime filter inclusion `P⊆H,K` is closed in the compact Hausdorff **patch** topology, because a violation is witnessed by a clopen compact-open generator. The relation R⊆Stone(M)×Spec_lat(L)_patch is compact Hausdorff and surjects onto Stone(M). Stone(M) is extremally disconnected since M is complete, so **classical Gleason projectivity** gives a continuous section. Pullbacks of the patch-clopen lattice generators give a bounded lattice predecessor p_fin≤h,k. This argument correctly invokes classical choice and ordinary Stone ultrafilters; those need not be points of Loc(M).

**Coherent upgrade check.** For coherent L, run the argument on compact K(L), not L_lat. Under L=Idl(K), the formula `p(a)=∨_{k≤a, k compact}p_K(k)` respects all joins: if compact k≤∨_i a_i, algebraicity first replaces a_i by all compact subpieces and compactness selects finitely many; distributivity supplies finite meets. This step is not valid for arbitrary L. A marked infinite zero-cover C forces every lattice lower selection to fail frame join preservation: p_fin(c)=0 for all c while p_fin(1)=1.

**Risk B:** The full topological projectivity/spectral duality statements are imported rather than reproved; referee should verify applicability to arbitrary complete Boolean M, possible empty spectra, and the exact common-lower relation. ## 4. Boolean SDκ product-transfer audit (A at algebraic level; B at topology/example level)

**Reconstructed finiteization.** For a basis `(b_i)_{i∈I}` of Y, expand each c in a product cover as `c=∨_i(u_{c,i}⊠b_i)`, where `u_{c,i}=∨{m:m⊠b_i≤c}`. For each i, a Boolean maximal disjoint partition chooses either the complement of `∨_c u_{c,i}` or a witness c with cell≤u_{c,i}. Strong SDκ yields one common partition of 1 for **all i** even if C and its partitions are uncountable. On a nonzero cell m, the selected basis elements b_i cover 1_Y: otherwise a nonzero product of the nonzero open m with the closed complement of their join would contradict the original C covering Z×Y. Group the selected b_i by their **original c labels**: `v_{c,m}=∨{b_i: selected label c}`; then `m⊠v_{c,m}≤c` and `∨_c v_{c,m}=1_Y`. If C finite, this is a **finite Y-cover**, not merely a countable one. The resulting cover-square inequality proves the lower D/D₂ inclusions. Projection naturality and Boolean `D(Z)=D₂(Z)=Δ_Z` prove the reverse.

## 6. Finiteization/chromatic and Baire/scattered program (A in core algebra; B in imported rigidity)
