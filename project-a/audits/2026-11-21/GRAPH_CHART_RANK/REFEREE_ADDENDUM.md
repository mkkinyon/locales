# Addendum to the independent referee report
## Project A — graph extensions, chart rank, and a finite-exception criterion
**21 November 2026**

This addendum records observations made *after* completion of my independent report. They are not corrections to the verdict on the submitted claims. One observation does, however, settle a broad question that I had left open in the report: **finite locally closed chart rank is not necessary for associative graph adjunction in general**. The meaningful necessity question should be narrowed accordingly.

As in the report, assertions about associativity inside \(\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\) are conditional on the imported rectangular composition rule **(RC)** and the audited positive calculus **(ALG)**.

## 1. A safe Cantor-to-Cantor surjection of infinite chart rank

Let \(C=2^{\mathbb N}\), choose \(c\in C\), let
\[
X=(C\times\{0\})\sqcup(C\times\{1\}),\qquad Y=C,
\]
and define
\[
f(y,0)=y,\qquad f(y,1)=c.
\]
Both \(X\) and \(Y\) are Cantor spaces (the disjoint union of two Cantor spaces is again a Cantor space); \(f\) is a continuous, proper, **surjective** map.

The fiber over \(c\) is infinite, so
\[
\rho_{lc}(f)=\infty,
\]
indeed no finite cover by *arbitrary* injective charts is possible: each chart contains at most one point of this fiber.

Nevertheless, placing \(X,Y\) in separate clopen summands of an ambient compact Hausdorff space \(G\), the localic graph decomposes as
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_f=\Gamma_h\ \vee\ q(C\times\{1\},\{c\}),
\qquad h:C\times\{0\}\overset\cong\longrightarrow Y,
\quad h(y,0)=y.
\quad}\tag{A1}
\]
Here \(h\) is an allowed locally closed partial homeomorphism (in fact, between clopen summands). The second term is a full localic rectangle; since one support is a singleton, it agrees with the point-generated rectangle:
\[
q(A,\{c\})=p(A,\{c\})=[A\times\{c\}].
\]
Consequently \(\Gamma_f\in\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\), as does its converse. **Conditional on (ALG), adjoining both graphs to the entire finite rectangular algebra is associative**, despite \(\rho_{lc}(f)=\infty\).

This is not merely the trivial constant-map example: it is a surjection between two perfect, compact metrizable, zero-dimensional spaces, with an identity sheet as well as a collapsed Cantor sheet. It is **not open**, since the second clopen sheet has singleton image. It therefore does not challenge the negative theorem (FIN) about the *open* projection \(C\times K\to C\).

There is an even simpler observation: for **any** continuous finite-image map between compact Hausdorff spaces,
\[
\Gamma_f=\bigvee_{y\in f(X)}q(f^{-1}(y),\{y\}),
\tag{A2}
\]
so its graph is already in the rectangular ideal. On an infinite source, such a map has an infinite fiber and hence infinite chart rank. If the target is finite discrete and \(f\) is onto, it may even be **open and proper**. Thus neither openness nor infinite fiber multiplicity, taken alone, determines safety.

## 2. Exact characterization of *functional graphs belonging to* \(\mathcal A_{lc}\)

The example suggests a natural replacement for pure chart rank: allow a finite number of **constant pieces**, which are represented by rectangles rather than partial-homeomorphism graphs.

**Proposition (finite-exception characterization).** Let \(f:X\to Y\) be continuous between compact Hausdorff spaces, and put \(G=X\sqcup Y\). Then the following are equivalent:

1. \(\Gamma_f\in\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\).
2. The source \(X\) has a finite cover by subsets of two types: locally closed partial-homeomorphism charts for \(f\), and subsets on which \(f\) is constant (with no local-closedness assumption on the latter).
3. There exists a **finite** set \(F\subseteq Y\) such that the restriction
   \[
   f:X\setminus f^{-1}(F)\longrightarrow Y\setminus F
   \]
   admits a finite cover by partial-homeomorphism charts whose domains and ranges are locally closed in the indicated source and target (equivalently, in \(X\) and \(Y\), respectively).

**Proof.** Suppose (1) holds. Write \(\Gamma_f\) as a finite join of permitted rectangle generators \(p(A,B),q(A,B)\) and graphs \(\Gamma_h\) of locally closed partial homeomorphisms. Every summand lies below \(\Gamma_f\). A nonzero rectangle has the ordinary points \(A\times B\), all of which therefore lie on the graph of the single-valued function \(f\). Hence \(B=\{y\}\) for some \(y\), and \(f(a)=y\) for every \(a\in A\). As observed above, this rectangle is just \(q(A,\{y\})=p(A,\{y\})\). Every partial-homeomorphism summand must similarly be a restriction of \(f\).

All the resulting summands are point-generated, and their finite join is point-generated by the union of their ordinary graphs. They must therefore cover the ordinary graph of \(f\). This gives (2). Collect the finitely many target values from the constant pieces into \(F\). The remaining points are covered by the finitely many partial-homeomorphism charts, and intersecting each chart's domain and range with the complements of the corresponding closed sets preserves local closedness. This gives (3).

Conversely, assume (3). Each chart of the restriction is locally closed also in the original \(X,Y\), because \(X\setminus f^{-1}(F)\) and \(Y\setminus F\) are open. The part of the graph over the exceptional values is
\[
\bigvee_{y\in F}q(f^{-1}(y),\{y\}),
\]
a finite rectangular join. Adding the finitely many chart graphs recovers \(\Gamma_f\), proving (1). The implication (2)\(\Rightarrow\)(1) follows directly from the same rectangle-and-chart decomposition. \(\square\)

This characterization concerns **membership in the particular associative algebra** \(\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\), not all possible ways an additional graph might adjoin associatively to the rectangular algebra. I am **not** claiming that associative adjunction itself forces membership in \(\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\).

**Corollary (finite-to-one case).** If every fiber of \(f\) is finite, then
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_f\in\mathcal A_{lc}(G)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{lc}(f)<\infty.}\tag{A3}
\]
Indeed, in (3), the inverse image \(f^{-1}(F)\) of a finite set is then finite; add each of its points as a singleton (closed) partial-homeomorphism chart. The converse is the already audited finite-chart sufficiency (G-SEL's algebraic mechanism, without its selection-theorem hypotheses). In particular, the packet's two-to-one Cantor map does **not** belong to \(\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\), consistently with its independently exhibited associator.

## 3. Revised formulation of the necessity problem

The unrestricted question in §12 of my report—whether safe graph adjunction *requires* finite chart rank—has the negative answer (A1). Its surviving, potentially useful versions are:

- **Finite-to-one necessity:** For continuous finite-to-one maps between compact metrizable spaces, does associative adjunction of \(\Gamma_f\) and \(\Gamma_f^{\smile}\) to the *whole* rectangular algebra imply \(\rho_{lc}(f)<\infty\)? Equivalently, by (A3), does it force \(\Gamma_f\in\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\)? No proof is offered here.
- **Beyond the known algebra:** Can a functional graph be safe without belonging to \(\mathcal A_{lc}(G)\)? The point-generated graph probe (NR) is a necessary condition for safety but has not been proved sufficient.

A practical advantage of this reformulation is that it cleanly separates two different reasons for a large fiber: a **constant rectangular component**, which is already harmless in the algebra, and the **nonrectangular image/pullback pathology** exhibited by the Cantor examples. The finite-to-one question removes the first source of easy counterexamples and isolates the more interesting localic issue.

### Relation to the original report

This addendum strengthens—not reverses—the report's verdict. It makes no new appeal to Michael's selection theorem. The finite-exception characterization and its corollary are elementary consequences of the packet's definitions and the fact that finite joins of point-generated sublocales are point-generated by the union of their ordinary points. The **associativity** consequences continue to depend on (RC)/(ALG) exactly as before.
