# Foundations and references for independent referee

## Conventions

- All localic mathematics is in **classical** Loc, with classical choice when selecting lifts of ordinary points.
- A spatial sublocale **[A]** is the least sublocale containing all singleton points in an ordinary subset A of a sober Hausdorff ambient space. This need not be the closure of A.
- B(K) denotes the least dense (double-negation / Booleanization) sublocale of a nonempty locale K. B(K) is always nonzero; when K is a Hausdorff space without isolated points, B(K) has **no ordinary points**.
- Composition R∘S has **diagrammatic order**: traverse R first, S second. It is the localic image of the genuine composability fiber product, not the ordinary pointwise composite.
- Sublocale pullback preserves arbitrary meets and **finite joins**. It need not preserve arbitrary joins. Image of sublocales, left adjoint to inverse image, preserves arbitrary joins. Composition therefore distributes over **finite** joins in either variable, not asserted over infinite ones.

## Baseline compact-Hausdorff rectangular theorem

For a compact Hausdorff ambient space G and ordinary A,B⊆G define

    p(A,B) = [A×B]_(G×G)           (point-generated)
    q(A,B) = [A]_G ×_Loc [B]_G      (full localic rectangle).

The full p/q rectangular composition theorem (previous research, but check it in this audit if any new result uses it incorrectly) asserts: for independently chosen r∈{p(A,B),q(A,B)} and s∈{p(C,D),q(C,D)}, their composite equals q(A,D) when [B]∧[C]≠0 and equals 0 otherwise. The finite joins of such rectangles form an associative converse-closed algebra.

**Underlying proof sketch (do not regard as a substitute for checking).** The upper bound follows from endpoints. If W=[B]∧[C]≠0, its least compact closed support K has B∩K and C∩K ordinarily dense. To prove the entire q(A,D) lower bound, one shows that for arbitrary compact Hausdorff endpoint spaces [A]×[D] is generated by B(L) for compact closed L⊆X×Z with the A-first and D-second traces each dense in L. This follows from ambient fittedness, an open separator and the least compact closed support of a missing part. For each L, pull the two bipartite point-generated relations back to L×K: both pullback sublocales contain dense ordinary traces, so their intersection contains B(L×K). Its image along the open-surjective projection to L contains B(L). Joining L gives the whole endpoint product. Enlarging either point-generated rectangle to a full rectangle preserves the lower bound. All of this is compact Hausdorff, not necessarily metrizable.

## Standard mathematical inputs to check

- **Fitness.** A regular locale is fit; in a fit locale *every sublocale is fitted*, hence can be separated from a genuinely larger sublocale by an ambient open. Igor Arrieta, *A New Diagonal Separation and its Relations With the Hausdorff Property*, *Topology and its Applications* 342 (2024), §2.3.
- **Sublocale inverse images.** Arrieta–Suarez, *The coframe of D-sublocales of a locale and the T_D duality*: inverse-image on sublocales preserves arbitrary meets and finite joins.
- **Spatial product with locally compact factor.** Standard locale theory (often cited as Johnstone, *Sketches of an Elephant*, C4.1.8): if one spatial factor is locally compact Hausdorff, the localic product with a spatial locale is spatial. This is *not* true for arbitrary pairs of spatial locales.
- **Resolvability.** Classical Hewitt resolvability result (or stronger countably compact regular Hausdorff formulation): every nonempty crowded compact Hausdorff space has two disjoint dense point sets. This is the only nontrivial outside **set-theoretic topology** input for the general nonmetrizable negative witness. A compact perfect metrizable space allows an elementary countable dense/co-dense split.
- **Simmons scatteredness theorem.** Compare Anna Laura Suarez, *Revisiting the relation between subspaces and sublocales*, arXiv:2010.05284, on scatteredness and the correspondence between spatial subspaces and sublocales. This is related literature, not a priority claim for the point-cover formulation.

## Invalid shortcuts the referee should actively seek

(1) A nonzero localic meet need not have an ordinary point. (2) A product of two spatial sublocales need not be spatial. (3) A locale-surjection is not automatically pullback-stable. (4) A dense ordinary point subset and the whole localic space need not have identical sublocales. (5) An arbitrary infinite rectangular decomposition is not a valid argument for distributivity of localic relation composition. (6) A closed graph by itself has associative powers on compact locales, but adjoining **nonclosed point-generated rectangles** is a different question. (7) Do not infer general HSIS necessity or a Level-2⇒Level-3 implication from the results in this packet.
