## Background and why it matters

A locale X is **totally unordered (TU)** if its oriented specialization sublocale \(\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X^2\), universally presented by \(u_1\le u_2\) for all opens u, equals the diagonal. Equivalently, for *every* locale E, comparable maps \(f\le g:E\to X\) must coincide. For sober T1 spaces, ordinary point specialization is equality, but \(\mathcal A\) may still carry pointless generalized comparisons.

A known, separately audited theorem says stable local compactness makes \(\mathcal A\) spatial, hence a stably locally compact sober T1 space is TU. An existing counterexample without point-fittedness shows local compactness alone does not ensure TU. The outstanding question (LC-PT) asks whether **locally compact + sober + T1 + every point fitted** suffices for TU.

The packet proposes a **negative** answer via a compact first-countable gluing of two Cantor sheets. This is an unusually consequential claim and demands an exact localic audit rather than verification solely at the level of ordinary points.
