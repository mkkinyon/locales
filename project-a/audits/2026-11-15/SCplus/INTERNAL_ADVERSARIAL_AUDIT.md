# Project A — Internal adversarial audit of the SC+ argument

**15 November 2026. Internal working audit; not part of the blind packet.**

## Verdict

**Provisional pass, with explicit background dependencies.** I did not find a mathematical gap in the proposed proof that, for a stably locally compact locale (X), the mixed meet \(\mathcal M=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D\) is spatial and

\[
\mathcal S=\mathcal M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every specialization interval of }X\text{ is a chain}.
\]

This is an **internal** verification only. The most valuable external check is the saturation lemma (A1), especially the identification of original opens with patch-open upper sets outside the compact case. No novelty/priority claim has been checked.

## 0. Precise scope

Classical mathematics, with the usual spatial representation of locally compact locales by sober spaces. Take \(Y=X\times X\) as a **localic** product; for locally compact sober \(X\), this agrees with the ordinary spatial product. Write \(\Delta\hookrightarrow Y\) for the diagonal image, \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{cl}_Y\Delta\), \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{fit}_Y\Delta\), \(\mathcal M=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D\). The oriented specialization sublocale \(\mathcal A\) is largest with \(\pi_1^*a\le\pi_2^*a\) on it for all opens \(a\) of \(X\), and \(\mathcal S=\mathcal A\vee\mathcal A^\smile\). The established all-locale inclusion is \(\mathcal S\le\mathcal M\).

Order convention: \(x\le y\) iff \(x\in\overline{\{y\}}\); opens are upper sets. A sublocale of a spatial locale is **not** generally determined by its points.

## A1. Main saturation lemma: adversarial check

**Claim.** If \(Z\) is stably locally compact sober and \(C\subseteq Z\) is saturated, the spatial sublocale \([C]\) is fitted. Consequently \(\operatorname{fit}_Z([B])=[\uparrow B]\) for every subspace \(B\subseteq Z\) for which the induced spatial sublocale notation is understood.

For \(w\in\mathcal O(Z)\), the subspace quotient nucleus is
\[
j_C(w)=\operatorname{int}_Z\bigl(w\cup(Z\setminus C)\bigr).
\]
The critical identity is
\[
j_C(w)=\bigvee_{\substack{u\in\mathcal O(Z)\\ C\subseteq u}}(u\to w).\tag{A1}
\]

**Reverse inequality rederived, without assuming normality.** Given \(z\in j_C(w)\), choose ordinary open \(V\ni z\) with \(V\cap C\subseteq w\). Local compactness gives a compact saturated neighborhood \(K\) with \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\subseteq K\subseteq V\). By stable local compactness, \(K\) is compact in the patch Hausdorff topology. Thus \(F=K\setminus w\) is patch-compact. The patch order is closed, so \(\downarrow F\) is patch-closed as the projection of the closed set \(\{(p,q):p\le q,\ q\in F\}\subseteq Z^{\mathrm{patch}}\times F\). Hence \(u=Z\setminus\downarrow F\) is patch-open and upper, so **ordinary open** by the proper-locally-compact-pospace correspondence. Since \(C\) is upper and avoids \(F\), \(C\subseteq u\). Also \(K\cap u\subseteq w\), whence \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\subseteq(u\to w)\). This proves (A1), since opens of a spatial locale are determined by points.

**Nucleus audit.** Each \(w\mapsto(u\to w)\) is the open nucleus \(j_u\). The join \(k=\bigvee_{C\subseteq u} j_u\) in the **nucleus frame** need not be computed pointwise. However \(j_u\le j_C\) for every \(u\), hence \(k\le j_C\); while (A1) says \(j_C(w)=\bigvee_u j_u(w)\le k(w)\). Thus \(k=j_C\) without any illicit pointwise-join interchange. This is exactly fittedness.

**Saturation of fitted point sets.** Every open sublocale has an upper point set, and arbitrary meets of sublocales induce intersections on points. Thus every fitted sublocale containing \([B]\) contains points \(\uparrow B\), and therefore contains the smallest sublocale generated by them, \([\uparrow B]\). Since the latter is fitted, \(\operatorname{fit}[B]=[\uparrow B]\).

**Dependencies to check externally:** (i) for stably locally compact sober \(Z\), the patch is locally compact Hausdorff with closed specialization order, (ii) ordinary opens are exactly patch-open uppers, and (iii) compact saturated subsets are patch-compact. We located these facts in Tix–Keimel–Plotkin and in Bice–Starling (references below). The argument **does not** assume all sublocales spatial or all fitted sublocales perfect.

## A2. Passing to \(Y\) and \(\mathcal I\)

**Product.** The finite product of properly locally compact patch pospaces remains properly locally compact: the upper closure of a compact set \(K\subseteq P\times P\) is closed (projection along compact \(K\)) and contained in the compact product \(\uparrow\pi_1 K\times\uparrow\pi_2 K\). Thus \(Y\) is stably locally compact, and the usual product of locally compact sober spaces represents the localic product.

**Closed subspace.** \(\mathcal I\) is the closed sublocale \(Y\setminus d_I\). Its underlying closed subset \(F\) is sober. It is stably locally compact: compact saturated neighborhoods of points in \(Y\) restrict to compact saturated neighborhoods in \(F\), since ambient compact saturated sets are patch-compact and \(F\) is patch-closed; finite intersections retain compactness. Thus it is legitimate to apply A1 inside \(\mathcal I\).

**Relative fitting identity, rederived directly:**
\[
\operatorname{fit}_{\mathcal I}(\Delta)
=\bigwedge_{\Delta\le O_{\mathcal I}(v)}O_{\mathcal I}(v)
=\mathcal I\wedge\bigwedge_{\Delta\le O_Y(w)}O_Y(w)
=\mathcal I\wedge\mathcal D.
\tag{A2}
\]
Every open of a sublocale comes from an ambient open by the quotient frame map; because \(\Delta\le\mathcal I\), the condition that its restriction contain \(\Delta\) is exactly the ambient condition. This does **not** say that \(\mathcal D\) localizes under arbitrary open subspaces of \(X\).

Apply A1 to \(Y,\Delta\) and to \(\mathcal I,\Delta\):
\[
\mathcal D=[\uparrow_Y\Delta],\qquad
\mathcal M=[\uparrow_{\mathcal I}\Delta].\tag{A3}
\]
Both are spatial. The second statement is stronger than what SC+ needs.

## A3. Point formulas and interval criterion

**For \(\mathcal D\):** \((x,y)\in\uparrow_Y\Delta\iff\exists r\le x,y\).

**For \(\mathcal I\):** Inseparable points have a common upper bound in a stably locally compact sober space. Given compact saturated neighborhoods \(K\ni x\) and \(H\ni y\), inseparability forces \(K\cap H\ne\varnothing\). Finite intersections of such neighborhoods again are neighborhoods, so the family \(K\cap H\) has the finite intersection property. Fix one member to obtain a patch-compact ambient set. All members are patch-closed, hence have nonempty total intersection. Intersecting all compact saturated neighborhoods of \(x\) gives \(\uparrow x\) (local compactness lets these refine every open neighborhood), similarly \(\uparrow y\). Thus \(\uparrow x\cap\uparrow y\ne\varnothing\). The reverse direction follows from opens being upper.

**For \(\mathcal M\):** Since \(\mathcal I\) carries the restricted specialization order, A3 says
\[
\mathcal M=[\{(x,y):\exists r,s\ (r\le x,y\le s)\}].\tag{A4}
\]

The points of \(\mathcal S\) are exactly comparable pairs by its defining oriented inequalities. Necessity of chain intervals follows from incomparable points in a common interval. For sufficiency, the interval-chain condition makes the point sets of \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathcal S\) equal; \(\mathcal M\) is spatial, so it is the **least** sublocale containing these points, whereas \(\mathcal S\le\mathcal M\) contains them. Hence equality. This use of points is justified by the spatiality of \(\mathcal M\), not by a blanket assumption about all sublocales.

**Finite check:** The four-element diamond \(r<a,b<s\) has \((a,b)\in\mathcal M\setminus\mathcal S\), confirming nontriviality. Earlier noncompact escaping-witness examples may have \(\mathcal D\) spatial but nonperfect: no contradiction.

## A4. Ranked objections and residual verification

1. **Highest risk: hidden compactness in patch separation.** Addressed by compact *local* \(K\), not global compactness of \(Z\). Still ask an independent referee to validate closedness of \(\downarrow F\) and the exact patch/order correspondence for noncompact spaces.
2. **High risk: lattice join of nuclei mistaken for pointwise join.** Addressed explicitly by the sandwich \(\bigvee j_u\le j_C\) and the pointwise identity giving the converse.
3. **High risk: relative fitting identity assumes open restrictions surjective.** Verified from sublocale quotient frames; ask external referee to recheck.
4. **Medium risk: spatiality of localic products and closed sublocales.** Covered by local compactness/sobriety, classical choice. Should be stated rather than assumed.
5. **Medium risk: common-upper point formula outside compact case.** Reproved using patch-compact neighborhoods; did not rely on global compactness.
6. **Scope boundary:** General non-stably-locally-compact locales remain open; no claim that arbitrary fitted sublocales are spatial. The arbitrary subset result concerns the fitting of a *spatial* sublocale.
7. **Originality boundary:** No literature-priority search has been completed for the saturation lemma or the SC+ theorem; the independent audit is about correctness.
