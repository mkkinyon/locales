1. **A one-real witness for E.1.** The independent-coordinate pair of §D is not needed. Take M₀ = RO(C), h₁(u) = ro(V_u), and k₁ = τ⁻¹∘h₁, where τ flips bit 0 only.
   - τ maps K_n to itself for every n ≥ 1. So for a cylinder R and large n, pick p ∈ K_n∩R; then p and τp both lie in any neighbourhood V of K_n. Hence h₁(u)∧k₁(u) meets R, and the pair matches every cover.
   - The equalizer is 0, because {x₀=1} and {x₀=0} are complementary.
   - So E.1 holds with one Cohen real c and its one-bit flip. §F's all-bit flip fails only because it moves every K_n off itself, so the warning in §F is about that particular pair, not about dependent coordinates in general.
   - It also shows D(A) is strictly larger than Δ_A ∨ B(A²), since this pair lives on a nowhere dense graph.

2. **A sharper B.1.** For Boolean Z, strongly Hausdorff Y and any f, g: Z → Y, let s be the support of eq(f,g). The binary cover {u_f ∨ (s⊠1), u_g} has matching value exactly s. So a pair of sections lies in D₂(Z×Y) if and only if f = g. With a third cover member the same conclusion holds over any subfit base.

3. **Fitting does not commute with base change.** E.1 says fit(Z×Δ_A) is strictly below Z×fit(Δ_A) inside Z×A², while closure does commute. 4. **Which bases witness strictness.** Atomic Z never does, since D of a disjoint sum of copies of A is the sum of the D(A). Any Z that adds a Cohen real does, by pulling back the witness.
