# Foundations appendix: explicit finite-fence inputs

This appendix gives the finite-fence inputs to Candidate Theorem 3.1. Its purpose is to let the referee verify the abstract hypotheses without requiring the unrelated global Cantor-fusion argument.

## 1. Finite-fence quotient

Let (P=[0,1]) carry the compact Hausdorff **closed height-one order** of the two-colour finite-fence construction. Its nonsingleton comparability components are pairwise disjoint finite zigzag fences (P_n,Q_n), with Euclidean edge lengths tending to zero; every other component is a singleton. Write (E) for the countable set of fence vertices.

In the explicit coordinate model, the vertex set satisfies

\[
\overline{E}^{\,P}=[0.1,0.8],
\]

and the complement of this interval consists entirely of singleton components. This observation is used in the negative-open part of Lemma F below.

The key geometric property is the **strong forcing condition**:

> Every Euclidean-closed component-saturated subset meeting infinitely many nonsingleton fence components contains all of (E).

Let (X) be the locale of Euclidean-open upper subsets of (P). The component quotient (q:X\to Q) is specified by the subframe of component-saturated upper opens. Thus a quotient open (V) has inverse image (q^*V\subseteq P) which is Euclidean-open, upper and component-saturated.

The generic point (p_\infty:1\to Q) is defined by

\[
p_\infty\in V\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q^*V\cap E\ne\varnothing.
\]

By strong forcing, the complement of a positive quotient open meets only finitely many fence components; hence finite intersections of positive opens remain positive. The rule is also completely prime under arbitrary unions. A negative quotient open is disjoint from (E).

Let (\mathscr F) be the family of all Euclidean-compact component-saturated subsets (F\subseteq P) with (F\cap E) finite. Each (F) is a closed sublocale of (X); because its complement is saturated upper-open, it is the pullback of a closed sublocale of (Q). Put (U_F=X\setminus F).

## 2. Exact one-point fibre

**Lemma F.** For the *actual* point fibre (G=X\times_Q[p_\infty]),

\[
\boxed{G=\bigwedge_{F\in\mathscr F}U_F.}\tag{F}
\]

**Proof supplied for examination.** Put (N=\bigwedge_{F\in\mathscr F}U_F). Since every (U_F) is a positive quotient neighbourhood of (p_\infty), (G\le N).

If (V) is a positive quotient open, its complement (F=P\setminus q^*V) is compact, saturated, and meets (E) only finitely by strong forcing. Therefore (F\in\mathscr F) and (q^*V=U_F) restricts to top on (N).

If (V) is negative, (W=q^*V) is Euclidean-open, upper, saturated and disjoint from (E). As (W) is open, it also misses (\overline E=[0.1,0.8]). Each (x\in W) therefore lies in a smaller Euclidean-open interval (W_x\subseteq F_x\subseteq W), where (F_x) is a compact closed interval containing only singleton components; hence (F_x\in\mathscr F). Since (N\le U_{F_x}), its meet with (W_x) is zero. The frame homomorphism restricting opens to (N) preserves arbitrary joins; because the (W_x) cover (W), its restriction to (N) is zero as well.

Thus the quotient-open frame homomorphism on (N) assigns *exactly* the truth values of (p_\infty), positive or negative. Consequently (N\to Q) factors through the one-point sublocale (p_\infty), so (N\le G). \(\square\)

**Warning.** In an arbitrary locale, the intersection of all positive neighbourhoods of a point may be strictly larger than the corresponding one-point sublocale. The negative-open test is essential above.

## 3. Cutoff rigidity

**Lemma C.** For every (F\in\mathscr F),

\[
\boxed{K\wedge(F\times F)=H\wedge(F\times F),}\tag{C}
\]

where (K=X\times_QX) and (H) is the sublocale of (X^2) generated by the ordinary pairs of points belonging to the same comparability component.

**Proof supplied for examination.** Let (A\subseteq F) be the finite union of all nonsingleton components contained in (F); finiteness follows since (F\cap E) is finite. Let (O=F\setminus A), the relative open sublocale consisting only of singleton components.

The quotient map restricted to (O) is a locale embedding. Indeed, if (W\subseteq O) is any relative upper open, then (F\setminus W) is Euclidean-compact and component-saturated. Its complement in (P) is a saturated upper open, hence a quotient open, whose restriction to (O) is precisely (W). Thus (\mathcal O(Q)\to\mathcal O(O)) is surjective. It follows that the kernel pair restricted to (O\times O) is the diagonal (\Delta_O\le H).

On the finite spatial locale (A), the kernel pair consists of the finite union of component squares (C_j\times C_j), which lie in (H). Mixed blocks (A\times O) and (O\times A) vanish in (K), detected by the saturated quotient open (P\setminus A).

The complementary closed/open decomposition (F=A\vee O) yields a finite four-block cover of (F\times F). Combining those block calculations proves (K\wedge(F\times F)\le H). The opposite inclusion follows from (H\le K). \(\square\)

The singleton remainder (O) can be infinite and accumulate on fence vertices; the proof of embedding is at the **frame level**, not merely pointwise.

## 4. Residual kernel-pair decomposition

Using the complemented-cutoff lemma (L) with the second projection (\nu:K\to X), one obtains

\[
K=\left(\bigvee_{F\in\mathscr F}\nu^{-1}F\right)\vee\nu^{-1}G.
\]

Equality of quotient-coordinate maps on (K) and the quotient-pullback property of the cutoffs imply

\[
\nu^{-1}F=K\wedge(F\times F)\le H,
\qquad\nu^{-1}G=G\times G=B.
\]

Since (H\le K\) and (B\le K), it follows that

\[
\boxed{K=H\vee B.}\tag{K}
\]

No relational composition and no global Cantor-fusion argument is required for this decomposition.

## 5. Technical conventions useful in the audit

- (H) contains the full localic diagonal (\Delta_X): (X) and its diagonal are spatial, and (H) is the sublocale generated by all ordinary same-component pairs, including all diagonal pairs. It is symmetric.
- Restriction of a localic relation (R\le K) to the fibre means (R_G=R\wedge(G\times G)), not a point-set restriction.
- The maps (C_{R,S}\to Q) obtained from its three coordinates agree because the two legs lie in (K).
- The sublocale lattice of any locale is a **coframe**, and the direct image of sublocales under any locale map preserves arbitrary joins (the left adjoint of sublocale pullback). Pullback preserves arbitrary meets and finite joins.
- The finite-fence construction gives a nonzero, pointless (G), but neither property is necessary for the abstract composition-localization theorem. Nonzeroness requires separate Cantor-support witnesses; it is not inferred merely from the existence of (p_\infty).
