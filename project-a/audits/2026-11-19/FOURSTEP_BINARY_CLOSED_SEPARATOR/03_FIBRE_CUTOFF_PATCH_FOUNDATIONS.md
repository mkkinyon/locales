# Exact generic fibre, finite-cutoff rigidity, coframe assembly, and patch comparison

**19 November 2026. Technical foundations accompanying the short candidate. For independent checking.**

## 1. Statement and exact dependencies

Let `P=[0,1]` be the compact Hausdorff pospace defined in the accompanying explicit-coordinate note. Its order has height one; its nontrivial comparability components are pairwise disjoint finite fences in two families `P_n,Q_n`, and every other component is a singleton. Let `X` be the upper locale, `q:X→Q` the component quotient (with `O(Q)` the frame of saturated upper opens), and

\[
H=H_G=[E_G]\le X\times X,\quad
T=H\circ H,\quad R=T\circ T,\quad K=K_q=X\times_Q X.
\]

Brackets `[E_G]` mean the point-generated **spatial sublocale** of `X²` generated by all ordinary pairs in the same comparability component. Composition is image-defined and need not be associative; the bracketing of `R` is fixed.

The quotient has a completely prime point `p_∞` (the filter of saturated opens meeting the set `E` of nontrivial fence vertices). Write `G=q^{-1}([p_∞])` for the pullback of the **one-point sublocale** of `Q`.

The **separate short candidate proof in Appendix 02** proposes to establish, under the geometric hypotheses verified by Appendix 04,

\[
\boxed{G\times G\le R.}\tag{GS}
\]

The lemmas below are the **conditional kernel-pair assembly** given (GS):

\[
\boxed{K=R=\operatorname{EqHull}(H).}\tag{THM}
\]

The proof does not use an image/descent theorem for the full patch counit. The exact special comparison is stated and argued in §8 below.

## 2. Cutoff family

Let `𝒟` consist of **all** Euclidean-compact/closed, component-saturated subsets `D⊂P` meeting `E` in finitely many points. Such `D` is both lower and upper for the fence order: any two comparable points belong to the same comparability component. Therefore `D` determines a *closed* sublocale of the upper locale `X` (and also a spatial locale with the induced upper topology). Let

\[
O_D=X\setminus D
\]

denote its complementary *open* sublocale. Its underlying upper-open subset is saturated under comparability components, so it descends to an open of `Q`.

The strong forcing lemma implies:

**(F1)** Every `O_D`, `D∈𝒟`, contains the generic quotient point `p_∞`: it meets all but finitely many fence components.

**(F2)** Conversely, if a quotient-open `V` contains `p_∞`, its closed saturated complement `D=P\setminus q^{-1}V` meets only finitely many fence components (otherwise forcing would put all `E` into `D`, contradicting `V∩E≠∅`). Thus `D∈𝒟`, and `q^{-1}V=O_D`.

**(F3)** If `V` does not contain `p_∞`, then `q^{-1}V` meets no fence vertex, so every point of this upper-open subset has a singleton comparability component.

All claims are about upper opens/closed sublocales. The exact finite-fence forcing and point construction are supplied separately in the coordinate note.

## 3. Exact characterization of the point fibre

**Lemma 1.**

\[
\boxed{G=\bigwedge_{D\in\mathscr D}O_D.}\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Put `N=⋀_{D∈𝒟}O_D`. By (F1), the pullback of every quotient open belonging to `p_∞` is top on `N`.

Take a quotient open `V` not belonging to `p_∞`, and set `W=q^{-1}V`. By (F3), `W∩E=∅`. Every `x∈W` admits a compact Euclidean closed interval `D_x⊂W` containing `x` in its relative Euclidean interior. As `D_x∩E=∅`, every point of `D_x` is a singleton component, so `D_x` is saturated, lower, and belongs to `𝒟`. Choose a smaller upper-open `W_x` with `x∈W_x⊂D_x`; for instance, a Euclidean-open subinterval in the interior of `D_x`, which is upper because it contains no fence vertices.

Since `N≤O_{D_x}` and `O_{D_x}∧D_x=0`, we have `N∧W_x=0`. The `W_x` form an *open cover* of `W`.

The last step must be made in the **frame of opens of `N`**: if `i:N↪X` denotes the sublocale embedding, its frame inverse image `i^*:O(X)→O(N)` preserves all joins. Thus

\[
i^*(W)=i^*\left(\bigvee_{x\in W}W_x\right)
=\bigvee_{x\in W}i^*(W_x)=0.
\]

Consequently the pullback of each quotient open *not* belonging to `p_∞` is zero on `N`. Together with the preceding positive-open tests, this says that the composite `N→X→Q` has the same frame inverse-image homomorphism as the constant map to the completely prime point `p_∞`. It therefore factors through its one-point sublocale `[p_∞]`. Hence `N≤G`.

Conversely, by (F1), `G` lies in every `O_D`, so `G≤N`. Equality follows. ∎

The point-sub-locale step is essential. In an arbitrary locale, an intersection of positive neighborhoods can be strictly larger than the one-point sublocale; the negative tests above are what supply equality here.

## 4. Kernel-pair rigidity on each cutoff

**Lemma 2.** For every `D∈𝒟`,

\[
\boxed{K\wedge(D\times D)=H\wedge(D\times D).}\tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Write `A⊂D` for the union of the nontrivial comparability components contained in `D`. There are only finitely many, because each such component meets `E` and `D∩E` is finite. Thus `A` is a finite, patch-closed, component-saturated subset, hence a closed sublocale of `D`. Put `O=D\setminus A`, the complementary open sublocale of `D`. Every point of `O` is a singleton comparability component.

We first prove that `q|_O:O→Q` is a **sublocale embedding**, not merely injective on points. Let `W⊂O` be any open of `O`. As `O` is relatively open in `D`, `W` is relatively open in `D` for its upper topology, hence patch-open. Therefore `D\setminus W` is patch-closed and compact. It is also saturated: all nontrivial components lie in `A⊂D\setminus W`, and each point of `O` is a singleton component. Consequently `X\setminus(D\setminus W)` is a saturated upper open, the pullback of some open `V_W∈O(Q)`. The trace of this open on `O` is exactly `W`.

Every open of `O` therefore lies in the image of the frame map `(q|_O)^*:O(Q)→O(O)`. This map is surjective, so `q|_O` is a sublocale embedding. Its localic kernel pair is exactly `Δ_O`.

On the finite sublocale `A`, the kernel pair has ordinary pairs exactly within the same finite component. Distinct components are separated by saturated opens such as `X\setminus C` for a whole finite component `C`. The kernel pair on each equal component is the full product of that component with itself; this is a *finite spatial locale*, and hence is generated by its ordinary pairs. It lies in the restriction of `H`.

The mixed kernel-pair pieces over `A×O` and `O×A` are zero: the saturated quotient-open `X\setminus A` has pullback zero on `A` and top on `O`, which is incompatible with equality of quotient coordinates.

Finally, `A` and `O` are complementary closed/open sublocales of `D`. Their four product rectangles form a **finite sublocale join** equal to `D×D`. Thus every restriction of `K∧D²` is contained in `H`, giving `K∧D²≤H`. The reverse inclusion follows from `H≤K`. ∎

This argument does **not** infer equality of the kernel pair from equality of its ordinary points. The monomorphism on the singleton remainder and spatiality of the finite part are indispensable.

## 5. Pure coframe decomposition

**Lemma 3 (decomposition across complemented cutoffs).** Let `f:Y→X` be *any* localic map. For `𝒟` and `G` as in Lemma 1,

\[
\boxed{
Y=\left(\bigvee_{D\in\mathscr D}f^{-1}D\right)\vee f^{-1}G.
}\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The lattice `Sub(Y)` is a **coframe**: finite joins distribute over arbitrary meets. Pullback of sublocales preserves arbitrary meets, finite joins and complements. Let `B=⋁_{D∈𝒟}f^{-1}D`. By Lemma 1,

\[
\begin{aligned}
B\vee f^{-1}G
&=B\vee\bigwedge_{D\in\mathscr D} f^{-1}O_D\\
&=\bigwedge_{D\in\mathscr D}(B\vee f^{-1}O_D)\\
&=\bigwedge_{D\in\mathscr D}Y=Y,
\end{aligned}
\]

since `B≥f^{-1}D` and `f^{-1}D∨f^{-1}O_D=Y` for each `D`. Importantly, the calculation does **not** pull an infinite join through `f^{-1}`. ∎

## 6. Proof of the theorem

Since `q` coequalizes the projections of `H`, we have `H≤K`. Kernel pairs are transitive, hence `T≤K` and `R≤K`.

Apply Lemma 3 with `Y=K` and `f=ν:K→X` the second projection:

\[
K=\left(\bigvee_{D\in\mathscr D}\nu^{-1}D\right)\vee\nu^{-1}G.
\]

As `D` is the inverse image of a **closed quotient sublocale** (its complementary open `O_D` descends to `Q`), quotient-coordinate equality ensures

\[
\nu^{-1}D=K\wedge(X\times D)=K\wedge(D\times D)\le H\le R
\]

by Lemma 2. Since `G` is the fibre of the **point sublocale** `[p_∞]`, the kernel-pair pullback gives

\[
\nu^{-1}G=K\wedge(X\times G)=G\times G\le R
\]

by `(GS)`. Hence all summands are contained in `R`, so `K≤R`. Together with `R≤K`, this yields `K=R`.

Finally, any localic equivalence relation `S` containing `H` is transitive and therefore contains `H∘H=T` and `T∘T=R=K`. The kernel pair `K` itself is an equivalence relation containing `H`. Thus `EqHull(H)=K=R`. Since `K` is the kernel pair of `q`, it is effective. ∎

## 7. Boundary of these foundations

The preceding assembly is **conditional on `(GS)`** and on the coordinate-level geometry: `(GS)` is a nontrivial separate localic composition theorem, *not* a consequence of the coframe lemma. The proposed new proof is Appendix 02. It uses the special comparison developed below.

Strict inclusions between intermediate powers are not asserted or needed for the proposed theorem.

The proof does not establish associativity for arbitrary localic relations, and does not purport to characterize equivalence hulls on arbitrary locales. Lemma 3, however, is general once its complemented-cutoff description of `G` is available.

**Suggested primary reference for the formal facts:** Picado–Pultr–Tozzi, *II. Locales*, §§2.6, 2.16, 3.7–3.8 (sublocales, coframe structure, pullbacks); `https://www.mat.uc.pt/~picado/publicat/Chapter2.pdf`.

## 8. The special pointless patch-fibre comparison

This is a claim about **this particular fibre**. It does not say that the full patch counit is proper or closed.

**Proposition 8.1.** If a sublocale $S\le X$ contains no ordinary minimal fence-vertex point, the canonical comparison

\[
p^{-1}S\longrightarrow S
\]

is an isomorphism. In particular, the fibre $G$ from §3 satisfies $\Lambda=p^{-1}G\simeq G$, and $\Lambda^2\simeq G^2$ over $X^2$.

**Proposed proof.** Given a Euclidean patch open $O\subseteq P$, define its largest upper-open core

\[
r(O)=\{x:\uparrow x\subseteq O\}
=P\setminus\downarrow(P\setminus O).
\]

The relation is closed and $P\setminus O$ is compact, so $r(O)$ is Euclidean open. The defect $O\setminus r(O)$ consists only of minimal fence vertices with an outgoing comparison to $P\setminus O$. On each compact $C\Subset O$, only finitely many such edges exist: each has length at least $\operatorname{dist}(C,P\setminus O)>0$, whereas all late edges have lengths tending uniformly to zero. Thus this defect is locally finite and relatively closed-discrete in $O$.

For any family of patch opens $(O_i)$, put $W=r(\bigcup_iO_i)$ and $U=\bigcup_i r(O_i)$. The difference $W\setminus U$ is relatively closed and locally finite in $W$, supported on minimal fence vertices: a nonminimal point in $W\setminus U$ must belong to some $O_i$, and then its upper cone is itself, putting it in $r(O_i)$. Every such minimal vertex has finite upper cone, allowing an upper-open neighborhood within $W$ meeting the difference only at that vertex. Hence $W\setminus U$ is a closed discrete **sublocale** of $W$.

Every nonzero sublocale of a discrete locale has an ordinary point: all nuclei on a powerset frame have the form $j(A)=A\cup j(\varnothing)$. If $\lambda:\mathcal O(X)\to\mathcal O(S)$ denotes restriction, $S\wedge(W\setminus U)=0$ follows from the hypothesis. Therefore

\[
\lambda r(\bigcup_iO_i)=\lambda(\bigcup_i r(O_i))
=\bigvee_i\lambda r(O_i).
\]

The operation $r$ preserves finite meets, so $\lambda r:\mathcal O(P_{\mathrm{patch}})\to\mathcal O(S)$ is a frame homomorphism. Its restriction to upper opens is $\lambda$, providing a section of the canonical comparison. Furthermore, the original patch defect $O\setminus r(O)$ is likewise locally discrete and supported on forbidden minimal vertices, so it has zero meet with $p^{-1}S$. Thus any patch open has the same restriction to $p^{-1}S$ as an upper open, proving surjectivity on frames; the section gives injectivity. ∎

For $S=G$, the complement of the finite component containing each ordinary minimal vertex is a **positive** generic quotient-open neighborhood omitting that vertex. Thus $G$ contains none of them, and Proposition 8.1 applies.

**Audit warning.** The step from relative local finiteness of these point sets to a discrete *sublocale* is precisely where a naïve point-set argument could fail. The referee should examine that step, the claimed vanishing of intersections with $S$, and the behavior under $p^{-1}$ independently.
