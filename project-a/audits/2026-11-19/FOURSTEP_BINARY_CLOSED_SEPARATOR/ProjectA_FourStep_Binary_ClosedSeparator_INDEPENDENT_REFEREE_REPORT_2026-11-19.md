# Independent blind referee report: binary-graph four-step effectiveness

**Packet:** `ProjectA_FourStep_Binary_ClosedSeparator_BLIND_AUDIT_2026-11-19.zip`  
**Date:** 19 November 2026 (packet date)  
**Scope:** The four documents in the packet, including their auxiliary assertions. No prior referee reports were consulted.

## Verdict

**Valid with specific repairs and clarifications, subject to one substantive lemma being supplied in full.** I found no counterexample to the proposed four-step equality. The shortened closed-separator argument is convincing once the special patch-fibre comparison is established. However, Proposition 8.1, as printed, passes too quickly from a defect of an arbitrary *union* of patch opens to a locally finite discrete sublocale. The preceding single-open argument does not, by itself, justify that passage. I give the missing compactness argument below; with it, the step appears sound. The localic discreteness and frame-map consequences should be written out explicitly in the definitive proof.

The conclusion is **not** that the result follows from density of ordinary point pairs alone. The actual-pullback arguments in Lemmas 4–5 and the fit/closed-separator argument do essential work.

## 1. Main repair: the arbitrary-union defect in Proposition 8.1

For a family of patch opens `O_i`, write `V=⋃_i O_i`, `W=r(V)`, `U=⋃_i r(O_i)`. The proof asserts that `W\U` is relatively locally finite in `W`. The earlier observation that `O\r(O)` is locally finite in `O` does **not** directly prove this: there is no uniform separation distance from the complements of the individual `O_i` as `i` varies.

Here is a suitable independent argument. Fix `x∈W`. Choose a relatively compact Euclidean-open neighborhood `N` of `x` such that `cl(N)⊂W`. The compact set `cl(N)` is covered by the `O_i`, so choose a finite subcover and a Lebesgue number `δ>0` for this finite cover, in the ambient Euclidean metric restricted to `cl(N)`. Shrink to `N'` with `x∈N'` and `cl(N')⊂N`. For all sufficiently late fences, every strict edge has length smaller than both `δ` and the distance from `cl(N')` to the complement of `N`. If a minimal vertex `v∈N'` is the source of such an edge, its entire (at most three-point) upper cone lies in `cl(N)` and has diameter smaller than `δ` (adjust the bound by a fixed factor if necessary). Therefore the upper cone is contained in **one** member `O_i` of the finite cover, so `v∈r(O_i)⊂U`. Nonminimal points belong to `U` whenever they belong to `W`. Consequently `N'∩(W\U)` consists of minimal vertices from **finitely many early fences**.

Thus `W\U` is relatively locally finite in the Euclidean topology, and it is relatively closed because `U` is upper-open in `W`. To obtain the *localic* conclusion, note that a minimal vertex `v` has a finite upper cone. An ambient Euclidean neighborhood of that cone can be chosen to avoid every other point of `W\U` locally; its upper core is an upper-open neighborhood of `v` whose intersection with `W\U` is `{v}`. Hence the closed complement sublocale `W\U` has the discrete induced upper topology, not merely a discrete Euclidean underlying set. It is a spatial discrete locale. Any sublocale of it is again spatial and, if nonzero, has an ordinary point. The assumption on `S` then indeed implies `S∧(W\U)=0`.

This is the most important repair. In particular, it should be inserted *before* the assertion that `λ∘r` preserves arbitrary joins. It also makes clear that the proof needs the uniform shrinking of **all** strict-edge diameters, not merely finiteness of each individual fence.

## 2. The frame-isomorphism step in Proposition 8.1

Once the defect lemma is proved, `λr` is a frame homomorphism: `r` preserves finite intersections, and its failure to commute with arbitrary unions is concentrated in the just-verified discrete defect, which has zero intersection with `S`. Since `r(V)=V` on upper opens, this is a retraction of the frame inclusion induced by the patch map.

For completeness, the last surjectivity claim should be expressed using the pullback `Λ_S=P_patch×_X S`. Every patch open `O` has the same restriction to `Λ_S` as the upper open `r(O)`, because `O\r(O)` is another discrete minimal-vertex defect whose inverse image on `Λ_S` is zero. Patch opens generate the frame of `Λ_S`; hence the comparison `Λ_S→S` induces a surjection on frames. The retraction supplies injectivity. This proves the isomorphism without invoking general properness of `p`.

The phrase “`S` contains no ordinary minimal fence-vertex point” should explicitly mean that no such point map factors through `S`. Otherwise, it might be mistaken for a statement about the point-set trace of an arbitrary nonspatial sublocale. The discrete-defect argument is what permits the stronger localic conclusion.

## 3. Other arguments checked

1. **Explicit geometry and forcing (04, §§1–5).** The two endpoint sets are disjoint and individually dense. The rational selection bands are nonempty and disjoint; their exclusions are nowhere dense plus finite. Interior vertices avoid other components. The edge-length estimate tends uniformly to zero, establishing a closed height-one order. Every late fence traverses its gap densely, and saturation plus closedness propagates infinitely many fences of either colour to *all* fences. This supplies the quantifiers needed for the generic quotient-open tests. The filter meeting `E` is completely prime, and it is distinct from any ordinary component point.
2. **Exact fibre (03, §3).** Positive tests yield `G≤⋀O_D`. Negative tests are not dispensable, but they are correctly supplied: a quotient open missing `E` is covered by relatively small upper-open singleton neighborhoods each killed on the meet `N`. The inverse-image frame of `N` preserves arbitrary joins, proving the required vanishing and hence `N≤G`.
3. **Finite-cutoff rigidity (03, §4).** On the singleton remainder `O`, every relative upper open extends to a saturated upper open of `X`; the key compactness claim for `D\W` follows because `D` is compact and `W` is relatively open. Thus `q|_O` is a sublocale embedding. The finite nontrivial component part is spatial. Mixed kernel-pair terms vanish by a quotient open separating `A` from `O`. The finite open/closed rectangle decomposition is valid at the sublocale level.
4. **Coframe assembly (03, §§5–6).** The displayed calculation uses the correct finite-join/arbitrary-meet distributivity of sublocales. It does not distribute an inverse image over an infinite join. On `K`, the second-coordinate cutoff restricts both coordinates, and the generic-point restriction is `G×G`.
5. **Closed-support and colour arguments (02, §II).** Least closed support gives the required density, and a compact first-coordinate projection with finitely many vertices is annihilated by a positive generic open. Point-coordinate sections are nowhere dense, because the fibre has no ordinary patch points. The two pairs of closed colour closures cover `C_0`; their four intersections cover it, so the Baire step is legitimate. Local infinitude follows from removal of finitely many nowhere-dense coordinate sections.
6. **Binary fusion (02, §III).** Two disjoint child rectangles in each coordinate suffice; no antichain or component-registration condition is used. Descendant anchors along zero tails stay in every subsequent rectangle, ensuring the selected-colour density, and the unique-limit argument gives a graph with both coordinate projections injective.
7. **Dense strips and actual composition (02, §IV).** Every nonempty open in `D` contains selected vertices of unbounded fence index, since `D` is perfect and the selected trace is dense. The edge traversal supplies dense *ordinary* pairs in the two patch strips. Inclusion of their Booleanizations in the patch pullback of `H` is then a genuine sublocale statement, not an equality inferred from point pairs. Pullback along the open-surjective coordinate projections preserves density; their common Booleanization factors through the **actual** composability pullback. The same argument on `Γ×M` proves the graph witness for the balanced second composition. No associativity claim is needed.
8. **Closed separator (02, §V).** On the compact regular patch square, fit supplies a patch-open `O` containing `\widehat R` but not `F_∞`. Its closed complement `C` annihilates `\widehat R` and meets `F_∞` nontrivially; these follow by the standard finite complement identities for open/closed sublocales. The resulting Cantor graph need only lie in the ordinary closed support `C`, not in `F_∞`. This is exactly enough to contradict `\mathcal B(Γ)≤\widehat R`.
9. **Nontriviality and final assembly (02, §VI).** On `M`, every negative generic quotient open has empty trace, and every positive one has dense-open trace. Its double-negation sublocale therefore maps constantly to `p_∞`, yielding `0≠\mathcal B(M)≤Λ`. Reflexivity of `H` follows from the spatial diagonal; `H≤T≤R≤K` is valid with the prescribed bracketing. The coframe decomposition completes the equality and the equivalence-hull conclusion.

## 4. Smaller clarifications and presentation suggestions

- In Lemma 3, specify that each inherited anchor lies in the interiors of **all** rectangles along its zero-descendant chain. This follows from the construction but is the precise reason the selected-colour vertex survives in `Γ`.
- In Lemma 4, make the product-projection maps and their domains explicit (patch locales first, then maps into the upper locales). This will prevent a reader from confusing patch products with localic relation pullbacks.
- The double-negation sublocale of a nonempty compact Hausdorff Cantor space is nonzero. It is worth recording that this relies on nonemptiness of the locale, not on the existence of a Booleanization point.
- In 03 §4, the sentence “`D\W` is patch-closed and compact” should explicitly say *relative to compact patch-closed `D`*. In 03 §8, distinguish `r(O)` as a patch-open subset which is also upper-open from an abstract right adjoint on frames (the latter need not preserve arbitrary joins).
- Since 03 §8 is indispensable for transporting the patch-square inclusion back to `G×G`, I would state its repaired defect lemma as a standalone lemma with the exact edge-shrinking hypothesis and place it before Proposition 8.1.

## Final assessment

The two substantive innovations of this candidate—the **binary fusion without antichains** and the **closed-separator contradiction without a global join-of-graphs theorem**—survive my checks. The proof of the generic square is not dependent on a fictitious graph factorization through the generic fibre. The remaining work is principally to strengthen the patch comparison exposition as above, particularly for the defect of arbitrary unions and for the passage to a discrete *sublocale*. With that repair written into the manuscript, I would regard the four-step theorem as proved on the packet's stated hypotheses.
