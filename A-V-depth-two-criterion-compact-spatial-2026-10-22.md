# Depth-two criterion and compact spatial collapse

Let L be a frame and S be the lower closure of D union B, where D consists of pairs (a,b) with a meet b=0. For a in L define V(a)=join{b:(a,b) in S}; for b define H(b)=join{a:(a,b) in S}. An H-then-V separation tree of depth at most two exists precisely when join{a:V(a)=1}=1. A V-then-H tree exists precisely when join{b:H(b)=1}=1. Proof: one-step vertical covers of (a,1) are equivalent to V(a)=1; horizontal root then covers by all good a. The symmetric claim is identical. This gives an algebraic obstruction test, not by itself a counterexample.

Theorem. If X is a compact spatial locale, every rectangular bar B in O(X) admits an H-then-V depth-two separation tree. Let T=O(X) coproduct O(X), and bar condition say join of D rectangles and B rectangles is 1. For each point x of X, pull the equality back along {x} times X, obtaining a cover of X by second-coordinate opens b_i from rectangles (a_i,b_i) in D union B with x in a_i. Compactness yields finitely many such rectangles with join b_i=1. Their first coordinates have intersection a_x containing x. Thus (a_x,1) has a vertical split into the finitely many leaves (a_x,b_i), each under D or B. The a_x cover X because every point belongs to its own a_x; spatiality implies their join is 1. Horizontal root followed by these vertical splits gives depth two. No regularity or spatiality of the localic square is used.

Consequence. If a strict depth-two bar counterexample exists, its locale is necessarily nonregular, noncompact-or-nonspatial, and lacks a join-dense completely join-prime basis (in view of other collapse results). None has yet been constructed. Compactness alone for arbitrary pointless locales is NOT claimed by this argument.
