# Project A: a countable almost-normal locale with NONCLOSED V

23 October 2026. **New working construction and theorem.** The central counterexample is proved with an explicit depth-3 localic bar and ordinary specialization points; it does not depend on product spatiality. The further exact-sublocale transfer statements use a spatial/scattered lemma and should receive focused audit before promotion.

This note settles the previously open implication
    almost normal L ⇒ V(L) closed?
**NEGATIVELY.**

In fact it gives a countable noncompact almost-normal T0 space X with
    I(X) ⊊ V(X) ⊊ U(X)=F(X),
    V(X) nonclosed,
and, with the scattered-sublocale lemma below, V(X)=V_3(X) != V_2(X)=U(X).

## 1. The universal finite-poset thickening

Start with ANY finite poset P. Write omega^op for the descending chain 0>1>2>..., and let
    B=P×omega^op,
    (p,n)≤(q,m) iff p≤_P q and n≥m.
Every principal upset ↑_B(p,n) is finite.

Let S={s_t:t∈Q_rat} be a countable set of isolated points. For each b=(p,n)∈B choose a DISTINCT irrational number α_b∈R\Q so that, for each fixed p∈P, the set {α_(p,n):n≥N} is dense in R for every N. Such a choice exists: choose distinct irrational cosets α_p+Q and enumerate each coset as n ranges over omega.

For each irrational α, let G_α be the filter on Q_rat generated by
    (J∩Q_rat)\N,
where J⊆R is an open interval containing α and N⊆Q_rat is nowhere dense as a subset of R.

Let X(P)=B ⊔ S as a SET, with opens A⊆X(P) determined by
    H_A=A∩B is an UPSET in B;
    T_A={t:s_t∈A} belongs to G_(α_b) for every b∈H_A.
(No constraint on T_A if H_A is empty.)

These conditions define a topology: unions preserve upward-closedness and filter membership for every boundary element contained in at least one summand; finite intersections use finite-meet closure of each G_α. The point s_t is open. Moreover {s_t} is CLOPEN: Q_rat\{t} is in each G_α since α irrational and a sufficiently short interval around α misses t. Therefore S is an open discrete subspace and B is a closed subspace.

The induced topology on B is precisely the UPSET/Alexandrov topology: any H∈Up(B) extends to the open H∪S of X(P).

For b∈B, basic neighborhoods are
    N(b;T)=↑_B b ⊔ S[T],
where T contains a finite union of intervals in Q_rat, one around each distinct attachment coordinate α_c for c∈↑_B b. Such neighborhoods form a base: because ↑b is finite, finitely many filter witnesses can be combined.

## 2. Almost-normality: every rather-below pair has a clopen endpoint

For T⊆Q_rat put
    Sigma(T)=cl_R(int_R(cl_R T)).
The elementary filter test is
    T meets every member of G_α ⇔ α∈Sigma(T).
Reason: if α is outside the closure of the interior of cl(T), choose J around α such that T∩J is nowhere dense, and remove it from J; conversely if α∈Sigma(T), every J around α contains an interval on which T is dense, so a nowhere-dense deletion cannot avoid T.

For any open A⊆X(P) and b∈B, the closure formula is
    b∈cl_X A
    iff [∃c∈↑b∩H_A] OR [∃c∈↑b with α_c∈Sigma(T_A)].
One direction follows from upward neighborhood traces. For the converse, ↑b is FINITE, so if no c lies in H_A and no α_c lies in Sigma(T_A), combine finitely many rational neighborhoods avoiding T_A into a neighborhood N(b;T') avoiding A.

In particular:
- If Sigma(T_A)=∅, then H_A must be empty (a trace in G_α is somewhere dense); the closure formula shows cl A=A, so A is clopen.
- If Sigma(T_A)≠∅, choose a nonempty real interval J⊆int(cl T_A). For each p∈P and each N, some n≥N has α_(p,n)∈J; hence (p,n)∈cl A for arbitrarily large n. If A≺C, i.e. cl A⊆C, then C contains such (p,n) for every p, and its upset property forces ALL (p,k)∈C. Thus C contains all B; its complement is contained in the open discrete S, so C is clopen.

Consequently, if A≺C, either A is clopen or C is clopen. In the former case choose interpolant A; in the latter case choose interpolant C. Clopen opens satisfy a≺a. Therefore the rather-below relation on O(X(P)) INTERPOLATES: **X(P) is almost normal**.

The space is noncompact: fix a minimal p∈P and A=↓_B(p,0)={ (p,n):n∈omega }. The open cover {X\A}∪{N((p,n);T_n):n∈omega} of X has no finite subcover, because the latter opens meet A only in finite prefixes.

## 3. Exact inseparability on the boundary

Write N_P={(p,q):∃r≥p,q in P}, the common-upper-bound relation, and R_P=↑_{P×P} N_P, its coordinatewise upward saturation. Let ~ denote the specialization order in X(P).

For b=(p,n),c=(q,m) in B:
    (b,c)∈pt(I_X) ⇔ (p,q)∈N_P.
If p,q have a common upper r, then (r,0)≥b,c and every pair of open neighborhoods intersects there. Conversely, if p,q have no common upper, ↑b and ↑c are disjoint finite boundary sets with distinct α coordinates. Choose their finitely many rational interval traces disjoint to obtain disjoint open neighborhoods of b,c in X.

All isolated singletons are clopen, so the only other inseparable pairs are diagonal pairs (s_t,s_t); no mixed B×S or S×B pairs are inseparable.

## 4. Uniform rank-three exclusion bar: pointwise V is the thickened R_P

For ANY b,c∈B with (b,c)∉↑pt(I), let A_b=↓_B b and A_c=↓_B c. The nonmembership means:
    for every a∈A_b and d∈A_c, ↑_B a∩↑_B d=∅.

Both A_b and A_c are closed subsets of X. Put e_b=X\A_b and e_c=X\A_c and
    w_(b,c)=(e_b⊠1)∨(1⊠e_c).

We give a frame-coproduct separation tree proving w_(b,c)∨d_I=1 (NO product spatiality used).

Root H-cover: X=e_b∪⋃_{a∈A_b}U_a, where
    U_a=↑_B a ∪ S[T_a]
is a basic open with T_a a finite union of rational intervals about α_h, h∈↑a. (Each a belongs to U_a.) Node (e_b,1) is a w-leaf.

Under (U_a,1), V-cover X=e_c∪⋃_{d∈A_c}V_(a,d), where
    V_(a,d)=↑_B d ∪ S[T'_(a,d)]
is basic open and the finitely many intervals making T'_(a,d) have REAL CLOSURES avoiding every α_h for h∈↑a. This is possible because ↑a∩↑d=∅ and the entire α family is pairwise distinct. Node (U_a,e_c) is a w-leaf.

Under (U_a,V_(a,d)), H-split U_a=H_(a,d)∪E_(a,d) with
    H_(a,d) = ↑a ∪ S[T_a\cl_R(T'_(a,d))],
    E_(a,d) = S[T_a∩cl_R(T'_(a,d))].
H_(a,d) is OPEN: each α_h for h∈↑a lies outside cl_R T' and T_a contains an interval about α_h; hence the remaining rational trace still satisfies every required filter. Also H_(a,d)∩V_(a,d)=∅: the boundary upsets are disjoint and the isolated traces are disjoint. Thus (H_(a,d),V_(a,d)) is a D-leaf. E_(a,d)⊆S⊆e_b, so (E_(a,d),V_(a,d)) is a w-leaf. This is an exact H-V-H rank≤3 tree.

The point (b,c) evaluates w_(b,c) to 0, so is NOT in pt(V). Conversely if (b,c) lies in ↑pt(I), every bar contains its inseparable predecessor and hence (b,c). Therefore, on B×B,
    pt(V_X)|_(B×B)=lift(R_P)
where lift(R_P) includes all coordinate indices n,m.
Every offdiagonal/mixed pair involving s_t is excluded by a rank≤2 clopen singleton strip, as in prior A-V-Claude-almost-normal-example-exact-V-equals-I-2026-10-23.md. So
    pt(V_X)=lift(R_P)∪Δ_S,
    pt(I_X)=lift(N_P)∪Δ_S.
In fact ALL off-V ordinary points have rank≤3 bar exclusion witnesses.

## 5. Concrete five-point counterexample to the almost-normal closedness implication

Choose P={p,q,r,u,v} with only strict order
    p<u, r<u, r<v, q<v.
Put b=(p,0), c=(q,0), B_1=(u,0), B_2=(v,0), z=(r,0).

By the preceding:
    (b,c)∉pt(V) because p and q (both minimal) have no common upper and no inseparable predecessor pair. An explicit H-V-H bar w_(b,c) excludes (b,c).
But
    (z,z)∈pt(Δ)⊆pt(I)⊆pt(V),
    (z,z)≤(B_1,B_2),
so (B_1,B_2) lies in EVERY bar and hence pt(V).
Yet
    (b,c)≤(B_1,B_2)
because p≤u and q≤v. The point set of any CLOSED sublocale is a closed, specialization-DOWNward subset of the locale's point space. Here pt(V) contains (B_1,B_2) but not (b,c). Thus
    **V(X(P)) is NOT CLOSED.**

Furthermore (B_1,B_2)∉pt(I): ↑B_1 and ↑B_2 are disjoint and their finite irrational attachment coordinates admit disjoint rational traces. Thus I⊊V. By the PREVIOUSLY EXTERNALLY AUDITED theorem AN1, almost-normality yields V⊆V_2=U=F; since U is closed and V is not, V⊊U. Therefore the example has
    I⊊V⊊U=F,  V nonclosed,
as claimed.

This conclusion is FULLY LOCALIC and does not need a spatiality assumption on Q: the pair in V lies above a diagonal point, the pair outside V is excluded by an actual bar, and closed sublocale point sets are specialization-downward.

## 6. Strengthening: spatial/scattered square and equality V=V3

The following extra conclusions are useful but not necessary for §5's negative result.

The boundary B=P×omega^op is a locally compact sober Alexandrov space. Local compactness: each principal ↑b is finite, open, compact. Sobriety: if A⊆B is a nonempty irreducible closed set, it is a directed lower set (usual Alexandrov criterion). Choose the minimum index n appearing in A, and a maximal p among points (p,n) in A. Directedness forces (p,n) to be the greatest point of A, hence A=↓(p,n). Thus each irreducible closed A has a unique generic point.

X is sober: every s_t is clopen, so an irreducible closed subset meeting S must be the singleton {s_t}; otherwise it is contained in B and has a generic point there.

The localic Q=O(X)⊕O(X) is spatial: the open sublocale O(S⊠S) is discrete spatial. Its complementary closed sublocale C(S⊠S) is the join C(S⊠1)∨C(1⊠S), respectively B×_loc X and X×_loc B. Both products are spatial by local compactness of B and sobriety of B,X. Finite joins of spatial sublocales are spatial, because the frame of their join embeds into the product of their quotient frames (join corresponds to pointwise meet of the nuclei). Hence Q spatial and Q≅O(X×_top X).

The space X is scattered: if a nonempty subset meets S take an isolated s_t; otherwise choose an element of smallest index n in its B-part and, among elements with that n, a maximal P-coordinate; an open principal-upset-type neighborhood isolates that element from the subset. Any finite ordinary product of scattered spaces is scattered by iterated projection/fiber isolation.

Every sublocale of a scattered spatial locale is spatial. Quick proof: a nonzero sublocale R is dense in its nonempty closed closure C(F), which has an isolated ordinary point p∈F; the corresponding nonzero open singleton in C(F) meets R, yielding a point of R. Applying this to nonzero locally closed sublocales of R shows any two opens in R are distinguished by a point.

Consequently every sublocale of Q is point-determined, including V and V3. The rank≤3 exclusion bars in §4 and the singleton strips show pt(V3)=pt(V), so
    V=V3
for EVERY X(P).

A further precise transfer: V is closed for X(P) iff R_P is downward closed, equivalently iff R_P=∪_i P_i×P_i (P_i comparability components). Necessity: if R_P not downward closed, the analogous point witness shows pt(V) not downward closed. Sufficiency: when R_P=∪P_i², the subset lift(R_P)∪Δ_S is closed in X×X. Its complement is open by case analysis: distinct isolated points have singleton product neighborhoods; mixed S/B pairs have neighborhoods separating off the isolated singleton; and cross-component boundary pairs admit rational-trace-disjoint basic opens with boundary parts in their separate components. Spatiality of V then identifies it with the closed sublocale supported on this set.

Similarly I=V for X(P) iff N_P=R_P, equivalently iff each comparability component is upward directed (the earlier finite Alexandrov classification). So the SAME full trichotomy as in the finite poset occurs within NONCOMPACT ALMOST-NORMAL spaces:
  - I=V closed, when components upward directed;
  - I⊊V closed, when R_P clopen but N_P proper (bottom fork);
  - I⊊V nonclosed, when R_P not downclosed (zigzag).
