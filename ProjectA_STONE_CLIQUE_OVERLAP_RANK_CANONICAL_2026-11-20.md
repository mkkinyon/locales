### 1. Localic relational calculus and Isbell's intersection

Composition R∘S means the image of the **actual** localic composability pullback R×_G S, traversing R first. A length-k word is fully parenthesized. For a reflexive R, define r_exists(R) as the minimum length of a word attaining the specified kernel pair and r_all(R) as the minimum length at which every bracketing attains it. Neither definition assumes associativity.

**Rectangular lemma.** For sublocales A,B,C,E of G,
\[
(A\times B)\circ(C\times E)=
\begin{cases}A\times E,&B\wedge C\ne0,\\0,&B\wedge C=0.\end{cases}\tag{1}
\]
The actual path locale is A×(B∧C)×E. If B∧C is nonzero, its map to the terminal locale is an **open surjection** in classical Loc (classical overtness). Pullback along A×E preserves this open surjection, giving the full-image formula. This does not use stability of arbitrary surjections.

In the **compact-Hausdorff finite rectangular calculus used here**, composition preserves **finite** joins on either side: the required projection/product stability and genuine localic image operations justify the finite expansion. This does **not** assert such distributivity for arbitrary triples of localic relations outside the verified setting, nor does it justify infinite-join exchange. Thus the subalgebra generated by a finite collection of rectangles and the diagonal (a two-sided unit) is associative. This statement does not apply to arbitrary localic relations.

For Cantor space C, take the induced spatial sublocales Q (countable dense, e.g. eventually-zero sequences) and I (dense complement). Isbell's density theorem and spatial exhaustion give
\[
0\ne\mathcal B(C)\le Q\wedge I,\qquad Q\vee I=C.\tag{2}
\]
Although Q and I have no common **ordinary points**, their localic meet is nonzero. They are not complemented sublocales.

### 2. The Stone chains and two exact formulas

For n≥0 let
\[
G_n=\coprod_{i=0}^n(C_i\amalg\{a_i\}\amalg\{b_i\}),\tag{3}
\]
where C_i are Cantor copies carrying Q_i,I_i as above. G_n and G_n² are spatial Stone locales. Write A↔B=(A×B)∨(B×A), with singleton braces suppressed in the formulas below. Define
\[
D_n=\Delta_{G_n}\vee\bigvee_i(a_i\leftrightarrow Q_i)\vee
\bigvee_i(I_i\leftrightarrow b_i)\vee
\bigvee_{i<n}(b_i\leftrightarrow a_{i+1}),\tag{4}
\]
\[
\widetilde D_n=D_n\vee\bigvee_{i<n}(I_i\leftrightarrow a_{i+1}).\tag{5}
\]
These are finite joins of spatial point-generated rectangles with a singleton factor and the spatial diagonal. Both are symmetric, reflexive, point-generated. Ordinary graph component diameters are at most 3 and 2, respectively.

**Theorem A (blind-refereed).** Each relation has terminal component quotient and kernel pair G_n²; all bracketings of any fixed length agree, and
\[
\boxed{r_{\exists}(D_n)=r_{\forall}(D_n)=3n+4,\qquad
r_{\exists}(\widetilde D_n)=r_{\forall}(\widetilde D_n)=2n+4.}\tag{6}
\]

*Proof checkpoints.* A coequalizing map is constant on Q_i with value at a_i and on I_i with value at b_i. Their nonzero overlap forces the two constants equal; Q_i∨I_i=C_i makes the map constant on C_i; bridge edges identify constants between i's. Thus the coequalizer is 1. The upper bounds use full rectangles: a_i×b_i appears in two steps through Q_i∧I_i; the added I_i→a_{i+1} gives a_i×a_{i+1} in two steps. The weighted hub routes yield 3n+4 and 2n+4, respectively, and the finite join of support rectangles is G_n². For lower bounds choose an ordinary x∈Q_0 and y∈I_n. A primitive rectangular chain reaching (x,y) must begin Q_0→a_0 and end b_n→I_n. For D_n use heights
\[
h(a_i)=3i,\quad h(Q_i)=h(I_i)=3i+1,\quad h(b_i)=3i+2.
\]
For the modified relation use heights 2i,2i+1,2i+2. Every primitive step changes height by ≤1 and nonzero intersecting consecutive supports have matching heights. Forced first and last factors give at least 3n+4 or 2n+4. Finite-join pullback along a point justifies exclusion from the **localic** image. See the preserved candidate for all endpoint costs.

### 3. Point-generated ordinary cliques — do not substitute whole products

Let E_0,…,E_{n+1} be the ordinary graph components of D_n, indexed in path order. Define induced spatial sublocales
\[
U_j=[E_j]_{G_n},\qquad
P_j=[E_j\times E_j]_{G_n^2},\qquad
H_n=\bigvee_{j=0}^{n+1}P_j.\tag{7}
\]
The point sets of E_j are disjoint; the ordinary graph of H_n is a disjoint union of **cliques**, so its ordinary component diameters are ≤1. Each U_j is spatial (its subspace of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff and sober). **Only** P_j≤U_j×U_j is asserted: P_j is a point-generated clique, not necessarily the entire localic support product.

The overlap graph of the U_j is the path:
\[
U_j\wedge U_{j+1}\ne0,\qquad U_i\wedge U_j=0\ \ (|i-j|\ge2).\tag{8}
\]
Adjacent intersections are nonzero and pointless, arising from Q_i∧I_i.

### 4. Exact diameter-one rank from referee's addendum

**Theorem B (referee-origin addendum, internally checked).**
\[
\boxed{r_{\exists}(H_n)=r_{\forall}(H_n)=n+2\quad(n\ge0).}\tag{9}
\]

*Upper bound for every parenthesization.* Given indices j_1,…,j_k such that U_{j_t}∧U_{j_{t+1}}≠0, induct on any binary tree to show that P_{j_1}∘…∘P_{j_k} contains **every ordinary point pair** (x,y) with x∈E_{j_1}, y∈E_{j_k}. At a root split after t, the left child contains all (x,p) with p∈E_{j_t}; because {x}×U_{j_t} is spatial and generated by those point pairs, this entire one-point-legged rectangle is contained in the left child. The right child similarly contains U_{j_{t+1}}×{y}. The rectangular lemma and the nonzero overlap yield {(x,y)} in their composite. A shortest index path has ≤n+2 vertices; pad by repeating indices. Every point pair of G_n² occurs in every n+2-word, and G_n² is spatial.

*Lower bound for every parenthesization.* Expand a word in H_n=∨P_j via **finite** join distribution. A nonzero indexed leaf word necessarily has U_{j_t}∧U_{j_{t+1}}≠0 for every adjacent pair: consider their lowest common ancestor in the binary tree and the endpoint supports of its two children. A summand containing the ordinary pair (a_0,b_n) must begin at j=0 and end at j=n+1 (private point supports). The path takes ≥n+1 overlap edges, hence ≥n+2 leaves. Pullback of finite joins along the point prevents it from entering any shorter word.

Theorem B says **every bracketing reaches the kernel pair at exactly this minimum length**; it does **not** assert associativity of intermediate composites.

### 5. The referee's general point-clique overlap lemma

Assume G² is spatial and U_1,…,U_m≤G are nonzero **spatial** sublocales whose ordinary points jointly cover those of G. Let
\[
P_i=\bigvee_{x,y\in\mathrm{pt}(U_i)}\{(x,y)\},\qquad H=\bigvee_iP_i.
\]
The **localic overlap graph** Λ has edge i-j iff U_i∧U_j≠0. If Λ is connected with diameter d, then **every** (d+1)-factor word in H equals G². If Λ has diameter-realizing vertices s,t with **private ordinary points** x∈U_s and y∈U_t that lie in no other U_i, then **no** k<d+1 word equals G²; both ranks equal d+1. The proof is exactly the all-tree one-point-legged induction and the adjacent-overlap lower bound in §4. No spatiality of U_i×U_j is used.

### 6. Finite connected graph realization (post-referee, not blind-audited)

For any nonempty finite connected graph Λ, take one clopen Cantor block C_e for each edge e={v,w}; assign Q_e to v and I_e to w, orienting edges arbitrarily. Add a private isolated point a_v for each vertex v. Let G_Λ be the finite coproduct of these blocks and points, and let E_v consist of a_v and all assigned ordinary parts on incident edges. The E_v partition the ordinary points, each U_v=[E_v] is spatial, and clopen blockwise checking plus Isbell density show
\[
U_v\wedge U_w\ne0\iff\{v,w\}\in E(\Lambda)\qquad(v\ne w).
\]
Set H_Λ=∨_v[E_v×E_v] in G_Λ². The ordinary components are cliques; the private a_v supply the lower-bound hypothesis. Hence
\[
\boxed{r_{\exists}(H_\Lambda)=r_{\forall}(H_\Lambda)=\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda)+1.}\tag{10}
\]
Its component quotient is terminal because a finite word is G_Λ². The single-vertex case has rank 1. **This realization was deduced after the referee's addendum, not submitted for that review.**

### 7. Exact pointwise filtration (post-referee, not blind-audited)

For any length k≥1 and any fully parenthesized k-factor word W_k of H_Λ,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{pt}(W_k)=
\bigcup_{\operatorname{dist}_{\Lambda}(v,w)\le k-1}E_v\times E_w.}\tag{11}
\]
For H_n, substitute |i-j|≤k−1. The forward implication expands into finite component-clique words and uses necessary adjacent overlaps. The reverse follows from the binary-tree induction, choosing a shortest overlap path and padding repeated indices. Therefore every bracketing at a fixed length has the same **ordinary points and spatialization**, but equality of its full localic value is **not asserted**.

### 8. Independent nonassociative Stone gadget and attachment (blind-refereed)

Let X=ℕ∪{*} be the one-point compactification, Y Cantor space, Q⊂Y countable dense, I=Y\Q dense, and f:ℕ→Q an enumeration. Put G_*=X⊔Y⊔{b,r}. Let Γ_f be the spatial graph of f in X×Y. Set
\[
D_*=\Delta_{G_*}\vee(X\times X)\vee\Gamma_f\vee\Gamma_f^\smile
\vee(I\leftrightarrow b)\vee(b\leftrightarrow r)
\vee\{(q_0,i_0),(i_0,q_0)\},\tag{12}
\]
where q_0∈Q and i_0∈I. The **last singleton bridge** repairs the former unmodified gadget by connecting its ordinary graph.

With A=D_*∘D_*, the restricted genuine two-step graph pullback ending at * gives Q×{*}≤A; composing b×I with it uses I∧Q≠0, while the actual b→X two-step pullback is ℕ×_Y I=0. Hence
\[
(D_*\circ A)\wedge(\{b\}\times\{*\})=\{(b,*)\},\quad
(A\circ D_*)\wedge(\{b\}\times\{*\})=0.\tag{13}
\]
The ordinary graph is connected of diameter ≤6, so every sixfold parenthesization is G_*² by the ordinary-point path lemma and spatiality of G_*². **No exact minimal rank for D_* is claimed.**

Attach this gadget at r to the diameter-two chain point a_0, with a single symmetric ordinary edge. The resulting F_n on T_n=G_n⊔G_* is point-generated, reflexive, symmetric, **nonassociative**, has terminal component quotient, and ordinary component diameter at most **8**, attained in the attached component. The collapse retraction T_n→G_n and laxity of relational direct image yield the lower bound; a triple of m-fold full-block words, m=max(2n+4,6), gives the upper:
\[
\boxed{2n+4\le r_{\exists}(F_n)\le3\max(2n+4,6).}\tag{14}
\]
This verifies unbounded rank alongside nonassociativity. The rank r_all(F_n) and exact minimal rank remain **uncomputed**. The **unmodified** earlier gadget's claimed six-step exact result is not a dependency and remains unverified: the former argument incorrectly enlarged a graph image to a product of spatial sublocales without proving that image equality.

### 9. Historical question and scope (clique part subsequently settled)

**Open:** For a point-generated clique relation H_Λ on a Stone locale with finite spatial overlap supports, must *all* bracketings of H_Λ of the same length agree as **localic sublocales**? They have identical ordinary point sets (§7) and all equal the kernel pair by diameter(Λ)+1, but could have distinct pointless intermediate parts. The first serious test is the two three-factor bracketings on a 3- or 4-vertex overlap path. A negative answer would be an associativity defect invisible to all ordinary pairs.

No theorem here asserts general associativity of localic relational composition, infinitary join interchange, a constructive version of the rectangle lemma, or an analogous rank bound without the spatiality hypotheses. The unmodified six-step gadget is explicitly excluded from certified results. Literature originality/priority is unchecked.
