# Universal depth-three normalization for continuous frames
22 October 2026.

THEOREM. If L is a continuous frame (for every a, a=∨{k:k≪a}, with domain-theoretic way-below relation), then for EVERY lower relation S⊆L×L,
 C(S)=H(V(H(S)))=V(H(V(S))).
In particular the universal H-V-H identity (*) holds for every rectangular bar on a continuous frame. This includes all locally compact locales (in the conventional continuous-frame sense) and strictly extends the preceding algebraic-frame proof.

Proof. For any S, R=V(H(S)) is V-closed and finitely H-closed, by the antitone-polarity finite-join argument. Let T=H(R), automatically H-closed. Suppose (a,b_i)∈T for all i, put b=∨b_i. For every k≪a, and every i, the definition of H(R) gives a cover a=∨_j a_ij with (a_ij,b_i)∈R. Form the directed set of finite joins of a_ij, whose supremum is a. Since k≪a, some finite subjoin covers k. Lowerness and finite H-closure of R give (k,b_i)∈R. Since R is V-closed, (k,b)∈R. Finally, a=∨_{k≪a}k, so (a,b)∈H(R)=T. Hence T is both H and V closed and equals C(S). The reversed equality follows by symmetry.

LOCALIZED VERSION. On an arbitrary frame L and arbitrary lower S, set R=V(H(S)), T=H(R). If (a,b_i)∈T for all i, b=∨ b_i, then (c(a),b)∈T, where c(a)=∨{k:k≪a} is the continuous approximation of a. Reason: each k≪a satisfies (k,b)∈R as above and their join belongs to H(R). Therefore any failure of vertical closure of T at (a,b) must be supported outside c(a), the part not generated by way-below elements. We do NOT assert c is a nucleus or idempotent.

CAUTION: Rational-bridge rank-three space X may or may not be locally compact; original rational copies of Q are not locally compact, so X certainly is not locally compact (open subspaces of locally compact Hausdorff spaces are locally compact, while Q is not). Thus this theorem does not establish sharpness of depth three within continuous frames. Nor does it imply universal depth three on arbitrary frames.

STATUS. Fully proven three-stage interchange for arbitrary lower relations on continuous frames, without special disjointness assumptions. General (*) for arbitrary frames still open.
