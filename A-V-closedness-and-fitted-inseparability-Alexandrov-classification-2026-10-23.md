# Project A: when is V closed, and when is I fitted?

23 October 2026. **Working structural note; audit before treating as final.**
This examines V=fit(I)=sc(d_I) in Q=L⊕L. It is not a claim of novelty over Picado–Pultr's semiclosed sublocales or Jakl–Suarez's closed-filter theory.

## 1. Intrinsic general criteria in any frame Q

Let d∈Q be arbitrary, write C(d) for the closed sublocale and O(w) for an open sublocale, and define

    F_d := {w∈Q: d∨w=1},
    S_d := fit(C(d)) = ⋂_{w∈F_d} O(w),
    j_d := nucleus of S_d.

By Picado–Pultr, *More on Subfitness and Fitness* (2013), §4.1.1,

    S_d = {x∈Q: ∀w∈F_d, w→x=x}.

Put

    k_d := j_d(0) = ⋀S_d
         = ⋀{x∈Q: ∀w∈F_d, w→x=x}.

Since d∈S_d, k_d≤d. The closure of S_d is C(k_d).

**PROPOSITION A.**

(i) C(d)=S_d iff k_d=d. This is equivalent to d∈F_1(Q) in Picado–Pultr §4.3.1.

(ii) S_d is closed iff F_{k_d}=F_d, equivalently

    ∀w∈Q,  d∨w=1 ⇒ k_d∨w=1.

If this holds S_d=C(k_d); if k_d<d, S_d is closed but strictly bigger than C(d).

(iii) Equivalently, S_d is closed iff the supplement-filter equivalence class

    [d]={a∈Q:F_a=F_d}

contains an element a∈F_1(Q). Such an a is unique, must satisfy a≤d, and is k_d. Indeed if F_a=F_d and C(a) is fitted, then S_d=S_a=C(a). Conversely if S_d=C(k_d), then k_d is fitted and F_{k_d}=F_d. Uniqueness follows from C(a)=S_a, so equal filters give equal closed sublocales, hence a=b. Moreover a≤b for every b in its supplement-filter equivalence class.

Proof of (i): C(d)⊆S_d⊆C(k_d). If k_d=d the endpoints coincide; conversely if S_d=C(d), their bottom elements coincide.

Proof of (ii): Since C(k_d) is the smallest closed sublocale containing S_d, S_d=C(k_d) iff C(k_d)⊆O(w) for all w∈F_d, i.e. iff k_d∨w=1 for every w∈F_d. Because k_d≤d, F_{k_d}⊆F_d automatically. Thus this is equality of filters.

Specialize all these statements to d=d_I in Q=L⊕L and S_d=V.

**Three mutually exclusive scenarios:** k_d=d (I=V); k_d<d and F_{k_d}=F_d (V closed, I<V); k_d<d and F_{k_d}⊊F_d (V not closed). In case 3 the closure C(k_d) strictly contains V.

## 2. Full order-theoretic classification for arbitrary Alexandrov locales

Let P be a preorder, L=Up(P), Q≅Up(P×P). Write x∼y when x,y lie in the same *comparability-connected component* P_i (generated by ≤ and ≥). Put

    N_P={(x,y): ↑x∩↑y !=∅}
       ={(x,y): x,y have a common upper bound},

and

    R_P=↑_{P×P} N_P
       ={(x,y):∃p≤x, q≤y, t≥p,q}
       =⋃_{t∈P} (↑↓t)×(↑↓t).

The complement of d_I is the down-set N_P. A bar is an up-set containing N_P, so the **least bar** is R_P. Thus

    V=O(R_P),   pt(I)=N_P,   pt(V)=R_P.

In particular V is OPEN for every Alexandrov locale.

**THEOREM B (closedness).** The following are equivalent:

1. V is closed;
2. R_P is clopen in P×P;
3. R_P=⋃_i P_i×P_i;
4. for every x,y in the same comparability component there exist p≤x, q≤y with a common upper bound.

Proof: O(R_P) is closed iff R_P is complemented. Complemented upper sets in an Alexandrov preorder are precisely unions of comparability components. The product components are P_i×P_j. Since N_P and hence R_P is nonempty on each diagonal component P_i² and R_P contains no points of off-diagonal component blocks, R_P clopen iff it equals ⋃_i P_i². This is exactly condition 4.

**THEOREM C (fittedness of I).** The following are equivalent:

1. I=V;
2. N_P=R_P;
3. N_P=⋃_i P_i×P_i;
4. every comparability component P_i is *upward directed*: every x,y∈P_i have a common upper bound.

Proof: I=C(d_I) has point set N_P, and V=O(R_P) has point set R_P, with these sublocales spatial (open and closed sublocales of the spatial Alexandrov square). Thus equality iff N_P=R_P. This equality makes N_P both up- and down-closed, forcing the union of the diagonal connected blocks, which is equivalent to directedness. The converse is immediate.

For connected P: V is closed iff R_P=P²; I=V iff P is upward directed. These are genuinely distinct conditions.

**Examples demonstrating the full trichotomy:**

A. Upward-directed connected P={p,q,t} with p<t, q<t: I=V=P², both closed.

B. Three-point *bottom fork* P={0,a,b}, 0<a,0<b: I<P², but (0,0)∈N_P implies R_P=P². Hence I<V=P², V closed.

C. Five-point *zigzag* P={p,q,r,u,v}, p<u,r<u,r<v,q<v: P is connected, yet (p,q)∉R_P because p,q are minimal without common upper bound; and (u,v)∈R_P witnessed by r≤u,v. As (p,q)≤(u,v), R_P is not down-closed. Hence V nonclosed. All four maximal pairs (u,u),(u,v),(v,u),(v,v) lie in R_P, so R_P is also DENSE; the closure of V is the whole square and k_{d_I}=0 in this example. Nevertheless V is proper.

A proper (not whole-square) closed V strictly greater than I can be obtained by adding an isolated second component to the three-point bottom fork.

## 3. Ordinary points on spatial localic squares; caveat

If Q≅O(T) is spatial (T=pt(Q), e.g. T=X×_top X when the localic square is spatial), then

    pt(V) = saturation(pt(I))
          = ↑_{specialization} pt(I).

Indeed bars are precisely the ordinary open subsets of T containing the closed point set pt(I), and the intersection of all these opens is the upward specialization saturation of pt(I).

Consequently:
- If V is closed, this saturation must be a closed subset of T.
- If I=V, pt(I) must be upward-saturated.
- Neither converse follows from spatiality of Q alone: V might be a nonspatial sublocale, and pointwise equality need not detect equality of sublocales.
- For a frame Q in which *every sublocale is spatial*, both pointwise criteria become sufficient as well.

Theorems B and C are unconditional in the Alexandrov class; they do not require a general theorem that all sublocales of an arbitrary spatial locale are spatial.

## 4. Another positive class: normal spaces with spatial localic square

**PROPOSITION D.** Let X be a normal topological space (closed disjoint sets can be separated by disjoint open neighborhoods; no T1 assumption), and assume Q=O(X)⊕O(X) is spatial. Then

    V=U=F

as sublocales.

Proof: Normality implies almost-normality, hence the previously audited arbitrary-frame theorem V_2=U=F and V⊆U. For any bar w and pair (x,y) outside w, the specialization closures cl{x},cl{y} are disjoint: otherwise z in their intersection implies (z,z)≤(x,y), so openness of w forces (z,z) outside w, contradicting the diagonal pullback δ*(w)=1. Normality separates these two closed sets by open neighborhoods whose closures can also be made disjoint (normal shrinking of closed sets). Thus (x,y) belongs to d_U. Spatiality of Q gives w∨d_U=1, so U⊆O(w) for every bar. Therefore U⊆V, giving equality.

This is NOT asserted for arbitrary nonspatial normal frames.

## 5. Research questions

1. Does almost-normality of arbitrary L imply **closedness** of V, even though it need not imply V=U? Existing classes (almost-normal Alexandrov, subfit, continuous Scott, Claude's two-chain counterexample) all give closed V, but this is not a proof.
2. Can one characterize F_{d_I} having a fitted representative using a directly two-coordinate frame law, rather than the general semiclosed-sublocale construction?
3. Seek a general reduction for the closure kernel k_{d_I}, perhaps via well-founded bar trees. The fittedness criterion I=V iff k=d_I may be easier to attack using rank-stratified nuclei than full closedness.
4. The Jakl–Suarez 2025 theory of **closed filters** F_a and their correspondence with fittings of closed sublocales (Prop 6.7 and Thm 6.8) is directly relevant; this section should be read before asserting priority for any complement-filter characterization.
