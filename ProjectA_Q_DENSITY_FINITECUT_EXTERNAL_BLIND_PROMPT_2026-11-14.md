## Scientific question

For a locale `X`, the full fitted diagonal `D(X)` is the intersection of all open-neighbourhood cover squares, whereas `D₂(X)` uses only finite covers. Let `W=cl D` and `W₂=cl D₂`. The universal conjecture **Q′** asks whether `W=W₂` for every locale. The weaker **Q** asks whether a rank-one finite-cover stabilization already forces full stabilization. **Both remain open.** A nonzero *closed/pointless* finite/full weak defect `D₂∧C(w_C)` need not disprove Q′: a *nonzero open* hole, or equivalent positive interior in the canonical `W₂`, is required.

The new work examines relative density in `W₂`, finite-coloring and incidence-profile restrictions on a hypothetical hole, an exact arbitrary-frame reduction of *all finite-cover tests to binary tests*, and two spatial negative controls. Most surprisingly, a Scott closed Baire relation has every finite-cover matching square dense but one countable-cover square zero. The same example separates finite- and full-pre-nucleus fixed-point sets while Q′ remains true, illustrating why the canonical `W₂` matters.
