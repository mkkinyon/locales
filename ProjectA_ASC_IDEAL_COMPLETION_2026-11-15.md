# Project A — Ideal completion and continuous-domain spatiality of the oriented relation

**Internally checked research note · 15 November 2026.** This is an extension of [the LC-A frontier note](ProjectA_ASC_LOCALLY_COMPACT_FRONTIER_2026-11-15.md). The deductions here are **not independently refereed**. They concern \(\mathcal A(X)\hookrightarrow X^2\), presented by \(u_1\le u_2\) for every \(u\in\mathcal O(X)\). The unrestricted (LC-A) question remains open.

## 1. The ideal-completion theorem

For a poset \(P\), write \(\operatorname{Idl}(P)\) for the dcpo of nonempty directed lower sets ordered by inclusion, with the Scott topology. Let
\[
P^\to=\{(p,q)\in P^2:p\le q\},
\]
with the coordinatewise order.

**Theorem 1.**
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(\operatorname{Idl}(P))\cong\operatorname{Idl}(P^\to)}
\tag{1.1}
\]
as locales, naturally in the poset \(P\). Thus the actual presented relation is spatial for the Scott locale of every algebraic dcpo, without stability or coherence assumptions.

### Frame-level proof

We give both inverse homomorphisms, since a pointwise correspondence of ideals would not prove (1.1).

The Scott frame of \(\operatorname{Idl}(P)\) is \(\operatorname{Up}(P)\), the frame of upper sets of \(P\). It is generated by \(e_p=\uparrow p\) with relations
\[
p\le q\Longrightarrow e_q\le e_p,\qquad
e_p\wedge e_q=\bigvee_{r\ge p,q}e_r,\qquad
\bigvee_{p\in P}e_p=1. \tag{1.2}
\]
Indeed, any family satisfying (1.2) in a frame \(L\) induces the unique frame map \(\operatorname{Up}(P)\to L\) given on an upper set \(U\) by \(U\mapsto\bigvee_{p\in U}e_p\). This verifies that (1.2) is a full presentation.

Write \(u_p,v_p\) for the generators of the two copies of \(\operatorname{Up}(P)\) in \(\mathcal O(\mathcal A(\operatorname{Idl}(P)))\). They each satisfy (1.2), together with \(u_p\le v_p\).

**First map.** For each \((p,q)\in P^\to\), put
\[
w_{p,q}:=u_p\wedge v_q.
\tag{1.3}
\]
These satisfy the ideal relations (1.2) for \(P^\to\). The covering relation follows from
\(\bigvee_{p\le q}w_{p,q}\ge\bigvee_p w_{p,p}=\bigvee_p u_p=1\).
For directedness, expand the product of two expressions (1.3) using the directedness relations for \(u\) and \(v\). A resulting term \(u_t\wedge v_s\) with \(t\not\le s\) may be rewritten, using \(u_t\le v_t\), as
\[
u_t\wedge v_s
=u_t\wedge v_t\wedge v_s
=\bigvee_{z\ge t,s}(u_t\wedge v_z).
\]
All the resulting comparable pairs are common coordinatewise upper bounds of the original pairs, and the reverse inclusion follows from the generator order relations. Hence
\[
w_{p,q}\wedge w_{r,s}
=\bigvee_{\substack{(t,z)\in P^\to\\ t\ge p,r;\ z\ge q,s}}w_{t,z}.
\]
Thus (1.3) induces a frame map \(F:\operatorname{Up}(P^\to)\to\mathcal O(\mathcal A(\operatorname{Idl}(P)))\).

**Reverse map.** In \(\operatorname{Up}(P^\to)\), let \(w_{r,s}=\uparrow(r,s)\), and define
\[
U_p=\bigvee_{\substack{(r,s)\in P^\to\\r\ge p}}w_{r,s},
\qquad
V_q=\bigvee_{\substack{(r,s)\in P^\to\\s\ge q}}w_{r,s}.
\tag{1.4}
\]
Both families satisfy (1.2): covers and order relations are immediate, and directedness follows by expanding the meets and using the corresponding directedness relation for \(w\). Also \(U_p\le V_p\), since \(r\ge p\) and \(r\le s\) imply \(s\ge p\). Therefore (1.4) induces a frame map \(G:\mathcal O(\mathcal A(\operatorname{Idl}(P)))\to\operatorname{Up}(P^\to)\).

**Inverses.** On the first-coordinate generator,
\[
F(G(u_p))=\bigvee_{r\ge p,\ r\le s}u_r\wedge v_s=u_p,
\]
because each term is below \(u_p\) and the term \(r=s=p\) is exactly \(u_p\). On the second-coordinate generator,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_q
 &=v_q\wedge\bigvee_r u_r
 =\bigvee_r u_r\wedge v_r\wedge v_q\\
 &=\bigvee_r\bigvee_{s\ge r,q}u_r\wedge v_s
 =F(G(v_q)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.5}
\]
The reverse inequality in (1.5) is included automatically in the displayed equality.

Finally, \(G(F(w_{p,q}))=U_p\wedge V_q=w_{p,q}\): one inequality follows because \(w_{p,q}\le U_p,V_q\); for the other, expand \(U_p\wedge V_q\) by directedness of \(P^\to\). Every surviving generator \(w_{t,z}\) satisfies \(t\ge p,\ z\ge q\), and is therefore below \(w_{p,q}\). The frame maps are mutually inverse. \(\square\)

**Spatiality.** The upper-set frame \(\operatorname{Up}(P^\to)\) is spatial: distinct upper sets are distinguished by evaluation at a pair \((p,q)\in P^\to\). No prime-ideal theorem is needed for this final step.

On ordinary points, (1.1) identifies comparable ideals \(I\subseteq J\) of \(P\) with the ideal
\(\{(p,q):p\in I,\ q\in J,\ p\le q\}\) of \(P^\to\). This point description is a consequence of the frame proof, not a substitute for it.

## 2. Continuous dcpos are retracts of their ideal completions

Let \(D\) be a **continuous dcpo**, with its Scott topology. Define
\[
s:D\longrightarrow\operatorname{Idl}(D),
\quad s(x)=\{a\in D:a\ll x\};
\qquad
r:\operatorname{Idl}(D)\longrightarrow D,
\quad r(I)=\sup I.
\tag{2.1}
\]

- \(s(x)\) is a nonempty directed lower set, by continuity of \(D\).
- \(s\) is Scott-continuous: the inverse image of the basic Scott open \(\{I:a\in I\}\) is \(\{x:a\ll x\}\), Scott-open by interpolation of \(\ll\) in a continuous dcpo.
- \(r\) is Scott-continuous: it preserves directed suprema of ideals, which are their unions.
- \(rs=\mathrm{id}_D\) by continuity.

Thus the Scott locale of \(D\) is a locale retract of the algebraic Scott locale of \(\operatorname{Idl}(D)\). This standard domain-theoretic retraction is important here because it does **not** assume stable local compactness.

## 3. The continuous-domain theorem

**Theorem 2.** For every continuous dcpo \(D\), with its Scott locale \(X\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(X)=[G_{\le_D}]\text{ is spatial}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

*Proof.* The functor \(\mathcal A\) preserves any locale retraction, since every locale map \(f\) sends a pair with \(u_1\le u_2\) to such a pair after composing with \(f\). Apply \(\mathcal A\) to the split pair (2.1). Then
\[
\mathcal A(D)\text{ is a locale retract of }
\mathcal A(\operatorname{Idl}(D)).
\]
The latter is spatial by Theorem 1, and a locale retract of a spatial locale is spatial. \(\square\)

This subsumes the earlier positive theorem for sober Alexandrov spaces: a sober Alexandrov poset has the ascending chain condition, hence is an algebraic dcpo with all directed suprema attained and every element compact.

## 4. What this does and does not prove

**Settled within this internally checked note:**
- all Scott locales of algebraic dcpos;
- all Scott locales of continuous dcpos (including nonalgebraic ones);
- all sober Alexandrov spaces, even noncoherent ones.

**Not settled:** The general locally compact sober space need not be the Scott space of a continuous dcpo. The retraction (2.1) is a statement about the *point order* of a continuous dcpo, not a retraction for an arbitrary locally compact sober space or an arbitrary continuous frame. In particular it does not repair the meet-stability failure of the frame ideal-completion method in [the canonical (A-SC) proof](ProjectA_ASC_CANONICAL_2026-11-15.md).

A negative (LC-A) example, if it exists, must therefore lie outside both the stably locally compact class and the continuous-dcpo Scott class, as well as escaping the previously documented open-cover, bottom-lift, and semilattice-retraction tests.
